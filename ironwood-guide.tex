\begin{abstract}
This volume constructs the Zcash Orchard shielded-payment protocol as
carried by its current value pool, Ironwood. The presentation follows
value through its lifecycle: a recipient prepares keys and
an address, a note is minted and delivered, it rests in the commitment
tree, it is spent --- retiring its nullifier, balancing its value
commitment, authorised by its re-randomised signature --- change
returns, and value crosses the boundary between the sealed Orchard
pool and Ironwood. Each piece of machinery is constructed at the
lifecycle stage that first demands it and assembled into the Action
statement every shielded transfer proves; the volume then descends to
the circuit beneath the statement, pays the deferred security debts in
end-to-end reductions, and closes with the proof-system lineage and
the quantum horizon. It assumes the \emph{Math Guide}, the
\emph{Crypto Guide}, the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}, and the
\emph{Consensus Guide}, and is non-normative throughout: the protocol
specification and the ZIPs remain authoritative.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: the life of a shielded zatoshi}\label{sec:intro}

% brief c0-5, c0-1
Halo 2 is a general-purpose proving system, and it has no notion of
money. It can attest that a prover knows a witness satisfying a fixed
arithmetic relation, but nothing in it says what a coin is, who owns
one, or when one has been spent twice. The Orchard application turns
that general instrument into a shielded-payment protocol by one act of
specification: it fixes a single relation --- the \emph{Action
statement} --- that every shielded transfer must prove, and lets the
consensus rules of a public chain enforce the rest. This volume
constructs that relation and everything around it, deriving each
cryptographic object as the answer to one sub-problem, tracing a
complete payment from Alice to Bob end to end, and only then stating
the Action relation formally (\S\ref{sec:action}). It assumes three
earlier Arboretum volumes: the \emph{Math Guide} for the underlying
mathematics, the \emph{Crypto Guide} for hash functions, commitments,
signatures, key agreement, zero knowledge, and the rest of the
primitive toolbox, and the \emph{Halo 2 Guide} for the proof system
itself. Here those tools are assembled into the Orchard protocol as
carried by its current pool, Ironwood --- and the protagonist of the
assembly is one \emph{zatoshi}, the atomic unit of ZEC, Zcash's native
currency, followed from the moment it turns shielded.

\subsection{Two pools, one protocol}\label{sec:intro-pools}

% brief c0-2, c0-3 (vocabulary lives here; sec:pools back-cites it)
Zcash changes its consensus rules by \emph{network upgrades}:
scheduled rule changes, each activating at a block height this volume
never needs numerically and carrying a name --- NU5, NU6.3 --- that
serves only to order events. A \emph{Zcash Improvement Proposal}
(ZIP) is the document form in which protocol changes are specified;
ZIP-229 fixes the vocabulary used throughout. The \emph{Orchard protocol} is the shared cryptographic
construction --- the curves, Sinsemilla, the Action circuit, notes,
commitments, nullifiers, keys, and note encryption, an inventory of
names the roadmap (\S\ref{sec:intro-roadmap}) assigns to the sections
that construct them --- and it survives the pool transition. The \emph{Orchard pool} is the legacy value pool:
it has run the Orchard protocol since NU5, and at NU6.3 it is sealed
to spend-only. The \emph{Ironwood pool} is the current pool, carrying
the same protocol. One protocol, two carriers: when a statement holds
for the shared cryptography we say ``Orchard'', and when it depends on
which pool a note lives in we name the pool.

% brief c3-17 (one sentence per door; formalised in sec:pools)
Value reaches a shielded pool through three doors, each public in
amount. A \emph{shielding} transaction spends transparent value into
the pool; \emph{shielded coinbase} lets the block reward --- freshly
issued coins --- arrive shielded directly; and \emph{migration} moves
value from another shielded pool, which for this volume's two pools
means out of sealed Orchard into Ironwood. All three doors are
formalised in \S\ref{sec:pools}; their publicity is by design, for
what a pool hides is who holds which note, never how much the pool as
a whole contains.

\subsection{The four requirements on a shielded payment}
\label{sec:intro-reqs}

% brief c0-6
A transparent ledger --- Bitcoin, or Zcash's transparent pool ---
records, for every unspent output, its owner and its denomination, and
a payment publicly transfers an output between owners. A shielded
payment must achieve the same effects --- transfer of value,
prevention of double-spending, conservation of total supply --- while
disclosing none of them. Four requirements must hold simultaneously:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\textbf{(R\arabic*)}]
\item represent value so that its owner, its amount, and its
  very existence stay hidden, yet the owner can later prove
  possession --- solved by notes and note commitments
  (\S\ref{sec:mint});
\item prove possession of a real, unspent note without identifying
  which note --- solved by the commitment tree with a membership
  proof (\S\ref{sec:tree});
\item prevent double-spending without a public list of which notes an
  owner has spent --- solved by nullifiers (\S\ref{sec:spend});
\item prove that no one created value without revealing any amount
  --- solved by value commitments and the binding signature
  (\S\ref{sec:spend}).
\end{enumerate}

Each stage of the journey ahead opens by naming the requirement it
discharges, and the loop is closed at the end: \S\ref{sec:security}
proves that the assembled protocol meets all four.

\subsection{Method and roadmap: the journey}\label{sec:intro-roadmap}

% brief c0-4 (method + roadmap; "three circuit versions" stated here)
A protocol this dense can be presented from its symbols or from the
problems it solves; we take the second way, and give it a narrative
spine. The volume follows value through the system --- a
recipient prepares to be paid, a note is minted and delivered, it
rests in the commitment tree, it is spent, change returns, and at the
end of its shielded life value exits back across the pool boundary,
in public amount --- with each piece of protocol machinery
constructed at the lifecycle stage that first demands it.

After the curve-level preliminaries and the Sinsemilla hash are packed
once (\S\ref{sec:toolbox}), each object arrives as the answer to one
sub-problem. The recipient's keys and addresses come first
(\S\ref{sec:keys}). Notes and note commitments represent value
(\S\ref{sec:mint}); note encryption and trial decryption deliver a
note privately and let its owner detect it (\S\ref{sec:delivery}); the
commitment tree and its membership path prove ownership of a real
unspent note without revealing which (\S\ref{sec:tree}); nullifiers
prevent double-spending, value commitments and the binding signature
conserve value without disclosure, and the RedPallas signature scheme
authorises the spend (\S\ref{sec:spend}). The unit these pieces
assemble into is the \emph{Action} --- one Action spends one old note
and creates one new one --- and \S\ref{sec:action} walks one payment
end to end, shows how a node verifies it without seeing any note, and
states the relation the proof carries. The two pools and their doors
follow (\S\ref{sec:pools}), then the seal and the turnstile that empty
the legacy pool (\S\ref{sec:turnstile}), the circuit beneath the
statement (\S\ref{sec:circuit}), and the end-to-end security
reductions that pay the volume's deferred debts (\S\ref{sec:security}).
A closing pair of sections looks backward and forward: the lineage
Sprout $\to$ Sapling $\to$ Orchard --- three generations of shielded
proof, and within Orchard three circuit versions ending at Ironwood
(\S\ref{sec:lineage}) --- and the quantum horizon (\S\ref{sec:pq}).

\section{Provisions: hashing on Pallas and Sinsemilla}\label{sec:toolbox}

Every stage of the journey ahead draws on the same two workhorses: a small
kit of curve-level encodings and hash-to-curve maps, and the Sinsemilla
hash built from them. Rather than interrupt the narrative to construct
these where each stage first needs them, we pack them here, once --- the
one deliberate non-narrative cost of the volume, paid up front and kept
tight. All curve-level primitives of this section operate on the Pallas
group $\Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})$: the prime-order group of points of the
Pallas curve, one half of the Pasta cycle of curves and fields constructed
in the \emph{Math Guide}, \S ``Elliptic curves''.

\subsection{Points to field elements: \texorpdfstring{$\Extract$}{Extract},
the star encoding, and \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{LE}_n$}{LEn}}
\label{sec:toolbox-encodings}

\paragraph{The coordinate map $\Extract$.}
The map $\Extract : \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}) \to \F_{p_{\Pallas}}$ sends a
curve point to its $x$-coordinate, $\Extract(x, y) := x$, with
$\Extract(\mathcal{O}) := 0$ by convention. It appears wherever a later
object must be a \emph{field element} rather than a curve point --- the
Merkle tree (\S\ref{sec:tree}), the nullifier set (\S\ref{sec:spend}),
and the proof's public inputs (\S\ref{sec:action}) are all field
elements. The map is not injective ($\pm(x, y)$ share an $x$),
which is harmless here: the protocol only ever requires the
$x$-coordinate as a binding digest, never to recover the point.

\paragraph{The point encoding $\star$.}
The starred form $P^\star \in \{0,1\}^{256}$ is the canonical
bit-encoding of a point $P \in \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})$: its
$x$-coordinate (an element of $\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$, which fits in $255$
bits since $p_{\Pallas} < 2^{255}$) packed into a $256$-bit string
together with one sign bit, placed in the otherwise-unused top bit,
recording which of the two square roots $\pm y$ is meant. Unlike
$\Extract$, this encoding is injective --- it determines $P$ completely
--- and it is what is fed, as a bit-string, into a hash or commitment.

\paragraph{Integer encodings $\mathrm{LE}_n$.}
The string $\mathrm{LE}_n(m) \in \{0,1\}^n$ is the $n$-bit little-endian
encoding of an integer $m \in \{0, \dots, 2^n - 1\}$: the bit string
$b_0 b_1 \cdots b_{n-1}$ with $m = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} b_i\, 2^{i}$ (a
field element encodes via its integer representative). Three widths
recur: $\mathrm{LE}_{32}$ indexes the Sinsemilla generator table
(\S\ref{sec:toolbox-sinsemilla}), $\mathrm{LE}_{10}$ tags the
Merkle-tree layers (\S\ref{sec:tree}), and $\mathrm{LE}_{64}$,
$\mathrm{LE}_{255}$ encode note values and field elements
(\S\ref{sec:mint}).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\GroupHash$}{GroupHash}, domain separation,
and nothing-up-my-sleeve generators}\label{sec:toolbox-grouphash}

\paragraph{The hash-to-curve $\GroupHash$.}
The map $\GroupHash^{\G} : \{0,1\}^* \times \{0,1\}^* \to \G$ takes a
pair (domain separator $D$, message $M$) and returns a point of the
group $\G$. It is \emph{deterministic} (the same input always yields the
same point), \emph{public} (anyone can evaluate it), and
\emph{efficiently computable}. It instantiates the IETF hash-to-curve
standard: $\mathrm{hash\_to\_field}$ expands $(D, M)$ --- via
$\mathrm{expand\_message\_xmd}$ over BLAKE2b-512, with $D$ embedded in
the domain-separation tag --- into \emph{two} base-field elements; each
passes through the simplified SWU map onto a curve isogenous to $\G$;
and the sum of the two resulting points is carried through the isogeny
to $\G$ (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S ``Hashing to a curve point'',
develops this construction in full). Everything that follows uses only
that $\GroupHash$ is deterministic, public, and behaves like a random
choice of point.

That last property is the purpose of the construction. Because every
stage is public and deterministic, the public strings $D, M$ pin down
the output $\GroupHash^{\G}(D, M)$, so no party can have chosen it to
satisfy a hidden relation such as $P_2 = [s]P_1$ for a secret $s$; such
points are called \emph{nothing-up-my-sleeve} generators. For the
security arguments we \emph{model} $\GroupHash$ as a random oracle into
$\G$ (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S ``The random oracle model''): its
outputs are treated as independent uniform points, so the family it
produces has no efficiently-findable non-trivial discrete-log relation
$\sum_i [a_i] P_i = \mathcal{O}$ (discrete logarithms: the \emph{Crypto
Guide}, \S ``The discrete logarithm problem'') --- precisely the
assumption the Sinsemilla binding and collision-resistance reductions
invoke (\S\ref{sec:security}). As with all random-oracle arguments this
is a strong, standard, but unprovable modelling heuristic about the
concrete hash, not a theorem.

\paragraph{Domain separators.}
A \emph{domain separator} $D \in \{0,1\}^*$ is a fixed ASCII byte string
naming the use site, fed to $\GroupHash$ so that logically distinct uses
draw independent, unrelated generators (a collision found in one context
cannot be transported to another). The strings are part of the protocol
specification; the ones relevant to this volume are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard}
  & \text{fixed generators } G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}, G^{\mathsf{nf}}
    \text{ (\S\ref{sec:keys}, \S\ref{sec:spend}),}\\
\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}gd}
  & \text{the diversified base } \gd \text{ (\S\ref{sec:keys}),}\\
\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}NoteCommit}
  & \text{note commitments (\S\ref{sec:mint}),}\\
\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}CommitIvk}
  & \text{the } \ivk \text{ commitment (\S\ref{sec:keys}),}\\
\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}MerkleCRH}
  & \text{the Merkle-tree node hash (\S\ref{sec:tree}),}\\
\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}cv}
  & \text{the value-commitment bases (\S\ref{sec:spend}),}\\
\mathtt{z.cash:SinsemillaQ},\ \mathtt{z.cash:SinsemillaS}
  & \text{the Sinsemilla } Q \text{ point and } S \text{ table
    (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-sinsemilla}).}
\end{array}
\]

\subsection{Sinsemilla: hash, commitment, and short forms}
\label{sec:toolbox-sinsemilla}

Two objects the journey will build --- the note commitment
(\S\ref{sec:mint}) and the Merkle-tree node hash (\S\ref{sec:tree}) ---
require the same primitive: a commitment/hash that the spender must
recompute \emph{inside the zero-knowledge circuit}, the arithmetised
form (the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S ``PLONKish arithmetisation'') of the
spend statement (\S\ref{sec:action}). In-circuit cost is therefore the
binding constraint, and it is what Sinsemilla (Bowe and Hopwood) is
designed to minimise.
Its collision resistance (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S ``Security
notions: preimage, second-preimage, and collision resistance'') reduces
to discrete-log hardness on a prime-order curve, so it introduces no
assumption beyond the DL hardness that Orchard's algebraic core already
requires. And it builds directly on the provisions above: its generators
are $\GroupHash$ outputs (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-grouphash}), and its short
form returns an $\Extract$ed coordinate (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}).

\begin{construction}[Sinsemilla]\label{def:sinsemilla}
Fix a prime-order group $\G$ (Orchard: $\Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})$), a
chunk width $k = 10$, and a maximum chunk count $c = 253$. From the
domain separator $D$,
derive the $2^k + 1$ Sinsemilla generators:
\begin{align*}
Q(D) &:= \GroupHash^{\G}(\mathtt{z.cash:SinsemillaQ},\, D) \in \G, \\
P[m] &:= \GroupHash^{\G}(\mathtt{z.cash:SinsemillaS},\,
        \mathrm{LE}_{32}(m)) \in \G, \qquad 0 \le m < 2^k.
\end{align*}
The point $Q(D)$ is the domain-dependent starting accumulator; the $2^k$
points $P[0], \dots, P[2^k - 1]$ form a fixed table indexed by a $k$-bit
chunk value, shared across all Sinsemilla domains. For a message $M$ of
at most $kc=2530$ bits, split into $k$-bit chunks
$m_1, \dots, m_\ell$ with $\ell \le c$ (so each
$m_i \in \{0, \dots, 2^k - 1\}$ indexes the table),
\[
\Sinsemilla_D(M) := \Acc_\ell, \quad \Acc_0 := Q(D), \quad
\Acc_{i+1} := (\Acc_i \dotplus P[m_{i+1}]) \dotplus \Acc_i,
\]
with $\dotplus$ \emph{incomplete} short-Weierstrass addition (the
\emph{Math Guide}, \S ``Elliptic curves''). The output
$\Acc_\ell \in \G$ is a curve point. The deployed hash zero-pads $M$ on
the right to a multiple of $k$ bits before chunking (protocol
specification \S\,5.4.1.9); every use in this volume fixes the input
bit-length, and the padding is why Theorem~\ref{thm:sinsemilla-cr}
below is restricted to fixed-length inputs.
\end{construction}

\begin{theorem}[Sinsemilla collision resistance]\label{thm:sinsemilla-cr}
For fixed-length inputs, $\Sinsemilla_D$ is collision-resistant assuming
DL on $\G$ and modelling the generator derivation as a random oracle.
\end{theorem}

The proof is deferred: Theorem~\ref{thm:sinsemilla-cr} is proved in
\S\ref{sec:security}, by reducing a collision to a non-trivial
discrete-log relation among the random-oracle-derived generators --- the
assumption named in \S\ref{sec:toolbox-grouphash}. Every later security
property of the volume follows the same pattern: stated where its object
is constructed, proved once in \S\ref{sec:security}.

\begin{definition}[Sinsemilla commitment and short forms]
\label{def:sinsemilla-commit}
The \emph{Sinsemilla commitment} adds a hiding term to the hash:
\[
\mathsf{SinsemillaCommit}_r(D, M) :=
  \Sinsemilla_{D \,\|\, \texttt{-M}}(M) + [r]\, H_D \;\in\; \G,
\]
where $r \in \F_{p_{\Vesta}}$ is the commitment randomness (trapdoor;
the Pasta cycle identifies $\F_{p_{\Vesta}}$ with the scalar field of
Pallas), the hash runs in the suffixed domain $D \,\|\, \texttt{-M}$,
and $H_D \in \G$ is a fixed blinding generator for the domain $D$,
derived as the $\GroupHash$ output
\[
H_D := \GroupHash^{\G}\bigl(D \,\|\, \texttt{-r},\ \varepsilon\bigr)
\]
--- the suffix \texttt{-r} appended to the domain string, with the empty
message $\varepsilon$ (a nothing-up-my-sleeve point,
\S\ref{sec:toolbox-grouphash}). The \emph{short commitment} returns only
its $x$-coordinate, a base-field element rather than a curve point:
\[
\ShortCommit_r(D, M) :=
  \Extract\bigl(\mathsf{SinsemillaCommit}_r(D, M)\bigr)
  \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Pallas}}.
\]
The unkeyed \emph{short hash} omits the blinding term altogether:
\[
\mathsf{ShortHash}_D(M) := \Extract\bigl(\Sinsemilla_D(M)\bigr)
  \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Pallas}},
\]
the unblinded analogue of $\ShortCommit$ --- no randomness argument,
collision-resistant but not hiding. Collision resistance of the two
short forms needs one case beyond Theorem~\ref{thm:sinsemilla-cr}: the
theorem binds curve points, while $\Extract$ identifies a point with
its negation (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}), so an $x$-coordinate
collision may also be a pair of opposite points. Both cases are
discharged together in \S\ref{sec:security}.
\end{definition}

Why is the commitment binding and hiding? Because $H_D$ is a
$\GroupHash$ output in its own sub-domain ($D \,\|\, \texttt{-r}$), no
party knows a discrete-log relation between it and the generators
$Q(D \,\|\, \texttt{-M}), P[m]$ that the hash uses; this independence is
exactly what keeps the commitment binding --- a party knowing such a
relation could trade the blinding term against the message and open one
commitment to two distinct messages. Hiding needs no assumption at all:
for uniform $r$ the term $[r] H_D$ is uniform on $\G$ and masks the hash
completely. This is the same division of roles the independent base $H$
supports in a Pedersen commitment (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S ``The
Pedersen commitment'').

For independent uniform $r$, the commitment primitive is therefore
\emph{perfectly hiding} and
\emph{computationally binding} (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S ``Hiding and
binding'') --- the two properties \S\ref{sec:mint} requires of
$\NoteCommit$. Indeed the note commitment $\NoteCommit_{\rcm}(\cdot)$ is
exactly $\mathsf{SinsemillaCommit}_{\rcm}$ under the domain
$\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}NoteCommit}$. The short forms appear
wherever the consumer requires a field element instead of a point:
$\mathsf{ShortHash}$ in the Merkle-tree node hash (\S\ref{sec:tree}),
$\ShortCommit$ in the incoming viewing key $\ivk$ (\S\ref{sec:keys}).
The latter use enters through a named instance that fixes the domain and
the message encoding:
\[
\CommitIvk_{\rivk}(x, y) := \ShortCommit_{\rivk}\bigl(
  \mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}CommitIvk},\
  \mathrm{LE}_{255}(x) \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{255}(y)\bigr),
\]
taking two field elements and concatenating their $255$-bit
little-endian encodings into the $510$-bit message. Its output lies in
$\{1, \dots, p_{\Pallas} - 1\}$: the zero output cannot occur for a
valid key, so $\ivk$ is a usable scalar.

\subsection{Why Sinsemilla: in-circuit cost}\label{sec:toolbox-cost}

% compute/spend_quantities.py: CommitIvk message 255 + 255 = 510 bits
%   = 51 ten-bit chunks
Each round of Construction~\ref{def:sinsemilla} consumes a whole
$k$-bit chunk via one table lookup of $P[m]$ and two incomplete
additions, so a $510$-bit message costs $51$ rounds rather than $510$
bit-operations. In a PLONKish circuit with lookups (the \emph{Halo 2
Guide}, \S ``PLONKish arithmetisation'' and \S ``The lookup argument'')
this is dramatically cheaper than the bit-by-bit Pedersen hash (the
\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S ``Commitment schemes'') of the previous
shielded generation, Sapling --- which is the reason the protocol
changed hash between generations. The provisions are packed; the
journey can now set out.

\section{The recipient: keys and addresses}\label{sec:keys}

The journey begins with someone able to receive. Before a single zatoshi
moves, the recipient must hold the material that later stages will lean
on, so we build the Orchard machinery bottom-up, beginning with the keys:
everything later --- addresses, note commitments, nullifiers --- derives
from them. A practical requirement shapes the key hierarchy: different
parties should be able to perform different operations. The owner can
spend. An auditor should be able to \emph{see} incoming and outgoing
payments without being able to spend. A point-of-sale terminal should be
able to \emph{detect} incoming payments without seeing outgoing ones.
Each of these is a distinct capability, and the key tree makes each
capability a separate derived key, so the owner can hand out a
lower-level key without surrendering the powers above it.

\subsection{From \texorpdfstring{$\mathsf{sk}$}{sk} to the spend-side
secrets}\label{sec:keys-spend}

\begin{definition}[Spending key]\label{def:sk}
The root secret of the Orchard key hierarchy is a $32$-byte
\emph{spending key} $\mathsf{sk}$. Key generation repeatedly samples a
candidate uniformly from $\{0,1\}^{256}$ and rejects it if it derives
$\ask=0$, or if either the external or internal $\CommitIvk$ output is
$0$ or $\bot$. In a deployed wallet the candidate typically derives
from a seed phrase via ZIP-32 hierarchical-deterministic derivation.
Every other key is a deterministic function of an accepted
$\mathsf{sk}$.
\end{definition}

Deriving sub-secrets from one root requires a pseudorandom function. A
\emph{pseudorandom function} (PRF) is a keyed family $\{f_K\}_K$ whose
outputs are, for a secret uniform key $K$, computationally
indistinguishable from those of a truly random function (the
\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Pseudorandom functions and permutations'').

\begin{definition}[Expansion function and reduction maps]%
\label{def:prfexpand}
The \emph{expansion function}
$\mathsf{PRFexpand} : \{0,1\}^{256} \times \{0,1\}^{*} \to
\{0,1\}^{512}$ is
\[
\mathsf{PRFexpand}(\mathsf{sk}, t) := \mathrm{BLAKE2b\text{-}512}
\bigl(\texttt{``Zcash\_ExpandSeed''},\ \mathsf{sk} \,\|\, t\bigr),
\]
unkeyed $\mathrm{BLAKE2b\text{-}512}$ --- a conventional,
non-arithmetisation-friendly hash with a $512$-bit ($64$-byte) digest
(the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``PRFs from hash functions: length extension
and keyed BLAKE2'') --- with the fixed $16$-byte personalisation string
$\texttt{``Zcash\_ExpandSeed''}$ and input $\mathsf{sk} \,\|\, t$. The
protocol obtains its PRF not from BLAKE2b's native keyed mode but as
\emph{personalised, unkeyed} BLAKE2b over the concatenation
$\mathsf{sk} \,\|\, t$: it is required to behave as a PRF of $t$ for a
secret uniform $\mathsf{sk}$.
The secret $\mathsf{sk}$ acts as the PRF key, and the leading bytes of
$t$ are a domain-separation tag identifying which sub-secret the call
produces; we write the key as a subscript,
$\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}(t)$, when convenient.

The $512$-bit output is reduced into the required field or scalar set by
one of two \emph{reduction maps}, which read it as an integer and reduce
modulo the relevant prime ($p_{\Pallas}$ and $p_{\Vesta}$ the Pallas
base- and scalar-field orders of \S\ref{sec:toolbox}):
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{ToScalar}(x) &:= \mathrm{LEOS2IP}_{512}(x) \bmod p_{\Vesta}
  \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Vesta}}, \\
\mathrm{ToBase}(x) &:= \mathrm{LEOS2IP}_{512}(x) \bmod p_{\Pallas}
  \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Pallas}},
\end{align*}
where $\mathrm{LEOS2IP}_{512}$ is the little-endian
octet-string-to-integer map sending a $64$-byte string to the integer
$\sum_{i=0}^{63} x_i\,256^{i} \in \{0, \dots, 2^{512}-1\}$.
\end{definition}

% compute/spend_quantities.py: 512-bit reduction bias
%   O(2^254/2^512) = O(2^-258)
The width is deliberate. Because the value being reduced is $512$ bits
wide while each modulus is just over $2^{254}$ (of bit length $255$),
the modular reduction is statistically close to uniform on
the target set: the modular bias is $O(2^{254}/2^{512}) = O(2^{-258})$,
negligible. Under the PRF assumption, the distinct tag bytes
$\mathtt{0x06}$, $\mathtt{0x07}$, $\mathtt{0x08}$ below computationally
domain-separate the three derived outputs.

\begin{construction}[Spend-side secrets and the spend validating key]%
\label{def:keys-spend}
From $\mathsf{sk}$ derive the three spend-side secrets
\begin{align*}
\ask &:= \mathrm{ToScalar}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}
  (\mathtt{0x06})\bigr) \in \F_{p_{\Vesta}}
  && \text{(the \emph{spend authorising key}),} \\
\nksym &:= \mathrm{ToBase}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}
  (\mathtt{0x07})\bigr) \in \F_{p_{\Pallas}}
  && \text{(the \emph{nullifier key}),} \\
\rivk &:= \mathrm{ToScalar}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}
  (\mathtt{0x08})\bigr) \in \F_{p_{\Vesta}}
  && \text{(the \emph{CommitIvk randomness}),}
\end{align*}
so that $\ask$ and $\rivk$ are scalars in $\F_{p_{\Vesta}}$ (the scalar
field of Pallas, i.e.\ the integers mod the Pallas group order
$p_{\Vesta}$), while $\nksym$ is a base-field element in
$\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$. The two targets differ only because the protocol
uses $\nksym$ as a field element --- it keys the PRF at the heart of
the derivation of the \emph{nullifier}, the base-field element whose
publication marks a note spent, constructed in \S\ref{sec:spend} ---
whereas $\ask$ and $\rivk$ are scalars multiplying group elements.

Let $G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}} := \GroupHash^{\Pallas}
(\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard}, \mathtt{G}) \in \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})$, a
fixed, publicly-known generator of the Pallas group (a
nothing-up-my-sleeve point, \S\ref{sec:toolbox}). The \emph{spend
validating key} is
\[
\aksym := [\ask]\,G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}} \in \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}).
\]
Key generation canonicalises its sign: if $[\ask]\,G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$
has odd $y$-coordinate, $\ask$ is replaced by $-\ask$, so that $\aksym$
always has even $y$ and the $x$-coordinate $\Extract(\aksym)$
(\S\ref{sec:toolbox}) alone determines the point.
\end{construction}

\subsection{Viewing keys}\label{sec:keys-view}

\begin{construction}[Viewing keys]\label{def:keys-view}
The \emph{full viewing key} bundles the three quantities needed to
recognise and decrypt one's own notes:
\[
\fvk := (\aksym,\, \nksym,\, \rivk) \;\in\;
\Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}) \times \F_{p_{\Pallas}} \times \F_{p_{\Vesta}}.
\]
From $\fvk$ derive
\begin{align*}
\ivk &:= \CommitIvk_{\rivk}\bigl(\Extract(\aksym),\ \nksym\bigr)
  \in \{1, \dots, p_{\Pallas}-1\} \subset \F_{p_{\Pallas}}, \\
K &:= \mathrm{LE}_{256}(\rivk) \in \{0,1\}^{256}, \\
R &:= \mathsf{PRFexpand}\bigl(K,\ \mathtt{0x82} \,\|\,
  \mathrm{I2LEOSP}_{256}(\aksym) \,\|\, \mathrm{I2LEOSP}_{256}(\nksym)
  \bigr) \in \{0,1\}^{512}, \\
(\dk, \ovk) &:= \bigl(R_{[0\,..\,256)},\ R_{[256\,..\,512)}\bigr),
\end{align*}
the incoming-viewing scalar $\ivk$, the \emph{diversifier key} $\dk$,
and the \emph{outgoing viewing key} $\ovk$. In protocol terminology the
full \emph{incoming viewing key} is the pair $(\dk,\ivk)$: $\ivk$ alone
performs trial decryption, while $\dk$ is additionally needed to derive
diversified addresses. Here $\CommitIvk$ is the named $\ShortCommit$ instance of
\S\ref{sec:toolbox}; $\mathrm{LE}_{256}$ is the $256$-bit little-endian
bit encoding of \S\ref{sec:toolbox}, giving $K$ the type
$\mathsf{PRFexpand}$ requires of its key; $\Extract(\aksym)$ is the
$x$-coordinate of $\aksym$, which after the sign canonicalisation of
Construction~\ref{def:keys-spend} determines the point; and
$\mathrm{I2LEOSP}_{256}$ denotes the $32$-byte little-endian encoding of
a field element or point.
\end{construction}

Precision on the $\dk/\ovk$ derivation, because the formula is easy to
misread: a \emph{single} $512$-bit $\mathsf{PRFexpand}$ output $R$,
keyed by $\rivk$ over the input
$\mathtt{0x82} \,\|\, \aksym \,\|\, \nksym$ (not over an empty message),
is split into its two $256$-bit halves --- $\dk$ the first, $\ovk$ the
second. The diversifier $d$ of the next subsection is \emph{not} part of
this split; it derives afterwards from $\dk$.

\subsection{Diversified addresses}\label{sec:keys-addr}

\begin{construction}[Diversified addresses]\label{def:keys-addr}
For any index $d_{\mathrm{plain}} \in \{0,1\}^{88}$, define
\begin{align*}
d &:= \mathsf{FF1\text{-}AES256}_{\dk}(d_{\mathrm{plain}})
  \in \{0,1\}^{88}
  && \text{(the \emph{diversifier}),} \\
\gd &:= \GroupHash^{\Pallas}(\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}gd},\ d)
  \in \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}), \\
\pkd &:= [\ivk]\,\gd \in \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}),
\end{align*}
where $\mathsf{FF1\text{-}AES256}_{\dk}$ is the NIST FF1
format-preserving encryption mode built on AES-256 (the \emph{Crypto
Guide}, \S``Format-preserving encryption and FF1''), keyed by $\dk$: a
keyed \emph{permutation} of the $88$-bit index space, so each
$d_{\mathrm{plain}}$ maps to a distinct $88$-bit diversifier and
different $d_{\mathrm{plain}}$ give unlinkable-looking addresses, all
derived from the one key. The \emph{diversified address} is the pair
$\mathsf{addr} := (d, \pkd) \in \{0,1\}^{88} \times
\Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})$.
\end{construction}

\paragraph{Choosing the index.} The receiving wallet chooses the
diversifier index freely: \emph{any} of the $2^{88}$ values yields a
valid, spendable address for the same $\ivk$; no derivation fixes it and
no index is forbidden --- any $88$-bit string is a valid diversifier
index. The wallet allocates indices on demand, conventionally
sequentially from $d_{\mathrm{plain}} = 0$ (index $0$ is the
\emph{default diversifier}), assigning a fresh index per payee or per
invoice to keep addresses mutually unlinkable. A randomly drawn index
would yield an equally valid address, but the specification states that
diversifier indices SHOULD NOT be chosen at random: ZIP-32 specifies
their usage in hierarchical-deterministic wallets, whose seed-only
recovery of issued addresses depends on deterministic allocation.

Every index works for a reason specific to Orchard: the hash-to-curve
$\GroupHash^{\Pallas}$ is total (\S\ref{sec:toolbox}) --- it returns a
point for every input --- so every index yields a well-defined $\gd$; in
the negligible-probability event that the two summed SWU outputs
(\S\ref{sec:toolbox}) cancel to the identity $\mathcal{O}$, the
specification substitutes the fallback
$\GroupHash^{\Pallas}(\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}gd}, \texttt{""})$,
the same domain separator with the empty message (protocol
specification \S\,5.4.1.6; crate \texttt{orchard},
\texttt{src/spec.rs}).
This is a deliberate improvement over Sapling, the previous shielded
generation, where the diversifier hash failed on roughly half of all
indices and the wallet had to increment $d_{\mathrm{plain}}$ until it
found a valid one; in Orchard no such rejection loop exists.

\paragraph{Rationale for routing the index through AES.} The step from
$d_{\mathrm{plain}}$ to $d$ is a keyed \emph{permutation}
(format-preserving encryption) for three reasons that no simpler map
satisfies together. (i) \emph{Unlinkability:} wallets allocate indices
in order $0, 1, 2, \dots$, and consecutive diversifiers would reveal a
shared owner and an issue count; encryption under $\dk$ scatters them
pseudorandomly over $\{0,1\}^{88}$. (ii) \emph{Bijection:} a permutation
never collides two indices onto one $d$ and is invertible --- with $\dk$
the wallet recovers the index behind any of its addresses, storing no
per-address secret; a plain hash is neither collision-free nor
invertible. (iii) \emph{Format preservation:} the address format fixes
the diversifier at exactly $88$ bits, and FF1 maps
$\{0,1\}^{88} \to \{0,1\}^{88}$ bijectively, which a truncated hash
cannot. The derivation runs \emph{wallet-side}, never inside the proof
circuit, so a conventional fast cipher (AES-256 under NIST FF1) is
appropriate; arithmetisation-friendliness, the concern of
\S\ref{sec:toolbox}, is irrelevant here.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Spend} --- $\mathsf{sk}$ / $\ask$. Whoever holds $\ask$ can
authorise spends; it sits at the top and nothing below it can recover
it.
\item \emph{See everything} --- $\fvk$ (hence $(\dk,\ivk)$ and $\ovk$). Detects
incoming notes (via $\ivk$, which recovers $\pkd$) and views outgoing
notes (via $\ovk$), but cannot spend, because deriving $\fvk$ from
$\mathsf{sk}$ is one-way.
\item \emph{Detect incoming} --- the scalar $\ivk$ alone. Sufficient to
recognise
notes addressed to the holder and scan the chain, nothing more --- the
key suitable for an always-online wallet server.
\item \emph{Receive} --- a diversified address $(d, \pkd)$. A public
handle anyone can pay; one incoming viewing key $(\dk,\ivk)$ generates
$2^{88}$ mutually unlinkable
addresses --- one per diversifier index
(Construction~\ref{def:keys-addr}), practically inexhaustible --- all
spendable by the same key.
\end{itemize}

Of these, only the diversified address is \emph{public}. The viewing
keys $\fvk$, $(\dk,\ivk)$, $\ovk$ are \emph{secret} key material ---
``viewing'' names what they let the holder \emph{do} (see transactions),
not that one may publish them. Disclosing a viewing key confers the
corresponding visibility on the recipient --- every payment to and from
the wallet for $\fvk$, incoming payments only for $\ivk$, outgoing only
for $\ovk$ --- so the owner shares one only deliberately (with an
auditor, say) and otherwise guards it like any other secret. The ladder
is useful precisely because these capabilities are \emph{separable}: one
can delegate ``scan incoming'' (the scalar $\ivk$) without delegating ``see
everything'' ($\fvk$), and neither confers the ability to spend.

Two questions of motivation that the bare formulas do not answer.
\emph{Why is $\ivk$ a commitment,
$\CommitIvk_{\rivk}(\Extract(\aksym), \nksym)$, rather than a plain
hash?} Because $\ivk$ must both bind \emph{and} hide. It binds $\aksym$
(spend authority) and $\nksym$ (nullifier derivation) to one identity,
and the Action statement proves the linkage in-circuit by recomputing
the commitment from the witnessed $\aksym$, $\nksym$, $\rivk$ (the
CommitIvk check, \S\ref{sec:action}) --- but binding alone a
collision-resistant hash would supply equally, and in-circuit
recomputation works for any hash: the membership check of
\S\ref{sec:action} recomputes the unblinded Sinsemilla Merkle-node
hash $\mathsf{MerkleCRH}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ (\S\ref{sec:tree}), and
Sapling even proved its BLAKE2s-based $\mathsf{CRH}^{\mathsf{ivk}}$
in-circuit. What the trapdoor $\rivk$ adds is \emph{hiding}: for
independent uniform $\rivk$, the blinded $\ShortCommit$ is perfectly
hiding (\S\ref{sec:toolbox}). In the deployed hierarchy, however,
$\rivk$ also keys the $\mathsf{PRFexpand}$ call that derives $\dk$ and
$\ovk$; when those outputs are exposed, hiding relies on a joint
assumption about that derivation and Pallas scalar multiplication, not
on perfect hiding alone. Under that assumption, $\ivk$ and public
$\pkd=[\ivk]\,\gd$ hide $\aksym$ and $\nksym$. This keeps the IN/OUT
tier below FULL VIEW: an $\ivk$ holder cannot extract $\nksym$ or
$\aksym$.
\emph{Why is $\nksym$ separate from $\ask$?} So that the owner can
delegate the capability to compute nullifiers --- which a viewing key
needs to determine which of the holder's notes are already spent
(\S\ref{sec:spend}) --- \emph{without} conferring the ability to spend:
the viewing key holds $\nksym$ but never $\ask$.

\subsection{One ladder, both pools}\label{sec:keys-scope}

The hierarchy just built carries one further property, worth stating at
the source rather than discovering later: it is scoped to the
\emph{protocol}, not to a value pool. Orchard spending-key and
viewing-key material grants authority to spend or view notes in
\emph{both} of the pools this volume follows --- the legacy Orchard pool
and the Ironwood pool (\S\ref{sec:intro}) --- and a diversified address
(a \emph{receiver}, in the vocabulary of the ZIPs), with its
corresponding $\ivk$, is likewise scoped to the Orchard protocol, not to
a pool (ZIP-229; ZIP-326). The entire ladder of this section therefore
survives the pool split unchanged: an existing wallet's keys and
addresses work in Ironwood exactly as they stand.

\section{Minting: a note is born}\label{sec:mint}

The recipient of \S\ref{sec:keys} now holds an address; a sender wishes
to pay it. This stage answers the first requirement of \S\ref{sec:intro},
transfer of value (R1): value must change hands on a public chain that
discloses neither payer, payee, nor amount. The vehicle is the
\emph{note}, and the first design problem is representational --- the
note must stay invisible on-chain yet remain provable by its owner. The
protocol's answer is never to place the note on the chain at all: what
the chain receives is only a \emph{hiding commitment} to it.

\subsection{The note and its commitment}\label{sec:mint-note}

\begin{definition}[Orchard note]\label{def:note}
An \emph{Orchard note} --- the private object representing an amount of
value --- is a tuple
\[
\mathbf{n} := (\mathsf{addr},\, v,\, \rho,\, \psi,\, \rcm),
\]
where $\mathsf{addr} = (d, \pkd)$ is the recipient's diversified address
(Construction~\ref{def:keys-addr}); $v \in \{0, \dots, 2^{64} - 1\}$ is
the value in zatoshi; $\rho \in \F_{p_{\Pallas}}$ is a per-note
uniqueness seed, fixed at note creation (\S\ref{sec:spend} constructs
its provenance); $\psi \in \F_{p_{\Pallas}}$ is randomness the sender
fixes through the note seed $\mathsf{rseed}$, from which it derives
deterministically (\S\ref{sec:mint-rseed}); and $\rcm \in
\F_{p_{\Vesta}}$ is the commitment trapdoor.
\end{definition}

\begin{construction}[Note commitment]\label{def:notecommit}
The protocol never publishes the note itself. What it records on-chain
is the \emph{note commitment}
\[
\cmsym := \NoteCommit_{\rcm}\bigl(\gd^\star \,\|\, \pkd^\star \,\|\,
  \mathrm{LE}_{64}(v) \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{255}(\rho) \,\|\,
  \mathrm{LE}_{255}(\psi)\bigr) \;\in\; \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}),
\]
where $\NoteCommit_{\rcm}$ is the Sinsemilla commitment
$\mathsf{SinsemillaCommit}_{\rcm}$ under the domain
$\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}NoteCommit}$
(Definition~\ref{def:sinsemilla-commit}). The chain stores the extracted
coordinate
\[
\cmx := \Extract(\cmsym) \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Pallas}},
\]
the $x$-coordinate of the commitment point ($\Extract$ is constructed
in \S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}), because the Merkle tree in which the
commitment will come to rest (\S\ref{sec:tree}) consumes a field
element, not a curve point.
\end{construction}

The primitive is perfectly hiding for an independent uniform trapdoor.
In an actual note, $\rcm$ is derived from $\mathsf{rseed}$ by
$\mathsf{PRFexpand}$, so hiding is computational (with negligible
reduction bias) under the PRF assumption: the chain learns no feasible
information about $\mathsf{addr}$, $v$, $\rho$, or $\psi$ from
$\cmsym$. The commitment is also \emph{computationally binding} --- the
sender cannot later claim the commitment was to a different note. These
are the two
properties of a commitment scheme from the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S~``Hiding and binding'', and \S\ref{sec:toolbox-sinsemilla}
established both for the Sinsemilla commitment. The scheme is Sinsemilla
for the reason given there: it is inexpensive to recompute \emph{inside
the circuit}, which the spender must one day do
(\S\ref{sec:toolbox-cost}).

The input encoding is worth a second look, because every byte of it
recurs later in the section. The three address-related quantities are
exactly the key material of \S\ref{sec:keys}: the diversifier $d \in
\{0,1\}^{88}$, the diversified base $\gd =
\GroupHash^{\Pallas}(\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}gd},\, d) \in
\Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})$ --- the per-address generator --- and the
\emph{transmission key} $\pkd = [\ivk]\,\gd$ identifying the recipient
(Construction~\ref{def:keys-addr}); $\mathsf{addr} = (d, \pkd)$. Under
the star encoding of \S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}, $\gd^\star,
\pkd^\star \in \{0,1\}^{256}$. The $\NoteCommit$ input is the bit-string
concatenation ($\|$) of these two point encodings with the little-endian
integer encodings $\mathrm{LE}_{64}(v) \in \{0,1\}^{64}$ and
$\mathrm{LE}_{255}(\rho), \mathrm{LE}_{255}(\psi) \in \{0,1\}^{255}$
(\S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}).

Why these five fields? The address and the value are the note's
\emph{content}: who is paid, and how much. The other three serve the
machinery. The trapdoor $\rcm$ is what makes the commitment hiding
(\S\ref{sec:toolbox-sinsemilla}), while $\rho$ and $\psi$ exist solely
to render the note's eventual \emph{nullifier} --- the token whose
publication will one day mark this note spent --- unique and
unpredictable, a role that becomes apparent only when the note is spent
(\S\ref{sec:spend}). A note carries randomness whose purpose is
downstream.

\subsection{The note seed: Ironwood derivations (lead byte
\texorpdfstring{$\mathtt{0x03}$}{0x03})}\label{sec:mint-rseed}

Definition~\ref{def:note} left two debts: the origins of $\psi$ and
$\rcm$. The sender does not draw them independently; it draws a single
uniform note seed $\mathsf{rseed} \in \{0,1\}^{256}$ (the key type
$\mathsf{PRFexpand}$ requires, Definition~\ref{def:prfexpand}) and
derives all per-note randomness from it --- alongside $\psi$ and
$\rcm$, an \emph{ephemeral secret key} $\mathsf{esk}$, the sender's key
half of the note encryption constructed in \S\ref{sec:delivery}. The
derivation is versioned: a note travels to its recipient as a \emph{note
plaintext} --- the byte serialisation, also constructed in
\S\ref{sec:delivery}, that the sender encrypts to the address --- and
the plaintext opens with a \emph{lead byte} naming its format. Which
derivation applies is the lead byte's decision.

An Ironwood note is the same tuple $\mathbf{n} = (\mathsf{addr}, v, \rho, \psi,
\rcm)$ of Definition~\ref{def:note}, committed by the same $\NoteCommit$
of Construction~\ref{def:notecommit} (\S\ref{sec:mint-note}); ZIP-2005
(Proposed; activation bound to the NU6.3 network upgrade) carries the
pool distinction on the note plaintext, through its lead byte. What
changes is that byte --- and, through it, the derivation of $\rcm$.

The format is specified and deployed. ZIP-2005 \S\,4.7.3 gives the
derivation, landed in protocol.tex at zips commit \texttt{69610984}, and
the released \texttt{orchard}~0.15.0 --- the version
\texttt{librustzcash}, the deployed Zcash wallet library, pins ---
implements it: \texttt{enum NoteVersion} (\texttt{V2}/\texttt{V3})
carries the lead byte, \texttt{Note::from\_parts} takes it as a fifth
argument, and \texttt{Note::rcm()} dispatches on it
(\texttt{src/note.rs}). The \texttt{bef8a27} development tree predates
the release: its \texttt{Note::from\_parts} (\texttt{src/note.rs:182})
still takes the four components (recipient, value, $\rho$,
$\mathsf{rseed}$) with no version tag, and its \texttt{Note::rcm()} is
the legacy single-tag trapdoor of Remark~\ref{rem:iw-legacy-rcm}.

% compute/spend_quantities.py: pre_rcm length
%   1 + 32 + 32 + 8 + 32 + 32 = 137 bytes
\begin{definition}[Ironwood note derivations]\label{def:iw-derive}
For a lead-byte-$\mathtt{0x03}$ note with note seed $\mathsf{rseed}$ and
uniqueness seed $\rho$ (Definition~\ref{def:note}), the sender derives,
in this order,
\begin{align*}
\mathsf{esk} &:= \mathrm{ToScalar}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{rseed}}
  (\mathtt{0x04} \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{256}(\rho))\bigr)
  \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Vesta}}, \\
\psi &:= \mathrm{ToBase}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{rseed}}
  (\mathtt{0x09} \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{256}(\rho))\bigr)
  \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Pallas}}, \\
\rcm &:= \mathrm{ToScalar}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{rseed}}
  (\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm})\bigr) \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Vesta}},
\end{align*}
where
\[
\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm} := \mathtt{0x0B} \,\|\, \gd^\star \,\|\, \pkd^\star
  \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{64}(v) \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{256}(\rho) \,\|\,
  \mathrm{LE}_{256}(\psi),
\]
each field in its canonical byte encoding --- the points star-encoded
($32$ bytes each), $v$ as $8$ and $\rho$, $\psi$ as $32$ little-endian
bytes --- so $\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm}$ is a $137$-byte string
($1 + 32 + 32 + 8 + 32 + 32$). In the rare event $\mathsf{esk} = 0$ the
sender redraws a fresh $\mathsf{rseed}$. (ZIP-2005 \S\,4.7.3 change;
protocol.tex at zips commit \texttt{69610984}, where the hash is named
$H^{\mathsf{rcm},\mathsf{Orchard}}_{\mathsf{rseed}}$.)
\end{definition}

Two observations about Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive}. First, the order
is forced: $\rcm$ now comes last because its preimage includes $\psi$
(ZIP-2005). Second, $\mathsf{esk}$ and $\psi$ are byte-identical to
their legacy lead-byte-$\mathtt{0x02}$ counterparts --- only $\rcm$
changes (Remark~\ref{rem:iw-legacy-rcm}) --- and the same $\gd^\star,
\pkd^\star$ encodings feed both $\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm}$ and the
$\NoteCommit$ input of Construction~\ref{def:notecommit}: the trapdoor
now commits, through $\mathsf{PRFexpand}$, to the same material the
commitment does. The purpose of this detour through BLAKE2b is
\emph{post-quantum binding}: every note field now traverses
$\mathsf{PRFexpand}$ on its way into the trapdoor, so an adversary
constrained to treat it as a random oracle cannot vary any field of a
committed note without changing $\rcm$ --- the property that makes every
Ironwood-pool output note, and no Sapling- or Orchard-pool output note,
a \emph{recoverable} note, whose security analysis, recovery statement,
and specified-but-undeployed $\mathsf{qsk}$/$\mathsf{qk}$ key branch
belong to \S\ref{sec:pq-zip2005} (ZIP-2005).

\subsection{Lead bytes across pools and the legacy derivation}
\label{sec:mint-leadbytes}

Which lead bytes a note plaintext may carry depends on the value pool
and the block height --- the lead byte is a consensus-constrained format
version, ordinarily encrypted inside the note plaintext, not a free
choice.

\begin{definition}[Allowed lead bytes, pool-parameterised]%
\label{def:iw-leadbytes}
Let $\mathsf{pool}$ be the chain value pool of a note received at block
height $h$ --- $\mathsf{SaplingPool}$, the value pool of the preceding
Sapling shielded generation, or $\mathsf{OrchardPool}$ or
$\mathsf{IronwoodPool}$, the formal names for the Orchard and Ironwood
pools of \S\ref{sec:intro} --- and let $h_{\mathrm{Canopy}}$ be the
activation height of the Canopy network upgrade, which deployed
ZIP-212's $\mathsf{rseed}$-derived note-plaintext format and its
$\mathtt{0x02}$ lead byte; the constant $\mathsf{ZIP212GracePeriod}$ is
the fixed length, set by ZIP-212, of the window after
$h_{\mathrm{Canopy}}$ in which both formats remain valid. Then
\[
\mathsf{allowedLeadBytes}^{\mathsf{pool}}(h) :=
\begin{cases}
\{\mathtt{0x01}\}
  & h < h_{\mathrm{Canopy}}, \\
\{\mathtt{0x01}, \mathtt{0x02}\}
  & h_{\mathrm{Canopy}} \le h < h_{\mathrm{Canopy}} +
    \mathsf{ZIP212GracePeriod}, \\
\{\mathtt{0x02}\}
  & \text{afterwards, } \mathsf{pool} \ne \mathsf{IronwoodPool}, \\
\{\mathtt{0x03}\}
  & \text{afterwards, } \mathsf{pool} = \mathsf{IronwoodPool}.
\end{cases}
\]
For an Ironwood-pool note plaintext the only allowed lead byte is
$\mathtt{0x03}$ (ZIP-2005 \S\,3.2.1 change; protocol.tex at zips commit
\texttt{69610984}).
\end{definition}

Coinbase outputs --- the outputs of the transaction that mints each
block's subsidy --- obey the same discipline: an Ironwood coinbase
output must carry lead byte $\mathtt{0x03}$ (ZIP-258), the Ironwood
analogue of the Sapling/Orchard rule that coinbase outputs use the
post-ZIP-212 $\mathtt{0x02}$ format (ZIP-213).

\begin{remark}[The legacy derivation]\label{rem:iw-legacy-rcm}
Lead-byte-$\mathtt{0x02}$ notes --- all Orchard-pool notes, before and
after NU6.3 --- derive
\[
\rcm := \mathrm{ToScalar}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{rseed}}
  (\mathtt{0x05} \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{256}(\rho))\bigr),
\]
with $\mathsf{esk}$ and $\psi$ exactly as in
Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive}. The specification packages both
derivations under one dispatcher,
$\mathsf{Derive}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}_{\rcm,\,\mathsf{rseed}}
(\mathsf{leadByte}, \dots)$, selecting $\mathtt{0x05} \,\|\,
\mathrm{LE}_{256}(\rho)$ for $\mathtt{0x02}$ and the hashed
$\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm}$ for $\mathtt{0x03}$
(ZIP-2005 \S\,4.7.3 change). The dev-tree \texttt{Note::rcm()} at
\texttt{bef8a27} implements only this legacy branch
(\texttt{src/note.rs:132-136}), with
\texttt{PrfExpand::ORCHARD\_RCM} $= \mathtt{0x05}$ (zcash\_spec 0.2.1,
\texttt{src/prf\_expand.rs:88}), no version dispatch, and no
$\mathtt{0x03}$/hashed-$\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm}$ branch; the released
\texttt{orchard}~0.15.0 that \texttt{librustzcash} pins adds the
\texttt{NoteVersion} dispatch --- \texttt{V2} to \texttt{rcm\_v2},
\texttt{V3} to \texttt{rcm\_v3} over the $\mathtt{0x0B}$-separated
$\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm}$ (\texttt{src/note.rs}) --- deploying the
derivation of Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive}.
\end{remark}

\section{Delivery: the note reaches its owner}\label{sec:delivery}

The mint succeeded too well. The on-chain $\cmx$ (\S\ref{sec:mint})
conceals the note entirely, leaving the question of how the recipient
learns of the note with which they were paid. The sender must deliver
the note to them, privately, over the same public chain. Orchard
achieves this with \emph{in-band secret distribution}: every Action
carries, beside its commitment, a ciphertext only the recipient can
open. This section constructs the two ciphertexts the sender attaches,
the trial decryption by which the recipient finds and verifies the
note, and the synchronisation procedure --- a wallet restoring from a
seed --- that the same machinery supports.

\subsection{Sender: one-shot Diffie--Hellman and the AEAD}
\label{sec:delivery-sender}

Alice holds Bob's address $(d, \pkd)$, with
\[
\pkd = [\ivk]\,\gd, \qquad
\gd = \GroupHash^{\Pallas}(\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}gd},\ d)
\]
(Construction~\ref{def:keys-addr}, \S\ref{sec:keys}). She forms the
\emph{note plaintext}
% numbers: brief c2-29 (lead byte 0x03; rseed 32 bytes; memo 512 bytes)
\[
\mathsf{np} := (\mathtt{0x03},\ d,\ v,\ \mathsf{rseed},\ \mathsf{memo}),
\]
where $\mathtt{0x03}$ is the Ironwood note-plaintext lead byte, the
$32$-byte note seed $\mathsf{rseed}$ --- drawn uniformly at random,
fresh for each output note --- is the master randomness from which the
note's $\rcm$ and $\psi$ were derived at minting
(Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive}, \S\ref{sec:mint}) and from which the
ephemeral secret $\mathsf{esk}$ below derives as well, and
$\mathsf{memo}$ is a fixed $512$-byte field of sender-chosen data. Each
of these derivations is one $\mathsf{PRFexpand}$ call keyed by
$\mathsf{rseed}$ and distinguished by a leading domain-separator byte
(the one derivation display, Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive});
$\mathsf{esk}$ and $\psi$ take as input $\rho := \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$,
encoded as $32$ little-endian bytes --- which binds the note's secret
randomness to its Action, $\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ being the base-field
nullifier of the note spent alongside (constructed in
\S\ref{sec:spend}) --- and $\rcm$, derived last, takes every note
field.

\begin{construction}[Note encryption]\label{def:delivery-enc}
Alice runs a one-shot Diffie--Hellman key agreement to the diversified
base (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S ``The Diffie--Hellman protocol'' and
\S ``Static and ephemeral keys''), yielding a \emph{fresh} key:
\[
\mathsf{epk} := [\mathsf{esk}]\,\gd, \qquad
\mathsf{s} := [\mathsf{esk}]\,\pkd, \qquad
K_{\mathrm{enc}} := \mathsf{KDF}(\mathsf{s},\ \mathsf{epk}),
\]
with $\mathsf{epk}$ the \emph{ephemeral public key}. She then seals the
plaintext under an AEAD --- authenticated encryption with associated
data, instantiated by ChaCha20-Poly1305 with the fixed all-zero $96$-bit
nonce and empty associated data (the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S ``The ChaCha20-Poly1305 AEAD''); we write
$\mathsf{Enc}_K/\mathsf{Dec}_K$ for its encryption and decryption under
the key $K$:
\[
C_{\mathrm{enc}} := \mathsf{Enc}_{K_{\mathrm{enc}}}(\mathsf{np}).
\]
The key-derivation function $\mathsf{KDF}$ (the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S ``Key-derivation functions'') is BLAKE2b-256 with personalisation
\texttt{Zcash\_OrchardKDF} over
$\mathsf{s}^\star \,\|\, \mathsf{epk}^\star$, where $\star$ is the point
encoding of \S\ref{sec:toolbox}.
\end{construction}

Why exactly the intended recipient? Because $\pkd = [\ivk]\,\gd$, Bob
can reach the same shared secret from his side as
\[
[\ivk]\,\mathsf{epk} \;=\; [\ivk][\mathsf{esk}]\,\gd
\;=\; [\mathsf{esk}]\,\pkd \;=\; \mathsf{s},
\]
so, beside the sender (who knows $\mathsf{esk}$), a party that can recover
the Diffie--Hellman secret --- in particular the holder of $\ivk$ ---
can recover $K_{\mathrm{enc}}$ from the published pair
$(\mathsf{epk}, C_{\mathrm{enc}})$.

\subsection{The outgoing ciphertext}\label{sec:delivery-out}

One ciphertext serves the recipient; a second can serve the sender's
own books. When an outgoing viewing key is supplied, the pair
$(\pkd, \mathsf{esk})$ is sealed under the
\emph{outgoing cipher key},
\[
\mathsf{ock} := \mathrm{BLAKE2b\text{-}256}\bigl(
  \texttt{Zcash\_Orchardock},\
  \ovk \,\|\, \cvsym^{\mathsf{net}} \,\|\, \cmx \,\|\, \mathsf{epk}
\bigr), \qquad
C_{\mathrm{out}} := \mathsf{Enc}_{\mathsf{ock}}(\pkd,\ \mathsf{esk}),
\]
points in $\star$-encoding, where $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$ is the
Action's net value commitment --- a Pallas-point commitment to the
Action's value change, constructed in \S\ref{sec:spend} --- and $\ovk$
is the outgoing viewing key of Construction~\ref{def:keys-view}. The
ciphertext $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ carries $(\pkd, \mathsf{esk})$ so that the
\emph{sender} --- or an auditor holding $\ovk$ --- can re-derive
$\mathsf{s} = [\mathsf{esk}]\,\pkd$ and read the note as well. Because
$\mathsf{ock}$ binds the Action's own $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$, $\cmx$,
and $\mathsf{epk}$, a $C_{\mathrm{out}}$ cannot be transplanted onto
another Action without detection under the hash and AEAD assumptions.
The protocol also permits $\ovk=\bot$; in that case the sender chooses a
random $\mathsf{ock}$ and random outgoing plaintext, deliberately making
$C_{\mathrm{out}}$ unrecoverable by an outgoing viewing key.

The Action publishes
\[
(\mathsf{epk},\ C_{\mathrm{enc}},\ C_{\mathrm{out}})
\qquad \text{alongside} \qquad
\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}},\ \nfsym,\ \rk,\ \cmx,
\]
with $\nfsym$ the Action's base-field nullifier and $\rk$ its
re-randomised validating key --- both, like $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$,
public fields of the Action constructed in \S\ref{sec:spend}.

\subsection{Recipient: trial decryption and re-derivation}
\label{sec:delivery-recv}

Nothing on chain marks which Action pays Bob, so his wallet
\emph{trial-decrypts every} Orchard-protocol output: for each it
recomputes
\[
\mathsf{s} := [\ivk]\,\mathsf{epk}, \qquad
K_{\mathrm{enc}} := \mathsf{KDF}(\mathsf{s},\ \mathsf{epk}), \qquad
\mathsf{np} := \mathsf{Dec}_{K_{\mathrm{enc}}}(C_{\mathrm{enc}}).
\]
The AEAD tag makes an incorrect guess fail cleanly (the decryption
returns $\bot$), so a successful open \emph{is} the detection that the
note was his.

After a successful open Bob re-derives the full note from
$(d, v, \mathsf{rseed})$ --- taking $\rho := \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ from
the same Action --- and performs the two checks that close the obvious
forgeries: (i)~that the sender formed the ephemeral key honestly,
$[\mathsf{esk}]\,\gd = \mathsf{epk}$ for the $\mathsf{esk}$ re-derived
from $\mathsf{rseed}$ (the ZIP-212 check, defeating a mauled
$\mathsf{epk}$); and (ii)~that the note is indeed the one the Action
committed on chain,
\[
\Extract\bigl(\NoteCommit_{\rcm}(\ldots)\bigr) = \cmx.
\]
Only when both hold does he accept. Under commitment binding and AEAD
integrity, a sender cannot feasibly make Bob accept a note inconsistent
with the commitment.

The re-derivation fixes an order. Because the
lead-byte-$\mathtt{0x03}$ trapdoor $\rcm$ depends on $\gd$, $\pkd$, and
$\psi$ (Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive}), the specification reorders both
the $\ivk$ and the $\fvk$ decryption procedures: recover $\gd$ and
$\pkd$ first, then $\psi$, then $\rcm$ (ZIP-2005,
\S\S\,4.20.2--4.20.3 changes). The routing is deployed: the released
\texttt{orchard}~0.15.0 routes the two lead bytes to two
note-encryption domains, \texttt{OrchardDomain} ($\mathtt{0x02}$) and
\texttt{IronwoodDomain} ($\mathtt{0x03}$)
(\texttt{src/note\_encryption.rs}), which
\texttt{zcash\_client\_backend} scanning consumes
(\texttt{src/scanning.rs}); the \texttt{orchard} \texttt{bef8a27}
development tree still carries a single $\mathtt{0x02}$-only
\texttt{OrchardDomain}. And
because keys are scoped to the protocol rather than to a pool
(\S\ref{sec:keys-scope}), the same $\ivk$ trial-decrypts both pools ---
one key, two domains (ZIP-326; \texttt{zcash\_client\_backend}
\texttt{src/scanning.rs}).

\subsection{Synchronisation, including from cold storage}
\label{sec:delivery-sync}

Trial decryption scales from one payment to the whole chain: it is the
operation a wallet performs when it restores from a seed. Restoring,
the wallet walks the chain forward from the account's ``birthday''
height and trial-decrypts every Action. A \emph{light wallet} --- one
that delegates block storage to a server it need not trust with keys
--- does so against \emph{compact blocks}, blocks stripped to the
fields trial decryption needs. Each compact output carries only the
% numbers: brief c2-39 (lead byte 0x03/0x02);
%   compute/spend_quantities.py: compact plaintext
%   1 + 11 + 8 + 32 = 52 bytes (= the C_enc partition boundary)
first $52$ bytes of $C_{\mathrm{enc}}$ --- those covering the
\emph{compact note plaintext}: the lead byte ($1$ byte;
$\mathtt{0x03}$ for Ironwood outputs, the only allowed value in that
pool --- $\mathtt{0x02}$ appears only when scanning legacy
Orchard-pool outputs), $d$ ($11$ bytes), $v$ ($8$ bytes), and
$\mathsf{rseed}$ ($32$ bytes) --- together with $\mathsf{epk}$,
$\cmx$, and the Action's nullifier $\nfsym$, which the light wallet
needs twice: it supplies $\rho := \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ for the
$\cmx$ recomputation that detects the note, and it is the source of
the chain's nullifier set (\S\ref{sec:spend}) against which spentness
is checked below (ZIP-307; \texttt{zcash\_client\_backend}
\texttt{proto/compact\_formats.proto}).

The wallet records each note that opens \emph{with its leaf position}
--- the index at which its $\cmx$ was appended, derived arithmetically
from the tree size at the block boundary and the commitments' order ---
and that position is precisely what the commitment tree
consumes: the wallet marks the position in its shard tree and can then
build the note's authentication path on demand, by root-only assembly,
without replaying every commitment (\S\ref{sec:tree} constructs the
tree, the shard store, and this assembly). To determine which recovered
notes remain spendable it additionally requires the nullifier key
$\nksym$ (\S\ref{sec:keys}): it computes each note's nullifier
(\S\ref{sec:spend}) and discards those whose nullifier already appears
in the chain's nullifier set. The end state is a set of unspent notes,
each with a value, a position, an authentication path, and a nullifier
--- exactly what the Action prover of \S\ref{sec:action} consumes.

The light-client surface follows the pool split. Compact blocks gain an
\texttt{ironwoodActions} list (the same \texttt{CompactOrchardAction}
message) and an \texttt{ironwoodCommitmentTreeSize}, and the
synchronisation just described runs per pool, against that pool's own
commitment tree (\texttt{zcash\_client\_backend}
\texttt{proto/compact\_formats.proto}; \S\ref{sec:tree}). The note has
reached its owner, who can detect it, verify it, and place it in the
tree; there it now rests.

\section{Resting: the note in the commitment tree}\label{sec:tree}

Minted (\S\ref{sec:mint}) and delivered (\S\ref{sec:delivery}), the
note now rests, and the journey reaches its second requirement (R2,
\S\ref{sec:intro}): when the note is eventually spent, its owner must
prove it \emph{valid} --- that someone actually created it --- without
revealing which note it is, for otherwise Alice could spend a note she
invented. On a transparent chain this is trivial: the output being
spent is present in the UTXO set (the set of unspent transparent
outputs) that every node maintains --- but pointing at the output would
deanonymise the payment. The shielded solution is this section's
object. The network maintains one append-only Merkle tree --- a binary
hash tree, each parent a hash of its two children, so the single root
digests every leaf --- whose leaves are every note commitment ever
published, in creation order; Alice proves \emph{membership}, by a
Merkle authentication path from her leaf $\cmx$ to a root the network
trusts. Inside the circuit the path is a private witness (the
membership check the Action statement imposes, \S\ref{sec:action}), so
the verifier learns that her note is one of the leaves but not which
one: the anonymity set is the entire tree.

\subsection{The tree, its hash, and its anchors}\label{sec:tree-def}

\begin{definition}[Orchard note commitment tree]\label{def:tree}
The \emph{note commitment tree} is an append-only Merkle tree of fixed
depth $32$ --- room for $2^{32}$ notes --- whose leaves are the
commitments $\cmx$ (\S\ref{sec:mint}) in creation order; positions not
yet used hold the \emph{uncommitted} sentinel value $2$. Number the
layers from the root, layer $0$, down to the leaves, layer $32$. The
parent at layer $\ell$ of a left child $l$ and right child $r$ at layer
$\ell + 1$ is the layer-tagged Sinsemilla short hash
\begin{align*}
&\mathsf{MerkleCRH}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}_{\ell}(l, r) :=\\
&\qquad \mathsf{ShortHash}_{\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}MerkleCRH}}
\bigl(\mathrm{LE}_{10}(31 - \ell) \,\|\, \mathrm{LE}_{255}(l) \,\|\,
\mathrm{LE}_{255}(r)\bigr) \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Pallas}},
\end{align*}
% compute/spend_quantities.py: MerkleCRH message 10 + 255 + 255 = 520
%   bits = 52 ten-bit chunks
the unkeyed short form of Definition~\ref{def:sinsemilla-commit} over
the $10 + 255 + 255 = 520$-bit message ($52$ ten-bit Sinsemilla
chunks), under the same little-endian input convention as $\CommitIvk$
and $\NoteCommit$. The ten-bit tag is the fold \emph{height} counted
from the leaves, $31 - \ell$: $0$ for the leaf-level combine, $31$ for
the combine that yields the root (protocol specification \S\,5.4.1.3,
$\mathsf{MerkleCRH}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$; crate \texttt{orchard},
\texttt{src/tree.rs}). Leaves, internal nodes, and the root all live
in $\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$. Appending a block's note commitments yields a new
root: the root of the tree as it stands after block $h$ is the
\emph{anchor} of block $h$, a single field element, so every main-chain
block defines exactly one anchor. A spend proves membership against the
anchor of any main-chain block, and all Actions of one bundle prove
against a single shared anchor (\S\ref{sec:action}).
\end{definition}

Every node of the tree is a single base-field element by necessity, not
convention. The spender recomputes the hash layer by layer
\emph{inside} the Action circuit to prove membership (the membership
check, \S\ref{sec:action}), and that circuit is arithmetic over
$\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$ (\S\ref{sec:toolbox}): every wire carries one such
field element. A curve-point node would carry two coordinates and an
on-curve constraint at each of the $32$ layers; reducing each node to
its $x$-coordinate halves the path data the circuit threads and removes
that bookkeeping. This is precisely why $\mathsf{MerkleCRH}$ ends in
$\Extract$: Sinsemilla yields a point, and $\Extract$ returns it to the
field, so that each layer's output is directly the next layer's input
and the one hash composes all the way to the root. Dropping the
$y$-coordinate at every node is harmless for the reason
\S\ref{sec:toolbox} gave for $\Extract$ itself: the tree uses each node
only as a binding digest, never to recover a child point, and a
collision in the $x$-coordinate alone yields either a collision in the
underlying Sinsemilla hash or a pair of \emph{opposite} points ($\Extract$
identifies $P$ with $-P$, \S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}) --- both
non-trivial discrete-log relations, the second by one extra case;
\S\ref{sec:security} discharges the two together alongside
Theorem~\ref{thm:sinsemilla-cr}. The layer
tag, finally, is replay protection: because the tag fixes the fold
height, a hash computed at one height cannot be replayed at another.

Nor is the sentinel arbitrary. At $x = 2$ the Pallas curve equation
$y^2 = x^3 + 5$ (the \emph{Math Guide}, \S``Elliptic curves'') gives
$y^2 = 2^3 + 5 = 13$, and $13$ is a non-square modulo both Pasta primes
(verified in the \emph{Math Guide}, \S``Number theory for
cryptography''), so $2$ is not the $x$-coordinate of any Pallas point:
it can never equal a valid $\cmx$, and an empty position can never be
silently confused with a genuine commitment.

\subsection{The authentication path from public data}\label{sec:tree-path}

\begin{construction}[Authentication path]\label{def:authpath}
Alice's commitment occupies a fixed \emph{position} $\mathsf{pos}$ ---
its index in creation order --- which her wallet records the moment
trial decryption succeeds (\S\ref{sec:delivery}). Fix an anchor, that
of any block at or after the one that mined her note, and let $n$ be
the number of leaves in that block's tree. Write
$\mathsf{pos} = b_{31} \cdots b_1 b_0$ in binary, and count levels
$i = 0, \dots, 31$ from the leaf upward (the level-$i$ fold applies the
layer-$(31 - i)$ hash, whose tag is therefore $i$,
Definition~\ref{def:tree}). The level-$i$ ancestor of her leaf is a left
child when $b_i = 0$ and a right child when $b_i = 1$, and the path's
level-$i$ entry is that ancestor's \emph{sibling} in the tree of size
$n$ --- one node per level, $32$ in all. The membership check
(\S\ref{sec:action}) reverses the construction: starting from $\cmx$ it
folds in one path entry per level --- placing the running node on the
side $b_i$ dictates, left when $b_i = 0$, right when $b_i = 1$, and the
sibling opposite --- and asserts that the final value equals the public
anchor; the bits $b_i$ and the siblings stay inside the witness.
\end{construction}

Each path entry is one of two kinds, and the difference organises
everything a wallet does. When $b_i = 1$ the sibling subtree lies to
the \emph{left} of her leaf: it spans commitments older than hers, so
every position in it is filled --- a complete subtree --- and, because
the tree appends only on the right, its root is final, identical at
every anchor. When $b_i = 0$ the sibling subtree lies to her
\emph{right} and its value depends on the anchor: its root hashes the
commitments it holds at size $n$, unfilled positions taking the
sentinel $2$; a sibling subtree still entirely empty at size $n$
contributes a per-level \emph{constant}, the sentinel folded up through
the layer-tagged hash. The path is therefore a deterministic function
of $\mathsf{pos}$ and the first $n$ public leaves, and of nothing else;
the only secret is which leaf is hers. Figure~\ref{fig:merklepath}
draws the computation at depth $3$.

\subsection{The wallet's witness: shardtree, shards, and the cap}
\label{sec:tree-shardtree}

In practice the wallet neither rebuilds the path from the leaves at
each spend nor retains the whole tree. As it scans the chain
(\S\ref{sec:delivery}) it appends every published $\cmx$ --- its own
and everyone else's --- but keeps only what a future path needs: the
nodes from each of its own notes up to the root, the complete-subtree
roots flanking those paths, and checkpoints at represented block
boundaries; blocks that append no commitment need not create a distinct
tree checkpoint. The ordinary recent set is bounded (currently $100$),
while explicitly retained durable anchors --- currently selected every
$288$ blocks after activation --- are exempt from that pruning. This is
the design of the \texttt{shardtree} crate
(\texttt{shardtree/src/\allowbreak store.rs}) that
\texttt{zcash\_client\_backend} drives. The wallet marks its note's
position the moment trial decryption succeeds; a \emph{checkpoint}
stores a tree state (empty or the position of the last leaf) together
with the set of marks removed since the preceding checkpoint. The
anchor itself is recomputed from the retained nodes on demand rather
than stored in the checkpoint. To produce the path for a spend against a
chosen block, the
wallet descends its retained tree to the marked leaf and, on the way
back to the root, reads off each sibling subtree's root: a complete
subtree contributes its stored root, a subtree lying wholly beyond the
leaf count $n$ contributes the per-level empty root, and the single
subtree straddling position $n$ is rehashed from its retained nodes.
The leaf count enters only as this cutoff --- every position $\ge n$
counts as empty --- so one retained tree reproduces the path against
any block it has checkpointed.

Nothing in Construction~\ref{def:authpath} requires Alice to have been
online: its inputs are public, so a freshly restored wallet --- a seed
phrase and nothing else --- rebuilds the same state by replaying the
chain's note commitments. Nor need it touch all $2^{32}$ positions.
Cut the tree at half its depth. Below the cut lie the \emph{shards},
the height-$16$ subtrees --- $2^{16}$ leaves apiece, up to $2^{16}$ of
them --- and above it a height-$16$ \emph{cap} whose own leaves are the
shard roots. (The backend pins the shard height to half the tree depth,
$32/2 = 16$: constant \texttt{ORCHARD\_SHARD\_HEIGHT},
\texttt{zcash\_client\_backend/src/\allowbreak data\_api.rs}.) A leaf's
$32$ siblings divide along the same cut: the low $16$, levels $0$
through $15$, lie inside its own shard, and the high $16$, levels $16$
through $31$, are cap nodes, each the root of a subtree spanning whole
shards. The two halves reach Alice by entirely different routes, and
that asymmetry is the economy of the scheme.

Her own shard she must hash from leaves: its $16$ low siblings are the
within-shard subtrees of the path descent --- complete to her left,
straddling or empty to her right --- so she scans that shard's
commitments block by block, her own and everyone else's, and folds
them; this is the one subtree whose $2^{16}$ leaves she handles
individually. Every other shard she takes as a single $32$-byte root
(the dashed far subtree of Figure~\ref{fig:merklepath}): the cap's
leaves are exactly those roots, which a light-client server streams one
per completed shard (the $\mathsf{GetSubtreeRoots}$ call of the
light-client protocol, \S\ref{sec:delivery};
\texttt{zcash\_client\_backend}, \texttt{proto/\allowbreak
service.proto}); she folds them upward with the same
layer-tagged $\mathsf{MerkleCRH}$, one application per cap level ---
the upper half of the tree's $32$ layers --- and reads her high
siblings off the result. A shard that does not yet exist (the empty
right of the cap) contributes the per-level empty constant rather than
a download, and her own shard she holds from leaves whether or not it
has completed, so she never needs its root from the server. Her
level-$16$ sibling is thus a neighbouring shard's root, and each
sibling above it a fold of $2, 4, 8, \dots$ roots she already holds.

\begin{example}[Witnessing the newest shard]
Let Alice's note be the last leaf of the newest shard, shard $k$. Below
the cut, all $16$ low siblings are complete left subtrees, tiling the
shard's earlier $2^{16} - 1$ positions, so she ingests essentially the
whole shard. Above the cut, the cap's right side is empty --- no shard
succeeds $k$, so those siblings are the empty constant --- while its
left side folds the roots of shards $0, \dots, k-1$, each supplied by
$\mathsf{GetSubtreeRoots}$. Stitching the halves --- her leaf folds up
to the shard-$k$ root, which folds up the cap to the anchor --- gives
the full $32$-level path.
\end{example}

The $2^{16}$ throughout bounds the cap's \emph{capacity} --- $2^{32}$
leaves over $2^{16}$ per shard --- not Alice's traffic. At leaf count
$n$, the service exposes one root for each completed shard,
$\lfloor n / 2^{16} \rfloor$ in all; the current partial shard is
reconstructed from its leaves. The whole history above her shard thus arrives
as a few kilobytes of roots in place of millions of leaves. And she
performs the fold locally, never asking a server for one particular
note's path --- which would reveal which note is hers; the finished
path is the private witness of the membership check
(\S\ref{sec:action}), and only the anchor is public.

\subsection{Anchor choice, reorgs, and the anonymity set}
\label{sec:tree-anchor}

By the time Alice's transaction reaches a block, the live tree has
advanced past her chosen anchor, and this costs nothing: appending
leaves creates new roots and alters no old one, so the anchor still
names one fixed historical tree, her path still folds to exactly that
root, and the membership check asserts exactly that equality ---
collision resistance of the layer-tagged hash
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sinsemilla-cr}) makes a path to that root for a
non-member infeasible. Consensus is equally generous: it accepts the
anchor of \emph{any} main-chain block from Orchard activation (the NU5
network upgrade) onward --- this for the legacy Orchard-pool tree; the
Ironwood tree's anchors run from its own activation
(\S\ref{sec:tree-pools}). In neither pool is there a sliding window of
admissible anchors, so proving is fully decoupled from broadcasting:
Alice need not race the chain tip.

The one recency caveat runs in the opposite direction. A
reorganisation --- the replacement of the chain's most recent blocks by
a competing branch (the \emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``Reorg rails,
maturity, and the finality floor'') --- can strike the anchoring block
from the main chain, invalidating the anchor and forcing Alice to
rebuild the transaction; deeper anchors make the event rarer, but no
depth rules it out. The often-cited $100$-block figure is the
specification's description of the reorganisation depth nodes are
expected to handle (protocol specification \S\,3.3); the deployed
validator, Zebra, actually rolls back automatically to a depth of at
most $1000$ blocks (constant \texttt{MAX\_BLOCK\_REORG\_HEIGHT}, crate
\texttt{zebra-chain}, \texttt{src/parameters/\allowbreak
constants.rs}). Both figures are node policy rather than consensus, as
the \emph{Consensus Guide} section just cited develops.

Deployed wallets nevertheless anchor close to the tip, by deliberate
choice rather than necessity. The anchor's tree is the anonymity set in
which the membership check conceals the spent note --- the proof
reveals only that the note is \emph{some} leaf committed by that anchor
--- so the most recent admissible anchor, naming the largest such tree,
maximises that set, while an idiosyncratically old or deep anchor would
instead fingerprint the spender. The wallet backend accordingly selects
the most recent block it has checkpointed lying at least a fixed number
of confirmations behind the tip --- by default, per ZIP-315, $3$
confirmations for notes the wallet produced itself and $10$ for notes
received from third parties (type \texttt{ConfirmationsPolicy},
\texttt{zcash\_client\_backend/src/\allowbreak data\_api/wallet.rs})
--- trading a sliver of reorg robustness for set size and uniformity.
Consensus mandates none of this; it is wallet policy. Nor is an old
anchor a soundness hole: membership in an older, smaller tree implies
the note was committed even earlier, which is precisely what Alice must
establish --- preventing a \emph{second} spend of the same note is not
the anchor's job but the nullifier's, the base-field element published
with every spend and constructed in \S\ref{sec:spend}.

\subsection{Two pools, two trees}\label{sec:tree-pools}

The volume's two pools (\S\ref{sec:intro}) are distinguished by
\emph{state}, not identity. Each keeps its own, fully independent note
commitment tree and nullifier set (ZIP-229), hence its own anchors, and
its own chain value pool balance --- the consensus-tracked net value
the pool holds. The Ironwood tree is built by the identical depth-$32$
$\mathsf{MerkleCRH}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ construction of
Definition~\ref{def:tree}, capacity $2^{32}$ (ZIP-258); it starts
\emph{empty} at the pool's activation (the NU6.3 network upgrade), and
an Ironwood Action proves membership against
$\mathsf{anchorIronwood}$, the anchor of the Ironwood tree, admissible
from that activation onward. The Orchard tree, meanwhile, keeps
growing after the split: every Orchard-pool Action still appends a
$\cmx$, because spends under the \emph{cross-address restriction} ---
the post-NU6.3 consensus rule confining each Orchard-pool Action's
created note to the spent note's own receiver --- are paired with
fabricated or \emph{dummy} zero-valued output notes (rule, fabrication,
and the dummy treatment are all taken up in \S\ref{sec:turnstile}), and
its anchors remain live for spend proofs. The wallet machinery of
\S\ref{sec:tree-shardtree} carries over unchanged --- the backend pins
\texttt{IRONWOOD\_SHARD\_HEIGHT} to the same $16$, in the same file, as
its Orchard twin. Where a note rests, and against which tree it may be
spent, are henceforth facts about \emph{which pool} --- state the rest
of the journey must thread through every proof.

\section{Spending: the nullifier, the balance, and the signatures}
\label{sec:spend}

The note has rested long enough; its owner now spends it, and the two
remaining requirements of \S\ref{sec:intro} come due at once:
R3, that no note can be spent twice, and R4, that no transaction creates
value --- each to be achieved while disclosing neither which note is
spent nor what it is worth. This section constructs the machinery the
spend contributes to an Action (the unit of shielded transfer named in
\S\ref{sec:intro}: one Action spends one old note and creates one new
one; \S\ref{sec:action} assembles it in full): the nullifier that
retires the old note, the value commitment and binding signature that
prove conservation, the transaction digest that both signatures sign,
and the re-randomised signature that proves spend authority. Each
security property is stated where its object is constructed and proved
once in \S\ref{sec:security}, the volume's standing division of labour.

\subsection{The nullifier, and the loop back to minting}
\label{sec:spend-nullifier}

The third requirement: to prevent the owner from spending the same note
twice, without a public registry of spent notes --- which would leak
exactly what is being hidden. Each note yields a single deterministic
tag, its \emph{nullifier}, that appears only when its owner spends the
note; the network maintains a set of all revealed nullifiers and
rejects any repeat. The nullifier must satisfy two properties that pull
in opposite directions: it must be \emph{deterministic} (the same note
always yields the same tag, making a second spend detectable) yet
\emph{unlinkable} (publishing it reveals nothing about which note or
address it came from).

% compute/spend_quantities.py:
%   p_Pallas < p_Vesta: True
%   both primes in (2^254, 2^255): bit lengths 255, 255
\begin{definition}[Orchard nullifier]\label{def:nullifier}
For a note $\mathbf{n}$ with commitment $\cmsym$ (\S\ref{sec:mint}) and
the owner's nullifier key $\nksym$ (\S\ref{sec:keys}),
\[
\nfsym := \Extract\bigl(\bigl[\, F_{\nksym}(\rho) + \psi \,\bigr]
G^{\mathsf{nf}} + \cmsym\bigr) \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Pallas}},
\]
where $F_{\nksym} : \F_{p_{\Pallas}} \to \F_{p_{\Pallas}}$ is a
circuit-efficient PRF --- Poseidon over the Pallas base field, keyed by
$\nksym$ (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Arithmetisation-friendly
hashing: Poseidon'');
$G^{\mathsf{nf}} := \GroupHash^{\Pallas}(\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard},
\mathtt{K}) \in \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})$ is a fixed nothing-up-my-sleeve
Pallas generator (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-grouphash}); the scalar
$F_{\nksym}(\rho) + \psi$ lives in $\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$, a valid Pallas
scalar because $p_{\Pallas} < p_{\Vesta}$; and $\Extract$ takes the
$x$-coordinate (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}). We write
$G^{\mathsf{nf}}$ for this base to distinguish it from the spend-auth
generator $G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$ of \S\ref{sec:keys}.
\end{definition}

We read the construction against the two requirements.
\emph{Determinism}: every ingredient ($\nksym$, $\rho$, $\psi$,
$\cmsym$) is fixed once the note exists, so $\nfsym$ is a fixed
function of the note --- spending it twice yields the same $\nfsym$
twice, and the network rejects the second. \emph{Unlinkability}: the
term $[F_{\nksym}(\rho) + \psi]\,G^{\mathsf{nf}}$ masks the commitment
with a value that appears random to anyone without $\nksym$, so no one
can tie the published $\nfsym$ back to $\cmsym$ or to the address ---
this is where the note's $\rho$ and $\psi$, carried since
\S\ref{sec:mint}, perform their function. Three security properties
sharpen this reading:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{No double-spend} (the \emph{Orchard Book} calls this
\emph{balance}, since double-spending is how one would create value):
each note has exactly one nullifier, and forging a colliding nullifier
for a different note reduces to the discrete-logarithm problem on
Pallas.
\item \emph{Spend unlinkability}: without $\nksym$, the published
$\nfsym$ is unlinkable to the note or address, under the Decisional
Diffie--Hellman (DDH) assumption on Pallas --- that
$([a]G, [b]G, [ab]G)$ is computationally indistinguishable from
$([a]G, [b]G, [c]G)$ for independent uniform $a, b, c$ (the
\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Diffie--Hellman: computational and
decisional'') --- \emph{or}, independently, the PRF assumption on $F$
(\S\ref{sec:keys}): either assumption alone suffices. The disjunction
is the design rationale for the nullifier's group structure ---
defence in depth, unlinkability surviving the failure of one
assumption (the \emph{Orchard Book}, \texttt{design/nullifiers.md}).
\item \emph{Forward consistency (Faerie resistance)}: a freshly created
note takes as its uniqueness seed the nullifier of the note spent
alongside it, $\rho_{\mathrm{new}} := \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$; since the
network rejects duplicate nullifiers within each pool, this forces
accepted notes' $\rho$ values to be unique within that pool. Equality
across pools is not checked and is instead cryptographically
infeasible, as is any accidental cross-pool nullifier collision. This
closes an attack from earlier
designs in which forged notes could be made to share a nullifier.
\end{itemize}

The last item closes the loop opened at minting: the $\rho$ that
\S\ref{sec:mint} could only type as a base-field element fixed at note
creation now acquires its provenance. Every note is born from a spend,
and inherits as $\rho$ the nullifier that spend reveals --- unique among
accepted nullifiers in its pool by the double-spend rule this section
establishes.

\begin{proposition}[Nullifier uniqueness]\label{thm:nf-unique}
Each note determines exactly one nullifier, and any efficient adversary
that produces two distinct notes sharing a nullifier yields a solver
for the discrete-logarithm problem on Pallas (modelling the derivation
of $G^{\mathsf{nf}}$ as a random oracle, \S\ref{sec:toolbox-grouphash}).
\end{proposition}

The proposition is proved in \S\ref{sec:security}; here we only record
what the construction is asked to deliver. The unlinkability claim
above is a design-level guarantee, not a deferred theorem: it enters
the volume's privacy statement as one assumption of the informal
end-to-end theorem that closes \S\ref{sec:security}.

Both pools of this volume use this derivation unchanged: an Ironwood
note's nullifier is Definition~\ref{def:nullifier} verbatim, and a
note's nullifier is checked only against its \emph{own} pool's
nullifier set --- the two pools keep separate, independent nullifier
sets (ZIP-229; the split's state is laid out in \S\ref{sec:pools}).

\subsection{Conservation without disclosure: value commitments and the
binding signature}\label{sec:spend-value}

The fourth requirement: to guarantee that a transaction creates no
value, while concealing every amount. The instrument is the additive
homomorphism of Pedersen commitments, constructed in the \emph{Crypto
Guide}, \S``The Pedersen commitment'': commit to each amount, and
verify that the commitments \emph{sum} to the publicly declared net
flow --- the individual values remain hidden, while their balance is
publicly verifiable.

\begin{definition}[Net value commitment]\label{def:cv}
Each Action commits to its own value change with a Pedersen commitment
\[
\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}} := [\,v_{\mathrm{old}} - v_{\mathrm{new}}\,]\,
V^{\mathsf{Orchard}} + [\rcv]\,R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}
\;\in\; \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}),
\]
where $v_{\mathrm{old}}$ is the spent value, $v_{\mathrm{new}}$ the
created value, $\rcv \in \F_{p_{\Vesta}}$ the commitment randomness (a
Pallas scalar), and
\begin{align*}
V^{\mathsf{Orchard}} &:= \GroupHash^{\Pallas}
(\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}cv}, \texttt{"v"}), \\
R^{\mathsf{Orchard}} &:= \GroupHash^{\Pallas}
(\mathtt{z.cash:Orchard\text{-}cv}, \texttt{"r"})
\;\in\; \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})
\end{align*}
are nothing-up-my-sleeve points (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-grouphash}), hence
independent Pallas generators.
\end{definition}

The spender draws each $\rcv$ uniformly at random and afresh for every
Action, at the moment of bundle assembly. In contrast to the note's
$\rcm$, $\psi$, and $\mathsf{esk}$ --- all derived deterministically
from the note seed $\mathsf{rseed}$ (\S\ref{sec:mint}) --- the trapdoor
$\rcv$ belongs to no note and is never transmitted; its uniformity and
secrecy are precisely what make $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$ hiding, and
what keep the aggregate $\mathsf{bsk}$ introduced next known only to
the transaction author.

We now apply the homomorphism. Summing over the $m$ Actions of a
transaction,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{m} \cvsym^{\mathsf{net},i} =
\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_i (v_{\mathrm{old},i} - v_{\mathrm{new},i})\Bigr]
V^{\mathsf{Orchard}} + [\mathsf{bsk}]\,R^{\mathsf{Orchard}},
\qquad
\mathsf{bsk} := \sum_i \rcv_i \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Vesta}},
\]
with $\mathsf{bsk}$ the \emph{binding signing key}. The transaction
declares its net flow in a signed cleartext field
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$, with the convention that a
positive value is net value flowing \emph{out} of the pool (paying fees
or transparent outputs, say). The network subtracts the declared part:
\[
\mathsf{bvk} := \sum_i \cvsym^{\mathsf{net},i} -
[\,v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}\,]\,V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}
\;\in\; \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Balance equals key knowledge]\label{thm:balance}
If and only if the transaction balances --- the summed value component
equals the declared $v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ --- does
the $V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$-component cancel, leaving
$\mathsf{bvk} = [\mathsf{bsk}]\,R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$: a public key
whose secret $\mathsf{bsk}$ the transaction author knows.
\end{proposition}

The idea is already visible in the displays: by the homomorphism the
aggregate commitment splits into a $V$-component carrying the net value
difference and an $R$-component carrying $\mathsf{bsk}$; subtracting
the declared flow leaves a pure $R$-multiple exactly when the books
balance, and only the author --- who chose the $\rcv_i$ --- knows their
sum. The full argument, including why an unbalanced author cannot know
a discrete logarithm of $\mathsf{bvk}$ to base $R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$,
is deferred: Proposition~\ref{thm:balance} is proved in
\S\ref{sec:security}.

Proving knowledge of $\mathsf{bsk}$ --- via a \emph{binding signature}
under the key $\mathsf{bvk}$ --- is therefore precisely proving that
the accounts balance, with no amount revealed (the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Binding signatures as a proof of knowledge of a discrete
logarithm''). The signature scheme is RedPallas, constructed in
\S\ref{sec:spend-auth}, here instantiated on the base
$R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ --- the value-commitment randomness base,
distinct from the spend-authorisation base $G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$. A
single signature over the whole transaction (concretely, over the
ZIP-244 \emph{signature digest} of \S\ref{sec:spend-sighash}) additionally binds
all its Actions together, so that no one can remove or transplant any
of them.

From NU6.3 --- the upgrade that opens the Ironwood pool
(\S\ref{sec:intro}) --- the construction runs once \emph{per pool}: a
v6 transaction (\S\ref{sec:pools}) carries independent
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ and
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Ironwood}}$ fields, and each pool's
Actions sum to their own $\mathsf{bvk}$, checked by their own binding
signature over the same bases
$V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}, R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ (ZIP-229). The Ironwood
component's conservation is thus this subsection verbatim, run over the
Ironwood Actions' $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$ values.

\subsection{What the signatures sign: ZIP-244 digests and their v6
form}\label{sec:spend-sighash}

The message both Orchard signatures sign is ZIP-244's
\emph{signature digest}, not the raw transaction. ZIP-244 first defines
the closely related transaction-identifier digest as a tree of
personalised $\mathrm{BLAKE2b\text{-}256}$ hashes, one node per
transaction component. Its root is
\[
\mathsf{txidDigest} := \mathrm{BLAKE2b\text{-}256}\bigl(
\texttt{ZcashTxHash\_} \,\|\, \mathsf{branchid},\
d_{\mathrm{hdr}} \,\|\, d_{\mathrm{trans}} \,\|\, d_{\mathrm{Sap}}
\,\|\, d_{\mathrm{Orch}}\bigr),
\]
where $\mathsf{branchid}$ is the $4$-byte identifier of the active
network upgrade and each $d_\bullet$ is a personalised digest of one
component's data, an absent component hashing the empty string. The
signature digest has the same root and shielded children but replaces
$d_{\mathrm{trans}}$ with a $d_{\mathrm{transSig}}$ that commits to the
applicable transparent input and sighash flags. For a coinbase
transaction or a transaction without transparent inputs, the two
transparent children coincide, so the signature digest equals
$\mathsf{txidDigest}$; otherwise they need not. Orchard
spend-authorisation and binding signatures sign the signature digest
(ZIP-244).

% compute/spend_quantities.py:
%   C_enc partition: [0:52] compact, [52:564] memo, [564:] tail
%   (52 + 512 = 564)
The Orchard branch commits to the entire bundle,
\begin{align*}
d_{\mathrm{Orch}} := \mathrm{BLAKE2b\text{-}256}\bigl(
&\texttt{ZTxIdOrchardHash}, \\
&d_{\mathrm{C}} \,\|\, d_{\mathrm{M}} \,\|\, d_{\mathrm{N}} \,\|\,
\mathsf{flagsOrchard} \,\|\,
\mathrm{LE}_{64}(v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}) \,\|\,
\mathsf{anchor}\bigr),
\end{align*}
through three digests that partition every Action's fields: the
\emph{compact} $d_{\mathrm{C}}$ over $\nfsym$, $\cmx$, $\mathsf{epk}$
and the first $52$ bytes $C_{\mathrm{enc}}[0{:}52]$ of the note
ciphertext (\S\ref{sec:delivery}); the \emph{memo} $d_{\mathrm{M}}$
over the $512$-byte $C_{\mathrm{enc}}[52{:}564]$; and the
\emph{non-compact} $d_{\mathrm{N}}$ over $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$, the
re-randomised validating key $\rk$ (constructed in
\S\ref{sec:spend-auth}), the tail $C_{\mathrm{enc}}[564{:}]$ and
$C_{\mathrm{out}}$.
Beside them sit the bundle-shared fields: the one-byte flag field
$\mathsf{flagsOrchard}$ (its bit assignments are laid out in
\S\ref{sec:turnstile}; the v6 renaming in \S\ref{sec:pools}), the
declared balance, and the $\mathsf{anchor}$ of \S\ref{sec:tree}.

Folding in every Action's $\nfsym$, $\cmx$, $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$,
$\rk$ and ciphertexts beside the shared $\mathsf{flagsOrchard}$,
balance and $\mathsf{anchor}$, these child digests pin the exact
bundle: no Action can be transplanted into another transaction nor any
field altered without changing the digest (ZIP-244). The
spend-authorisation signature of \S\ref{sec:spend-auth} under each $\rk$
and the binding signature under $\mathsf{bvk}$ both sign the signature
digest.

\paragraph{The v6 form.} From NU6.3 the tree gains an Ironwood branch
(Figure~\ref{fig:sighash-tree}):
\[
\mathsf{txidDigest} := \mathrm{BLAKE2b\text{-}256}\bigl(
\texttt{ZcashTxHash\_} \,\|\, \mathsf{branchid},\
d_{\mathrm{hdr}} \,\|\, d_{\mathrm{trans}} \,\|\, d_{\mathrm{Sap}}
\,\|\, d_{\mathrm{Orch}} \,\|\, d_{\mathrm{Irw}}\bigr),
\]
with five new Ironwood personalisations ---
\texttt{ZTxIdIronwd\_H\_v6} for the branch,
\texttt{ZTxIdIrnActCH\_v6}, \texttt{ZTxIdIrnActMH\_v6},
\texttt{ZTxIdIrnActNH\_v6} for its compact, memo, and non-compact
digests, and \texttt{ZTxAuthIrnwdH\_v6} on the authorizing-data side
--- and four revised v6 personalisations for the Sapling and Orchard
branches (\texttt{ZTxIdSSpendNH\_v6}, \texttt{ZTxAuthSapliH\_v6},
\texttt{ZTxIdOrchardH\_v6}, \texttt{ZTxAuthOrchaH\_v6}); an absent
component hashes the empty string under its personalisation (ZIP-229).

\subsection{Spend authorisation: RedPallas re-randomisation}
\label{sec:spend-auth}

One capability remains unproven: that the spender is \emph{authorised}
to spend the note --- that they hold $\ask$ (\S\ref{sec:keys}) ---
established without linking this spend to any other spend by the same
key. RedPallas, a re-randomisable Schnorr signature, provides exactly
this. It is the Schnorr template of the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``RedDSA: re-randomisable Schnorr for Orchard'', instantiated on
Pallas, with hash $H^{*}$ --- BLAKE2b-512 reduced mod $p_{\Vesta}$.

% compute/spend_quantities.py:
%   GenRandom input: 80 bytes = 640 bits
%   reduction bias bound: log2(p_Vesta/2^640) = -386.00
%   (i.e. O(2^254/2^640) = O(2^-386))
The novel ingredient is \emph{key re-randomisation}: the spender draws
a randomiser $\alpha \in \F_{p_{\Vesta}}$ uniformly at random, fresh
for every Action --- RedPallas's $\mathsf{GenRandom}$ hashes $80$
uniform bytes with $H^{*}$, making $\alpha$ statistically uniform on
$\F_{p_{\Vesta}}$ (reduction bias $O(2^{254}/2^{640}) = O(2^{-386})$)
--- and sets
\[
\rsk := \ask + \alpha \;\in\; \F_{p_{\Vesta}},
\qquad
\rk := \aksym + [\alpha]\,G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}
     = [\rsk]\,G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}
\;\in\; \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}).
\]
The spender publishes the \emph{randomised} validating key $\rk$, not
the real $\aksym$, and signs the signature digest with $\rsk$.

Unlinkability is retained alongside authority. Because $\alpha$ is
fresh per spend, the key $\rk$ is uniformly random and unlinkable
across spends (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Key re-randomisation and
unlinkability''), yet remains a valid key for the underlying authority.
The circuit enforces that $\rk$ is a re-randomisation of the witnessed
$\aksym$ (the key re-randomisation check, \S\ref{sec:action}) and that
this $\aksym$ is the key bound into the note's address (the CommitIvk
check, \S\ref{sec:action}), so a valid Action proves the spender knows
$\ask$ --- without ever revealing $\aksym$.

\begin{proposition}[Unforgeability under re-randomisation]
\label{thm:redpallas-euf}
RedPallas is existentially unforgeable under chosen-message attack
(EUF-CMA; the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Syntax and security goal'') in
the random oracle model under discrete-logarithm hardness on Pallas,
and remains so under key re-randomisation: signatures under
re-randomised keys $\rk = \aksym + [\alpha]\,G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$,
for randomisers $\alpha$ known to the adversary, give no advantage in
forging under $\aksym$ or any of its re-randomisations.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{thm:redpallas-euf} is proved in \S\ref{sec:security}.

The spend's contributions are now complete. Alongside the delivery
fields $(\mathsf{epk}, C_{\mathrm{enc}}, C_{\mathrm{out}})$ of
\S\ref{sec:delivery}, an Action publishes the nullifier $\nfsym$, the
net value commitment $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$, the randomised validating
key $\rk$ with its spend-authorisation signature, and the new note's
$\cmx$ (\S\ref{sec:mint}); the transaction adds one binding signature
per pool. What remains is to assemble these pieces into the Action and
its zero-knowledge statement --- the work of \S\ref{sec:action}.

\section{The Action assembled: walkthrough, verification, and the
statement}\label{sec:action}

The pieces are on the table: an address (\S\ref{sec:keys}), a note and
its commitment (\S\ref{sec:mint}), the delivery ciphertexts
(\S\ref{sec:delivery}), the tree (\S\ref{sec:tree}), and the spend's
nullifier, value commitment, sighash, and signatures
(\S\ref{sec:spend}). This section assembles them: one payment end to
end, the network's side of it, and the formal statement carrying both.
The unit of
shielded transfer is the \emph{Action}: one Action simultaneously
spends one old note and creates one new note --- a deliberately fixed
shape that conceals whether a given Action is ``really'' a spend, an
output, or both.

\subsection{One Action, one payment: the walkthrough}
\label{sec:action-walk}

% compute/action_walkthrough.py:
%   5 ZEC = 5 * 10^8 zatoshi = 500000000
%   net value v_old - v_new = 500000000 - 500000000 = 0
Consider the simplest possible payment: Alice pays Bob $5$ ZEC ($v = 5
\cdot 10^{8}$ zatoshi, at $10^{8}$ zatoshi per ZEC) from a note worth
exactly $5$ ZEC. This payment is one Action. (The names A1--A10 are
the checks of Definition~\ref{def:action} below.)

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Bob hands Alice an address.} Bob derives a diversified
address $(d, \pkd)$ from his incoming viewing key $(\dk,\ivk)$
(Construction~\ref{def:keys-addr},
\S\ref{sec:keys}) and gives it to Alice. It reveals nothing and is
unlinkable to his other addresses.

\item \textbf{Alice builds the Action.} Alice owns an old note
$\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{old}}$ worth $5$ ZEC, whose commitment
$\cmx_{\mathrm{old}}$ is a leaf of the tree. She computes its nullifier
$\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ (Definition~\ref{def:nullifier}), which its
publication will retire; constructs Bob's new note
$\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{new}} := (\mathsf{addr}_{\mathrm{Bob}},\, 5 \cdot
10^{8},\, \rho_{\mathrm{new}},\, \psi_{\mathrm{new}},\,
\rcm_{\mathrm{new}})$ with $\rho_{\mathrm{new}} :=
\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$, and its commitment $\cmx_{\mathrm{new}}$
(Construction~\ref{def:notecommit}); forms the net value commitment
$\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$ with $v_{\mathrm{old}} - v_{\mathrm{new}} = 5
\cdot 10^{8} - 5 \cdot 10^{8} = 0$ (Definition~\ref{def:cv}); selects a
randomiser $\alpha$ and forms $\rk$ (\S\ref{sec:spend-auth}); and reads
off a recent anchor and her note's Merkle path
(Construction~\ref{def:authpath}, \S\ref{sec:tree}).

\item \textbf{Alice proves the Action statement.} She runs the Halo 2
prover on the Action relation, with public inputs $(\mathsf{anchor},
\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}, \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}, \rk,
\cmx_{\mathrm{new}})$ and her note, keys, path, and randomness as
private witness. The proof attests, in one shot and revealing nothing:
the old note is in the tree (A3), its nullifier follows correctly (A5),
she is authorised to spend it (A6, A7), the spent and created notes are
well-formed (A1, A2), and the value commitment is consistent (A4).

\item \textbf{Alice assembles and signs the transaction.} She encrypts
$\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{new}}$ to Bob (\S\ref{sec:delivery}), attaches a
\emph{spend-authorisation signature} (SpendAuthSig) under $\rk$
(\S\ref{sec:spend-auth}), and signs the bundle with the binding
signature under $\mathsf{bvk}$ (\S\ref{sec:spend-value}), which proves
balance. Both signatures sign the ZIP-244 signature digest
(\S\ref{sec:spend-sighash}), welding the Action into this transaction.

\item \textbf{The network verifies.} Consensus checks that it
recognises the anchor, that $\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ is not already in
the nullifier set (no double-spend), that the Halo 2 proof verifies,
and that the SpendAuthSig under $\rk$ and the binding signature under
the recomputed $\mathsf{bvk}$ verify (authority; balance). If all
pass, it adds $\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ to the nullifier set and appends
$\cmx_{\mathrm{new}}$ as a new tree leaf.

\item \textbf{Bob receives.} Scanning with his $\ivk$, Bob
trial-decrypts the ciphertext (\S\ref{sec:delivery}), recovers
$\mathbf{n}_{\mathrm{new}}$, and now owns a $5$-ZEC note he can later
spend by repeating the entire procedure.
\end{enumerate}

That sequence constitutes the entire protocol; the formal Action
statement of \S\ref{sec:action-stmt} is precisely the list of
in-circuit checks step 3 invokes. Equal values are the special case: a
more valuable old note would return the difference to Alice as change
in a further note, subject from NU6.3 to the routing rule constructed
in \S\ref{sec:turnstile}.

After the payment, the chain has gained one nullifier and one
commitment. Its cryptographic contents do not reveal sender, recipient,
or hidden amount, but the transaction still exposes its action count,
bundle structure, timing, fees, transparent components, and any public
pool balance. The privacy claim rests on the proof's zero knowledge and
the composition of every piece of the journey;
\S\ref{sec:security} states its scope.

\subsection{What a node verifies, without ever observing the note}
\label{sec:action-verify}

Nothing in the walkthrough handed the network a note. A validating
node holds no viewing key, cannot trial-decrypt, and so never learns
the sender, recipient, or amount of any Action; all it can confirm is
that the hidden data is internally consistent, and for that it relies
entirely on the proof, never on the note.

What the node receives is an Orchard-protocol \emph{bundle}, the unit
into which the transaction format groups one pool's data: the
per-Action public fields; \emph{one} anchor shared by every Action in
the bundle; a \emph{single} aggregate Halo 2 proof covering all of the
bundle's Actions; and one SpendAuthSig per Action (ZIP-225; crate
\texttt{zebra-chain},
\texttt{src/orchard/\allowbreak shielded\_data.rs}). The declared net
flow $v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ and the binding
signature (\S\ref{sec:spend-value}) appear once per bundle, and the
flags $\mathsf{enableSpends}$, $\mathsf{enableOutputs}$, and --- from
NU6.3 --- $\mathsf{enableCrossAddress}$ (bit $2$ of the flags byte
$\mathsf{flagsOrchard}$ of \S\ref{sec:spend-sighash}, reserved as $0$
in v5 transactions before NU6.3) are likewise bundle-level fields
(ZIP-225 reserves the bit; ZIP-229 assigns it).

Using only the public fields $(\mathsf{anchor}, \cvsym^{\mathsf{net}},
\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}, \rk, \cmx)$ of each Action, the node checks:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item each encoded field element and curve point parses canonically, and
the randomised validating key $\rk$ is not the identity;
\item the bundle's \emph{Halo 2 proof} of the Action statement
(\S\ref{sec:action-stmt}) --- this forces each spent note of nonzero
witnessed value to be a genuine tree leaf (a zero-value \emph{dummy}
spend, the shape by which an Action pads with no real input, skips the
membership check by A3's gate, by design), the nullifiers and new
commitments to follow correctly, and the keys to align;
\item the \emph{anchor} is a Merkle root the node has seen on its
accepted main chain (\S\ref{sec:tree-anchor});
\item the nullifier $\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ is \emph{not already} in
the nullifier set --- the one check that cannot sit inside a single
proof, because it concerns the global history (this prevents a
double-spend);
\item the \emph{spend-authorisation signature} verifies under $\rk$
and the \emph{binding signature} under the recomputed $\mathsf{bvk}$
(\S\S\ref{sec:spend-value}--\ref{sec:spend-auth}), settling authority
and balance.
\end{itemize}
If every Action passes, the node performs the two state changes of
step 5 --- recording each $\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$, appending each
$\cmx_{\mathrm{new}}$ --- which permit the next spender to prove
membership and make a second spend of the same note fail. Thus the
SNARK together with the two signatures enforces a payment's validity,
never the inspection of the note: the node confirms that the accounts
balance and that no one forged anything \emph{while learning nothing
about the note itself}. This is the precise sense in which the
knowledge soundness of the Action SNARK performs all the work --- the
network trusts the proof exactly because a real witness extractably
backs a valid one (the extraction argument of \S\ref{sec:security}).

\subsection{The Action statement, formally}\label{sec:action-stmt}

The relation itself remains. The proof the node verifies is a Halo 2
proof (the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``The Halo 2 proof system''); how
the relation becomes a circuit is \S\ref{sec:circuit}'s subject, and
what its soundness yields is \S\ref{sec:security}'s. Every check is
typed over primitives the \emph{Crypto Guide} constructs ---
commitments (\S``Commitment schemes''), PRFs (\S``Pseudorandom
functions and permutations''), Schnorr keys (\S``Digital
signatures'') --- as instantiated on Pallas in
\S\S\ref{sec:toolbox}--\ref{sec:spend}, and closes with the
requirement it discharges.

% compute/action_walkthrough.py:
%   named public inputs: 8; instance elements: 1+2+1+2+1+1+1+1 = 10
%   disableCrossAddress index: 9 (last of 10)
\begin{definition}[Orchard Action statement, Ironwood circuit
(NU6.3)]\label{def:action}
The Action SNARK proves, with public inputs
\[
(\mathsf{anchor},\, \cvsym^{\mathsf{net}},\, \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}},\,
\rk,\, \cmx,\, \mathsf{enableSpends},\, \mathsf{enableOutputs},\,
\mathsf{disableCrossAddress})
\]
--- eight named inputs; the two Pallas points enter by affine
coordinates, so the instance vector has ten elements --- and with the
two notes' fields, the keys, the Merkle path, the randomiser $\alpha$,
and the value-commitment trapdoor $\rcv$ as private witness, the
conjunction:

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A1] Old-note commitment integrity} --- an equation between
Pallas points, over the Sinsemilla commitment of
Construction~\ref{def:notecommit}:
\[
\cmsym_{\mathrm{old}} = \NoteCommit_{\rcm_{\mathrm{old}}}\!\bigl(
\gd_{\mathrm{old}}^\star \,\|\, \pkd_{\mathrm{old}}^\star \,\|\,
\mathrm{LE}_{64}(v_{\mathrm{old}}) \,\|\,
\mathrm{LE}_{255}(\rho_{\mathrm{old}}) \,\|\,
\mathrm{LE}_{255}(\psi_{\mathrm{old}})\bigr)
\]
--- or $\NoteCommit$ returns $\bot$, the specification's escape hatch
for Sinsemilla's incomplete-addition exceptions
(Remark~\ref{rem:action-bot}). The spent object really is a note (R1's
representation).

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A2] New-note commitment integrity}, with $\rho_{\mathrm{new}}
:= \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$: over the same commitment and the coordinate
extraction of \S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings},
\[
\Extract\bigl(\NoteCommit_{\rcm_{\mathrm{new}}}(\ldots)\bigr) \in
\{\cmx, \bot\} \qquad (\text{with } \Extract(\bot) := \bot).
\]
The created note is well-formed (R1's representation again), and its
uniqueness seed chains to the spent note's nullifier --- the Faerie
resistance of \S\ref{sec:spend-nullifier}.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A3] Membership, gated by $v_{\mathrm{old}}$} --- a Merkle
recomputation over the layer-tagged hash of
Definition~\ref{def:tree}: recomputing the root from
$\Extract(\cmsym_{\mathrm{old}})$ up the witness path of length $32$
gives $\mathsf{root}$, and $v_{\mathrm{old}} \cdot (\mathsf{root} -
\mathsf{anchor}) = 0$ in $\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$. The gate permits a
zero-value dummy spend to
skip the membership check --- how an Action pads with no real input.
The spent note is a real leaf of the tree (R2).

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A4] Net-value commitment integrity} --- the Pedersen
commitment of Definition~\ref{def:cv}: with witnessed
$(\mathrm{magnitude}, \mathrm{sign})$,
\[
v_{\mathrm{old}} - v_{\mathrm{new}} =
\mathrm{magnitude} \cdot \mathrm{sign}, \qquad
\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}} = [\,v_{\mathrm{old}} - v_{\mathrm{new}}\,]\,
V^{\mathsf{Orchard}} + [\rcv]\, R^{\mathsf{Orchard}},
\]
with $v_{\mathrm{old}}, v_{\mathrm{new}} \in [0, 2^{64})$ and
$\mathrm{sign} \in \{-1, +1\}$. The amounts are consistent and in
range: value is conserved (R4).

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A5] Nullifier integrity} --- the PRF-plus-group derivation of
Definition~\ref{def:nullifier}:
\[
\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}} = \Extract\bigl(
[\,F_{\nksym}(\rho_{\mathrm{old}}) + \psi_{\mathrm{old}}\,]\,
G^{\mathsf{nf}} + \cmsym_{\mathrm{old}}\bigr).
\]
The published nullifier is indeed this note's; with the chain's
nullifier set, this prevents double-spending (R3).

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A6] Spend authority} --- a Schnorr-key re-randomisation
(\S\ref{sec:spend-auth}): $\rk = \aksym + [\alpha]\,
G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$. The published validating key belongs to the
note owner's spend authority.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A7] Address integrity} --- the short commitment
$\CommitIvk$ and the scalar multiplication of
Construction~\ref{def:keys-view}: $\ivk = \bot$, or
\[
\ivk = \CommitIvk_{\rivk}\bigl(\Extract(\aksym), \nksym\bigr)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\pkd_{\mathrm{old}} = [\ivk]\,\gd_{\mathrm{old}}.
\]
The keys, the address, and the nullifier key all belong to one
identity; with A6, the spender is authorised (R1's possession
clause).

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A8] / [A9] Spend / output gating} --- field products against
the bundle flags of \S\ref{sec:action-verify}: $v_{\mathrm{old}}
\cdot (1 - \mathsf{enableSpends}) = 0$ and $v_{\mathrm{new}} \cdot
(1 - \mathsf{enableOutputs}) = 0$.
The transaction builder sets these bundle-level flags and consensus
rules constrain them: coinbase transactions (those minting each
block's subsidy, \S\ref{sec:mint-leadbytes}) must set
$\mathsf{enableSpends} = 0$ (ZIP-229). Spending Ironwood notes is
otherwise permitted --- the $\mathsf{enableSpends} = 0$ requirement is
coinbase-scoped, not a pool-wide prohibition (protocol specification
\S\,7.1, transaction consensus rules).

% compute/action_walkthrough.py:
%   A10 product constraints: 2 points * 2 coordinates = 4
\medskip\noindent
\textbf{[A10] Cross-address gating} --- when
$\mathsf{disableCrossAddress} \ne 0$, the created note pays the spent
note's own receiver: $\gd_{\mathrm{new}} = \gd_{\mathrm{old}}$ and
$\pkd_{\mathrm{new}} = \pkd_{\mathrm{old}}$ as full curve points,
enforced by four product constraints of the A3 shape, one per affine
coordinate. The flag is a verifier-supplied boolean public input ---
the tenth, at instance index $9$; its semantics, its instance and wire
encodings, and the consensus requirement that every post-NU6.3
Orchard-pool Action set it are all constructed in
\S\ref{sec:turnstile}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The $\bot$-weakening]\label{rem:action-bot}
Checks A1, A2, and A7 take the specification's $\bot$-weakened forms
because the circuit cannot exclude Sinsemilla's incomplete-addition
exceptional cases: the relation proved is the disjunction, not the
plain conjunction. The weakening costs only a negligible soundness
term --- producing a $\bot$ case is itself a non-trivial
discrete-logarithm relation among the $\GroupHash$ generators (the
closing step of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sinsemilla-cr}, given in
\S\ref{sec:security}), so no efficient prover exhibits one.
\end{remark}

Each of A1--A7 is one entry from the four-requirement map of
\S\ref{sec:intro}: A1--A2 represent notes, A3 proves membership, A5
(with the chain's nullifier set) prevents double-spending, A4
conserves value, and A6--A7 authorise the spender; A8--A10 stand
outside the map as consensus-level gating constraints. This is the
relation $\Rel_{\mathsf{Orchard}}$, and every Action is an instance of
it. Halo 2 produces one aggregate proof \emph{per bundle}, covering
all of the bundle's Actions, while each Action carries its own
SpendAuthSig. Figure~\ref{fig:action-hub} draws the statement in one
place: every public field and every witness component, the checks each
feeds, and, per check, the object it constrains and the section that
constructed it. How the Action circuit arithmetises this relation is
\S\ref{sec:circuit}'s subject; how its knowledge soundness yields the
protocol's security properties is \S\ref{sec:security}'s.

uniqueness seed to the spent note's nullifier. The delivery
ciphertexts (dashed) feed no check: the proof never touches them, and
only the holder of $\ivk$ or $\ovk$ can open them
(\S\ref{sec:delivery}).}

\section{Two pools, one protocol: doors and the v6 transaction}
\label{sec:pools}

Every stage so far has moved value \emph{inside} a shielded pool: note
to note, owner to owner, with the Action of \S\ref{sec:action} as the
unit of transfer. Value also crosses the pool boundary --- entering
from the transparent ledger, arriving as fresh issuance, or migrating
between shielded pools --- and each crossing is public in amount, by
design: what a pool hides is who holds which note, never how much the
pool as a whole contains. This section formalises the three doors
through which value enters, the ledger that meters every crossing, and
the transaction format that carries a two-pool bundle; it closes with
the piece of the split most easily missed --- that the two pools share
one circuit and one verifying key.

The split itself is already in hand. The NU6.3 network upgrade
(ZIP-258, Draft) seals the value pool that has carried Orchard payments
since NU5 and starts a second pool, \emph{Ironwood}, running the same
cryptography. ZIP-229 (Draft) fixed the vocabulary and
\S\ref{sec:intro} adopted it: one \emph{Orchard protocol} --- the
shared design, its Action circuit as modified by ZIP-2006 and its note
encryption as modified by ZIP-2005 --- and two value pools of that one
protocol, the sealed \emph{Orchard pool} and the current \emph{Ironwood
pool}, each with its own note commitment tree, anchor, and chain value
pool balance (\S\ref{sec:tree-pools}). Nothing in the preceding
sections is discarded; what changes is which pool new value may enter
--- this section's subject --- and how an Ironwood note derives its
trapdoor, already constructed as Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive}
(\S\ref{sec:mint}).

The volume's deployed-behaviour citations are pinned to a fixed
baseline, stated here once: the released crate \texttt{orchard}~0.15.0
(the version \texttt{librustzcash} pins; the Ironwood note-format,
note-encryption, and v6-digest work ships in it), the \texttt{orchard}
git tree at \texttt{bef8a27} (a post-0.14.0 development tree predating the
release, cited for the circuit and cross-address work it carries),
\texttt{librustzcash} at commit \texttt{e30517e4}, and
\texttt{protocol.tex} and the ZIPs at \texttt{zips} commit
\texttt{69610984}. The later deployment and migration status of ZIPs~258
and~318 is rechecked against \texttt{zips} commit \texttt{dd1667ff}
(2026-09-01). Forward-looking claims carry one of three epistemic bins:
\emph{specified}, \emph{designed but unspecified}, or \emph{open problem}.

\subsection{The doors: shielding, coinbase, migration}
\label{sec:pools-doors}

Value enters a shielded pool through three doors, each public in
amount.

% numbers: brief c3-18 (dummy v_old = 0; zero-confirmation default
%   sweep policy of propose_shielding)
The first door is the \emph{shielding} transaction, and its instrument
is the sign of the balancing value: by the convention of
\S\ref{sec:spend-value}, a positive
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ is net value leaving the
pool, so a shielding transaction is one whose transparent inputs fund a
\emph{negative} balancing value. Its Actions spend \emph{dummy} notes
--- zero-value placeholders, $v_{\mathrm{old}} = 0$, for which the
Action statement gates off the membership check (check A3,
\S\ref{sec:action}) --- and create real ones, and the binding signature
of \S\ref{sec:spend-value} proves the books balance. An observer learns
the entering amount --- the balancing field is a cleartext transaction
field --- but not the receiving note or address. The deployed
construction path is function \texttt{propose\_shielding}
(\texttt{zcash\_client\_backend},
\texttt{data\_api/\allowbreak wallet.rs} and
\texttt{input\_selection.rs}): it sweeps the given source addresses'
UTXOs (\S\ref{sec:tree}), at zero confirmations under the default
policy, and wallets auto-shield transparent receipts to \emph{internal}
keys --- address material the wallet derives for itself and never
publishes. From NU6.3 this door opens into the Ironwood pool alone: a
shielding transaction funds a negative
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Ironwood}}$, the Orchard entry being
sealed (\S\ref{sec:turnstile}).

The second door is \emph{shielded coinbase} (ZIP-213). Since the
Heartwood network upgrade, new issuance may enter a shielded pool
directly --- ``[c]oinbase transactions MAY contain Sapling outputs''
--- extended at NU5 to the Orchard pool and re-scoped at NU6.3: from
then coinbase may carry ``Sapling-pool and/or Ironwood-pool outputs'',
and ``it is only possible to mine to an Orchard address by sending the
funds to the Ironwood pool'' (ZIP-213). Two riders keep this auditable
and consistent. First, coinbase \emph{maturity} --- the rule that a
block's newly minted outputs may not be spent until a fixed depth has
passed --- is amended to bind transparent outputs only; the amended
rule is the one the \emph{Consensus Guide} reads through its theorem in
\S``Reorg rails, maturity, and the finality floor''. Second, every
shielded coinbase output ``MUST have valid note commitments'' when
recovered under the \emph{all-zero} outgoing viewing key $\ovk$
(\S\ref{sec:keys}): coinbase shielding is publicly decryptable by
construction, so anyone can verify what issuance entered which pool,
while the notes spend exactly like any others afterwards. An Ironwood
coinbase output also carries lead byte $\mathtt{0x03}$, the rule of
Definition~\ref{def:iw-leadbytes} (\S\ref{sec:mint-leadbytes}).

The third door is \emph{migration}: value moving from one shielded pool
into another without touching the transparent ledger. Between this
volume's two pools, migration passes through the one-way turnstile ---
NU6.3's rules confining the sealed Orchard pool to outward flow ---
constructed in \S\ref{sec:turnstile}. Here it is enough that migration,
like the other two doors, is public in amount, because every door
writes to the ledger we now examine.

\subsection{Pool balances: ZIP-209 and its Ironwood analogue}
\label{sec:pools-balances}

Every entry and exit is metered by ZIP-209's pool balances. For each
shielded pool, the \emph{chain value pool balance} is the negation of
the sum of that pool's balancing fields over the whole chain --- for
the earliest shielded pool, Sprout, which declared entry and exit in
a pair of cleartext fields, it is the sum of
$\mathtt{vpub\_old} - \mathtt{vpub\_new}$ --- and a block is invalid if
it would drive any pool balance negative --- shielded, transparent, or
the deferred fund (ZIP-2001's \emph{lockbox}, a chain value pool
accruing a portion of each block subsidy for later disbursement) ---
or total supply past the protocol's fixed cap $\mathsf{MAX\_MONEY}$
(ZIP-209; ZIP-2001). Entry, transfer, and exit therefore reconcile
publicly at pool granularity while individual notes stay hidden.

From NU6.3 the ledger gains one row. The Ironwood pool keeps its own
chain value pool balance (\S\ref{sec:tree-pools}), the negation of the
summed $v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Ironwood}}$ fields of
\S\ref{sec:spend-value}, under the same non-negativity rule. The doors
of \S\ref{sec:pools-doors} write to that row --- a shielding
transaction or a shielded coinbase output raises the Ironwood balance,
value leaving through a positive balancing field lowers it --- while
the sealed Orchard pool's balance can only fall: the accounting shape
of the turnstile, whose rules \S\ref{sec:turnstile} states.

\subsection{The v6 transaction and the Ironwood component}
\label{sec:pools-v6}

% numbers: ZIP-258 (branch id 0x37A5165B; minimum protocol version 170160)
The upgrade has a concrete identity. NU6.3 carries consensus branch
identifier $\mathtt{0x37A5165B}$; ZIP-258 now records both networks'
activation heights and sets minimum network protocol version $170160$ on
both. The deployed validator fixes the same values in
\texttt{zebra-chain/src/parameters/\allowbreak constants.rs}, with
\texttt{librustzcash} hardcoding the same values in
\texttt{zcash\_protocol/src/\allowbreak consensus.rs}. ZIP-258 remains
Draft, but these deployment parameters are no longer
to-be-determined. From activation onward, the ZIP expects Zebra to be
the network's sole full-validator implementation.

% numbers: brief c5-12 (V6_TX_VERSION = 6; version group 0xD884B698;
%   bit 2 of flagsOrchard = enableCrossAddress; 820 bytes per Action)
A two-pool bundle needs a carrier, and the current carrier is the
\emph{v6 format} (ZIP-229): the v5 format's Orchard component (ZIP-225)

Two pointers close the format. At the note level, ZIP-229 states the

\subsection{One circuit, one verifying key}\label{sec:pools-onekey}

For all this new state, nothing new is proved. Both pools share the
post-NU6.3 Action circuit and its proving and verifying keys; the pools
are distinguished by their trees, nullifier sets, balances, and
component position --- not by separate circuits. The released
\texttt{orchard}~0.15.0 encodes the pairing precisely: a
\texttt{BundleVersion} pairs a \texttt{ValuePool} (\texttt{Orchard} or
\texttt{Ironwood}) with a \texttt{ProtocolVersion}
(\texttt{InsecureV1}, \texttt{V2}, or \texttt{V3});
\texttt{circuit\_version()} maps \texttt{V3} to
\texttt{OrchardCircuitVersion::PostNu6\_3} for \emph{both} pools;
\texttt{note\_version()} maps \texttt{Orchard} to \texttt{V2} and
\texttt{Ironwood} to \texttt{V3} --- the lead-byte split of
\S\ref{sec:mint} --- and
\texttt{permits\_cross\_address\_transfers()} is false exactly for the
pair (\texttt{V3}, \texttt{Orchard}) (\texttt{src/lib.rs},
\texttt{src/bundle.rs}). The in-circuit gate is
\texttt{supports\_cross\_address\_restriction()} on
\texttt{OrchardCircuitVersion}, true only for \texttt{PostNu6\_3}
(\texttt{src/circuit.rs}); the \texttt{bef8a27} development tree's
\texttt{BundleFormat} enum and \texttt{Ironwood} circuit-version name
were reworked into these before release.

% numbers: brief c3-27 (3 circuit versions; nine-element public-input
%   tuple of the legacy statement)
\begin{remark}[The three Action-circuit versions]
\label{rem:action-versions}
The deployed implementation distinguishes three Action-circuit
versions: the insecure NU5 original, the NU6.2 correction (ZIP-257,
Final), and the NU6.3 Ironwood circuit --- and only the last constrains
the cross-address flag
(\texttt{OrchardCircuitVersion::supports\_cross\_address\_restriction},
released crate \texttt{orchard}~0.15.0, \texttt{src/circuit.rs}). The
legacy NU5/NU6 statement is the relation A1--A9 alone
(\S\ref{sec:action}), over the nine-element public-input tuple ending
at $\mathsf{enableOutputs}$. Consensus selects the verifying key by
branch (ZIP-258), so from NU6.3 every Action --- in either pool,
whatever the transaction version --- verifies against the Ironwood key.
How the stack came to have three versions --- what the original got
wrong and what the correction changed --- is \S\ref{sec:lineage}'s
story; this remark is the operational list that story cites.
\end{remark}

One door remains open. The shielding and coinbase doors lead into the
Ironwood pool; leaving the sealed Orchard pool --- the migration the
first subsection deferred --- passes through a turnstile with rules of
its own: the seal, the cross-address restriction the verifying-key
selection just made binding, and the one-way flow the pool balances
record. Constructing that turnstile is the next section's work
(\S\ref{sec:turnstile}).

\section{The seal and the turnstile: leaving the Orchard pool}
\label{sec:turnstile}

Every door of \S\ref{sec:pools} opens inward, and from NU6.3 all of
them open into the Ironwood pool. This section constructs the outward
passage that remains: how value held in the sealed Orchard pool leaves
it. Consensus arranges the exit as two instruments --- a \emph{seal},
three rules that stop value entering the pool or circulating inside
it, and a \emph{turnstile}, the one-way, publicly metered gate through
which the sealed pool drains. We take them in order: the three rules,
the in-circuit restriction that is the deepest of them, what spending
under that restriction looks like, the turnstile and the migrating
transaction, and the wallet posture built on top --- closing with the
one-place table of everything NU6.3 changes
(Table~\ref{tab:nu63-delta}).

\subsection{The seal: three rules}\label{sec:turnstile-seal}

% numbers: brief c5-7 (enableCrossAddress = 0; v_balance >= 0)
From NU6.3 activation, three consensus rules seal the Orchard pool to
spend-only, and all three bind every transaction \emph{regardless of
its version} (ZIP-258):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{No issuance.} A coinbase transaction must carry an
\emph{empty} Orchard component: the shielded-coinbase door of
\S\ref{sec:pools-doors} no longer opens into the Orchard pool.
\item \textbf{No circulation.} Every Orchard-pool Action must have
cross-address transfers disabled, $\mathsf{enableCrossAddress} = 0$
(\S\ref{sec:turnstile-cross}). The rule is enforced by the
Action-circuit verifying key, which consensus selects by branch
(Remark~\ref{rem:action-versions}, \S\ref{sec:pools-onekey}), so a v5
transaction cannot bypass it --- ZIP-229 states this
version-independence explicitly.
\item \textbf{No entry.} Per transaction,
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}} \ge 0$: net value may flow
out of the pool, never in (\S\ref{sec:turnstile-migration}).
\end{enumerate}

The deepest of the three is the second, because it lives in the
circuit: from NU6.3, value inside the Orchard pool can no longer move
between addresses --- an Orchard-pool spend can pay only its own
receiver. The motivation is the recoverability guarantee whose
analysis \S\ref{sec:pq-zip2005} owns: legacy
lead-byte-$\mathtt{0x02}$ notes
(\S\ref{sec:mint-leadbytes}) are unrecoverable, so consensus stops
their circulation and funnels the pool's value out through the
turnstile (\S\ref{sec:turnstile-migration}), while still letting
owners spend what they hold.

\subsection{The cross-address restriction (A10)}
\label{sec:turnstile-cross}

Definition~\ref{def:action} (\S\ref{sec:action}) listed check A10 and
deferred its semantics to this section. We give the constraint in
full, then its two encodings --- in the proof's instance vector and on
the wire --- and the backward compatibility both preserve.

\begin{definition}[The A10 constraint in full]\label{def:iw-action}
% numbers: brief c5-32 (instance index 9; 4 coordinate equalities);
%   compute/action_walkthrough.py:
%     instance elements: 1+2+1+2+1+1+1+1 = 10
%     disableCrossAddress index: 9 (last of 10)
%     A10 product constraints: 2 points * 2 coordinates = 4
The tenth public input of Definition~\ref{def:action},
$\mathsf{disableCrossAddress}$, sits at instance index $9$ --- after
$\mathsf{anchor}$, the two affine coordinates of
$\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$, $\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$, the two coordinates
of $\rk$, $\cmx$, $\mathsf{enableSpends}$, and
$\mathsf{enableOutputs}$. Writing $\delta :=
\mathsf{disableCrossAddress}$, and $x(\cdot), y(\cdot)$ for a Pallas
point's affine coordinates (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}), constraint
A10 (cross-address gating) is the conjunction
\begin{gather*}
\delta \cdot \bigl(x(\gd_{\mathrm{old}}) - x(\gd_{\mathrm{new}})\bigr)
= 0, \qquad
\delta \cdot \bigl(y(\gd_{\mathrm{old}}) - y(\gd_{\mathrm{new}})\bigr)
= 0, \\
\delta \cdot \bigl(x(\pkd_{\mathrm{old}}) - x(\pkd_{\mathrm{new}})\bigr)
= 0, \qquad
\delta \cdot \bigl(y(\pkd_{\mathrm{old}}) - y(\pkd_{\mathrm{new}})\bigr)
= 0
\end{gather*}
in $\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$: four coordinate equalities which, whenever
$\delta \ne 0$, force $\gd_{\mathrm{old}} = \gd_{\mathrm{new}}$ and
$\pkd_{\mathrm{old}} = \pkd_{\mathrm{new}}$ as full curve points ---
equality of receivers, not merely of $x$-coordinates. (Crate
\texttt{orchard} at \texttt{bef8a27}, \texttt{src/circuit.rs}:
instance offsets \texttt{ANCHOR} through
\texttt{DISABLE\_\allowbreak CROSS\_\allowbreak ADDRESS}; rows
assigned in \texttt{synthesize\_\allowbreak cross\_\allowbreak
address\_\allowbreak checks}.)
\end{definition}

The constraint shape is deliberately unoriginal: it is the product
form check A3 already uses --- $v_{\mathrm{old}} \cdot (\mathsf{root}
- \mathsf{anchor}) = 0$ --- reused on four extra rows of the same
custom gate (the rows just cited from
Definition~\ref{def:iw-action}'s implementation).
% numbers: brief c5-33 (nine-element historical instance encoding)
For $\delta=0$, the added coordinate preserves the commitment to the
historical nine-element instance column, which supports compatibility
with existing instance data. The changed circuit nevertheless has its
own verifying key; A10 does not leave pre-NU6.3 proofs or verifying keys
unchanged (crate \texttt{orchard} at \texttt{bef8a27},
\texttt{src/circuit.rs}).

% numbers: brief c5-34 (bits 0b001 / 0b010 / 0b100; canonical flags
%   0b0000_0011 Orchard, 0b0000_0111 Ironwood)
On the wire the flag travels in the opposite polarity: bit $2$ of the
component's flags byte (\S\ref{sec:pools-v6}) carries
$\mathsf{enableCrossAddress}$ --- the \emph{enabled} sense, negated
into the instance's $\delta$ --- with the bit assignments spends
$\mathtt{0b001}$, outputs $\mathtt{0b010}$, cross-address
$\mathtt{0b100}$. Deployed encoders carry both canonical flags
values, $\mathtt{0b0000\_0011}$ for an Orchard component and
$\mathtt{0b0000\_0111}$ for an Ironwood one (\texttt{librustzcash},
\texttt{pczt/src/\allowbreak orchard.rs}).

The polarity is backward-compatible by design. Bit $2 = 0$ --- the
restricted state consensus requires of post-NU6.3 Orchard-pool spends
--- is exactly what v5 signers treating the bit as reserved-zero
already produce, so existing hardware signers (ZIP-229 names the
Keystone) keep signing v5 Orchard unshielding transactions --- ones
whose positive balancing value pays out of the pool
(\S\ref{sec:pools-doors}) --- without a firmware update. The Ironwood
component carries the same flag and \emph{sets} it: cross-address
transfers stay enabled there.

\subsection{Spending under the restriction}\label{sec:turnstile-spend}

% numbers: brief c5-36 (zero-valued fabricated output)
An Action spends one note and creates one (\S\ref{sec:action-walk});
under the restriction the created note must pay the spent note's own
receiver. An owner therefore pays anyone by \emph{exiting the pool}:
the real value leaves through a positive
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$
(\S\ref{sec:turnstile-migration}), and the Action's output slot is
filled with a \emph{fabricated} zero-valued note to the spent note's
own receiver --- the fabricated outputs that keep the Orchard tree
growing after the split (\S\ref{sec:tree-pools}).

The fabrication must be thorough. The wallet fills the fabricated
output's ciphertext $C_{\mathrm{enc}}$ (\S\ref{sec:delivery}) with
\emph{random bytes}: a real encryption would let any $\ivk$ holder ---
including a quantum adversary who recovered $\ivk$ from a published
address, the very adversary \S\ref{sec:pq-hndl} confronts --- decrypt
it
and link the Action's nullifier to the address (ZIP-326).

Between construction and signing, the pretence is dropped. In a PCZT
--- a \emph{partially created Zcash transaction}, the format that
carries an in-progress transaction between its constructor, prover,
and signers --- the fabricated output carries its recipient, value,
and $\mathsf{rseed}$ explicitly, so a signer can classify it as a
tolerable dummy rather than an opaque payment (ZIP-326;
\texttt{librustzcash}, \texttt{pczt/src/\allowbreak orchard.rs}).

One gap remains open by construction. Paying into an Orchard-pool
receiver post-NU6.3 requires a spend at that same receiver, hence the
spending key, so consensus cannot fully prevent \emph{self}-sends;
ZIP-326's wallet rules close the gap by forbidding sends to external
Orchard-pool receivers (external as against the internal address
material a wallet derives for itself, \S\ref{sec:pools-doors}).

% numbers: brief c5-40 (nine-line stub; created 2025-06-20)
A final oddity of provenance: the cross-address restriction is
deployed and enforced by consensus, but its owning ZIP does not yet
exist as text --- ZIP-2006 (``Restricting Transfers into the Orchard
Pool'') is a nine-line Reserved stub, owner Daira-Emma Hopwood,
created 2025-06-20, discussion \texttt{zcash/\allowbreak
zips\#1305}. In the bins
of \S\ref{sec:pools}, \emph{specified} (ZIP-229; ZIP-258) are the
flag's encoding, its consensus rule, and the verifying-key selection
by branch; \emph{designed but unspecified} are the restriction's
normative semantics --- ZIP-326 phrases it as equality of ``expanded
receivers'' and carries a \texttt{[TODO define]} in its own text ---
together with the $\mathsf{enableCrossAddress} \to
\mathsf{disableCrossAddress}$ instance mapping and the dummy-spend
treatment, reconstructable only from ZIP-229, ZIP-258, ZIP-326, and
crate \texttt{orchard} at \texttt{bef8a27}, as
Definition~\ref{def:iw-action} does.

\subsection{The turnstile and migration}\label{sec:turnstile-migration}

The exit itself is older than the pool it now drains.

\begin{definition}[Turnstile]\label{def:turnstile}
The \emph{turnstile} is the mechanism by which value leaves the
Orchard pool: a one-way, publicly metered gate, the same instrument
Zcash used for the Sprout retirement (\S\ref{sec:lineage}). Its
consensus content consists of exactly two rules, both encountered
already: the per-transaction non-negativity rule (the seal's third,
\S\ref{sec:turnstile-seal}) and the Ironwood pool-balance rule
(\S\ref{sec:pools-balances}), made precise below.
\end{definition}

The first rule, once more and exactly: from NU6.3 activation,
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}} \ge 0$ holds per transaction,
for \emph{any} transaction version (ZIP-258). Value may exit the
Orchard pool; it may never enter.

% numbers: brief c6-3 (MAX_MONEY bound on the sum of pool balances)
The second rule gives the destination pool its ledger row. Writing
\[
\mathsf{IronwoodPoolBalance} := -\sum_{\mathrm{tx}}
v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Ironwood}}(\mathrm{tx}),
\]
the chain maintains an Ironwood analogue of every pool-balance rule of
\S\ref{sec:pools-balances} (the ZIP-209 extension): the balance is
zero before NU6.3, may never go negative, and the sum of all pool
balances is bounded by $\mathsf{MAX\_MONEY}$ (ZIP-209; ZIP-258).

A migrating transaction, then, has a fixed shape: it spends
Orchard-pool notes with $v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}} > 0$
--- a positive balancing field being net value flowing \emph{out} of
the pool (the sign convention of \S\ref{sec:spend-value}), into the
transaction's transparent funds, from which fees and transparent
outputs are also paid --- and absorbs that value into an
Ironwood component with $v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Ironwood}} <
0$. Both balancing fields are cleartext: the turnstile \emph{reveals
the amounts crossing between pools in every transaction}, exactly as
ZIP-229's privacy analysis states. Consensus specifies no
migration-specific cap; ordinary transaction and $\mathsf{MAX\_MONEY}$
bounds still apply, and ZIP-318's wallet policy recommends migration
outputs no larger than $10{,}000$ ZEC.

% numbers: brief c6-6 (tree capacity 2^32; 3 history-tree fields)
The remaining Ironwood consensus rules are this volume's Orchard rules
re-instantiated (ZIP-258). The anchor $\mathsf{anchorIronwood}$ must
be an earlier main-chain block's final Ironwood treestate --- the
Ironwood tree as that block left it (\S\ref{sec:tree-pools}) --- and
the Ironwood commitment tree has the same capacity $2^{32}$ as its
Orchard twin (\S\ref{sec:tree-pools}). Beyond the trees, the ZIP-221
\emph{history tree} --- the Merkle mountain range over the chain's
history whose root every block header commits to --- gains three
Ironwood fields, the pool's earliest root, latest root, and
transaction count, aggregated exactly like the Orchard trio (ZIP-221;
ZIP-244; ZIP-258, \S``ZIP 221 changes'').

\subsection{Change and wallet posture}\label{sec:turnstile-wallet}

Consensus built the gate; wallets decide the traffic. ZIP-2005's
posture is unambiguous: wallets SHOULD proactively move \emph{all}
funds --- transparent, Sprout, and Sapling included, not merely
Orchard --- into the Ironwood pool, the only pool whose output notes
are recoverable (\S\ref{sec:mint}; the analysis in
\S\ref{sec:pq-zip2005}),
and SHOULD treat the sweep as an ongoing process rather than a
one-off event.

Deployed change-routing logic implements the turnstile's grain, and is
the routing rule the walkthrough of \S\ref{sec:action-walk} deferred:
change from Ironwood value stays in Ironwood; after activation, change
may return to the Orchard pool only when the transaction spends
Orchard notes \emph{and} strictly less value returns than the spends
remove; otherwise change is diverted to Ironwood (function
\texttt{select\_change\_pool}, \texttt{librustzcash},
\texttt{zcash\_client\_backend/src/fees/\allowbreak common.rs}). The
strict inequality keeps every such transaction on the right side of
the seal's third rule: change never asks new value to enter the pool.

Scanning closes down symmetrically (ZIP-326). External Orchard
receivers scan from the wallet birthday (\S\ref{sec:delivery-sync})
through the block immediately before NU6.3 activation --- from the
activation block onward, compliant wallets no longer
pay external Orchard receivers. Internal Orchard receivers scan until
the wallet observes a \emph{zero} Orchard balance after activation ---
change may still trickle back under the routing rule above. Ironwood
receivers scan from the later of birthday and activation to the chain
tip. The zero-balance watermark is sound because two seal rules act
together, not because of non-negativity alone. The non-negativity rule
closes the entry doors --- no transaction moves new value into the pool
--- but an ordinary intra-pool payment satisfies it while raising its
recipient's balance; what blocks that channel is the cross-address
restriction: paying the wallet's Orchard receiver post-NU6.3 requires
a spend at that same receiver, hence the wallet's own notes --- none
remain at balance zero --- or its spending key
(\S\ref{sec:turnstile-spend}). With entry and circulation both shut, a
wallet whose Orchard balance has reached zero can never see it become
nonzero again (ZIP-326; ZIP-258).

\subsection{Scheduled Orchard-to-Ironwood migration (ZIP-318)}
\label{sec:turnstile-scheduled}

ZIP-318 now supplies the wallet playbook that the consensus rules leave
open. Its Draft recommends decomposing value into canonical denominations
of the form $m\,10^k$ ZEC, where $m \in \{1,2,5\}$, from $0.01$ through
$10{,}000$ ZEC, leaving any sub-$0.01$ ZEC residual unmigrated.
For mobile wallets, preparation transactions SHOULD contain exactly $16$
Orchard Actions.
Each scheduled migration transaction then has one Orchard spend padded to
two Actions, one unpadded Ironwood output, no transparent inputs or outputs,
and the canonical fee (ZIP-318, ``Creating Migration Transactions'').

The Draft also coordinates timing so that individual wallets do not choose
distinctive anchors and expiry heights. It provisionally draws anchors from

distribution, and places expiry heights on a $34{,}560$-block grid displaced
by $69{,}120$ blocks (ZIP-318, ``Anchor Heights'' and ``Expiry Heights'').
Wallets schedule and randomise broadcasts, offer network-layer privacy
options, and obtain user consent before beginning. These wallet rules reduce the
information disclosed by the public crossing amounts of

\section{The circuit beneath the journey}\label{sec:circuit}

Every stage of the journey deposited checks into the Action statement;
this section descends one level, from statement to prover. It is
deliberately a map, not a territory.

\subsection{From relation to circuit}\label{sec:circuit-relation}

% brief c4-1
The Action statement of Definition~\ref{def:action}
(\S\ref{sec:action-stmt}) is a \emph{relation}: it declares which
(public input, witness) pairs are acceptable and prescribes no
computation. What the prover executes is the \emph{Action circuit} ---
a single fixed PLONKish circuit (the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``PLONKish
arithmetisation''), identical for every Action, whose satisfiability
Halo~2 proves in zero knowledge. The circuit encodes the conjunction
A1--A10 and is supplied the statement's private witness: the note, the
keys, the Merkle path, the randomiser $\alpha$, and the
value-commitment trapdoor $\rcv$. What follows is a sketch of this
encoding; gate-level detail is delegated to the \emph{halo2 Book} and
the \emph{Orchard Book}.

% brief c4-2
The circuit is assembled from a small set of reusable \emph{gadgets}
(in the halo2 API, \emph{chips}), each a pre-wired block of custom
gates and lookups realising one operation. The dependency runs
backwards through the volume: the Orchard designers chose the
journey's primitives so that these gadgets would be inexpensive under
PLONKish arithmetisation --- why the journey met Sinsemilla and
Poseidon rather than a bit-oriented hash (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-cost}).

% brief c4-3
How any gadget is \emph{built} --- how its gates, lookups, and cell
assignments are declared, and how synthesis turns the filled columns
into the polynomials the proof commits to --- is the subject of the
\emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``From statement to circuit: arithmetisation in
practice''. This volume records only what each gadget \emph{enforces}.

\subsection{The gadget set}\label{sec:circuit-gadgets}

Five gadget families carry the ten checks.

% brief c4-4
\paragraph{ECC.}
The ECC gadget implements the Pallas group law: point addition by the
complete addition formulas, with no exceptional cases (the \emph{Math
Guide}, \S``Elliptic curves''); variable-base scalar multiplication as
a double-and-add ladder of cheaper incomplete-addition rounds finished
by a complete tail; fixed-base scalar multiplication from precomputed
tables; and witnessing that a point lies on the curve. (The
$j$-invariant-$0$ endomorphism --- the \emph{Math Guide}, \S``The
$j$-invariant and the GLV endomorphism'' --- serves only the recursion
layer (the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``Accumulation and the Halo
trick''), cheapening multiplication by verifier challenges; the
Action circuit does not use it.) The gadget realises every group operation in
the statement: $\aksym = [\ask]\, G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$ and $\rk =
\aksym + [\alpha]\, G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$ (A6); $\pkd = [\ivk]\,
\gd$ (A7); $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}} = [v_{\mathrm{old}} -
v_{\mathrm{new}}]\, V^{\mathsf{Orchard}} + [\rcv]\,
R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ (A4); and the point $[F_{\nksym}(\rho) + \psi]\,
G^{\mathsf{nf}} + \cmsym$ inside the nullifier (A5).

% brief c4-5: 10-bit chunk; 1 table lookup + 2 incomplete additions
\paragraph{Sinsemilla.}
The Sinsemilla gadget implements Construction~\ref{def:sinsemilla}
by realising the generator table $P[m]$ as a Halo 2 lookup (the
\emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``The lookup argument''): each $10$-bit chunk
costs one table lookup and two incomplete additions
(\S\ref{sec:toolbox-cost}). On it rest the two composed commitment
gadgets the \emph{Orchard Book} names: $\NoteCommit$, proving the note
commitments of A1 and A2, and $\CommitIvk$, the in-circuit short
commitment proving $\ivk = \CommitIvk_{\rivk}(\Extract(\aksym),
\nksym)$ in A7 --- each bundling the Sinsemilla rounds with the
bit-decomposition and canonicity checks of its field inputs.

% brief c4-6: 32 layers (tree depth, sec:tree)
\paragraph{Merkle path.}
The Merkle-path gadget stacks $32$ layer-tagged
$\mathsf{MerkleCRH}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ hashes --- themselves
Sinsemilla short hashes (Definition~\ref{def:tree}). At each layer a
conditional swap orders the running node and its witnessed sibling by
one bit of the leaf position; the gadget asserts the recomputed root
equal to the public $\mathsf{anchor}$ --- check A3, gated by
$v_{\mathrm{old}}$ so that a dummy spend may skip it.

% brief c4-7: x^5 is Poseidon's only non-linearity
\paragraph{Poseidon.}
The Poseidon gadget implements the Poseidon permutation (the
\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Arithmetisation-friendly hashing:
Poseidon''), whose only non-linearity is $x \mapsto x^5$, to evaluate
the nullifier PRF $F_{\nksym}(\rho) = \mathsf{PoseidonHash}(\nksym,
\rho)$ of Definition~\ref{def:nullifier} cheaply in-circuit; its
output feeds the ECC gadget for A5.

% brief c4-8: v in [0, 2^64); sign in {-1, +1}
\paragraph{Decomposition.}
Lookup-based decomposition, range, and boolean gadgets split field
elements into the bit-chunks the other gadgets consume and range-check
witnessed values: $v_{\mathrm{old}}, v_{\mathrm{new}} \in [0, 2^{64})$
and $\mathrm{sign} \in \{-1, +1\}$ (A4), and canonical point and
field-element encodings.

\subsection{Flags, public inputs, and circuit parameters}
\label{sec:circuit-params}

% brief c4-9: enableCrossAddress at bit 2; disableCrossAddress the
%   tenth public input (instance index 9, Definition def:action)
The flags $\mathsf{enableSpends}$, $\mathsf{enableOutputs}$, and
$\mathsf{enableCrossAddress}$ (bit $2$) need no gadget at all. They
are verifier-supplied public inputs: ZIP-229's flags bytes,
$\mathsf{flagsOrchard}$ and $\mathsf{flagsIronwood}$, fix them to bits
outside the circuit, and they enter it only through the plain
multiplication gates of A8--A10 --- the third negated, wired as the
Ironwood circuit's tenth public input $\mathsf{disableCrossAddress}$
(Definition~\ref{def:action}; semantics in
\S\ref{sec:turnstile-cross}).

% brief c4-10: 2^11 = 2048 rows (k = 11, vs Sinsemilla chunk width
%   k = 10); ten advice columns
The gadgets compose into one fixed circuit whose native field is the
Pallas base field $\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$ --- the field of Pallas
coordinates, so the ECC gadget runs natively --- proved with the
Halo~2 inner-product argument on Vesta, whose scalar field the Pasta
cycle makes exactly $\F_{p_{\Pallas}}$ (the \emph{Halo 2 Guide},
\S``The inner-product polynomial commitment''). The circuit occupies
$2^{11} = 2048$ rows --- the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}'s circuit-size
exponent $k = 11$, not Sinsemilla's chunk width $k = 10$
(Construction~\ref{def:sinsemilla}) --- with ten advice columns
beside the fixed columns and the Sinsemilla lookup table (the
\emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``From statement to circuit: arithmetisation
in practice''). Every Action, whatever it spends or creates, is one
instance of this same circuit; a bundle's Actions are proved together
in the one aggregate proof a node checks from the public inputs alone
(\S\ref{sec:action-verify}); and batch verification --- the deferred
linear multi-scalar multiplications merged across proofs (the
\emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``Accumulation and the Halo trick'') ---
amortises the shared multi-scalar-multiplication work across many
proofs, while retaining proof-specific logarithmic work. The machinery
the journey met one stage at a time is,
beneath it all, a single two-thousand-row table whose satisfiability
is the payment's validity.

\section{End-to-end security}\label{sec:security}

The journey's debts now come due. Each property was stated where its
object was constructed and deferred here: Sinsemilla collision
resistance (Theorem~\ref{thm:sinsemilla-cr},
\S\ref{sec:toolbox-sinsemilla}), nullifier uniqueness
(Proposition~\ref{thm:nf-unique}, \S\ref{sec:spend-nullifier}),
RedPallas unforgeability under re-randomisation
(Proposition~\ref{thm:redpallas-euf}, \S\ref{sec:spend-auth}), and the
balance equivalence (Proposition~\ref{thm:balance},
\S\ref{sec:spend-value}). This section pays them in order, adds the
two properties with no single construction site --- zero knowledge and
the soundness of the composed whole --- and closes with the volume's
final theorem.

\subsection{Guarantees provided by a valid Action proof}
\label{sec:sec-proof}

A SNARK $\pi$ accepted on the public inputs
\[
(\mathsf{anchor},\, \cvsym^{\mathsf{net}},\, \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}},\,
\rk,\, \cmx,\, \mathsf{enableSpends},\, \mathsf{enableOutputs},\,
\mathsf{disableCrossAddress})
\]
testifies, with overwhelming probability, that the prover
\emph{knows} a witness satisfying the checks A1--A10 of
Definition~\ref{def:action}. This is the knowledge
soundness of Halo 2 (the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``A rigorous treatment
of knowledge soundness''): from any prover that convinces the verifier,
an extractor recovers such a witness. Everything on the soundness side
--- nullifier uniqueness, spend authority, balance --- then follows
from one or more conjuncts of A1--A10 together with algebraic
properties of the primitives those conjuncts invoke, and
\S\ref{sec:sins-cr} supplies the primitive reduction they all share.
Privacy rests instead on the zero knowledge of the SNARK
(\S\ref{sec:halo-zk}) together with the DDH, Poseidon-PRF, and KDF/AEAD
assumptions that Theorem~\ref{thm:orchard-e2e}(iv) adds.

\subsection{Sinsemilla collision resistance}\label{sec:sins-cr}

\begin{proposition}[Sinsemilla collisions are discrete-log relations]
\label{prop:sins-cr}
For distinct equal-length input bitstrings $M\ne M'$, a collision
$\Sinsemilla_D(M)=\Sinsemilla_D(M')$ yields an explicit non-trivial
discrete-log relation among the random-oracle-derived generators
$Q(D),P[0],\dots,P[2^k-1]$ of Construction~\ref{def:sinsemilla}; so does
an $x$-coordinate collision $\mathsf{ShortHash}_D(M)=
\mathsf{ShortHash}_D(M')$, and, with the blinding base $H_D$ joining the
generator set, two distinct fixed-length openings of $\ShortCommit$ or
of the extracted commitment $\cmx$. Consequently
Theorem~\ref{thm:sinsemilla-cr} holds --- for fixed-length inputs,
$\Sinsemilla_D$ is collision-resistant assuming DL on $\G$ --- and the
fixed-length short forms of Definition~\ref{def:sinsemilla-commit}
inherit it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Equal bit length ensures that padding produces distinct chunk vectors of
the same chunk count $\ell\le253$. Suppose first that no
incomplete-addition exception occurs. Unfolding the update rule of
Construction~\ref{def:sinsemilla},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sins-unfold}
\Sinsemilla_D(M) \;=\; [2^{\ell}]\, Q(D) \;+\;
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell} \bigl[2^{\ell - i}\bigr]\, P[m_i].
\end{equation}
A repeated chunk value uses the same table generator, so collect its
coefficient. For $0\le j<2^k$, let
\[
\chi_j(m):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}2^{\ell-i}\,[m_i=j]
 \pmod {p_{\Vesta}}.
\]
The vector $\chi(m)$ is injective: before reduction, the binary digits
of its $j$th coordinate mark exactly the positions at which $m_i=j$,
and the bound $\ell\le253$ prevents wraparound modulo
$p_{\Vesta}$. A collision therefore gives
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{2^k-1}[\chi_j(m)-\chi_j(m')]P[j]=\mathcal O.
\]
Because $m\ne m'$ implies $\chi(m)\ne\chi(m')$, this is a non-trivial
linear relation over $\F_{p_{\Vesta}}$ on the table generators. Since
hash-to-curve modelled as a random oracle samples the generators
independently (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-grouphash}), finding such a relation
is the multi-generator discrete-log-relation problem, and no efficient
adversary produces one without solving DL --- the reduction the
\emph{Crypto Guide} gives in its Pedersen-hash collision analysis,
\S``Sinsemilla: an algebraic hash-based commitment''. (The
fixed-length hypothesis is load-bearing, not a convenience: the
deployed hash zero-pads its input to a chunk multiple,
Construction~\ref{def:sinsemilla}, so two messages of different
bit-lengths agreeing after padding collide with no discrete-log
relation at all --- variable-length collision resistance is simply
false.) An $x$-coordinate collision adds one further case: $\Extract$
identifies $P$ with $-P$ (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-encodings}), so
$\Extract(\Sinsemilla_D(M)) = \Extract(\Sinsemilla_D(M'))$ forces
$\Sinsemilla_D(M) = \pm \Sinsemilla_D(M')$. The $+$ case is the point
collision just treated; in the $-$ case, moving everything to one side
of equation~\eqref{eq:sins-unfold} gives
\[
[2^{\ell + 1}]\, Q(D) + \sum_{j=0}^{2^k-1}
[\chi_j(m)+\chi_j(m')]\,P[j] = \mathcal{O},
\]
whose $Q(D)$-coefficient $2^{\ell + 1}$ is non-zero modulo
$p_{\Vesta}$: a non-trivial relation whether or not any chunk differs.
For the blinded forms, subtracting two openings adds
$[r-r']H_D$ to the $+$ relation (and $[r+r']H_D$ to the $-$ relation);
the nonzero message coefficient vector or the nonzero $Q(D)$
coefficient keeps the relation non-trivial. Finally, the
incomplete-addition exceptions ---
inputs on which some $\dotplus$ is undefined --- are equalities between
accumulator points, themselves non-trivial discrete-log relations among
the same generators, so exhibiting one is negligible too --- the step
that priced the $\bot$-weakening of Remark~\ref{rem:action-bot}.
\end{proof}

Collision resistance is the primitive ingredient of note-commitment
binding: since the blinding base $H_D$ of $\NoteCommit$ is independent
of the hash generators (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-sinsemilla}), opening one
commitment to two distinct notes --- even at the level of the
extracted $\cmx$, by the $\pm$ case above --- would again exhibit a
relation Proposition~\ref{prop:sins-cr} excludes: two distinct notes
cannot share a commitment, nor a $\cmx$. Checks A1 and A2 are
therefore genuine integrity
statements about $\cmsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ and the new note's $\cmx$,
and with knowledge soundness they entail that the prover knows the
\emph{specific} opening of each commitment --- the fact every argument
below consumes.

\subsection{Nullifier uniqueness}\label{sec:sec-nullifier}

The extraction just established discharges the double-spend property:
two distinct notes share a nullifier only with probability negligible
in the DL hardness of Pallas, with the derivation of
$G^{\mathsf{nf}}$ modelled as a random oracle
(\S\ref{sec:toolbox-grouphash}) --- exactly the hypotheses
Proposition~\ref{thm:nf-unique} names. No pseudorandomness of $F$ is
invoked, nor could it be: the adversary who forges the colliding notes
holds the PRF key $\nksym$ itself, and every coefficient below,
$F_{\nksym}(\rho)$ included, is known to the reduction.

\begin{proof}[Sketch of Proposition~\ref{thm:nf-unique}]
A collision $\nfsym(\mathbf{n}) = \nfsym(\mathbf{n}')$ for notes
$\mathbf{n} \ne \mathbf{n}'$ is an equality of $x$-coordinates
(Definition~\ref{def:nullifier}), hence
\[
\bigl[\, F_{\nksym}(\rho) + \psi \,\bigr]\, G^{\mathsf{nf}} + \cmsym
\;=\; \pm\Bigl(\bigl[\, F_{\nksym'}(\rho') + \psi' \,\bigr]\,
G^{\mathsf{nf}} + \cmsym'\Bigr),
\]
a non-trivial linear relation among $G^{\mathsf{nf}}$, $\cmsym$, and
$\cmsym'$. Expanding $\cmsym$ and $\cmsym'$ through their known
openings --- extracted by knowledge soundness, or supplied by the
adversary who forged the notes --- over the Sinsemilla generators and
the blinding base $H_D$ turns this into a \emph{known} non-trivial
discrete-log relation among the independent $\GroupHash$ outputs
$\{G^{\mathsf{nf}}, Q, P[0], \dots, P[2^k - 1], H_D\}$: distinctness of
the notes forces a coefficient mismatch somewhere. Producing such a
relation reduces to DL on Pallas exactly as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:sins-cr}.
\end{proof}

Check A5 (nullifier integrity) plus knowledge soundness then ensures
that the $\nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}$ among the public inputs is genuinely
\emph{the} nullifier of a specific committed note, not an arbitrary
value; the chain's duplicate-nullifier rejection
(\S\ref{sec:action-verify}) does the rest.

\subsection{RedPallas unforgeability}\label{sec:sec-redpallas}

The signature scheme's debt, Proposition~\ref{thm:redpallas-euf},
carries a sharpened reading: a forger that, given $\aksym$ and
signatures under re-randomised keys, produces a valid signature under
$\rk = \aksym + [\alpha]\, G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$ for an $\alpha$ of
its own --- possibly $\aksym$-dependent --- choice yields a DL solver
for $\aksym$.

\begin{proof}[Sketch of Proposition~\ref{thm:redpallas-euf}]
The standard forking argument (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Security in
the random oracle model and the forking lemma'', via the
Bellare--Neven general forking lemma) reduces a Schnorr forger to a DL
adversary, and it covers re-randomisation because RedPallas is
\emph{key-prefixed}: the challenge $H^{*}(R \,\|\, \rk \,\|\, m)$
hashes the verification key, so shifting responses cannot transport a
signature from one key to another. The reduction embeds the DL
challenge as $\aksym := X$. It answers signing queries under any
$\rk_i = \aksym + [\alpha_i]\, G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$ without knowing
any secret, via the honest-verifier zero-knowledge simulator with
random-oracle programming: sample $c, s$ uniformly, set $R := [s]\,
G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}} - [c]\, \rk_i$, and program $H^{*}(R \,\|\,
\rk_i \,\|\, m) := c$, aborting on programming collisions exactly as in
the Schnorr EUF-CMA theorem of the same section. It then forks the
adversary at the forgery's critical query $H^{*}(R^{*} \,\|\, \rk^{*}
\,\|\, m^{*})$, obtaining two accepting transcripts that share $R^{*}$
--- and, since both forks share the critical query's input, the same
$\rk^{*}$, hence the same $\alpha^{*}$ --- with distinct challenges.
Two-special soundness (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The Schnorr
identification protocol'') extracts $\rsk^{*}$, the discrete logarithm
of $\rk^{*}$, and the output $\ask = \rsk^{*} - \alpha^{*}$, with
$\alpha^{*}$ the adversary-supplied randomiser, solves DL for
$\aksym$.
\end{proof}

A separate, weaker statement covers honest signers only: for uniform
signer-chosen $\alpha$, sampling $\alpha$ at random and back-deriving
$\aksym := \rk - [\alpha]\, G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}$ reproduces the
joint view of $(\aksym, \alpha, \rk)$ exactly, so honest
re-randomisations are distributed as plain Schnorr keys --- the
unlinkability statement of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Key
re-randomisation and unlinkability'', which does not by itself cover
adversary-chosen $\alpha$; the forking reduction above is what does.

Check A6 (spend authority) plus knowledge soundness ensures the prover
knows the $\alpha$ relating $\rk$ to $\aksym$; combined with the
SpendAuthSig under $\rk$ at the transaction layer
(\S\ref{sec:spend-auth}), this enforces that the spender knows the
$\ask$ behind $\aksym$ --- and hence, through check A7's binding of
$\aksym$ into the address, the authority over $\pkd_{\mathrm{old}}$.

\subsection{Balance preservation}\label{sec:sec-balance}

\begin{proposition}[Balance]\label{prop:balance-sound}
Any accepted Orchard bundle satisfies
\[
\sum_i \bigl(v_{\mathrm{old},i} - v_{\mathrm{new},i}\bigr) =
v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}},
\]
except with probability negligible in the DL hardness of the Pasta
curves in the random-oracle model --- Vesta for the knowledge soundness
of the Action SNARK supplying the A4 openings, Pallas for the
binding-signature extraction and the independence of the
value-commitment bases.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By A4 and knowledge soundness, each $\cvsym^{\mathsf{net},i}$ commits
to the true per-Action difference $v_{\mathrm{old},i} -
v_{\mathrm{new},i}$, and by the Pedersen homomorphism the bundle sum
$\sum_i \cvsym^{\mathsf{net},i}$ splits into a
$V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$-component carrying $\sum_i (v_{\mathrm{old},i} -
v_{\mathrm{new},i})$ and an $R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$-component carrying
$\sum_i \rcv_i$ (\S\ref{sec:spend-value}). Consensus computes
$\mathsf{bvk}$ and checks the binding signature; a valid binding
signature is a Fiat--Shamir proof of knowledge of
$\log_{R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}} \mathsf{bvk}$ (the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Binding signatures as a proof of knowledge of a discrete
logarithm''), so the forking extraction from the proof of
Proposition~\ref{thm:redpallas-euf} recovers $\mathsf{bsk}$ with
$\mathsf{bvk} = [\mathsf{bsk}]\, R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$. Combined with
the extracted A4 openings, a non-zero
$V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$-component of $\mathsf{bvk}$ would yield a
discrete-log relation between the independent bases
$V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ and $R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$, contradicting DL.
Hence the value component equals
$[v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}]\, V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ and
the total value flow matches the declared transparent balance. This
also completes Proposition~\ref{thm:balance}: the ``if'' direction was
algebraic at the construction site, and the extraction just given shows
that an unbalanced author cannot know a discrete logarithm of
$\mathsf{bvk}$ to base $R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$.
\end{proof}

Balance preservation is the fundamental conservation property:
shielded transactions cannot mint zatoshi out of nothing.

\subsection{Zero knowledge}\label{sec:halo-zk}

Halo 2 is \emph{statistical} zero knowledge in the random-oracle model
(the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``Zero knowledge: hiding the witness in
Halo 2''). On input the instance $(\mathsf{anchor},
\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}, \nfsym_{\mathrm{old}}, \rk, \cmx, \ldots)$ ---
and no witness --- the simulator operates as follows:
% compute/security_error.py: n = 2^11 = 2048  (so k = log2 n = 11)
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item sample the Fiat--Shamir challenges $\theta, \beta, \gamma, y, x,
x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4$ and the inner-product argument (IPA) challenges
$u_1, \dots, u_k$ ($k = 11$ rounds, the circuit-size exponent of the
$2^{11}$-row circuit, \S\ref{sec:circuit}) uniformly, programming the
random oracle to return them on the relevant transcript prefixes;
\item sample the final IPA scalar $a$ uniformly;
\item use the freedom in the blinding terms $l_j, r_j$ added at each
IPA round and in the polynomial blinders added to each committed prover
polynomial --- the advice columns in phase~1, the running products in
phase~3, the quotient chunks in phase~4 --- to satisfy the verifier's
equations algebraically, without ever knowing the witness.
\end{enumerate}
The resulting transcript is statistically indistinguishable from a
real proof; the only gap from \emph{perfect} zero knowledge is the
random-oracle programming, which a verifier that observes only the
transcript cannot detect.

Zero knowledge supplies the proof's share of Orchard's privacy: the
SNARK transcript reveals nothing beyond the public inputs of each
Action. That the published data leak nothing \emph{further} is a
separate, computational matter: the nullifier --- itself a public input
(\S\ref{sec:spend-nullifier}) --- hides its note under the PRF security
of Poseidon or, independently, DDH on Pallas (the disjunction stated
there), and the $\mathsf{epk}$ and ciphertexts published alongside
the public inputs (\S\ref{sec:delivery}) hide recipient and value under
the DDH and KDF/AEAD assumptions that
Theorem~\ref{thm:orchard-e2e}(iv) adds.

\subsection{Composition: the end-to-end theorem}\label{sec:sec-compose}

The full soundness of the Action SNARK composes three ingredients.
% compute/security_error.py:
%   log2(p_Pallas) = 254.0000; n = 2^11 = 2048; log2(n/|F|) = -243.00
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{PIOP soundness.} Each polynomial-identity check of the
underlying polynomial interactive oracle proof (PIOP; the \emph{Halo 2
Guide}, \S``Polynomial IOPs and the SNARK design pattern'') carries a
Schwartz--Zippel error (the \emph{Math Guide}, \S``Polynomials over a
field''), summed across constraints. For $\F = \F_{p_{\Pallas}}$ with
$|\F| \approx 2^{254}$ and constraint degree at most $d_{\max}$, the
error per check is at most $d_{\max}\, n / |\F| \approx d_{\max} \cdot
2^{-243}$: the gate polynomial composes degree-$d_{\max}$ gates with
column polynomials of degree $n - 1$ for $n = 2^{11}$ rows, so a
cheating prover's recombined quotient $h \cdot Z_H$ has degree below
$d_{\max}\, n$. The bound is overwhelmingly small.
\item \emph{PCS extractability.} Extraction for the inner-product
commitment reduces to DL on the IPA curve, Vesta (the \emph{Halo 2
Guide}, \S``The inner-product polynomial commitment''), with the
accumulation analysis (the \emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``Accumulation and
the Halo trick'') covering the deferred-MSM batching by which a node
amortises verification across proofs (\S\ref{sec:circuit}); the same
analysis would carry soundness across recursion, which Orchard's
deployment does not use.
\item \emph{Fiat--Shamir soundness in the random-oracle model (ROM).}
The Fiat--Shamir theorem of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The
Fiat--Shamir transform: from interactive to non-interactive'', covers
$\Sigma$-protocols, losing roughly a factor $Q$ in the prover's
random-oracle query budget; for the $\log d$-round Halo 2 transcript
the naive multi-round analysis loses $Q^{O(\log d)}$, a bound vacuous
already at $Q = 2^{80}$. The negligible-error conclusion rests on the
tighter treatments the \emph{Halo 2 Guide} presents in \S``The
algebraic group model'': the direct ROM-plus-DL analysis of BCMS20, or
Ghoshal--Tessaro's tight Fiat--Shamir bound in the AGM.
\end{itemize}

% compute/security_error.py:
%   lambda = floor(log2(order)/2) = 127; log2 sqrt(pi*q/12) = 126.0
Each step's error is negligible at Orchard's parameters: $|\F| \approx
2^{254}$, security parameter $\lambda = 127$ under the convention that
the group order is about $2^{2\lambda}$ --- effectively about $126$
bits after the automorphism speedups (the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Instantiation on elliptic curves; the Pasta curves'') --- and the
union bound across the steps is itself negligible. Combined with the
four application-level reductions ---
Propositions~\ref{prop:sins-cr}, \ref{thm:nf-unique},
\ref{thm:redpallas-euf}, and~\ref{prop:balance-sound} --- the full
chain yields the volume's final theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Orchard end-to-end security, informal]
\label{thm:orchard-e2e}
Under DL hardness on both Pasta curves (Pallas for the
application-level primitives, Vesta for the IPA commitment) in the
random-oracle model --- and, for property (iv), additionally DDH on
Pallas, the PRF security of Poseidon (\S\ref{sec:spend-nullifier}), and
the KDF/AEAD securing the note ciphertext (\S\ref{sec:delivery}) --- no
efficient adversary can:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}, leftmargin=*]
\item produce an accepted Action without knowing a witness satisfying
A1--A10; in particular, any Action whose witnessed $v_{\mathrm{old}}$
is non-zero must spend a genuine leaf of the commitment tree
(zero-value dummy spends, admitted by A3's gate, are accepted by
design);
\item cause two accepted Actions in the same pool and chain history to
share a nullifier without spending the same note twice;
\item cause a bundle's declared transparent balance
$v_{\mathrm{balance}}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ to differ from its true net
value flow $\sum_i (v_{\mathrm{old},i} - v_{\mathrm{new},i})$ --- in
particular, to exceed it, which would create value
(Proposition~\ref{prop:balance-sound});
\item use the Action's cryptographic fields to link it to any specific
recipient or hidden value beyond what is explicit in the public data.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The four properties are, respectively, the soundness of the Action
SNARK, nullifier uniqueness, balance preservation, and zero knowledge
--- and they discharge the four requirements of \S\ref{sec:intro}:
property (i) covers R1 and R2 (value moves only through well-formed
notes with proven membership), (ii) is R3, (iii) is R4, and (iv) is
the disclosure clause over them all. The first three reduce,
informally, to DL on the Pasta curves in the random-oracle model ---
Pallas for the application-level reductions, Vesta for the IPA
extraction. For the fourth, the zero knowledge of the SNARK transcript
is \emph{statistical} and does not depend on application-commitment
hiding or on DL. Full cryptographic unlinkability of an Action, however,
additionally
rests on the DDH assumption on Pallas, the PRF security of Poseidon
(\S\ref{sec:spend-nullifier}), and the KDF/AEAD securing the note
ciphertext (\S\ref{sec:delivery}), and is therefore computational ---
the typical asymmetry of the discrete-log SNARK family: soundness is
computational (a cheating prover is bounded, never impossible), while
the zero knowledge of the transcript is information-theoretic. This is
not a claim that the whole transaction hides its public balance,
component and Action counts, transparent data, timing, address reuse,
voluntary disclosures, or wallet/network metadata. The
journey's debts are paid; \S\ref{sec:lineage} sets these proofs in
their history.

\section{Lineage: three generations of shielded proof}
\label{sec:lineage}

The machinery whose security \S\ref{sec:security} has just established
did not spring up whole: Zcash has carried shielded payments through
three generations of proof system, and concrete trade-offs drove each
transition --- trusted-setup risk, recursion compatibility,
post-quantum posture, and on-chain efficiency. This closing survey
sets the volume's stack in that history.

\subsection{Sprout (2016--present, deprecated)}
\label{sec:lineage-sprout}

% numbers: brief c4-27 (296 bytes; 2016 ceremony; six parties; 2018
%   flaw). compute/lineage_sizes.py: BCTV14 proof = 7*33 + 65 = 296
The original shielded protocol proved its statements with a
\emph{BCTV14} SNARK (Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, Tromer, Virza) over the
pairing-friendly curve BN254 --- a scheme of the pairing-based family
the \emph{Crypto Guide} surveys in \S``SNARKs: succinct
non-interactive arguments of knowledge'' --- with constant-size proofs
($296$ bytes) and constant verification time. Its parameters came from
a per-circuit trusted setup: the 2016 ceremony, a six-party
multi-party computation generating the BCTV14 proving and verification
keys for the fixed Sprout circuit, sound provided at least one
participant destroyed their secret share. Sprout demonstrated that
zk-SNARK shielded payments were feasible at scale, but it remained a
transitional design: the prover was slow, and the circuit hashed with
SHA-256 --- a bit-oriented hash whose boolean operations are
non-native to arithmetic constraints, hence costly in-circuit.
Decisively, a flaw in BCTV14 discovered in 2018 would have allowed
undetected counterfeiting; the pool was deprecated, and users were
enabled and encouraged to migrate shielded Sprout value to later pools.
Residual Sprout value may remain.

\subsection{Sapling (2018--present)}
\label{sec:lineage-sapling}

% numbers: brief c4-28 (192 bytes; three pairings; 2018 MPC).
%   compute/lineage_sizes.py: Groth16 proof = 2*48 + 96 = 192
The second network upgrade, Sapling, replaced this stack with Groth16
--- $192$-byte proofs, three-pairing verification --- over BLS12-381,
with the embedded curve Jubjub serving the in-circuit group operations
and a Pedersen hash (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Commitment
schemes'') serving the Merkle tree. The result was a far faster
prover, a smaller proof, and cheaper in-circuit hashing. Two
structural limitations remained. Groth16 still requires a per-circuit
trusted setup --- the 2018 Sapling MPC, whose universal first phase
was the Powers of Tau ceremony with many participants --- and no known
elliptic-curve cycle extends BLS12-381 (the \emph{Halo 2 Guide},
\S``A cycle of curves for efficient native recursion''), so the proving system
admits no efficient recursion.

\subsection{Orchard and the Pasta cycle (2022--present)}
\label{sec:lineage-orchard}

% numbers: brief c4-29 (2022, NU5)
Orchard, activated by NU5 in 2022 and carried into the Ironwood pool
from NU6.3 with a new note format, Action circuit, and verifying key,
replaced the Sapling stack with the Halo 2 /
Pasta stack this volume has used throughout. Four choices are
principal.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Halo 2}: a PLONKish PIOP compiled with the IPA polynomial
commitment, accumulation-friendly, and with no trusted setup (the
\emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``The Halo 2 proof system'').
\item \emph{The Pasta cycle}: Pallas as the application curve, Vesta
as the proving curve in the IPA layer, designed for recursion (the
\emph{Halo 2 Guide}, \S``Recursive proof composition and accumulation
schemes'').
\item \emph{Sinsemilla}: an algebraic hash whose collision resistance
reduces to DL on Pallas --- an assumption the protocol already
requires elsewhere --- and whose chunk-table structure maps directly
onto Halo 2's lookup argument (\S\ref{sec:toolbox-sinsemilla}).
\item \emph{Poseidon}: serves the nullifier PRF
(\S\ref{sec:spend-nullifier}), where a keyed in-circuit PRF rather
than a collision-resistant hash is required; unlike Sinsemilla, its
security rests on cryptanalytic confidence in algebraic-attack
resistance rather than on a hardness assumption such as DL.
\end{itemize}
The shift followed one consistent design principle: ``transparent
SNARK, recursion-ready'' was the target, and the designers engineered
the Pasta cycle to fit it.

% numbers: brief c4-31 (three circuit versions; orchard 0.15.0)
The stack has since carried one correction and one split. Its primitive
suite and Halo 2/Pasta proving stack remain, while the Action circuit
exists in three versions
--- NU5's original, the NU6.2 emergency correction (ZIP-257, Final),
and the NU6.3 Ironwood circuit with the cross-address input
(\S\ref{sec:turnstile-cross}) --- named \texttt{InsecurePreNu6\_2},
\texttt{FixedPostNu6\_2}, and \texttt{PostNu6\_3} in the released
crate \texttt{orchard}~0.15.0 (\texttt{src/circuit.rs}; the
\texttt{bef8a27} development tree names the third variant
\texttt{Ironwood}); the operational list is
Remark~\ref{rem:action-versions} (\S\ref{sec:pools}). And from NU6.3
the one protocol carries two value pools, the sealed Orchard pool and
the current Ironwood pool (ZIP-229, Terminology; \S\ref{sec:pools}).
The Halo 2 / Pasta stack is unchanged throughout.

\section{The horizon: quantum adversaries and recoverability}
\label{sec:pq}

The lineage of \S\ref{sec:lineage} looked backward; this closing
section looks forward, and asks the one question the thesis journey of
\S\ref{sec:intro} leaves open: whether the value survives its owner's
adversaries. A \emph{cryptographically relevant quantum computer}
(CRQC) --- one large and reliable enough to run Shor's algorithm at
cryptographic parameters (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The quantum
caveat: Shor's algorithm'') --- computes discrete logarithms in
polynomial time, and with them falls every group-structured assumption
of the end-to-end theorem: DL on both Pasta curves and, for
property~(iv), DDH on Pallas (Theorem~\ref{thm:orchard-e2e}). The
theorem's remaining assumptions --- the PRF security of Poseidon and
the security of the KDF/AEAD --- are symmetric, and face only the
generic quantum speedups of \S\ref{sec:pq-grover}.

\subsection{The reversibility taxonomy and the epistemic bins}
\label{sec:pq-bins}

Since the assumptions all fail together, asking \emph{whether} they
fail is unproductive. The productive question is when the failure
matters, and the axis that answers it is reversibility.

\begin{definition}[Reversibility of a quantum break]
\label{def:pq-reversibility}
A quantum break of a deployed protocol is \emph{retroactive} when data
already recorded on chain becomes exploitable on the day the adversary
exists: no later consensus change can help, because the data is public
and permanent. A break is \emph{prospective} when its exploitation
requires a validator to \emph{accept} a forged object after the
adversary exists: disabling the affected protocol before that day
removes every future acceptance event and mitigates the break
completely.
\end{definition}

% numbers: brief c6-15 (exactly one retroactive break on the Orchard
%   assumption surface)
Within the generic discrete-log channels catalogued by ZIP-2005,
Orchard --- in both pools --- has one identified retroactive break:
harvest-now-decrypt-later against the note encryption
(\S\ref{sec:pq-hndl}). The other cryptographic breaks considered here
are prospective --- the discrete-log integrity
stack of \S\ref{sec:pq-integrity} --- or faces only generic speedups
--- the symmetric primitives of \S\ref{sec:pq-grover}. The section is
organised accordingly, and its scope is the Orchard core seen from inside.

\begin{remark}[Method]\label{rem:pq-bins}
Every forward-looking claim below sits in one of the three epistemic
bins of \S\ref{sec:pools}: \emph{specified} --- a published artefact
pins the construction down, and is cited; \emph{designed but
unspecified} --- an informal source describes the design, and the text
says what a specification would still need to fix; \emph{open problem}
--- no artefact exists. Deployed-behaviour claims instead cite
implementation and specification, as throughout the volume.
% numbers: brief c6-17 (verification pins: zips 69610984, 2026-07-09;
%   orchard bef8a27e = v0.14.0, 2026-06-16; released orchard 0.15.0)
The ground truth is pinned: \texttt{protocol.tex} and the ZIPs at
\texttt{zips} commit \texttt{69610984} (2026-07-09); crate
\texttt{orchard} at git commit \texttt{bef8a27e}
($0.14.0\text{-}18\text{-}g\mathtt{bef8a27}$, 2026-06-16)
for circuit claims; the released \texttt{orchard}~0.15.0 --- the
\texttt{librustzcash} pin --- for the lead-byte-$\mathtt{0x03}$ note
path. Each artefact cited below was verified at these pins.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The retroactive break: harvest now, decrypt later}
\label{sec:pq-hndl}

Each Action publishes the pair $(\mathsf{epk}, C_{\mathrm{enc}})$
(\S\ref{sec:delivery}). The confidentiality of $C_{\mathrm{enc}}$
rests on exactly one discrete-log-dependent link: the one-shot
Diffie--Hellman on Pallas between $\mathsf{esk}$ and $\pkd$
(Construction~\ref{def:delivery-enc}; protocol \S\,5.4.5.5; crate
\texttt{orchard}, \texttt{src/note\_encryption.rs}, with the
$\mathsf{esk}$ derivation in \texttt{src/note.rs} and the key
agreement in \texttt{src/keys.rs}). The KDF and the AEAD above that
link are symmetric, and are not the weak layer.

The attack this admits is \emph{harvest now, decrypt later} (HNDL):
record the chain today, break the link when the machine arrives. A
CRQC that knows a recipient's address $(d, \pkd)$ computes
% numbers: brief c6-19 (one discrete logarithm per address lifetime)
$\ivk = \log_{\gd} \pkd$ by Shor --- one discrete logarithm for the
lifetime of the address --- and then runs the recipient's own
trial-decryption loop of \S\ref{sec:delivery-recv} over the recorded
chain: for every Action, $[\ivk]\,\mathsf{epk}$ recovers
$K_{\mathrm{enc}}$, and the AEAD tag identifies which ciphertexts
belong to the address. The adversary obtains, for every note ever sent
to that address, the full plaintext $(d, v, \mathsf{rseed},
\mathsf{memo})$: every amount, every memo, the complete receiving
history --- and equally every \emph{future} ciphertext to the same
address.

Two limits bound the damage. First, $\ivk$ is a viewing capability,
not a spending one (the ladder of \S\ref{sec:keys-ladder}): no spend
authority is conferred, and $\ask$ is not exposed --- its exposure is
a prospective matter, taken up in \S\ref{sec:pq-integrity}. Second,
the attack is keyed by the address: without $(d, \pkd)$ the adversary
has neither the base $\gd$ nor the target $\pkd$, and no known efficient
generic discrete-log attack recovers this channel from the ciphertext
alone. This statement excludes public metadata and auxiliary
information about the recipient. ZIP-2005 records the
same boundary: the exposure requires ``both the ciphertext \dots{} and
the receiver address''.

The break is retroactive by construction. The pair $(\mathsf{epk},
C_{\mathrm{enc}})$ is consensus data, replicated on every full node
forever; its secrecy was fixed at recording time by one specific
Diffie--Hellman instance; and any protocol upgrade alters only
ciphertexts not yet created (ZIP-2005, Non-requirements). The
adversary needs no quantum computer today --- only storage --- and
every Orchard output recorded since NU5 activation is already in that
corpus, which accrues Ironwood outputs from NU6.3 activation onward.

% numbers: brief c6-22 (zips#1133 open since 2022, zips#1134 open
%   since 2016; zero occurrences of ML-KEM, Kyber, ML-DSA, or a
%   lattice scheme at zips commit 69610984)
Bin: \emph{open problem}. Nothing is deployed, and nothing is
specified, that protects even future ciphertexts. ZIP-2005 (Proposed)
excludes privacy by an explicit Non-requirement, states that the
exposure persists for all pools --- Ironwood included --- and says
only that mitigations for future transfers are ``under
consideration''. The tracking issues \texttt{zcash/zips\#1133} (open
since 2022) and \texttt{zcash/zips\#1134} (open since 2016) carry
discussion, not drafts; and the zips repository at commit
\texttt{69610984} contains no occurrence of ML-KEM or its
pre-standardisation name Kyber (the NIST post-quantum key
encapsulation), of ML-DSA (the corresponding signature standard), or
of any lattice scheme. A future hybrid key encapsulation would in any
case protect future outputs only; the recorded ciphertexts are
unrepairable.

\subsection{Prospective breaks: the discrete-log integrity stack}
\label{sec:pq-integrity}

Shor's algorithm invalidates the group assumptions used by the security
reductions of \S\ref{sec:security}; it does not turn those reductions
into attacks automatically. The prospective-break inventory below
therefore gives a separate concrete attack or consequence for each
affected construction.

\emph{Sinsemilla binding.} Collision resistance reduces to the
discrete-log relation problem among the $\GroupHash$-derived
generators (Proposition~\ref{prop:sins-cr}). A CRQC computes every
pairwise logarithm, after which each Sinsemilla commitment opens to
arbitrary messages: a second note under an on-chain $\cmx$ ---
breaking balance through $\NoteCommit$ non-binding --- a fabricated
membership path under any anchor (\S\ref{sec:tree}), and alternative
pairs $(\aksym', \nksym')$ opening the same $\CommitIvk$ --- the
``Spendability'' attacks ZIP-2005 catalogues, forgeries of the kind
the Faerie resistance of \S\ref{sec:spend-nullifier} excludes. A balance
violation committed this way is bounded only by the ZIP-209 turnstile
(\S\ref{sec:turnstile-migration}) and need not be detected at all.

\emph{Value commitments and the binding signature.} The binding of
$\cvsym^{\mathsf{net}}$ is the unknown discrete-log relation between
the bases $V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$ and $R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$
(Definition~\ref{def:cv}, Proposition~\ref{thm:balance}), and the
binding signature is Schnorr under DL in the random-oracle model.
Knowledge of $\log_{V^{\mathsf{Orchard}}} R^{\mathsf{Orchard}}$
reopens any recorded or fresh $\cvsym$ to any value. A CRQC can
separately solve the discrete logarithm of a target $\mathsf{bvk}$ to
obtain its $\mathsf{bsk}$ and forge the binding signature: silent
inflation, again turnstile-bounded (crate \texttt{orchard},
\texttt{src/value.rs}).

\emph{Spend authorisation.} RedPallas unforgeability is DL in the
random-oracle model (Proposition~\ref{thm:redpallas-euf}). One
logarithm --- of $\aksym$, or of any published $\rk$ --- forges spend
authorisation at will; combined with the proof-system break below it
completes arbitrary theft (crate \texttt{orchard},
\texttt{src/primitives/redpallas.rs}).

\emph{Proof soundness.} The Action SNARK is knowledge-sound under DL
on Vesta with Fiat--Shamir in the random-oracle model
(\S\ref{sec:sec-compose}; crate \texttt{halo2\_proofs},
\texttt{src/poly/commitment.rs}). A CRQC breaks the discrete-log
binding/extractability assumption of the IPA polynomial commitment,
invalidating the stated knowledge-soundness reduction. That permits
commitment equivocation; a universal method for forging every false
Action statement does not follow from the reduction alone and requires
an attack on the full proof protocol.

None of the four is retroactive, and the reason deserves spelling out.
Each attack must place a forged object --- a proof, a signature, a
commitment opening --- before a validator and have it \emph{accepted},
and acceptance happens at current height under current consensus
rules. Disabling the DL-dependent pools before a CRQC exists therefore
removes every future acceptance event; transactions already recorded
were validated when the assumptions held, and are never
re-adjudicated. Forking in time is a complete mitigation for the
integrity stack --- with two residues. First, switch-off must precede
the first forgery: an attack landed inside the exposure window is not
repaired afterwards. Second, honest funds inside a disabled pool need
some other way out --- which is precisely the problem ZIP-2005 exists
to solve (\S\ref{sec:pq-zip2005}).

Recorded $\cvsym$ values are perfectly hiding for uniform independent
value-commitment randomness; the randomiser $\alpha$
information-theoretically masks the validating key; and proof
transcripts are statistically simulatable without the witness
(\S\ref{sec:halo-zk}). Nullifiers additionally rely on the secret
$\nksym$ and Poseidon PRF security for unlinkability. Thus no known
efficient generic discrete-log attack extracts these secrets merely
from the recorded cryptographic fields without the receiver address.
This deliberately narrower claim excludes public transaction metadata,
side information, and failures of the symmetric assumptions.

\subsection{Primitives facing only generic speedups}
\label{sec:pq-grover}

Two primitive families in Orchard carry no discrete-log structure.
BLAKE2b serves $\mathsf{PRFexpand}$ and with it every key and
note-randomness derivation (Definition~\ref{def:prfexpand},
\S\ref{sec:keys}; Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive}), the KDF and the
outgoing cipher key (\S\ref{sec:delivery}), the RedPallas challenge
hash, and the ZIP-244 transaction/signature digests
(\S\ref{sec:spend-sighash}); the ZIP-32
(Final) Orchard key tree is hardened-only, built entirely from these
derivations, with no elliptic-curve operation anywhere on the
derivation path (crate \texttt{orchard}, \texttt{src/zip32.rs}).
Poseidon serves the nullifier PRF, keyed by $\nksym$
(\S\ref{sec:spend-nullifier}; crate \texttt{orchard},
\texttt{src/note/nullifier.rs}).

% numbers: brief c6-31 (2^256 -> ~2^128 preimage under Grover; BHT
%   2^(n/3) needing ~2^92 bits of quantum random-access memory)
Against these a quantum adversary gets generic speedups and nothing
more. Grover's search algorithm halves effective preimage security:
$2^{256}$ classical evaluations become about $2^{128}$ quantum ones, a
margin the parameters absorb (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The quantum
caveat: Shor's algorithm''). For collisions the picture is better
still: the Brassard--H\o{}yer--Tapp (BHT) algorithm finds collisions
in $2^{n/3}$ oracle queries but requires random access to a quantum
memory of about $2^{92}$ bits at these output sizes, and the best
\emph{known} implementable generic quantum collision attack remains
the classical parallel one --- the assessment ZIP-2005 adopts, flagged
there as best-known rather than provably best.

One caveat attaches to Poseidon: its PRF security is a cryptanalytic
heuristic with no standard-problem reduction, classically and
quantumly alike (the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Arithmetisation-friendly hashing: Poseidon''). A quantum adversary
gains no identified structural advantage. Recorded-nullifier
unlinkability nevertheless depends on Poseidon behaving as the required
PRF; keeping $\nksym$ off chain does not preserve that claim if the PRF
assumption itself fails.

The survey's load-bearing consequence: the symmetric backbone survives
a discrete-log break intact. A seed holder can still prove, by
re-executing the hardened derivations, that their keys descend from
their seed --- ownership remains \emph{provable} after the assumption
that made it \emph{enforceable} has fallen. This is the fact
ZIP-2005's quantum recoverability builds on.

\subsection{ZIP-2005: quantum recoverability}\label{sec:pq-zip2005}

% numbers: brief c6-34 (ZIP-2005 created 2025-03-31)
ZIP-2005 (``Ironwood Quantum Recoverability''; status Proposed,
created 2025-03-31) is the deployed-track response to the prospective
breaks. It does not make Orchard quantum-secure, and says so; what it
arranges is that funds survive the eventual switch-off.
% numbers: brief c6-35 (NU6.3 consensus branch id 0x37A5165B)
Its activation is bound to the NU6.3 network upgrade (ZIP-258, Draft;
consensus branch $\mathtt{0x37A5165B}$, activation constants by
pointer in \S\ref{sec:pools-v6}), a scheduling that follows the NU6.2
emergency circuit correction (ZIP-257, Final) --- which is why the
originally planned earlier deployment slipped. The consensus half of
the response --- the seal and the turnstile --- was constructed in
\S\ref{sec:turnstile}; this subsection owns the note format and its
security analysis.

% numbers: brief c6-37 (lead byte 0x03; 0x0B domain separator; legacy
%   0x05 || rho input)
The format half is the Ironwood note: note plaintexts carry lead byte
$\mathtt{0x03}$ (\S\ref{sec:delivery-sender}), and with it the hashed
trapdoor of Definition~\ref{def:iw-derive} --- $\rcm$ derived from the
$\mathtt{0x0B}$-domain-separated $\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm}$ concatenating
every note field, in place of the legacy $\mathtt{0x05} \,\|\, \rho$
input. Every note field thereby traverses BLAKE2b on its way into the
trapdoor. The purpose was promised in one sentence at the construction
site (\S\ref{sec:mint-rseed}); here is the argument in full.

Under a CRQC, $\NoteCommit$ alone is not binding: its binding is
Sinsemilla collision resistance, first on the integrity stack to fall
(\S\ref{sec:pq-integrity}). The Ironwood derivation restores binding
\emph{conditionally}. Model $\mathsf{PRFexpand}$ as a random oracle.
An adversary who would open one commitment to two distinct notes, both
openings respecting the derivation, cannot vary any note field freely:
every field enters $\mathsf{pre}_{\rcm}$, so any change re-randomises
$\rcm$, and landing two honestly-derived note--trapdoor pairs on the
same commitment point is a collision-type event in the oracle ---
generically hard, with no group structure for Shor to exploit, in the
sense Theorem~\ref{thm:iw-qrom} makes precise. The \emph{composite} of
the derivation and $\NoteCommit$ is therefore post-quantum binding ---
\emph{provided a verifier checks the derivation}. No deployed Action
constraint checks that derivation: the circuit checks the commitment,
not the derivation that produced its trapdoor. The binding claim is
conditional on that future check, which is exactly the Recovery
Statement's job.

% numbers: brief c6-39 (one BLAKE3 derive_key compression)
The key half is a rederivation path for $\CommitIvk$. The randomness
$\rivk$ descends from $\mathsf{sk}$ through $\mathsf{PRFexpand}$
(Construction~\ref{def:keys-view}) --- or, where no single
$\mathsf{sk}$ serves --- for FROST-generated keys, threshold signing
in which $\ask$ arises from distributed shares rather than from
$\mathsf{sk}$, and for hardware-held keys, where proof authority must
remain separate from the on-device spend key --- ZIP-2005 derives
$\rivk$ from a \emph{quantum key} $\mathsf{qk}$, produced by one BLAKE3
\texttt{derive\_key} compression from a secret $\mathsf{qsk}$, with
signatures of knowledge --- proofs of knowledge of a secret, bound to
a message the way a signature is --- over the transaction sighash
tying the claimed keys to the spend. A caveat: the ZIP types that
sighash only as a message hash, and pins no sighash algorithm.

% numbers: brief c6-40 (7 BLAKE2b-512 compressions, 1 BLAKE3
%   compression, 2 Sinsemilla commitments, 2 Pallas scalar
%   multiplications, 1 Merkle path)
The \emph{Recovery Statement} (non-normative) is what a holder would
one day prove, in a post-quantum knowledge-sound proof system, over a
re-hashed commitment tree: the key derivations ---
$\mathsf{BindKeys}$, ZIP-2005's name for the derivations of $\ask$,
$\aksym$, and $\nksym$ from $\mathsf{sk}$, plus the $\rivk$ branch
above --- then $\ivk = \CommitIvk$, $\pkd = [\ivk]\,\gd$, the $\psi$
and $\rcm$ derivations, the note commitment, a membership path, the
nullifier, and the $\alpha$-re-randomisation tying the public $\rk$ to
$\aksym$. The cost: $7$ BLAKE2b-512 compressions, $1$ BLAKE3
compression, $2$ Sinsemilla commitments, $2$ Pallas scalar
multiplications, and a Merkle path, with the BLAKE2b compressions
dominating.

The binding arguments carry published bounds, stated in the
\emph{quantum random-oracle model} (QROM): the random-oracle model of
the classical proofs (the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Security in the
random oracle model and the forking lemma''), with the adversary
permitted superposition queries to the oracle.

\begin{theorem}[Ironwood binding bounds, as published]
\label{thm:iw-qrom}
% numbers: brief c6-41 (QROM bound O(q^3/N); N ~ 2^254 = Pallas
%   scalar order)
In the QROM, with $q$ oracle queries and $N \approx 2^{254}$ the
oracle output-space size (the Pallas scalar order, $p_{\Vesta}$ in
this volume's notation),
\[
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{QROM}}_{\mathrm{kb}} \le O(q^3/N),
\qquad
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{QROM}}_{\mathrm{Spend}} \le O(q^3/N) +
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{QROM}}_{\mathrm{kb}},
\]
for the key-binding game --- that the derivations bind the recovered
keys --- and the Spendability game of the attack catalogue of
\S\ref{sec:pq-integrity}, respectively (ZIP-2005).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Idea]
The quantum analogue of collision resistance is Unruh's
\emph{collapsing} property: measuring the adversary's superposition of
hash inputs is undetectable to it once the outputs are measured.
Fehr's theorem for HAIFA-mode hashes --- the counter-carrying
iteration mode BLAKE2b instantiates --- reduces collapsing of the
$\rcm$ derivation to modelling BLAKE2b's compression function as a
random oracle, the same modelling the classical proofs already use.
Both classical reductions --- key binding and Spendability --- are
straight-line, running the adversary once without rewinding, and never
reprogram the oracle, so they lift to the QROM unchanged; by Zhandry's
compressed-oracle technique the classical $O(q^2/N)$ collision bound
becomes $O(q^3/N)$.
\end{proof}

% compute/pq_bounds.py:
%   log2(N) = log2(p_Vesta) = 254.0000
%   q = 2^60; log2(q^3/N) = -74.0; log2(q^2/N) = -134.0
Evaluated at $q=2^{60}$ queries, the cubic term is about $2^{-74}$ and
the quadratic comparison is about $2^{-134}$; for $q\ll2^{60}$ both
bounds are correspondingly smaller. Saturating
the cubic bound needs the memory-infeasible BHT-style algorithm of
\S\ref{sec:pq-grover}.

% numbers: brief c6-43 (pre-Ironwood Orchard lead byte 0x02)
The scope is deliberately narrow: binding only, Orchard only. ZIP-2005
repairs binding, not privacy --- the HNDL exposure of
\S\ref{sec:pq-hndl} persists unchanged for every pool, Ironwood
included. Sapling is excluded, with the rationale quoted: ``to reduce
overall protocol complexity and analysis effort'' --- the ZIP-32
Sapling derivations differ significantly from Orchard's hardened-only
tree and would require their own analysis. The consequence is forced
migration: Sapling, Sprout, and pre-Ironwood Orchard (lead byte
$\mathtt{0x02}$) notes are unrecoverable, hence permanently
unspendable once their protocols switch off --- whence the wallet
posture of \S\ref{sec:turnstile-wallet}: move all funds into Ironwood
notes, and treat the sweep as ongoing, since non-recoverable notes may
arrive at any time.

The bins of Remark~\ref{rem:pq-bins}, applied:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Specified and deployed.} The lead-byte-$\mathtt{0x03}$
derivation and the consensus rules sealing the Orchard pool
(\S\ref{sec:turnstile-seal}) are enforced from NU6.3 activation, so
every Ironwood output uses the recoverable note format (ZIP-229;
ZIP-258).
\item \emph{Specified but undeployed.} ZIP-2005 (Proposed) specifies the
$\mathsf{qsk}$/$\mathsf{qk}$ key path, but it is not a consensus rule or
an enforced part of NU6.3.
\item \emph{Designed but unspecified.} The Recovery Protocol itself.
ZIP-2005 presents the Recovery Statement in outline and states that
its details are ``subject to change''; a specification would still
need to pin down the post-quantum proof system (a stated requirement
is that none be assumed now), the collapsing hash for the re-hashed
commitment tree, the linking commitments between circuits, and the
concrete signature-of-knowledge instantiations.
\item \emph{Open problem.} Two items. A post-quantum proof system and
signature scheme for \emph{ongoing} spends --- as opposed to one-shot
recovery --- exist nowhere as drafts (\texttt{zcash/zips\#1134}
carries discussion only). And the switch-off schedule: every
recoverability guarantee is conditional on legacy switch-off preceding
the first quantum forgery, and attacks landed in the exposure window
--- Spendability roadblocks that permanently block recovery,
spend-authority theft, turnstile-bounded inflation
(\S\ref{sec:pq-integrity}) --- are not repaired retroactively.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strategy: recoverability, not resistance}
\label{sec:pq-strategy}

% numbers: brief c6-47 (ragu-tachyon book pin: commit 830bbcda,
%   2026-07-05)
One more artefact completes the picture. Ragu, the recursive proof
system under development for project Tachyon --- a scalability
programme beyond this volume's scope --- is deliberately based on the
elliptic-curve discrete-log problem over the same Pasta cycle, its
authors citing ``reduced concern (in the medium term)'' for
post-quantum soundness risks (ragu-tachyon book,
\texttt{src/protocol/index.md}, commit \texttt{830bbcda}, 2026-07-05).
Bin: designed but unspecified.

Read together, Ragu's choice and ZIP-2005 fix Zcash's quantum strategy
precisely: \emph{recoverability, not resistance}. Keep discrete-log
cryptography while it stands, because its integrity failures are
prospective and fork-mitigable (\S\ref{sec:pq-integrity});
pre-position notes so that funds provably survive the eventual
transition (\S\ref{sec:pq-zip2005}). The one cost this strategy cannot
answer is the retroactive privacy channel: the HNDL corpus of
\S\ref{sec:pq-hndl} accrues damage now.

% numbers: brief c6-49 (twelve-to-eighteen-month horizon claimed)
The strategy should also be read against the publicity. Public claims
that Zcash will be ``fully post-quantum'' on a twelve-to-eighteen-month
horizon, with ML-KEM and ML-DSA under test (CoinDesk, 2026-05-08),
correspond to no ZIP, no draft, and no repository artefact at the pins
of Remark~\ref{rem:pq-bins}; ZIP-2005 itself disclaims making the
protocol secure against quantum adversaries. The record supports a
narrower claim. Without a receiver address there is no known efficient
generic discrete-log route to recorded note plaintexts, and closing the
affected pools before the first forgery would prevent later acceptance
of those forgeries. Ironwood's note format is designed to support a
future proof of ownership, but the recovery protocol and switch-off
schedule remain unspecified. That is where the journey of this volume
ends.
