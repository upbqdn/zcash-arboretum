\begin{abstract}
This volume of \emph{The Zcash Arboretum} states the protocol by which a light
client decides which of several claimed chains carries the most work while
receiving a number of block headers logarithmic in the chain length. It
constructs Merkle mountain ranges under the two bagging orders in use, with
inclusion paths, prefix roots, position binding and aggregate binding; fixes
the security model of the FlyClient paper of B\"unz, Kiffer, Luu and Zamani;
constructs the FlyClient protocol from the header chain commitment and the
per-sample check through the optimal sampling distribution to the
non-interactive protocol and its security theorem, with every hypothesis
named; and states the chain-history commitment of ZIPs 221, 244 and 258, to
which every Zcash block header after the Heartwood activation block commits.
Each component of a Zcash FlyClient is classified as specified, designed but
unspecified, or an open problem. The volume describes the protocol as
specified for NU7, whose deployment ZIP, ZIP 259, has status Draft. It assumes
the \emph{Math Guide}, the \emph{Crypto Guide} and the \emph{Consensus Guide},
and cites the \emph{Ironwood Guide} for network upgrades, ZIP statuses and
transaction-level objects. It is non-normative: the Zcash Protocol
Specification and the Zcash Improvement Proposals are authoritative.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

This section fixes the subject of the volume, its status, its sources and its
notation before any construction: the scope; the named network upgrades and
ZIP statuses on which later rules depend; the three classes in which the
deployment boundary places each component of a Zcash FlyClient; the primary
paper and the policy for its printed defects; the normative sources; and one
table of conventions closing with a symbol index.

\subsection{Scope and status}\label{sec:intro-scope}

This volume of \emph{The Zcash Arboretum} states three objects and the
relation between them. The first is the FlyClient protocol of B\"unz,
Kiffer, Luu and Zamani (ePrint 2019/226). The second is the chain-history
commitment of ZIP 221 (Final), to which every block header after the
Heartwood activation block (Definition~\ref{def:upgrades}) commits: directly,
as the header field $\mathsf{hashLightClientRoot}$, in blocks validated under
the Heartwood or Canopy rules, and, from NU5, as the first input of the hash
$\mathsf{hashBlockCommitments}$ of ZIP 244 (Final). The header fields are
named in ``The header consensus rules'' (\S\ref{sec:history-rules}), and
their values are defined in ``The epoch tree and its root commitment''
(\S\ref{sec:history-tree}) and ``The block commitment hash''
(\S\ref{sec:history-blockcommit}). The
third is the boundary between the protocol and the commitment: each
component of a Zcash FlyClient is classified as specified, designed but
unspecified, or an open problem (Definition~\ref{def:classes}). The volume is
non-normative: the Zcash Protocol Specification, cited as the protocol
specification with the title of a section, and the Zcash Improvement
Proposals are authoritative.

The volume assumes the following, each cited by italic volume name and
section title. From the \emph{Math Guide}: probability, densities and
asymptotics (\S``Probability, asymptotics, and computation'', with
\S``Beyond finite spaces: countable additivity, limits, and densities'' and
\S``Asymptotic notation''). From the \emph{Crypto Guide}: hash functions and
their security notions (\S``Hash functions and the random oracle model'',
with \S``Security notions: preimage, second-preimage, and collision
resistance'' and \S``The random oracle model''); the provable-security
model (\S``The provable-security model'', with \S``Adversaries and the
security parameter'', \S``Security as a game'' and \S``Reductions'');
interactive arguments (\S``Interactive protocols, proofs, and arguments'');
the Fiat--Shamir transform
(\S``The Fiat--Shamir transform: from interactive to non-interactive''); and
the binding and inclusion soundness of Merkle trees (\S``Merkle trees and
commitments to sets'', with its subsections \S``Binary Merkle trees'',
\S``Authentication paths and membership proofs'', \S``Soundness: forging a
path implies a collision'', \S``Layer and domain separation'',
\S``Append-only and incrementally updatable trees'' and \S``Vector
commitments''). From the \emph{Consensus Guide}: work and the most-work rule
(\S``The block tree and the most-work rule'' and \S``Work, and the best
chain''), memoryless clocks (\S``Memoryless clocks and the attribution
sequence''), the retarget rule under NU7 (\S``Difficulty in motion'') and
Equihash (\S``Equihash and the memoryless model''). From the \emph{Ironwood
Guide}, only for the objects it constructs: block chain, height, network
upgrades, ZIPs and the status Draft (\S``Scope and status''); the Orchard
pool and the Ironwood pool (\S``The Orchard protocol and its two pools''); the
encoding $\mathsf{LEBS2OSP}$ (\S``Fields, groups, and encodings''); pool
roots, final treestates and anchors (\S``The note commitment tree'' and
\S``Anchors''); branch IDs and the valid transaction formats, version~5 and
version~6 under NU7 (\S``The transaction format''; ZIP 2003 (Draft);
ZIP 259 (Draft)); and transaction identifiers, the personalised hash
$\mathsf{h}$ and the branch-ID encoding $\beta$ (\S``Transaction digests and
signatures'').
The light-client model is cited directly to ZIP 307 (Draft), ``Security
Model'' and ``Block header validation''.

\begin{definition}[ZIP statuses]\label{def:zip-status}
A \emph{ZIP} and its status \emph{Draft}, the status of every ZIP on
submission, are those of the \emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Scope and status'',
Definition ``ZIP''. Two further statuses occur in this volume
(ZIP 0 (Active), ``ZIP Status Field''):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Final}, the status of a Consensus or Standards ZIP that is both
  implemented and activated on the Zcash network;
\item \emph{Active}, the status, typically of a Process or Informational
  ZIP, reached once rough consensus on it has been reached; ZIP 0 itself has
  this status.
\end{enumerate}
The status is a property of the document and does not decide whether its
rules are in force. The rules of ZIP 258 (Draft) and ZIP 229 (Draft) apply
from NU6.3 activation: ZIP 258, ``NU6.3 deployment'', lists ZIP 229 among
the primary sources of the NU6.3 consensus changes, and ZIP 258, ``Changes to
ZIP 221'', amends the history-tree node of ZIP 221. Every ZIP is cited with
its status at its first citation in the volume.
\end{definition}

The volume describes the protocol as specified for NU7, whose deployment ZIP,
ZIP 259, has status Draft (ZIP 259, preamble). NU6.3 is active.

\begin{definition}[Epistemic classes]\label{def:classes}
A \emph{component} of a Zcash FlyClient is a data structure, rule, check or
proof format of the protocol, or a hypothesis of its security theorem. A
component is
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{specified} when normative text of the protocol specification or
  of a ZIP fixes it, whatever the status of that ZIP
  (Definition~\ref{def:zip-status});
\item \emph{designed but unspecified} when a published design with an
  analysis exists but no ZIP or section of the protocol specification fixes
  it for Zcash;
\item an \emph{open problem} when no design exists.
\end{enumerate}
A property proved in this volume of a specified object is classified by its
object: the object is specified, and the property is proved here.
\end{definition}

The class \emph{specified} is that of the \emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Scope and
status'', Definition ``Epistemic classes''; that volume applies
\emph{designed but unspecified} to a property that a deployed construction is
designed to satisfy but that no specification, ZIP or proof establishes,
whereas this volume applies it to a component that exists only as a published
design; the two uses share the condition that no normative text fixes the
object, and the sense of the \emph{Ironwood Guide} is not used here.

\subsection{Sources and conventions}\label{sec:intro-sources}

The primary source is B.~B\"unz, L.~Kiffer, L.~Luu and M.~Zamani,
``FlyClient: Super-Light Clients for Cryptocurrencies'', IACR ePrint
2019/226, in its sixth revision, the last revision on the ePrint page and the
revision of record; the paper also appeared at the IEEE Symposium on Security
and Privacy. Every statement taken from the paper is cited by the section,
definition, theorem, lemma, algorithm or printed page of that revision. A
section of the paper is written ``Section~5.3'', without the section sign,
which this volume reserves for sections of the protocol specification and of
the \emph{Arboretum}.

Where a statement of this volume rests on a printed statement of the paper
that is defective, the volume states the corrected form at that point and
names the departure. Only defects that touch a stated result are mentioned,
and there is no separate register of them. Claims of the paper that this
volume does not prove are stated as the paper's, with their gaps: for
example Assumption~\ref{ass:rewriting} (``Transition rewriting'') and
Remark~\ref{rem:lemma1} (``Lemma 1 of the paper'').

The normative sources are the following. The protocol specification, in the
sections \S``The Block Chain'', \S``Integers, Bit Sequences, and
Endianness'', \S``Constants'', \S``BLAKE2 Hash Functions'',
\S``Transaction Encoding and Consensus'', \S``Block Header Encoding and
Consensus'', \S``Difficulty filter'', \S``Difficulty adjustment'',
\S``nBits conversion'' and \S``Definition of Work''. ZIP 0 (Active),
which defines the ZIP process and its statuses; ZIP 200 (Final); ZIP 204
(Draft); ZIPs 205, 206, 250, 251, 252, 253, 255 and 257 (Final), the
deployment ZIPs of the upgrades named in Definition~\ref{def:upgrades}
before NU6.3; ZIP 218 (Draft); ZIP 221 (Final); ZIP 229 (Draft); ZIP 244
(Final); ZIP 258 (Draft); ZIP 259 (Draft); ZIP 307 (Draft); and ZIP 2003
(Draft). A ZIP is cited by its number and the title of a section, the title
following the number in the same sentence; the protocol specification is
cited as protocol specification, \S``Title''.

The conventions of the volume and the symbols of later sections are fixed in
Table~\ref{tab:notation}. The paper overloads several symbols: $n$ denotes
both the honest participation and the chain length; $k$ the persistence
depth, the samples per interval and $\log_c \delta$; $\lambda$ both the
security parameter and a statistical exponent; $\delta$ both the weight
fraction checked in full and the backbone parameter for which
$\mu = 1 - \delta$ bounds the adversary's mining power; $L$ both
the fork threshold and the number of blocks of the checked suffix; and $c$
both the weight bound and a ratio of mining power. The table assigns one
meaning to each symbol, and the index that closes it records where each is
defined.

\section{The verification problem}\label{sec:problem}

This section fixes the object a light client verifies, the Zcash block
header, and the baseline procedure that verifies a chain of headers in full.
It states the trust model of the light-client protocol specified for Zcash
and the problem the volume solves, with each term of its solution defined in
one clause and constructed in a later section.

\subsection{Header-only verification}\label{sec:problem-spv}

\begin{definition}[Light client]\label{def:light-client}
A \emph{light client} is a client that is not a full participant in the
network of Zcash peers. It can send and receive payments, but does not store
or validate a copy of the block chain (ZIP 221, ``Terminology'').
\end{definition}

The verifier of the FlyClient paper is a light client given the additional
inputs fixed in Definition~\ref{def:verifier-setting} (``Security and
succinctness of chain proofs'', \S\ref{sec:model-defs}).

Byte strings and integers are related by the maps of the \emph{Ironwood
Guide}, \S``Fields, groups, and encodings'', Definition ``Integer and byte
encodings'': the map $\mathsf{I2LEOSP}_k$ sends an integer in
$\{0, \dots, 2^k - 1\}$ to its $\lceil k/8 \rceil$-byte little-endian
encoding, and the map $\mathsf{LEOS2IP}_k$ sends a $k/8$-byte string to the
integer it encodes in little-endian order.

% [sec:problem-spv] CompactSize: 1 byte for 0..252, 3 for 253..0xFFFF, 5 for ..0xFFFFFFFF, 9 above; minimal encoding
\begin{definition}[CompactSize]\label{def:compactsize}
The \emph{CompactSize encoding} of an integer
$v \in \{0, \dots, 2^{64} - 1\}$ is the byte string
\[
  \mathsf{CompactSize}(v) :=
  \begin{cases}
    \mathsf{I2LEOSP}_8(v)
      & \text{if } 0 \le v \le 252, \\
    \mathtt{0xFD} \,\|\, \mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{16}(v)
      & \text{if } 253 \le v \le \mathtt{0xFFFF}, \\
    \mathtt{0xFE} \,\|\, \mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{32}(v)
      & \text{if } \mathtt{0x10000} \le v \le \mathtt{0xFFFFFFFF}, \\
    \mathtt{0xFF} \,\|\, \mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{64}(v)
      & \text{if } \mathtt{0x100000000} \le v \le
        \mathtt{0xFFFFFFFFFFFFFFFF},
  \end{cases}
\]
of length $1$, $3$, $5$ or $9$ bytes respectively. Only this encoding, the
shortest, is valid for $v$: a byte string that decodes to $v$ under one of
the other three prefixes MUST be rejected (ZIP 204, ``CompactSize'').
\end{definition}

ZIP 204 has status Draft; the minimal-encoding rule binds block headers
independently, through the protocol specification,
\S``Block Header Encoding and Consensus'', which requires the header field
$\mathsf{solutionSize}$ to be encoded with the minimum number of bytes.
That rule prevents a miner from testing several encodings of one Equihash
solution against the difficulty filter.

The upgrade epochs delimited by the network upgrades of
Definition~\ref{def:upgrades} are fixed in ``Upgrade epochs and epoch trees''
(\S\ref{sec:history-epochs}).

\begin{definition}[Block header]\label{def:header}
A Zcash \emph{block} consists of a block header and a sequence of
transactions. A \emph{block header} $\mathrm{hdr}$ is the concatenation, in the
following order, of the fields
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}r l p{0.52\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Bytes & Field & Content \\
\midrule
$4$ & $\mathsf{nVersion}$ & signed 32-bit integer; the consensus rules
  require $\mathsf{nVersion} \ge 4$ \\
$32$ & $\mathsf{hashPrevBlock}$ & the block hash of the previous block \\
$32$ & $\mathsf{hashMerkleRoot}$ & the root of the transaction Merkle tree
  over the identifiers of the block's transactions, stated in ``The
  authorising-data root'' (\S\ref{sec:history-authdata}) \\
$32$ & commitment field & named as below \\
$4$ & $\mathsf{nTime}$ & unsigned 32-bit integer, the block timestamp \\
$4$ & $\mathsf{nBits}$ & unsigned 32-bit integer, an encoding of the
  target threshold \\
$32$ & $\mathsf{nNonce}$ & $32$ arbitrary bytes \\
$3$ & $\mathsf{solutionSize}$ & $\mathsf{CompactSize}(1344)$ \\
$1344$ & $\mathsf{solution}$ & an Equihash solution \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
with integers encoded little-endian. The commitment field is named
$\mathsf{hashReserved}$ before Sapling, $\mathsf{hashFinalSaplingRoot}$ in
Sapling and Blossom, $\mathsf{hashLightClientRoot}$ in Heartwood and Canopy,
and $\mathsf{hashBlockCommitments}$ from NU5; its value in each upgrade
epoch is fixed in ``The header consensus rules''
(\S\ref{sec:history-rules}). The \emph{block hash} of $\mathrm{hdr}$ is the
$32$-byte string
\[
  \mathsf{BlockHash}(\mathrm{hdr}) := \mathrm{SHA256d}(\mathrm{hdr}),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{SHA256d}(x) := \mathrm{SHA256}(\mathrm{SHA256}(x)),
\]
taken over the whole header serialisation, $\mathsf{solutionSize}$ and
$\mathsf{solution}$ included, in internal byte order, the order in which the
hash function outputs its bytes. Let
$\mathsf{ToTarget} \colon \{0, \dots, 2^{32} - 1\} \to \N$ be the conversion
of protocol specification, \S``nBits conversion''. The header $\mathrm{hdr}$
\emph{satisfies proof of work} when $\mathsf{solution}$ is a valid Equihash
solution for $\mathrm{hdr}$ and
\[
  \mathsf{LEOS2IP}_{256}(\mathsf{BlockHash}(\mathrm{hdr})) \le
  \mathsf{ToTarget}(\mathsf{nBits})
\]
(protocol specification, \S``Difficulty filter''). The consensus rules
further require, for a block at height $\eta$, that
$\mathsf{nBits} = \mathsf{ThresholdBits}(\eta)$, the value the
difficulty-adjustment rule of protocol specification, \S``Difficulty
adjustment'' assigns to that height.
\end{definition}

The definition follows protocol specification, \S``Block Header Encoding and
Consensus'' for the field table and its consensus rules, and ZIP 221, ``Block
header semantics and consensus rules'' and ZIP 244 for the renaming of the
commitment field. The hash $\mathrm{SHA256d}$ is registered in the
\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The transparent layer's hashes''. The Equihash
condition is cited, not reconstructed: \emph{Consensus Guide},
\S``Equihash and the memoryless model''. The \emph{work} of a block whose
header carries $\mathsf{nBits}$ is
\[
  \mathsf{work}(\mathrm{hdr}) :=
  \left\lfloor \frac{2^{256}}{\mathsf{ToTarget}(\mathsf{nBits}) + 1}
  \right\rfloor,
\]
and the \emph{total work} $\omega(C)$ of a chain $C$ is the sum of the work
of its blocks; a node considers the valid chain of greatest total work to
be best, breaking ties between leaf blocks in favour of the block received
first (\emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``The block tree and the most-work rule'',
and its subsection \S``Work, and the best chain''; protocol specification,
\S``The Block Chain'' and \S``Definition of Work'').

% [sec:problem-spv] header = 4 + 32 + 32 + 32 + 4 + 4 + 32 = 140 fixed bytes + 3 (CompactSize of 1344) + 1344 (solution) = 1487 bytes
% [sec:problem-spv] solution share 1344/1487 = 90.38%, about 90.4 per cent
The fixed-width fields of Definition~\ref{def:header} occupy
$4 + 32 + 32 + 32 + 4 + 4 + 32 = 140$ bytes. Since
$253 \le 1344 \le \mathtt{0xFFFF}$, the field $\mathsf{solutionSize}$
occupies $3$ bytes, and with the $1{,}344$-byte solution a Zcash block
header is $1{,}487$ bytes, of which the solution is $1344/1487$, about
$90.4$ per cent.

\begin{definition}[Simplified payment verification]\label{def:spv}
A \emph{claimed chain} of length $N \ge 1$ is a sequence of block headers
$C = (\mathrm{hdr}_0, \dots, \mathrm{hdr}_{N-1})$ in which $\mathrm{hdr}_0$
is the header of the genesis block; header $\mathrm{hdr}_\eta$ is the header at
height $\eta$. Given one or more claimed chains, a
\emph{simplified-payment-verification} (SPV) client downloads every header of
every claimed chain and checks, for each chain $C$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(S\arabic*)]
\item \emph{linkage}: for $1 \le \eta \le N - 1$, the field
  $\mathsf{hashPrevBlock}$ of $\mathrm{hdr}_\eta$ equals
  $\mathsf{BlockHash}(\mathrm{hdr}_{\eta-1})$;
\item \emph{proof of work}: for $0 \le \eta \le N - 1$, header
  $\mathrm{hdr}_\eta$ satisfies proof of work in the sense of
  Definition~\ref{def:header}, and its field $\mathsf{nBits}$ equals
  $\mathsf{ThresholdBits}(\eta)$, computed from $\mathrm{hdr}_0, \dots,
  \mathrm{hdr}_{\eta-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
It rejects every chain that fails a check and accepts, among the rest, the
chain $C$ of greatest total work $\omega(C)$, under the best-chain rule of
the \emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``Work, and the best chain''.
\end{definition}

The definition is that of ePrint 2019/226, Section~1, p.~2, instantiated
with the header checks of protocol specification, \S``Block Header Encoding
and Consensus''. For a claimed chain of $N$ blocks the client receives $N$
headers, $1{,}487\,N$ bytes, so its communication is $\Theta(N)$. A block is
at most $2{,}000{,}000$ bytes (same section), so the saving over
downloading full blocks is at most a constant factor, not an asymptotic
one.

An SPV client checks neither the validity of transactions nor any consensus
rule beyond those of the header, and therefore relies on the following
assumption.

\begin{assumption}[SPV assumption]\label{ass:spv}
``The chain with the most PoW solutions follows the rules of the network
and will eventually be accepted by the majority of miners.''
(ePrint 2019/226, Section~1, p.~2, Assumption~1.)
\end{assumption}

The paper formalises the property an SPV client checks as
Definition~\ref{def:spv-predicate}, and strengthens
Assumption~\ref{ass:spv} quantitatively as Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} (``The
$(c, L)$-adversary'', \S\ref{sec:model-adversary}).

\subsection{The light-client model of ZIP 307}\label{sec:problem-zip307}

ZIP 307 (Draft) specifies a light-client protocol in which a light client
obtains, from a Zcash node and a proxy, \emph{compact blocks}: per-block
messages that may carry the block header. The trust model is stated in
ZIP 307, ``Security Model'': the node and proxy combination is assumed to be
``honest but curious'' and ``is trusted to provide a correct view of the
current best chain state and to faithfully transmit queries and
responses''.

ZIP 307, ``Block header validation'' describes, as a proposed enhancement
that the ZIP records as only partially implemented (only check (Z2) is
made), checks of the SPV kind on each compact block that carries a header. With
the fields named as in
Definition~\ref{def:header}, the checks are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(Z\arabic*)]
\item the field $\mathsf{nVersion}$ is at least the minimum block version;
\item the field $\mathsf{hashPrevBlock}$ equals the block hash of the
  previous compact block;
\item the Equihash solution is valid;
\item the target $\mathsf{ToTarget}(\mathsf{nBits})$ is non-zero and at most
  the proof-of-work limit $\mathsf{PoWLimit}$ (protocol specification,
  \S``Constants'');
\item when the last $27$ compact blocks all carry headers, the field
  $\mathsf{nBits}$ is correct under the difficulty-adjustment rule;
\item the block hash, read as a little-endian integer, is at most
  $\mathsf{ToTarget}(\mathsf{nBits})$.
\end{enumerate}
In that proposal, a compact block that fails a check MUST be discarded. The
same section of ZIP 307 further describes a check that the commitment field
equals the root of the Sapling note commitment tree recomputed from the
compact block; from Heartwood that check no longer applies, since the field
changes meaning
(Definition~\ref{def:header}; ZIP 221, ``Block header semantics and
consensus rules''). Checks (Z1) to (Z6), where made on every header, cost
communication linear in the chain length $N$, as in
Definition~\ref{def:spv}.

Of the trust that ZIP 307 places in the node and proxy, a FlyClient verifier
would replace only the trust in which header chain carries the most work,
under Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} (``The $(c, L)$-adversary''). It would replace
the linear header checks by the logarithmic sampling of ``The FlyClient
protocol'' (\S\ref{sec:protocol}); it does not replace faithful transmission
of queries and of compact-block contents. Such a verifier is designed but
unspecified in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:classes}; its status is
fixed in ``A Zcash verifier'' (\S\ref{sec:boundary-verifier}).

\subsection{Statement of the problem}\label{sec:problem-statement}

The problem is the following. Under the SPV assumption
(Assumption~\ref{ass:spv}), strengthened quantitatively as
Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} in ``The $(c, L)$-adversary''
(\S\ref{sec:model-adversary}), a light client is to accept the head of the
chain of greatest total work after communication sublinear in the chain
length $N$. The inputs and the chain to be selected are those of
Definition~\ref{def:spv}; the assumption is strengthened to
Assumption~\ref{ass:cl}, the selection holds except with a failure
probability fixed in ``The FlyClient protocol'' (\S\ref{sec:protocol}), and
the communication falls from $\Theta(N)$ to sublinear.

A client that does not check every proof of work ``can be tricked into
accepting an invalid chain by a malicious prover who can precompute a
sufficiently-long chain using its limited computational power''
(ePrint 2019/226, Section~1, p.~3); the adversary is fixed in ``The
FlyClient security model'' (\S\ref{sec:model}).

The solution uses four objects. A \emph{Merkle mountain range} is a binary
hash tree over a sequence that admits appending a leaf without changing its
peaks or any node beneath them (``Merkle mountain ranges'', \S\ref{sec:mmr}). A
\emph{header chain commitment} is a root, carried in every header after the
genesis header, of such a range over all blocks strictly before that header
(``Header chain commitments and per-sample checks'',
\S\ref{sec:protocol-commit}). A \emph{work-weighted
sample} is a block drawn at a random point of cumulative work and checked
against the commitment of the head (``The sampling distribution'',
\S\ref{sec:protocol-sampling}). A \emph{suffix} of the chain is downloaded
and checked in full, its extent fixed in the same subsection.
\emph{FlyClient} is the protocol that verifies a proof-of-work chain by
drawing logarithmically many such samples and checking each against the
commitment of the head (ePrint 2019/226, Section~1). The commitment of the
paper covers the chain from the genesis block, whereas the commitment of
ZIP 221 covers one upgrade epoch: the blocks from the activation height of
the preceding network upgrade up to the committing block, exclusive (ZIP 221,
``Specification''; ``Upgrade epochs and epoch trees'',
\S\ref{sec:history-epochs}).

\section{Merkle mountain ranges}\label{sec:mmr}

This section constructs the commitment on which the FlyClient protocol and the
chain-history commitment of ZIP 221 rest: a binary hash tree over a sequence
of values whose shape is fixed by the length of the sequence, extended by one
value at logarithmic cost, and opened at one position $j$ by a path from
which the root over the prefix of length $j-1$ is recomputed, and, under the
right fold, also the root over the prefix of length $j$. The
construction is parametrised by the order in which its perfect subtrees are
joined: the right fold of ePrint 2019/226 and the left fold of ZIP 221,
``Background''. The fixed-depth trees of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Binary
Merkle trees'' and \S``Append-only and incrementally updatable trees'', are the
special case of a sequence whose length is a power of two.

Throughout, $H\colon\{0,1\}^*\to\{0,1\}^\kappa$ is a hash function drawn from
a family that is collision resistant in the sense of the \emph{Crypto Guide},
Definition ``Collision resistance'' (\S``Security notions: preimage,
second-preimage, and collision resistance''); ePrint 2019/226, Definition~8,
states the same assumption. A statement holds \emph{barring a collision}
when a deterministic procedure, given any input on which the statement fails,
outputs two distinct strings with equal images under $H$, using the number of
evaluations of $H$ stated with it. Such a procedure is a tight reduction in
the sense of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, Definition ``Reduction loss and
tightness'' (\S``Reductions''), so a probabilistic polynomial-time adversary
makes the statement fail with negligible probability.

\subsection{The recursive definition}\label{sec:mmr-def}

\begin{definition}[Merkle mountain range]\label{def:mmr}
Let $n\ge 1$ and $x_1,\dots,x_n\in\{0,1\}^\kappa$. The \emph{Merkle mountain
range} $M_n$ over $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ is the rooted ordered binary tree, every
internal node of which has exactly two children, a left and a right one, with
a value in $\{0,1\}^\kappa$ at every node, defined by recursion on $n$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $n=1$, $M_1$ is the single leaf with value $x_1$;
\item if $n>1$, let $i=\lfloor\log_2(n-1)\rfloor$, so that $2^i<n\le 2^{i+1}$
  and $2^i$ is the largest power of two strictly below $n$; the root has as
  left child the range $M_{2^i}$ over $(x_1,\dots,x_{2^i})$ and as right child
  the range $M_{n-2^i}$ over $(x_{2^i+1},\dots,x_n)$, where
  $1\le n-2^i\le 2^i$.
\end{enumerate}
Every internal node with children of values $v_\ell$ and $v_r$ has value
$H(v_\ell\,\|\,v_r)$, the image of the $2\kappa$-bit concatenation of its
children's values. The leaves are ordered from left to right and leaf $j$
holds $x_j$. The \emph{root} $\mathsf{rt}_n$ is the value of the root node;
\emph{range} abbreviates Merkle mountain range.

The \emph{depth} of a node is the number of edges from the root to it, and
the depth of a tree the maximal depth of a leaf. A \emph{perfect subtree of
altitude $a$} is a subtree with $2^a$ leaves, all at depth $a$ below its
root; its root node $v$ has \emph{altitude} $\mathrm{alt}(v)=a$. The
\emph{right spine} is the sequence of nodes that starts at the root and
continues from each internal node to its right child, ending at a leaf.
\end{definition}

ZIP 221, ``Terminology'', calls a Merkle mountain range a binary hash tree
that admits appends of new leaves without changing the value of existing
nodes; Theorem~\ref{thm:append} and Proposition~\ref{prop:append-cost} below
establish it for Definition~\ref{def:mmr} for the peaks and every node
beneath them. An append rehashes only the right-spine nodes above the first
perfect one, the counterparts of the bagging nodes of ZIP 221,
``Background''.
Definition~\ref{def:mmr} is ePrint
2019/226, Definition~11, with the base case $n=1$, implicit there, made
explicit. Every node of $M_n$ is the root of a range over a run of
consecutive values, by the recursion. Its depth is fixed in
Lemma~\ref{lem:mmr-left-perfect}.

% compute/mmr.py:
% [sec:mmr-def] depth of M_n = ceil(log2 n) for all 1 <= n < 5000
% [sec:mmr-def] left children along the right spine, then the first perfect
%   spine node, have the sizes of the binary expansion of n, 1 <= n <= 129
\begin{lemma}[Left children are perfect]\label{lem:mmr-left-perfect}
Let $n\ge 1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A range over $2^a$ values is a perfect subtree of altitude $a$.
\item Every left child in $M_n$ is perfect: the left child of a node over
  $m\ge 2$ leaves is the perfect range over $2^{\lfloor\log_2(m-1)\rfloor}$
  leaves.
\item Descend the right spine of $M_n$ from the root, stopping at the first
  node whose leaf count is a power of two. The left children of the nodes
  passed, followed by the node where the descent stops, are perfect subtrees
  of pairwise distinct power-of-two sizes, decreasing from the root
  downwards, covering consecutive runs of leaves from left to right; their
  sizes are the terms of the binary expansion of $n$.
\item The range $M_n$ has depth exactly $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, which is $0$
  for $n=1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) By induction on $a$. For $a=0$ the range is a leaf. For $a\ge 1$,
$\lfloor\log_2(2^a-1)\rfloor=a-1$, so the root has two children over
$2^{a-1}$ values each, perfect of altitude $a-1$ by hypothesis; all leaves
lie at depth $a$.

(2) Every node over $m\ge 2$ leaves is a range $M_m$, whose left child is a
range over $2^{\lfloor\log_2(m-1)\rfloor}$ values, perfect by (1).

(3) Let a right-spine node have $m$ leaves. If $m$ is a power of two, the
node is perfect by (1) and the descent stops. Otherwise
$2^{\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor}<m$, hence $m-1\ge 2^{\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor}$ and
$\lfloor\log_2(m-1)\rfloor=\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor$: the left child takes the
highest term $2^{\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor}$ of the binary expansion of $m$ and
the right child the remaining terms. By induction along the spine, the left
children passed take the terms of the expansion of $n$ from the highest
downwards, and the descent stops when one term is left. The runs are
consecutive because each left child precedes its sibling's leaves.

(4) By induction on $n$. For $n=1$ the depth is $0$. For $n>1$ let
$i=\lfloor\log_2(n-1)\rfloor$. The left child is perfect over $2^i$ leaves,
of depth $i$; the right child has $n-2^i\le 2^i$ leaves and depth
$\lceil\log_2(n-2^i)\rceil\le i$ by hypothesis. The depth of $M_n$ is
therefore $i+1$, which equals $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ since
$2^i<n\le 2^{i+1}$.
\end{proof}

% compute/mmr.py:
% [sec:mmr-def] 6 = 4 + 2 (4 = 2^floor(log2 5)); 7 = 4 + 3 and 3 = 2 + 1,
%   so 7 = 4 + (2 + 1)
\begin{example}[Six and seven leaves]\label{ex:mmr-small}
For $n=6$, $i=\lfloor\log_2 5\rfloor=2$ and $6=4+2$. The left child is the

\subsection{Peaks and bagging}\label{sec:mmr-peaks}

For $n\ge 1$, $\mathrm{popcount}(n)$ is the number of ones in the binary
expansion of $n$.

\begin{definition}[Peaks]\label{def:peaks}
Let $n\ge 1$ and write $n=2^{a_1}+\dots+2^{a_d}$ with
$a_1>\dots>a_d\ge 0$, so that $d=\mathrm{popcount}(n)$, and let
$s_i=\sum_{l<i}2^{a_l}$ for $1\le i\le d$. The \emph{peaks} of the sequence
$(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ are $P_1,\dots,P_d$, where $P_i$ is the perfect range over
$(x_{s_i+1},\dots,x_{s_i+2^{a_i}})$, of altitude $\mathrm{alt}(P_i)=a_i$.
There is one peak per set bit of $n$, of altitude that bit's index; the
altitudes strictly decrease from the left, and the peaks cover consecutive
runs of leaves. The symbol $P_i$ denotes both the subtree and its root value.
\end{definition}

The peaks are the mountains of ZIP 221, ``Background'', whose leftmost peak is
the highest. The same section numbers nodes in the order of their creation;
that numbering enters no committed value and no check, and the root is a
function of the leaf sequence and the bagging order of
Definition~\ref{def:bagging} alone. No node numbering is used in this volume.

\begin{definition}[Bagging orders]\label{def:bagging}
Let $P_1,\dots,P_d$ be the peaks of $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$. The \emph{right fold}
and the \emph{left fold} of the peaks are
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{Bag}_R(P_1,\dots,P_d)
    &= H\bigl(P_1\,\|\,H(P_2\,\|\,\cdots H(P_{d-1}\,\|\,P_d)\cdots)\bigr),\\
  \mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_d)
    &= H\bigl(\cdots H(H(P_1\,\|\,P_2)\,\|\,P_3)\cdots\|\,P_d\bigr);
\end{align*}
a single peak is its own bag under either fold. The left fold is the order
of ZIP 221, ``Background'', which repeatedly joins the two leftmost peaks. For
$b\in\{R,L\}$ the tree $M^b_n$ consists of the $d$ perfect peak subtrees
joined by the $d-1$ internal \emph{bagging nodes} of $\mathrm{Bag}_b$, and
$\mathsf{rt}^b_n$ is its root value. Both are determined by $n$, $b$ and the
leaf sequence.
\end{definition}

% compute/mmr.py:
% [sec:mmr-peaks] Bag_R root = recursive root, 1 <= n <= 129
\begin{proposition}[Peaks of the recursive form]\label{prop:peaks-recursive}
For every $n\ge 1$ the peaks of $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ are exactly the left
children of the right-spine nodes of $M_n$, from the root down to the first
perfect right-spine node, followed by that node. Hence $M_n=M^R_n$ and
$\mathsf{rt}_n=\mathrm{Bag}_R(P_1,\dots,P_d)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mmr-left-perfect}(3) the nodes named are perfect subtrees
whose sizes are the terms of the binary expansion of $n$, largest first,
covering consecutive runs of leaves from the left: they are $P_1,\dots,P_d$
in order. Along the spine, the $t$-th node passed has value
$H(P_t\,\|\,v_{t+1})$ with $v_{t+1}$ the value of the next spine node, and
the last spine node considered is $P_d$; unwinding gives the right fold.
\end{proof}

% compute/mmr.py:
% [sec:mmr-peaks] per-leaf path lengths equal the closed forms under both
%   folds for all 1 <= n < 5000 (leaf by leaf on built trees for n <= 129);
%   max under Bag_R = ceil(log2 n), under Bag_L = floor(log2 n) + popcount(n)
%   - 1 <= 2 floor(log2 n), attained by P_1's leaves; the maxima differ iff
%   d >= 3
% [sec:mmr-peaks] n = 7: max path length 3 under Bag_R, 4 under Bag_L
% [sec:mmr-peaks] n = 127: max path length 7 under Bag_R, 12 under Bag_L
\begin{lemma}[Path lengths under the two baggings]\label{lem:bag-depth}
Let $n\ge 1$ have peaks $P_1,\dots,P_d$ of altitudes $a_1>\dots>a_d$, let
$b\in\{R,L\}$, and let leaf $j$ lie in $P_i$. The depth
$\mathrm{depth}_b(n,j)$ of leaf $j$ in $M^b_n$ is
\[
  \mathrm{depth}_R(n,j)=\begin{cases}
    a_i+i & i<d,\\ a_d+d-1 & i=d,\end{cases}
  \qquad
  \mathrm{depth}_L(n,j)=\begin{cases}
    a_1+d-1 & i=1,\\ a_i+d-i+1 & i\ge 2.\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the depth of $M^R_n$ is $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$; the depth of
$M^L_n$ is $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+\mathrm{popcount}(n)-1\le
2\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$, attained by the leaves of $P_1$; and the two depths
differ exactly when $d\ge 3$. For $n=7$ they are $3$ and $4$; for $n=127$
they are $7$ and $12$. Leaf depths are functions of $(b,n,j)$; for $d\ge 2$
they are not all equal.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The leaf lies at depth $a_i$ below the root of $P_i$; it remains to find the
depth of that root in the bag. Under $\mathrm{Bag}_R$ the $t$-th bagging node
lies at depth $t-1$ and has left child $P_t$ for $t\le d-1$, and the last
bagging node has right child $P_d$; so $P_i$ lies at depth $i$ for $i<d$ and
$P_d$ at depth $d-1$. Under $\mathrm{Bag}_L$ the root has right child $P_d$
and left child $\mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_{d-1})$, and inductively $P_i$ lies
at depth $d-i+1$ for $i\ge 2$ while $P_1$ shares depth $d-1$ with $P_2$; for
$d=1$ the single peak lies at depth $0$.

Maxima. Under $R$, $a_i\le a_1-(i-1)$ gives $a_i+i\le a_1+1$ and
$a_d+d-1\le a_1$; the maximum is $a_1+1$ for $d\ge 2$ and $a_1$ for $d=1$,
which is $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ in both cases, as $n$ is a power of two
exactly when $d=1$. Under $L$, for $i\ge 2$,
$a_i+d-i+1\le a_1+d-2i+2\le a_1+d-2$, so the maximum is $a_1+d-1$, attained
by $P_1$; with $a_1=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ and
$d=\mathrm{popcount}(n)\le\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1$ this is the stated value
and bound. The maxima $a_1+1$ and $a_1+d-1$ coincide for $d=2$, both equal
$a_1$ for $d=1$, and differ for $d\ge 3$. For $d\ge 2$ a leaf of $P_1$ and a
leaf of $P_d$ differ in depth under $R$ ($a_1+1$ against at most $a_1$), and
a leaf of $P_1$ and a leaf of $P_2$ under $L$ ($a_1\ne a_2$).
\end{proof}

A \emph{cycle} of $H$ is a value $v$ and a nonempty sequence of evaluations
of $H$ that, starting from inputs that include $v$, recomputes $v$.

% compute/mmr.py:
% [sec:mmr-peaks] Bag_R root = recursive root, 1 <= n <= 129; Bag_R and Bag_L
%   trees and roots coincide iff d <= 2
\begin{theorem}[Fold order]\label{thm:fold-order}
Let $n\ge 1$ have $d$ peaks. The shapes $M^R_n$ and $M^L_n$ coincide if and
only if $d\le 2$. For $d\ge 3$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the roots $\mathsf{rt}^R_n$ and $\mathsf{rt}^L_n$ are distinct hash
  expressions over the same peaks, and from values of the peaks for which
  $\mathsf{rt}^R_n=\mathsf{rt}^L_n$ a collision or a cycle of $H$ is computed;
\item for every leaf $j$, the sequence of sides of the siblings on leaf $j$'s
  path above its peak differs between $M^R_n$ and $M^L_n$;
\item the peaks and the perfect subtrees beneath them coincide, and so do the
  asymptotic costs of append, of storage of the peaks and of a path, each
  logarithmic in $n$; the leaf depths differ as in
  Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}, so the bound $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ on the path
  length of ePrint 2019/226 does not hold for $M^L_n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Definition~\ref{def:mmr} splits $n=2^{\lfloor\log_2(n-1)\rfloor}+(\text{rest})$
with the first peak on the left and recurses on the rest, which is the right
fold (Proposition~\ref{prop:peaks-recursive}). For $d=1$ both folds are $P_1$
and for $d=2$ both are $H(P_1\,\|\,P_2)$. For $d\ge 3$ the root's left child
is $P_1$ under $R$ and a bagging node under $L$, so the shapes differ.

(1) The root inputs are $P_1\,\|\allowbreak\,\mathrm{Bag}_R(P_2,\dots,P_d)$
and $\mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_{d-1})\,\|\allowbreak\,P_d$. If the root
values agree and these $2\kappa$-bit strings differ, they are a collision.
Otherwise $P_1=\mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_{d-1})$ and
$P_d=\mathrm{Bag}_R(P_2,\dots,P_d)$, each a value recomputed from itself by
$d-2\ge 1$ evaluations of $H$: a cycle.

(2) Above $P_1$ the path has under $R$ the single sibling
$\mathrm{Bag}_R(P_2,\dots,P_d)$ and under $L$ the $d-1\ge 2$ siblings
$P_2,\dots,P_d$. Above $P_d$ it has under $R$ the $d-1\ge 2$ siblings
$P_{d-1},\dots,P_1$ and under $L$ the single sibling
$\mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_{d-1})$. Above $P_i$ with $2\le i<d$ the first
sibling is on the right under $R$, namely $\mathrm{Bag}_R(P_{i+1},\dots,P_d)$,
and on the left under $L$, namely $\mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_{i-1})$.

(3) Both trees are built on the peaks of Definition~\ref{def:peaks}, which
depend on the leaf sequence alone; there are $d\le\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1$
of them. Appending $x_{n+1}$ joins the last peaks of equal altitude with
$x_{n+1}$, at most $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1$ evaluations of $H$, and
re-bagging the at most $\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor+1$ new peaks costs one
evaluation fewer than their number, under either fold. A path has at most
$2\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ siblings under either fold by
Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}, whose depths are the stated ones.
\end{proof}

Collision resistance alone does not exclude a cycle, so part (1) does not
assert that the two roots differ numerically. No later statement of this
volume uses such an inequality; the later sections use the shapes and path
lengths of part (2) and Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}.

\subsection{Appending a leaf}\label{sec:mmr-append}

\begin{construction}[Append]\label{def:append}
Every node $v$ of a range carries its leaf count $m$. Procedure
$\mathrm{Append}(v,x)$ takes the root $v$ of a range over $m$ leaves and a
value $x\in\{0,1\}^\kappa$ and returns the root of a range over $m+1$ leaves:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $m$ is a power of two ($v$ is perfect; $m=1$ included), return a new
  node with left child $v$, right child the leaf $x$, value $H(v\,\|\,x)$ and
  leaf count $m+1$;
\item otherwise, with children $v_\ell$ and $v_r$, replace $v_r$ by
  $\mathrm{Append}(v_r,x)$, set the value of $v$ to
  $H(v_\ell\,\|\,v_r)$ with the new $v_r$, set its leaf count to $m+1$, and
  return $v$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{construction}

The construction is ePrint 2019/226, Algorithm~5. The order $H(v\,\|\,x)$ in
step~1 is that of the algorithm and of Definition~\ref{def:mmr}; the base
case of the paper's proof of its Theorem~6 writes the reverse order.

\begin{theorem}[Correctness of append]\label{thm:append}
For every leaf count $n\ge 1$, $\mathrm{Append}(M_n,x_{n+1})$ is $M_{n+1}$
over $(x_1,\dots,x_{n+1})$: the leaves of $M_n$ followed by $x_{n+1}$ as the
rightmost leaf.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $n$, following ePrint 2019/226, Theorem~6. If $n=2^i$,
Definition~\ref{def:mmr} for $n+1$ has $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor=i$ and right
part $1$: left child $M_n$, right child the leaf $x_{n+1}$, which is step~1.
Otherwise $n=2^i+m$ with $1\le m<2^i$, and $n+1=2^i+(m+1)$ with the same $i$,
since $m+1\le 2^i$. The left child $M_{2^i}$ is unchanged, and the right child
is $\mathrm{Append}$ applied to the range $M_m$ over
$(x_{2^i+1},\dots,x_n)$, which is $M_{m+1}$ over
$(x_{2^i+1},\dots,x_{n+1})$ by hypothesis; step~2 rehashes the root.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Cost of append]\label{prop:append-cost}
Procedure $\mathrm{Append}(M_n,x_{n+1})$ creates or rehashes only
right-spine nodes of $M_n$ and one new node, with at most
$\lceil\log_2 n\rceil+1$ evaluations of $H$. Every left child of $M_n$, and
the perfect right-spine node at which the recursion stops, is a subtree of
$M_{n+1}$ with unchanged values.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The recursion descends the right spine until the first node whose leaf count
is a power of two, at depth at most $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:mmr-left-perfect}(4)), evaluates $H$ once to create the new
node, and once more at each spine node above it. Left children and the node
where the descent stops are never modified.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:mmr} shows the case $n=6$: two evaluations of $H$ create
$H(P_2\,\|\,x_7)$ and $\mathsf{rt}_7$.

\subsection{Inclusion paths and prefix roots}\label{sec:mmr-paths}

% compute/mmr.py:
% [sec:mmr-paths] paper Algorithm 4 (length check |Pi| = ceil(log2(n - 1)))
%   rejects 1225 of the 8384 valid leaf paths of M_n, 2 <= n <= 129; without
%   the length check its side rule accepts all 8384
% [sec:mmr-paths] examples: both leaves of M_2 (length 1 against
%   ceil(log2 1) = 0); leaf 5 of M_6 (depth 2 against ceil(log2 5) = 3)
\begin{definition}[Inclusion path]\label{def:mmr-path}
Let $b\in\{R,L\}$, $n\ge 1$, $1\le j\le n$, and
$m=\mathrm{depth}_b(n,j)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}). Let
$u_0,u_1,\dots,u_m$ be the nodes from leaf $j$ to the root of $M^b_n$. The
\emph{inclusion path} of leaf $j$ is $\pi=(\pi_1,\dots,\pi_m)$, where $\pi_t$
is the value of the sibling of $u_{t-1}$, listed bottom-up. The \emph{side}
$\sigma_t\in\{\text{left},\text{right}\}$ records whether that sibling is
the left or the right child of $u_t$. The sides and $m$ are functions of
$(b,n,j)$ through the shape of $M^b_n$.

The verifier $\mathrm{Verify}_b(\mathsf{rt},n,j,x,\pi)$, on
$\mathsf{rt},x\in\{0,1\}^\kappa$ and a sequence $\pi$ of $\kappa$-bit values:
\begin{enumerate}
\item rejects unless $|\pi|=\mathrm{depth}_b(n,j)$;
\item sets $y_0=x$ and, for $t=1,\dots,m$, $y_t=H(\pi_t\,\|\,y_{t-1})$ if
  $\sigma_t=\text{left}$ and $y_t=H(y_{t-1}\,\|\,\pi_t)$ otherwise;
\item accepts if and only if $y_m=\mathsf{rt}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

By construction $\mathrm{Verify}_b$ accepts $(x_j,\pi)$ with $\pi$ the
inclusion path of leaf $j$ against $\mathsf{rt}^b_n$. Under $\mathrm{Bag}_R$
every path has at most $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ siblings
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}); ePrint 2019/226, Definition~10, counts at most
$\log_2 n+1$ hashes, the leaf pair included. For $d\ge 2$, path lengths
differ between leaves (Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}). The paper's Algorithm~4
requires $|\pi|=\lceil\log_2(n-1)\rceil$ and so rejects valid paths: $1{,}225$
of the $8{,}384$ leaf paths of the ranges $M_n$, $2\le n\le 129$, among them
both leaves of $M_2$ and leaf $5$ of $M_6$. The verifier above fixes the length
from $(b,n,j)$ instead. For $n$ a power of two, $M^b_n$ is a perfect tree, and
the inclusion path and its verifier coincide with the \emph{Crypto Guide}'s
Definition ``Authentication path'' and Definition ``Path verification''
(\S``Authentication paths and membership proofs'') at index $j-1$, with the bits
$b_t$ fixed by $j$ instead of carried in the path.

\begin{theorem}[Prefix roots from one path]\label{thm:prefix-root}
For $1\le j\le n$, from $x_j$ and the inclusion path of leaf $j$ in $M_n$ a
verifier computes $\mathsf{rt}_j$ and, for $j\ge 2$, $\mathsf{rt}_{j-1}$, the
roots of the ranges over $(x_1,\dots,x_j)$ and $(x_1,\dots,x_{j-1})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $\mathsf{rt}_j$, by induction on $n$, following ePrint 2019/226,
Theorem~7, with siblings bottom-up. If $j=n$, $\mathsf{rt}_j=\mathsf{rt}_n$
is the recomputed root $y_m$. Otherwise $n>1$; let $2^i$ be the largest power
of two strictly below $n$, so that the root's children are $M_{2^i}$ and a
range over $n-2^i$ values.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $j=2^i$, $\mathsf{rt}_j$ is the value of the root's left child,
  which is $y_{m-1}$.
\item If $j<2^i$, the path without its top sibling is the inclusion path of
  leaf $j$ in the left child $M_{2^i}$, and the hypothesis applies.
\item If $2^i<j<n$, then $j-1\ge 2^i$ and $j<2^{i+1}$, so $2^i$ is also the
  largest power of two strictly below $j$. The top sibling is $P$, the value
  of $M_{2^i}$, and the path without it is the inclusion path of leaf
  $j-2^i$ in the right child; by hypothesis it yields the root $r'$ over
  $(x_{2^i+1},\dots,x_j)$, and $\mathsf{rt}_j=H(P\,\|\,r')$ by
  Definition~\ref{def:mmr}.
\end{enumerate}
For $\mathsf{rt}_{j-1}$, the bottom-up fold of Lemma~\ref{lem:left-siblings},
whose proof does not use this theorem. The paper's proof lists the path
top-down with the path nodes included, while its Algorithm~3 outputs the
siblings bottom-up; the bottom-up format of Definition~\ref{def:mmr-path} is
the one fixed here.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Left siblings cover the prefix]\label{lem:left-siblings}
Let $b\in\{R,L\}$, $1\le j\le n$, and consider the inclusion path of leaf $j$
in $M^b_n$. Its left siblings are subtrees whose leaf runs partition
$\{1,\dots,j-1\}$, higher siblings covering earlier leaves; its right
siblings cover $\{j+1,\dots,n\}$ and enter none of the folds below. Let
$j\ge 2$ and let leaf $j$ lie in peak $P_i$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Under $\mathrm{Bag}_R$ the left siblings, from the top down, are the
  peaks of $(x_1,\dots,x_{j-1})$. The \emph{bottom-up fold} (set $r$ to the
  lowest left sibling, then $r\leftarrow H(\pi\,\|\,r)$ for each further left
  sibling $\pi$ upwards) yields $\mathsf{rt}_{j-1}$; started instead from
  $r=x_j$ and applied to every left sibling, it yields $\mathsf{rt}_j$.
\item Under $\mathrm{Bag}_L$, for $i\ge 2$ the topmost left sibling is
  $\mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_{i-1})$ and the others lie in $P_i$, with
  sizes the terms of the binary expansion of $j-1-s_i$; for $i=1$ all left
  siblings lie in $P_1$. The \emph{top-down fold} (set $r$ to the topmost
  left sibling, then $r\leftarrow H(r\,\|\,\pi)$ for each further left sibling
  $\pi$ downwards) yields $\mathsf{rt}^L_{j-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A leaf $l<j$ leaves the path of leaf $j$ at their lowest common ancestor, in
whose left child $l$ lies; that left child is a left sibling on the path.
Distinct ancestors give disjoint runs, higher ancestors earlier runs. The
same argument for $l>j$ gives the right siblings. Inside a perfect subtree
the left siblings of the leaf at offset $o$ from its first leaf are perfect
subtrees whose sizes are the terms of the binary expansion of $o$, largest
highest.

Leaf $j$ lies at offset $o=j-1-s_i$ in $P_i$, and $j-1=s_i+o$, so the peaks of
$(x_1,\dots,x_{j-1})$ are $P_1,\dots,P_{i-1}$ followed by the left siblings
inside $P_i$, top-down. Under $R$ the siblings $P_{i-1},\dots,P_1$ lie above
$P_i$ as left children of bagging nodes (proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}); the bottom-up fold is then the right fold of the
peaks of $(x_1,\dots,x_{j-1})$, which is $\mathsf{rt}_{j-1}$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:peaks-recursive}. Started from $x_j$, it is the right
fold of those peaks followed by $x_j$. The largest power of two strictly
below $j$ is the highest term of $j-1$, the size of the first of these
peaks, and the recursion of Definition~\ref{def:mmr} on $j$ splits off the
peaks of $j-1$ in turn and ends at the single leaf $x_j$; this fold is
therefore $\mathsf{rt}_j$. Under $L$ with $i\ge 2$ the root of $P_i$ is the
right child of the bagging node $H(\mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_{i-1})\,\|\,P_i)$,
and every higher node on the path is a left child; the top-down fold is the
left fold of the peaks of $(x_1,\dots,x_{j-1})$. For $i=1$ the root of $P_1$
is a left child and every higher node on the path too.
\end{proof}

\begin{construction}[Prefix root]\label{def:prefix-root}
Procedure $\mathrm{PrefixRoot}_b(n,j,\pi)$ takes $b\in\{R,L\}$,
$2\le j\le n$ and a sequence $\pi$ of $\kappa$-bit values with
$|\pi|=\mathrm{depth}_b(n,j)$, with sides $\sigma_t$ as in
Definition~\ref{def:mmr-path}. It selects the $\pi_t$ with
$\sigma_t=\text{left}$ and outputs their bottom-up fold for $b=R$ and their
top-down fold for $b=L$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:left-siblings}).
\end{construction}

Correctness of the construction is Lemma~\ref{lem:left-siblings}. The
construction corrects the printed pseudocode of ePrint 2019/226,
Algorithm~6, which uses an undefined index, ends in a
vestigial boolean comparison, and has a call signature that does not match
its call in Algorithm~2, and it fixes the siblings bottom-up.

\begin{theorem}[Position binding]\label{thm:position-binding}
Let $b\in\{R,L\}$, $n\ge 1$ and $1\le j\le n$. From any
$\mathsf{rt}\in\{0,1\}^\kappa$, chosen by the adversary, and two pairs
$(x,\pi)\ne(x',\pi')$ both accepted by
$\mathrm{Verify}_b(\mathsf{rt},n,j,\cdot,\cdot)$, a collision of $H$ is
computed with at most $2\,\mathrm{depth}_b(n,j)$ evaluations of $H$. In
particular $x\ne x'$ yields a collision, and the siblings of an accepted path
and every value it recomputes are functions of $(\mathsf{rt},b,n,j)$, barring
a collision.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both chains $y_0,\dots,y_m$ and $y'_0,\dots,y'_m$ of
Definition~\ref{def:mmr-path} have the length $m=\mathrm{depth}_b(n,j)$ and
the sides fixed by $(b,n,j)$, and $y_m=y'_m=\mathsf{rt}$. Since
$(x,\pi)\ne(x',\pi')$, some step has differing inputs; let $t$ be the highest
step with $(y_{t-1},\pi_t)\ne(y'_{t-1},\pi'_t)$. Then $y_t=y'_t$: for $t=m$
both equal $\mathsf{rt}$, and for $t<m$ the inputs of step $t+1$ agree. The
two $2\kappa$-bit inputs of step $t$ place their operands on the same sides
and differ, and $H$ maps both to $y_t$: a collision. For $n=1$ the path is
empty and $\mathrm{Verify}_b$ accepts only $x=\mathsf{rt}$, so no two
distinct pairs are accepted. The extractor is deterministic and the
reduction tight in the sense of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Reductions''; it
is the first-level-of-agreement argument of the \emph{Crypto Guide},
Proposition ``Binding of the root'' (\S``Binary Merkle trees'') and
\S``Soundness: forging a path implies a collision''.
\end{proof}

The theorem extends the \emph{Crypto Guide}'s Theorem ``Merkle trees are
succinct position-binding vector commitments'' (\S``Vector commitments'') from
perfect trees to the shapes fixed by $n$ and the bagging order. It is stated
for a root chosen by the adversary, not for a fixed honest root as in the
\emph{Crypto Guide}'s Remark ``Scope: a fixed honest root'': in FlyClient the
root of the head comes from the prover.

\begin{corollary}[Prefix binding and root sensitivity]\label{cor:prefix-binding}
Let $b\in\{R,L\}$ and $1\le j\le n$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\mathsf{rt}=\mathsf{rt}^b_n$ over $(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ and
  $\mathrm{Verify}_b$ accepts $(x,\pi)$ at position $j$, then $x=x_j$ and,
  for $j\ge 2$, $\mathrm{PrefixRoot}_b(n,j,\pi)=\mathsf{rt}^b_{j-1}$ over
  $(x_1,\dots,x_{j-1})$, barring a collision: one accepted path binds
  $x_1,\dots,x_j$ as a prefix of the committed sequence.
\item If $(x'_1,\dots,x'_m)$ differs from $(x_1,\dots,x_m)$ at some
  $i\le m$, then $\mathsf{rt}^b_m(x')\ne\mathsf{rt}^b_m(x)$, barring a
  collision: changing $x_i$ changes every root $\mathsf{rt}^b_m$ with
  $m\ge i$.
\end{enumerate}
When $x_i$ is the hash of block $i$, binding the blocks themselves requires
in addition collision resistance of that hash.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(1) The honest pair $(x_j,\pi^*)$ is accepted. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:position-binding} the pairs $(x,\pi)$ and $(x_j,\pi^*)$ are
equal barring a collision, so $x=x_j$ and the fold of
Construction~\ref{def:prefix-root} on $\pi$ is the fold on $\pi^*$, which is
$\mathsf{rt}^b_{j-1}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:left-siblings}. (2) The two trees
have the same shape $M^b_m$. The honest paths of leaf $i$ in both are
accepted against the common root, with $x_i\ne x'_i$;
Theorem~\ref{thm:position-binding} yields a collision.
\end{proof}

Part (1) is ePrint 2019/226, Corollary~3, and part (2) its Corollary~4,
stated for both bagging orders and with the collision named.

\subsection{Aggregating ranges}\label{sec:mmr-aggregate}

A verifier that samples leaves by cumulative weight needs each path to bind
the sampled leaf's weight interval, and a verifier that constrains unsampled
leaves needs aggregates over their runs; both follow from metadata
propagated up the tree (ePrint 2019/226, Section~2 and Section~6).

% compute/mmr.py:
% [sec:mmr-aggregate] injective but not prefix-free: ser(x) = 0,
%   ser(y) = 01, ser(z) = 10 give ser(x) || ser(z) = ser(y) || ser(x) = 010
\begin{definition}[Aggregating range]\label{def:aggregating}
An \emph{aggregating range} extends Definition~\ref{def:mmr}, after ZIP 221,
``Specification'', which propagates metadata ``by either summing the metadata
of both children, or inheriting the metadata of a specific child''. It is
given by:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a set of \emph{node tuples} $v=(v.\mathsf{h},v.f_1,\dots,v.f_r)$ with
  $v.\mathsf{h}\in\{0,1\}^\kappa$;
\item a \emph{serialisation} $\mathrm{ser}$ from tuples to bit strings that is
  injective and prefix-free, no value of $\mathrm{ser}$ being a proper prefix
  of another, so that $\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r)$ determines the
  pair $(\ell,r)$;
\item a \emph{leaf map} from a leaf's data to its tuple;
\item for each field $f_i$ one rule: inherit from the left child, inherit
  from the right child, or sum, in $\Z$ or in $\Z/2^{256}\Z$;
\item the \emph{parent map} $\mathrm{par}(\ell,r)$, whose hash field is
  $H(\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r))$, over the children's full
  serialisations, and whose other fields follow the rules.
\end{enumerate}
A labelling of the shape $M^b_n$ with leaf tuples at the leaves is
\emph{consistent} when every internal node is $\mathrm{par}$ of its
children; its \emph{root} is the root's tuple. Inclusion paths and
$\mathrm{Verify}_b$ are those of Definition~\ref{def:mmr-path} with tuples in
place of values and $\mathrm{par}$ in place of $H(\cdot\,\|\,\cdot)$;
$\mathrm{Verify}_b$ accepts if and only if the recomputed root tuple equals
the given one.
\end{definition}

A serialisation of fixed width is prefix-free. Injectivity alone does not
suffice: with $\mathrm{ser}(x)=0$, $\mathrm{ser}(y)=01$ and
$\mathrm{ser}(z)=10$, the pairs $(x,z)$ and $(y,x)$ have the same
concatenation $010$.

\begin{lemma}[Aggregate binding]\label{lem:aggregate-binding}
Let $\mathrm{ser}$ be injective and prefix-free, $b\in\{R,L\}$ and $n\ge 1$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item From two consistent labellings of $M^b_n$ with equal root tuples that
  differ at some node, a collision of $H$ is computed; barring a collision,
  equal roots imply agreement on every field of every node.
\item From any root tuple, chosen by the adversary, and two distinct accepted
  paths (leaf tuple and sibling tuples) at one position $j$, a collision of
  $H$ is computed: an accepted path binds the full tuple of every node it
  recomputes and of every sibling it carries.
\item For $2\le j\le n$ and the inclusion path of leaf $j$ in a consistent
  labelling, the folds of Lemma~\ref{lem:left-siblings} run with
  $\mathrm{par}$ in place of $H(\cdot\,\|\,\cdot)$ yield the root tuple of
  the consistent labelling of $M^b_{j-1}$ over the first $j-1$ leaf tuples.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Unique decoding: if $\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r)=
\mathrm{ser}(\ell')\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r')$, one of $\mathrm{ser}(\ell)$,
$\mathrm{ser}(\ell')$ is a prefix of the other, hence they are equal, and
$\ell=\ell'$ by injectivity; then $\mathrm{ser}(r)=\mathrm{ser}(r')$ and
$r=r'$.

(1) Take a node at which the labellings differ and whose parent they agree
on; one exists because they agree at the root. The parent tuples are equal,
so their hash fields are, while the child pairs differ; by unique decoding
the two hash inputs differ: a collision.

(2) As in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:position-binding}, with tuples: at the
highest step whose inputs differ, the outputs are equal tuples, hence have
equal hash fields, and the inputs are distinct pairs, hence by unique
decoding distinct strings: a collision.

(3) In a consistent labelling the tuple of every subtree, a bagging node
included, is determined by its leaf tuples through $\mathrm{par}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:left-siblings} the left siblings are, under $R$, the peak
tuples of the consistent labelling over the first $j-1$ leaves, and so are
they under $L$ with $i\le 2$. Under $L$ with $i\ge 3$, the topmost is the
tuple of the bagging node $\mathrm{Bag}_L(P_1,\dots,P_{i-1})$ of that
labelling, and the others are its peak tuples inside $P_i$. The folds of
that lemma, run with $\mathrm{par}$, therefore yield the root tuple of
$M^b_{j-1}$. The engine of (1) and (2) is the \emph{Crypto Guide},
Proposition ``Binding of the root'' (\S``Binary Merkle trees'').
\end{proof}

%   the left-sibling weight sum on leaf j's path equals w_1 + ... + w_{j-1}
%   under Bag_R and Bag_L
\begin{lemma}[Cumulative-weight interval of a leaf]\label{lem:weight-interval}
Let $\mathrm{ser}$ be injective and prefix-free, let $w$ be a summed field,
$w_j$ the $w$-field of the tuple of leaf $j$, and $W$ the sum of the
$w$-fields of the left siblings on an accepted path of leaf $j$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Binding, for any root tuple chosen by the adversary: barring a
  collision, $W$ and $w_j$ are functions of the root, $b$, $n$ and $j$. One
  accepted path therefore binds the interval $[W,W+w_j)$ of leaf $j$.
\item Meaning, for the path of a consistent labelling:
  $W=\sum_{i<j}w_i$, under either bagging order. For a sum taken modulo
  $2^{256}$, this holds as an identity of integers when the sum of $w$ over
  all $n$ leaves is below $2^{256}$.
\end{enumerate}
Part (2) is not claimed for a root chosen by the adversary, whose siblings
off the path are never recomputed from their children.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}(2). (2) In a consistent labelling the
summed field of a node is the sum over its leaves, by induction on height;
with the total below $2^{256}$ no reduction occurs. The left siblings
partition $\{1,\dots,j-1\}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:left-siblings}, a property of
any binary tree.
\end{proof}

Two instances follow. The difficulty Merkle mountain range of ePrint
2019/226, whose nodes aggregate difficulty and time, is
Definition~\ref{def:diff-mmr} in \S\ref{sec:protocol-diffmmr}. The node
vector of ZIP 221, whose nodes aggregate work, heights and pool data, is
Definition~\ref{def:node-vector} in \S\ref{sec:history-nodes}.

\section{The FlyClient security model}\label{sec:model}

This section fixes the model in which the FlyClient theorem is stated: the
variable-difficulty backbone with its unit of work, the adversary and
persistence; the retarget rule, the attack it excludes and the limit of that
exclusion for a sampling verifier; the $(c, L)$ assumption; and the
definitions of security and succinctness for chain proofs.

\subsection{The variable-difficulty backbone model}\label{sec:model-backbone}

The model of this section is the one ePrint 2019/226 adopts in Section~3.1
and Section~3.2, after Garay, Kiayias and Leonardos (the variable-difficulty
backbone, CRYPTO 2017), and it is stated as the paper adopts it. Whether a
Zcash chain satisfies its hypotheses is classified in
\S\ref{sec:boundary}, not here.

\begin{definition}[Variable-difficulty backbone model]\label{def:backbone}
Fix the security parameter $\lambda$ (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``The security
parameter'') and a hash length $\kappa$. The execution proceeds in rounds
$r = 1, 2, \ldots$. The proof-of-work function is a random oracle
$H \colon \{0,1\}^* \to \{0,1\}^\kappa$ (\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The random
oracle model''), its outputs read as integers in $[0, 2^\kappa)$. In round
$r$ there are $\nu_r$ honest and $t_r$ adversarial \emph{active players},
and each active player makes $q$ sequential queries to $H$ per round. A block
$B$ declares a \emph{target} $T \in [1, 2^\kappa]$ in its header, and $B$
carries a \emph{valid proof of work} at $T$ if and only if $H(B) < T$. A
message broadcast in round $r$ is delivered to every player in round $r + 1$,
in an order the adversary chooses. The honest participation sequence
$(\nu_r)_r$ is \emph{$(\gamma, s)$-respecting}: for every set $S$ of at most
$s$ consecutive rounds,
\[
  \max_{r \in S} \nu_r \le \gamma \min_{r \in S} \nu_r .
\]
Each block also records the round in which it was mined, which
Definition~\ref{def:retarget} reads.
\end{definition}

The paper's paraphrase in Section~3.1 omits ``consecutive''; read literally,
for $s \ge 3$ it bounds participation over the whole execution.

The paper writes $\lambda$ for the oracle output length and $n_r$ for the
honest player count; here the length is $\kappa$, $\lambda$ being the
security parameter, and the count is $\nu_r$, $n$ being a leaf count
(\S\ref{sec:intro-sources}).

\begin{definition}[Difficulty level and total work]
\label{def:difficulty-level}
A single query to $H$ meets target $T$ with probability $T / 2^\kappa$. The
\emph{difficulty level} of $T$ is
\[
  D(T) = 2^\kappa / T ,
\]
the expected number of queries per block valid at $T$. A harder target is a
smaller $T$ and a larger $D(T)$: the level is a work-like quantity inversely
proportional to the target and is never the target itself. The \emph{total
work} of a finite sequence of blocks $B_1, \ldots, B_m$ with declared targets
$T_1, \ldots, T_m$ is
\[
  \omega = \sum_{i=1}^{m} D(T_i) ,
\]
the paper's \emph{cumulative difficulty}. A chain is \emph{heavier} than
another when its total work is larger.
\end{definition}

The paper also calls a level a ``target'' in its Definition~7; in this volume
$D$ denotes only a level and $T$ only a target. The Zcash analogue, the work
of a block computed from its compact target, is defined in the
\emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``Work, and the best chain'', from protocol
specification, \S``Definition of Work''. It is a different normalisation of
the same ordering and is not used by the model.

\begin{definition}[Adversary]\label{def:adversary}
Fix $\mu$ with $0 < \mu < 1$. The \emph{adversary} is a PPT algorithm
(\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Algorithms, running time, and PPT''; \emph{Crypto
Guide}, \S``Adversaries and the security parameter'') that controls the $t_r$
adversarial players of each round $r$, with $t_r < \mu \nu_r$, so that it
makes at most $t_r q$ queries to $H$ in round $r$. It is \emph{rushing}: in
each round it receives every honest message of the round before choosing its
own, and it orders the delivery of all messages. Its goal is to make a light
client accept a chain whose total work is at least that of the honest chain.
\end{definition}

The prior knowledge of the verifier is fixed once, in
Definition~\ref{def:verifier-setting}.

\begin{assumption}[Persistence and liveness]\label{ass:persistence}
The backbone parameters $(\mu, \gamma, s, \tau, E, f)$, with $\tau$, $E$
and $f$ the constants of Definition~\ref{def:retarget}, are such that there
is a \emph{persistence depth} $\ell$ with, except with probability negligible
in $\lambda$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (persistence) for all honest players $P_1, P_2$ and rounds
  $r_1 \le r_2$, the chain of $P_1$ at round $r_1$ with its last $\ell$
  blocks removed is a prefix of the chain of $P_2$ at round $r_2$;
\item (liveness) a transaction given to every honest player for a number of
  consecutive rounds fixed by the parameters lies in that stable prefix of
  every honest chain.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

ePrint 2019/226, Section~3.1 (p.~7), adopts this as a parameter choice,
citing Theorems~26 and~27 of its reference [22], Garay, Kiayias and
Leonardos, ``The Bitcoin backbone protocol: analysis and applications''
(EUROCRYPT 2015), a fixed-difficulty analysis; it names no theorem of its
reference [23] (CRYPTO 2017), the source of the variable-difficulty model.
No cited theorem establishes the assumption in the model of
Definition~\ref{def:backbone}.

The tips of honest chains need not coincide: persistence gives agreement
only on all but the last $\ell$ blocks. The paper's sentence that
persistence and liveness give ``a single chain adopted by all honest full
nodes'' overstates this; the assumption states the corrected form. The
persistence depth $\ell$ is distinct from the catch parameter $k$ of
\S\ref{sec:protocol}.

\subsection{Retargeting and the difficulty-raising attack}
\label{sec:model-retarget}

\begin{definition}[Target recalculation with dampening]\label{def:retarget}
Fix constants $E \ge 1$ (the \emph{retarget-epoch length}, in blocks),
$\tau > 1$ (the \emph{dampening factor}), an initial participation estimate
$\nu_0 > 0$ and an initial target $T_0$. Targets are constant within each
\emph{retarget epoch} of $E$ consecutive blocks. At the end of an epoch whose
$E$ blocks were mined at target $T$ in $\Delta$ rounds, read from the rounds
the blocks record, the \emph{participation estimate} is
\[
  \hat\nu(T, \Delta) = \frac{2^\kappa E}{q T \Delta} ,
\]
and the target of the next epoch is
\[
  \mathrm{Retarget}(T, \Delta) =
  \begin{cases}
    T / \tau & \text{if } (\nu_0 / \hat\nu(T, \Delta))\, T_0 < T / \tau, \\
    \tau T & \text{if } (\nu_0 / \hat\nu(T, \Delta))\, T_0 > \tau T, \\
    (\nu_0 / \hat\nu(T, \Delta))\, T_0 & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
\]
that is, the value $(\nu_0 / \hat\nu)\, T_0$ clamped to
$[T / \tau, \tau T]$. Let $f = q \nu_0 T_0 / 2^\kappa$, the expected number
of blocks per round of $\nu_0$ players at $T_0$. A chain \emph{respects} the
rule when every epoch's target is the image under $\mathrm{Retarget}$ of the
previous epoch's target and round span.
\end{definition}

The unclamped value is the \emph{raw retarget}
\[
  \frac{\nu_0}{\hat\nu(T, \Delta)}\, T_0
  = \frac{\nu_0 T_0 \, q T \Delta}{2^\kappa E}
  = \frac{T \Delta f}{E} ,
\]
which restores $f$ blocks per round at the estimated participation. The
current target $T$ is an input of the rule; the paper's list of constants
omits it. The definition is ePrint 2019/226, Definition~1 (Section~3.1,
p.~7), after Garay, Kiayias and Leonardos, Definition~2. The function is
written $\mathrm{Retarget}$ rather than the paper's $D(n_0, T_0)$, because
$D$ is a difficulty level, and $\nu_0$ and $T_0$ are the initial constants of
the cited definition, not the previous-epoch quantities of the gloss in
the paper's Definition~1. The retarget epoch is distinct from the upgrade
epoch of \S\ref{sec:history}.

Under variable difficulty the total work of a chain, not its length, decides
between chains. An adversary may therefore mine fewer blocks at higher
levels and use the larger variance of a sum of few heavy terms to exceed a
more powerful honest chain: the \emph{difficulty-raising attack} of Bahack
(arXiv:1312.7013), recalled in ePrint 2019/226, Section~3.1.

% compute/sampling.py:
% [sec:model-retarget] 1 - e^(-1/3) = 0.28347, about 28.3 per cent (paper:
%   'roughly 28%'); finite level W: 1 - (1 - 1/W)^(W/3) within 1/W of it
%   for W >= 10^3
\begin{proposition}[Success of the difficulty-raising attack]
\label{prop:raising}
Model block discovery by exponential clocks (\emph{Consensus Guide},
\S``Memoryless clocks and the attribution sequence''): honest queries arrive
at rate $\Lambda$ per unit time and adversarial queries at rate
$\Lambda / 3$, one third of the honest power; each query succeeds
independently with probability $1 / D$ at level $D$, so that the successes of
a party querying at rate $\Lambda'$ at level $D$ form an exponential clock of
rate $\Lambda' / D$. Fix a time $t_0 > 0$ and let $W = \Lambda t_0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The expected total work of the blocks the honest players find in
  $[0, t_0]$ is $W$, whatever their targets, so the expected total work of
  the honest chain at $t_0$ is at most $W$.
\item Suppose the adversary may mine one block at an arbitrary level, in
  violation of Definition~\ref{def:retarget}, and sets its level to $D = W$.
  Then it finds that block, whose work alone equals $W$, before $t_0$ with
  probability
  \[
    1 - e^{-1/3} \approx 0.2835 ,
  \]
  about $28.3$ per cent, a constant independent of $\lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) An honest query at target $T$ yields a block of work $D(T)$ with
probability $T / 2^\kappa = 1 / D(T)$, hence has expected work $1$ whatever
the target. By linearity of expectation the expected work found in
$[0, t_0]$ equals the expected number of honest queries in $[0, t_0]$, which
is $\Lambda t_0 = W$ (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Exponential clocks, Poisson counts, and the Chernoff method'', Theorem ``Poisson arrival
counts''). The honest chain carries at most the work found.

(ii) At level $D = W$ the adversary's successes form an exponential clock of
rate $(\Lambda / 3) / W = 1 / (3 t_0)$. Its first arrival exceeds $t_0$ with
probability $e^{-t_0 / (3 t_0)} = e^{-1/3}$ (\emph{Math Guide}, same section,
Lemma ``Tail, mean, and memorylessness''), so it occurs before $t_0$ with
probability $1 - e^{-1/3}$.
\end{proof}

In the discrete query model the same statement holds as an inequality: if
$W / 3$ is an integer, the adversary's $W / 3$ queries at level $W$ all fail
with probability $(1 - 1/W)^{W/3} \le e^{-1/3}$, since $1 - x \le e^{-x}$, so
it succeeds with probability at least $1 - e^{-1/3}$. Since
$\log(1-x)\ge -x-x^2$ for $0\le x\le 1/2$,
$(1-1/W)^{W/3}\ge e^{-1/3-1/(3W)}\ge e^{-1/3}(1-1/(3W))$, so for every
such $W\ge 3$ the two probabilities differ by at most $e^{-1/3}/(3W)<1/W$.
The paper states ``roughly
28\%'' without derivation (ePrint 2019/226, Section~3.1, p.~7); the
comparison, as in the paper, is with the expected honest work.

\begin{remark}[The clamp as a defence]\label{rem:clamp}
Definition~\ref{def:retarget} excludes the attack of
Proposition~\ref{prop:raising}: a target changes only at epoch ends and by
at most the factor $\tau$, so no block may declare an arbitrary level. The
paper, citing Garay, Kiayias and Leonardos, states that this rule suffices
against difficulty-raising attacks for full nodes, which see every
transition and reject a chain that violates it (ePrint 2019/226,
Section~3.1, p.~7); that sufficiency is the cited authors' result and is not
proved here. A verifier that samples few blocks sees few transitions. The
clamp constrains the transitions it does not see only through data that the
chain commits to, which \S\ref{sec:protocol-diffmmr} constructs; no
conclusion about that construction is drawn here. The Zcash retarget rule is
a different function, a per-block rule over an averaging window, defined in
protocol specification, \S``Difficulty adjustment'', and described in the
\emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``Difficulty in motion''; it is not an instance of
Definition~\ref{def:retarget}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$(c, L)$}{(c, L)}-adversary}
\label{sec:model-adversary}

Fix an honest player and let $C$ be its chain at round $r$, the
\emph{honest chain}. A \emph{fork} of a chain $C'$ against $C$ has
\emph{fork point} $a$, the last block common to $C$ and $C'$; its blocks are
those of $C'$ after $a$. A block of the fork is \emph{validly mined} if it
carries a valid proof of work at its declared target
(Definition~\ref{def:backbone}), and \emph{invalid} otherwise; its
\emph{validly mined work} is the total work of its validly mined blocks.

\begin{assumption}[$(c, L)$-adversary]\label{ass:cl}
The printed form (ePrint 2019/226, Assumption~2, Section~3.2, p.~8) reads:
``There exists no adversary in the variable-difficulty model that respects
the target recalculation function from Definition~1 and can, with
non-negligible probability, produce a fork that contains more than $L$
blocks such that a $c$ fraction of the difficulty weight in these blocks is
honest.''

The operative form, for $0 < c < 1$ and $L \ge 1$: for every PPT adversary
respecting Definition~\ref{def:retarget} in the model of
Definition~\ref{def:backbone}, except with probability negligible in
$\lambda$, every fork it produces that has more than $L$ blocks after its
fork point $a$, and total work after $a$ at least the total work of $C$
after $a$ at the same round, has validly mined work less than $c$ times its
total work after $a$. Such a fork is therefore at least a $(1 - c)$ fraction
invalid by weight.
\end{assumption}

The operative form departs from the printed one in three stated respects.
The word ``honest'' in the printed form means validly mined. The length
condition is ``more than $L$ blocks'', the printed assumption's own wording,
where the paper's preceding sentence (Section~3.2, p.~8) says ``of length
$L$ or longer''; the
choice is consistent with checking the last $L$ blocks in full
(Remark~\ref{rem:short-forks}). The operative form quantifies only over
forks whose work after the fork point reaches that of the honest chain,
following the same sentence (``a $c$ fraction of the honest forks
weight''): the unrestricted reading fails for every $\mu > 0$, since
the adversary's own fully valid private fork of more than $L$ blocks has
valid fraction $1$, and only forks that reach the honest weight can win
under Definition~\ref{def:verifier-setting}.

% compute/sampling.py:
% [sec:model-adversary] rho/(1 + rho): rho = 1/2 gives 1/3; rho = 0.9 gives
%   9/19 = 0.47368, about 47.4 per cent (the paper's 47.3 truncates)
\begin{remark}[Two readings of $c$]\label{rem:c-readings}
In Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} the parameter $c$ bounds the validly mined
fraction of a competitive long fork's weight. The paper's evaluation
(ePrint 2019/226, Section~7.1, p.~20, and Section~7.2) reads it instead as a
power ratio $\rho$, adversarial to honest mining power, so that the
adversary's share of total power is $\rho / (1 + \rho)$: one third at
$\rho = 1/2$, and $9/19 \approx 0.4737$, about $47.4$ per cent, at
$\rho = 0.9$, where the paper prints $47.3$ per cent by truncation. The
identification of $c$ with $\rho$ is not part of the assumption; it needs a
bridge from mining power to fork weight, which
Remark~\ref{rem:cl-status} classifies.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Short forks and the checked suffix]\label{rem:short-forks}
Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} constrains only forks of more than $L$ blocks, by
design: over a short window the variance of mining lets even an adversary
with small power produce a heavier short fork with non-negligible
probability (ePrint 2019/226, Section~3.2, p.~8). A verifier relying on the
assumption must therefore check in full a suffix of the claimed chain
containing at least its last $L$ blocks. At constant difficulty the paper
takes $L = \delta n$; under variable difficulty it states the checked suffix
as a weight fraction $\delta$ (Section~6, p.~17), leaving its relation to
the block count $L$ implicit. The size of the suffix under variable
difficulty is fixed in \S\ref{sec:protocol-sampling}
(Remark~\ref{rem:suffix}); no conclusion about it is drawn here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Lemma 1 of the paper]\label{rem:lemma1}
The paper's only derivation of Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} from mining power is
its Lemma~1 with Corollary~1 (ePrint 2019/226, pp.~8--9), stated here to
register their defect. Lemma~1 as printed: in the constant-difficulty
backbone, if $X$ is the number of blocks any adversary mines while the
honest chain adopts $L$ blocks, and the adversary's block rate is at most
$\mu$ times the honest adoption rate, then for $c > \mu$,
\[
  \Pr[X \ge cL] \le e^{L(c - \mu)} (c / \mu)^{-cL} .
\]
Corollary~1: for $L = \Theta(\lambda)$ and every $\mu$ there is $c < 1$ for
which the assumption holds at constant difficulty.

The printed bound is the Chernoff bound of a Poisson variable of mean
$\mu L$ (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Exponential clocks, Poisson counts, and the Chernoff method'', Corollary ``Chernoff bound for a
Poisson variable''). The proof asserts without proof that such a variable
models $X$, and it switches to the undefined symbols $1 - \delta$ and $n$, a
footnote setting $\mu = 1 - \delta$. Under independent exponential block
clocks the count $X$ is negative binomial (same section, Theorem
``Negative-binomial arrival count''), and the printed expression is not an
upper bound for it in general: the \emph{Math Guide}'s Example ``The Poisson
expression is not a bound for the arrival count'', in the same section,
exhibits $\mu = 1/2$, $c = 3/4$, $L = 200$ and $L = 400$ with the exact tail
above the printed expression. Lemma~1 and Corollary~1 are therefore
unproved as printed; Proposition~\ref{prop:const-bound} replaces them in the
clock model.
\end{remark}

The clock model identifies, at constant difficulty, honest adoption and
adversarial block discovery after a fork point $a$ with independent
exponential clocks started at $a$, of rates $1$ and $\mu$, the rates
normalised to the honest adoption rate (\emph{Consensus Guide},
\S``Memoryless clocks and the attribution sequence''). For $M \ge 1$ let
$X_{a,M}$ be the number of adversarial arrivals strictly before the $M$-th
honest arrival after $a$. The count $X_{a,M}$ is nondecreasing in $M$.

\begin{proposition}[Constant-difficulty bound]\label{prop:const-bound}
In the clock model, count as validly mined in a fork from $a$ only blocks
the adversary finds after $a$. Let $0 < \mu < c < 1$ and
\[
  \beta = \Bigl(\frac{1 + c}{1 + \mu}\Bigr)^{1 + c}
          \Bigl(\frac{\mu}{c}\Bigr)^{c} ,
  \qquad
  \theta = \Bigl(\frac{c (1 + \mu)}{\mu (1 + c)}\Bigr)^{c} .
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for every fork point $a$ and $M \ge 1$,
  $\Pr[X_{a,M} \ge cM] \le \beta^{M}$, and $\beta < 1$;
\item for any set $A$ of candidate fork points, the probability that for
  some $a \in A$ a fork from $a$ exists at some time with more than $L$
  blocks, total work after $a$ at least that of the honest chain, and
  validly mined fraction at least $c$, is at most
  \[
    |A| \, \frac{(1 + \theta)\, \beta^{L + 1}}{1 - \beta} .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
For $|A|$ polynomial in $\lambda$ and $L = \Theta(\lambda)$ this is
negligible in $\lambda$, so Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} holds in the clock
model, which recovers Corollary~1 there. The proposition says nothing about
the variable-difficulty backbone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) By memorylessness the clocks restart at $a$, and the bound is the
\emph{Math Guide}'s Theorem ``Chernoff bound for the arrival count'', with
the law of Theorem ``Negative-binomial arrival count'' (\S``Exponential clocks, Poisson counts, and the Chernoff method''),
which also shows $\beta < 1$. That proof attains $\beta$ at
$e^{t^*} = c (1 + \mu) / (\mu (1 + c)) > 1$ as
$e^{-t^* c} \Phi_1(t^*) = \beta$, where
$\Phi_M(t) = (1 + \mu - \mu e^{t})^{-M}$ is the moment generating function of
$X_{a,M}$; note that $\theta = e^{t^* c}$.

(ii) Fix $a \in A$. At constant difficulty the work of a block is constant,
so work is block count. Consider a time at which the honest chain has $m$
blocks after $a$, and a fork from $a$ at that time with $L''$ blocks, total
work at least that of the honest chain and validly mined fraction at least
$c$. Then $L'' \ge \max(m, L + 1)$. The validly mined blocks were found by
the adversary after $a$ and before the $(m + 1)$-th honest arrival, so there
are at most $X_{a,m+1}$ of them, and the fraction condition gives
\[
  X_{a,m+1} \ge c L'' \ge c \max(m, L + 1) .
\]
If $m \le L$, then $X_{a,L+1} \ge X_{a,m+1} \ge c(L + 1)$, an event of
probability at most $\beta^{L+1}$ by (i). If $m = M - 1$ with $M \ge L + 2$,
the event is $X_{a,M} \ge cM - c$; the Chernoff method (\emph{Math Guide},
same section, Theorem ``The Chernoff method'') at $t^*$ bounds its
probability by
\[
  e^{-t^* (cM - c)} \Phi_M(t^*) = e^{t^* c}\,
  \bigl(e^{-t^* c} \Phi_1(t^*)\bigr)^{M} = \theta \beta^{M} .
\]
The union bound (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``The union bound and a birthday
calculation'') over these events gives, for the fork point $a$,
\[
  \beta^{L+1} + \theta \sum_{M \ge L + 2} \beta^{M}
  \le \beta^{L+1} + \frac{\theta \beta^{L+2}}{1 - \beta}
  \le \frac{(1 + \theta)\, \beta^{L+1}}{1 - \beta} ,
\]
and the union over $A$ contributes the factor $|A|$. For negligibility,
$\beta$ and $\theta$ are constants with $\beta < 1$, so for
$L = \Theta(\lambda)$ the bound is a polynomial in $\lambda$ times a
function exponentially small in $\lambda$, which is negligible (\emph{Math
Guide}, \S``Polynomial, exponential, and negligible functions'').
\end{proof}

The source omits step (ii). The identification of the fork experiment with
the clock model is the paper's (proof of Corollary~1) and is not derived from
Definition~\ref{def:backbone}; the counting hypothesis on validly mined
blocks is returned to in \S\ref{sec:protocol-forks}.

\begin{remark}[Status of the $(c, L)$ assumption]\label{rem:cl-status}
The paper conjectures that $(c, L)$ can be derived from the backbone
parameters $(\mu, \gamma, s, \tau, E, f)$ and leaves the derivation out of
scope (``not trivial and out of the scope of this paper'', ePrint 2019/226,
Section~3.2, p.~8). No such derivation exists: in the classes of
Definition~\ref{def:classes} it is an open problem.
Proposition~\ref{prop:const-bound} covers only the constant-difficulty clock
model and does not connect the variable-difficulty backbone to
Assumption~\ref{ass:cl}. Every result of this volume that uses the
assumption is conditional on it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Security and succinctness of chain proofs}\label{sec:model-defs}

\begin{definition}[SPV predicate]\label{def:spv-predicate}
Let $C = (B_0, \ldots, B_\eta)$ be a chain with genesis block $B_0$ and head
$B_\eta$. The \emph{SPV predicate} of $C$ is $1$ if and only if every block
$B_{\eta'}$ carries a valid proof of work at the target that
Definition~\ref{def:retarget} assigns to its position, and for
$1 \le \eta' \le \eta$ the hash of $B_{\eta'-1}$ is contained in the header
of $B_{\eta'}$.
The statement a prover proves at round $r$ is that it knows a chain ending
in $B_\eta$, with total work $\omega$ (Definition~\ref{def:difficulty-level}),
whose SPV predicate is $1$.
\end{definition}

The definition is ePrint 2019/226, Definition~2 (Section~3.3, p.~9). The
paper writes $B_n$ and $D$ for the head and the cumulative difficulty; here
$\eta$ is a height and $\omega$ the total work, $n$ and $D$ being reserved.

\begin{definition}[Equivalence up to the last blocks]\label{def:last-ell}
Two chains are \emph{equivalent up to the last $\ell$ blocks}, with $\ell$
the persistence depth of Assumption~\ref{ass:persistence}, if removing the
last $\ell$ blocks of each leaves one a prefix of the other. The verifier
treats valid proofs of equivalent chains as proofs of one chain and takes
the one of greatest total work as its representative; no predicate is
evaluated on the last $\ell$ blocks.
\end{definition}

The paper writes $k$ for this depth (Section~3.3, p.~9); here $\ell$, $k$
being the catch parameter.

\begin{definition}[Verifier setting]\label{def:verifier-setting}
The verifier is a light client (Definition~\ref{def:light-client}) whose
only prior knowledge of the chain is the genesis block $B_0$, and which has
oracle access to $H$ to check proofs of work. It receives a set
$\mathcal{P}$ of proofs from several provers, at least one of which is
honest, and accepts the SPV predicate of the proof whose head carries the
greatest total work, up to Definition~\ref{def:last-ell}.
\end{definition}

The honest-prover hypothesis places eclipse attacks, in which every prover
is adversarial, outside the model (ePrint 2019/226, Section~3.3, p.~9).

\begin{definition}[Security of a chain proof protocol]\label{def:security}
A \emph{chain proof protocol} is a pair $(\mathsf{P}, \mathsf{V})$ of
algorithms with oracle access to $H$: the prover $\mathsf{P}$ maps a chain
to a proof, and the verifier $\mathsf{V}$ maps a set of proofs to an output
$(B, \omega)$, a head and a total work, or to rejection. The \emph{security
game}, for an environment, a PPT adversary $\Adv$ as in
Definition~\ref{def:adversary} and a round $r$, is as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The backbone execution of Definition~\ref{def:backbone} runs to round
  $r$, with $\Adv$ controlling the adversarial players.
\item At the start of round $r$ the verifier receives a set $\mathcal{P}$
  containing at least one proof that $\mathsf{P}$ computes on the chain of
  an honest full node; $\Adv$ chooses the other proofs.
\item The adversary wins if either of the following clauses fails.
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item At the end of round $r$ the verifier outputs the SPV predicate of
    greatest total work for some head $B$: a pair $(B, \omega)$ such that
    the SPV predicate holds for a chain ending in $B$ of total work
    $\omega$, and no proof in $\mathcal{P}$ of a chain whose SPV predicate
    holds carries more total work, up to Definition~\ref{def:last-ell}.
  \item Every honest full node at round $r$ holds a chain of which the chain
    committed by $B$, with its last $\ell$ blocks removed, is a prefix.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
The protocol is \emph{secure} if for every environment, every PPT $\Adv$
and every round $r$ the probability that $\Adv$ wins is negligible in
$\lambda$.
\end{definition}

The definition states both clauses of ePrint 2019/226, Definition~3
(Section~3.3, p.~9). The game idiom is that of the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Security as a game''; the multi-prover game is defined here.

\begin{definition}[Succinctness]\label{def:succinct}
A chain proof protocol $(\mathsf{P}, \mathsf{V})$ is \emph{succinct} if for
every PPT prover and every round $r$, every proof produced at round $r$ has
size $O(\mathrm{polylog}(N))$ bits, where $N$ is the number of blocks of the
honest chain, $\mathrm{polylog}(N) = (\log N)^{O(1)}$ as fixed in
\S\ref{sec:intro-sources}, and the asymptotics are in $N$ with $\lambda$,
$c$ and $L$ fixed.
\end{definition}

The definition is ePrint 2019/226, Definition~4 (p.~9), after Kiayias,
Miller and Zindros, ``Non-interactive proofs of proof-of-work'',
Definition~4.

\begin{definition}[Non-interactive proof of proof of work]\label{def:nipopow}
A \emph{non-interactive proof of proof of work} is a chain proof protocol
for the SPV predicate in which each prover sends a single message and no
message passes from verifier to prover, and which is secure
(Definition~\ref{def:security}) and succinct (Definition~\ref{def:succinct}).
\end{definition}

The term names this class of protocols. Kiayias, Miller and Zindros use the
same name for their superblock construction, a different member of the
class.

\section{The FlyClient protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

This section constructs the protocol of ePrint 2019/226 in the model of
\S\ref{sec:model}, each object before its first use: the header chain
commitment and the check applied to one sampled block, two lemmas on forks,
the difficulty Merkle mountain range with its node checks, the sampling
distribution, the interactive and the non-interactive protocol, the security
theorem with its hypotheses and status, and verification after acceptance.
Throughout, $H\colon\{0,1\}^*\to\{0,1\}^\kappa$ is the hash function of
\S\ref{sec:mmr}, which is also the proof-of-work oracle of
Definition~\ref{def:backbone}; $\log$ is the natural logarithm and
$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{col}}_H(\mathcal{B})$ the collision advantage of an
algorithm $\mathcal{B}$ (\emph{Crypto Guide}, Definition ``Collision
resistance'', \S``Security notions: preimage, second-preimage, and collision
resistance''), the security parameter left implicit.

\subsection{Header chain commitments and per-sample checks}
\label{sec:protocol-commit}

Blocks are written $B_0$ (the genesis block), $B_1$, $B_2$, \ldots, with
$B_\eta$ the block at height $\eta$ and $\mathrm{hdr}(B)$ the header of $B$.

\begin{construction}[Header chain commitment]\label{def:chain-commitment}
Fix an aggregating range (Definition~\ref{def:aggregating}) under the
bagging $\mathrm{Bag}_R$ (Definition~\ref{def:bagging}) whose leaf map sends
a block $B$ to a node tuple $\mathrm{leaf}(B)$ with hash field
$\mathrm{leaf}(B).\mathsf{h}=H(\mathrm{hdr}(B))$. Leaf $j$ of every range is
$\mathrm{leaf}(B_{j-1})$, so leaf $j$ is the block at height $j-1$. For every
height $\eta\ge 1$ let $M_\eta$ be the range over the $\eta$ leaves
$\mathrm{leaf}(B_0),\dots,\mathrm{leaf}(B_{\eta-1})$ and $\mathsf{rt}_\eta$
its root tuple. The producer of $B_\eta$ obtains $M_\eta$ from $M_{\eta-1}$
by Construction~\ref{def:append} applied to $\mathrm{leaf}(B_{\eta-1})$, with
the parent map $\mathrm{par}$ in place of $H(\cdot\,\|\,\cdot)$ ($M_1$ is the
single leaf $\mathrm{leaf}(B_0)$), and records the hash field
$\mathsf{rt}_\eta.\mathsf{h}$ in the header of $B_\eta$. The genesis header
records no root. A full node accepts $B_\eta$ only if its header records
$\mathsf{rt}_\eta.\mathsf{h}$.
\end{construction}

The construction has the following invariant: the header of $B_\eta$
commits, through one root, to exactly the blocks strictly before it, and a
head $B_\eta$ commits to $n=\eta$ leaves, $n$ denoting this leaf count
below. The root's fields other than its hash
field are not recorded; a verifier recomputes the root tuple from its two
children, which the hash field binds (Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}).
The range of ePrint 2019/226, Section~2, is the instance whose tuples carry
the hash field alone; the paper's difficulty Merkle mountain range is the
instance of Definition~\ref{def:diff-mmr}. The one further header field is
the paper's only consensus change (Section~4.2). The paper indexes the
committing block and its range inconsistently; the invariant fixes one
indexing, and the paper's is not carried. In the classes of
Definition~\ref{def:classes}, this commitment from the genesis block is the
paper's design and no Zcash rule specifies it; the specified commitment is
the epoch-scoped instance of ``The chain-history commitment''
(\S\ref{sec:history}), from which nothing is concluded here.

One sample at index $j\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ against a head $B_\eta$ must establish
three facts: that the sampled block is leaf $j$ under the head's root, that
the leaf's tuple is the one its header determines, and that the root in the
sampled header commits to exactly the first $j-1$ of the same leaves.

\begin{construction}[Per-sample check]\label{def:per-sample}
The inputs are a root tuple $\mathsf{rt}$ with leaf count $n$ and an index
$1\le j\le n$. The prover sends the header $\mathrm{hdr}_j$ of the block at
height $j-1$, the tuple $v_j$ of leaf $j$, and its inclusion path $\pi$ in
$M_n$ (Definition~\ref{def:mmr-path}) with every sibling as a full tuple. The
verifier performs the following checks.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Leaf}: $v_j.\mathsf{h}=H(\mathrm{hdr}_j)$, and every other field
  of $v_j$ that the leaf map determines from the header alone equals its
  value computed from $\mathrm{hdr}_j$.
\item \emph{Inclusion}: $\mathrm{Verify}_R(\mathsf{rt},n,j,v_j,\pi)$ of
  Definition~\ref{def:mmr-path}, in the tuple form of
  Definition~\ref{def:aggregating}, accepts; the side of each sibling and the
  path length $\mathrm{depth}_R(n,j)$ are fixed by $(j,n)$.
\item \emph{Prefix}: for $j\ge 2$, the hash field of
  $\mathrm{PrefixRoot}_R(n,j,\pi)$ (Construction~\ref{def:prefix-root}), run
  with $\mathrm{par}$ in place of $H(\cdot\,\|\,\cdot)$, equals the root
  recorded in $\mathrm{hdr}_j$; for $j=1$, $\mathrm{hdr}_1$ equals the
  genesis header, which the verifier knows.
\item \emph{Proof of work}: $\mathrm{hdr}_j$ carries a valid proof of work
  at the target $T$ it declares, $H(\mathrm{hdr}_j)<T$
  (Definition~\ref{def:backbone}).
\end{enumerate}
Any failure rejects.
\end{construction}

ePrint 2019/226, Algorithm~2, lists checks (b) and (c); checks (a) and (d)
come from its Definition~5 and Section~6.1. Check (b) uses the length rule
of Definition~\ref{def:mmr-path}, not that of the paper's Algorithm~4.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of the per-sample check]\label{prop:sample-check}
Let $\mathsf{rt}$ be any root tuple, possibly chosen by the adversary, with
leaf count $n$, and let $1\le j\le n$. There is an explicit reduction
$\mathcal{B}$ such that, for every adversary $\Adv$ that outputs samples at
position $j$ against $(\mathsf{rt},n)$, the probability that every sample
passes Construction~\ref{def:per-sample} while one of the following fails is
at most $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{col}}_H(\mathcal{B})$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item any two passing samples at position $j$ carry the same leaf tuple and
  the same header, so the header is bound to position $j$ of the sequence
  that $\mathsf{rt}$ commits;
\item every header-determined field of that leaf tuple is its value computed
  from that header;
\item for $j\ge 2$, the root recorded in that header is the hash field of a
  prefix-root tuple that is the same for every passing sample at $j$, hence a
  function of $(\mathsf{rt},n,j)$; if $\mathsf{rt}$ is the root of a
  consistent labelling over $v_1,\dots,v_n$, it is the root of the
  consistent labelling over $v_1,\dots,v_{j-1}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The reduction $\mathcal{B}$ runs $\Adv$ and inspects two passing samples
$(\mathrm{hdr},v,\pi)$ and $(\mathrm{hdr}',v',\pi')$ at position $j$.
(i) If $(v,\pi)\ne(v',\pi')$, Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}(2), the tuple
form of Theorem~\ref{thm:position-binding} under the prefix-free
serialisation of Definition~\ref{def:aggregating}, yields a collision at the
highest step whose inputs differ. If $v=v'$ but
$\mathrm{hdr}\ne\mathrm{hdr}'$, check (a) gives
$H(\mathrm{hdr})=v.\mathsf{h}=H(\mathrm{hdr}')$, a collision. (ii) is check
(a). (iii) Barring the collisions of (i), $\pi=\pi'$, so the fold of check
(c) over the left siblings of $\pi$ is the same tuple for every passing
sample, and check (c) equates its hash field with the recorded root. For a
consistent labelling, Lemma~\ref{lem:left-siblings} with
Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}(3) identifies the fold with the root over
$v_1,\dots,v_{j-1}$, as Theorem~\ref{thm:prefix-root} does for plain values.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Forks against a committed chain}\label{sec:protocol-forks}

A claimed chain $C$ \emph{forks} from the honest chain at its fork point, the
last common block (\S\ref{sec:model-adversary}). Its head records the root
of a range over the leaf tuples $v'_1,\dots,v'_n$ of its blocks, and $C$
\emph{places} a block $B$ at leaf position $j$ when
$v'_j.\mathsf{h}=H(\mathrm{hdr}(B))$.

\begin{lemma}[Honest blocks after the fork point]\label{lem:no-honest}
Let the head of $C$ record the hash field of the root of a consistent
labelling over $v'_1,\dots,v'_n$. Let $B$ be a block of the honest chain at
height $\eta$ above the fork point, whose header records the hash field of
the honest root over the honest leaf tuples $v_1,\dots,v_\eta$, and let $C$
place $B$ at leaf position $j\ge 2$. Suppose that
\begin{enumerate}
\item the sequences $(v'_1.\mathsf{h},\dots,v'_{j-1}.\mathsf{h})$ and
  $(v_1.\mathsf{h},\dots,v_\eta.\mathsf{h})$ differ, possibly in length; and
\item no block header equals $\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r)$ for
  node tuples $\ell,r$ (leaf--internal separation).
\end{enumerate}
Then from a sample of $B$ that passes Construction~\ref{def:per-sample}
against the head of $C$ an explicit reduction computes a collision of $H$,
or finds a leaf position $i<j$ of $C$ whose tuple $v'_i$ has hash field
$H(\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r))$ for node tuples $\ell,r$. A
sample at $i$ passes check~(a) only on a header hashing to that value, which
by hypothesis~2 is a collision. Consequently, barring a collision, either a
sample on $B$ rejects or leaf $i$ does, and that block or leaf belongs, in
the analysis of \S\ref{sec:protocol-sampling}, to the invalid set of the
fork.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}(iii) a passing sample makes the root
recorded in $\mathrm{hdr}(B)$ equal to the hash field of the root of the
consistent labelling of $M_{j-1}$ over $v'_1,\dots,v'_{j-1}$. That value is
also the hash field of the honest root of $M_\eta$. Descend both labellings
from their roots, keeping a pair of nodes with equal hash fields whose leaf
subsequences differ in their hash fields. At a pair of internal nodes, equal
hash fields $H(\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r))=
H(\mathrm{ser}(\ell')\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r'))$ with $(\ell,r)\ne(\ell',r')$
are a collision by unique decoding (proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}); otherwise the children agree as tuples,
the leaf subsequences of the left children or of the right children differ,
and the descent continues there. Two leaves with equal hash fields do not
have different hash-field sequences, so the descent ends at a leaf opposite
an internal node. If the leaf is honest, its hash field is $H(\mathrm{hdr})$
for the header $\mathrm{hdr}$ of an honest block, and
$H(\mathrm{hdr})=H(\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r))$ with distinct
inputs by hypothesis~2 is a collision. If the leaf is a leaf $v'_i$ of $C$,
$i\le j-1$, opposite an honest internal node with children $\ell,r$, then
$v'_i.\mathsf{h}=H(\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r))$, which is the
second outcome. The descent takes at most
$\max(\lceil\log_2(j-1)\rceil,\lceil\log_2\eta\rceil)$ steps.
\end{proof}

The lemma is ePrint 2019/226, Section~5.1 with Corollary~4 (here
Corollary~\ref{cor:prefix-binding}), extended to a block placed at a height
other than its own, where the two roots have different leaf counts and
hypothesis~2 is needed. It concerns honest blocks placed after the fork
point and states nothing about blocks the adversary mines; those are the
subject of the next lemma.

\begin{lemma}[No reuse of work across the fork point]\label{lem:no-reuse}
Model $H$ as a random oracle with $\kappa$-bit outputs (\emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``The random oracle model''), and let at most $q_H$ queries to $H$ be made
in the experiment, by $\Adv$, by the verification of its output, and by the
computation of the prefix root at every position of the output fork after
its fork point. Call a
query $H(x)$ \emph{reused} if $x$ is the header of a block $B$ of the output
fork after its fork point, $x$ records the hash field of the prefix root of
the fork at $B$'s position, and the query precedes the first query whose
answer is that recorded value. Then
\[
  \Pr\bigl[\Adv\text{ outputs a fork with a block mined by a reused query}
  \bigr]\le q_H^2\,2^{-\kappa}+\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{col}}_H(\mathcal{B})
\]
for an explicit reduction $\mathcal{B}$. Consequently, except with that
probability, only adversarial work done after the fork's prefix is fixed
counts against the honest chain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Construction~\ref{def:chain-commitment} the root recorded in a header
after the fork point is the hash field of a tuple over the fork's prefix; by
Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}(iii) that tuple is a function of the
fork's root, position and leaf count, barring a collision, which accounts
for the term $\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{col}}_H(\mathcal{B})$. Its hash field is
the answer of a query, made at the latest during that computation. A reused
query fixes a recorded value $r$ before any query has answer $r$. The first
query with answer $r$ has an input not queried before, since a repeated
input returns an earlier answer; its answer is uniform on $\{0,1\}^\kappa$
and independent of all earlier queries, so it equals one of the at most
$q_H$ values recorded in earlier query inputs with probability at most
$q_H\,2^{-\kappa}$. The union bound over the at most $q_H$ queries
(\emph{Math Guide}, \S``The union bound and a birthday calculation'') gives
$q_H^2\,2^{-\kappa}$.
\end{proof}

The paper asserts the statement in one sentence (ePrint 2019/226,
Section~3.2); the proof above is this volume's. The bound is in the number of
oracle queries, not in the mining rate.

\subsection{The difficulty Merkle mountain range}\label{sec:protocol-diffmmr}

A sampling verifier sees few difficulty transitions, so the transitions it
does not see must be constrained by committed aggregates.

\begin{definition}[Difficulty Merkle mountain range]\label{def:diff-mmr}
The \emph{difficulty Merkle mountain range} (\emph{difficulty MMR}) is the
instance of Definition~\ref{def:aggregating} whose node $v$ is the tuple
\[
  v=(v.\mathsf{h},\,v.\mathsf{w},\,v.\mathsf{t},\,v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}},
  \,v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}},\,v.\mathsf{n}),
\]
with a serialisation that concatenates injective fixed-width encodings of
the six fields, hence prefix-free. For a block $B$ whose header declares
target $T$, the leaf map sets
\begin{itemize}
\item $v.\mathsf{h}=H(\mathrm{hdr}(B))$;
\item $v.\mathsf{w}=v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}=D(T)$, the difficulty
  level of $T$ (Definition~\ref{def:difficulty-level});
\item $v.\mathsf{t}$ the number of rounds between the round recorded by the
  predecessor of $B$ and the round recorded by $B$ ($0$ for the genesis
  block);
\item $v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}$ the level of the target that
  Definition~\ref{def:retarget} assigns to the block after $B$;
\item $v.\mathsf{n}=1$.
\end{itemize}
The parent of $\ell$ and $r$ has hash field
$H(\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r))$, over both children's complete
tuples; its fields $\mathsf{w}$, $\mathsf{t}$ and $\mathsf{n}$ are the sums
of the children's; it inherits $\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$ from $\ell$ and
$\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}$ from $r$.
\end{definition}

The fields $\mathsf{h}$, $\mathsf{w}$, $\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$ and
$\mathsf{n}$ of a leaf are determined by its header alone and are checked by
item (a) of Construction~\ref{def:per-sample}; the fields $\mathsf{t}$ and
$\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}$ depend on neighbouring blocks and are
constrained only by Construction~\ref{def:node-checks}. The definition makes
three precisions against ePrint 2019/226, Definition~7. The paper writes
$H(lc, rc)$ without fixing its input; hashing the children's hash fields
alone would leave their other fields unbound. The paper's ``timestamp''
$\mathsf{t}$ is an elapsed time, and its ``targets'' $\mathsf{w}$,
$\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$ and $\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}$ are
difficulty levels. The paper's checks name fields $t_{\mathrm{start}}$,
$t_{\mathrm{end}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{end}}$ that its Definition~7 never
declares; the declared aggregate $\mathsf{t}$ suffices for every timing
check below, so the field set is the declared one. The paper's data field is
omitted, since no check uses it. No encoding of the fields is fixed by the
paper.

\begin{lemma}[Weight binding for the difficulty MMR]\label{lem:weight-binding}
Let a sample at leaf $j$ pass Construction~\ref{def:per-sample} against a
root $\mathsf{rt}$ with leaf count $n$, the shape being fixed by $n$ under
$\mathrm{Bag}_R$. Except with probability
$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{col}}_H(\mathcal{B})$ for an explicit reduction
$\mathcal{B}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the leaf weight $v_j.\mathsf{w}$ is the difficulty level $D(T)$ of the
  target $T$ at which the header's proof of work is valid;
\item the interval $[W,W+v_j.\mathsf{w})$, with $W$ the sum of the fields
  $\mathsf{w}$ of the left siblings on the path, is a function of
  $(\mathsf{rt},n,j)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(1) Check (a) sets $v_j.\mathsf{w}=D(T)$ for the declared target $T$, check
(d) validates the proof of work at that $T$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}(i) and (ii) bind the header and its
fields to position $j$. (2) Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}(1). Whether $W$
equals the true prefix weight is part~(2) of that lemma, which holds for a
consistent labelling only and is not claimed here.
\end{proof}

\begin{construction}[Node checks]\label{def:node-checks}
Assume, with ePrint 2019/226, Section~6.1, that the retarget-epoch length
$E$ of Definition~\ref{def:retarget} and the leaf count $n$ are powers of
two, so that the retarget epochs are the perfect subtrees of altitude
$\log_2 E$. For every parent $p$ on a sample's path, with children $\ell$
and $r$ (one of them a carried sibling), the verifier checks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $p=\mathrm{par}(\ell,r)$ under Definition~\ref{def:diff-mmr};
\item $\ell.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}=r.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$;
\item for each of $\ell$ and $r$, internal consistency:
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item a node $v$ of altitude below $\log_2 E$ lies inside one retarget
    epoch and has $v.\mathsf{w}=v.\mathsf{n}\cdot
    v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$, and
    $v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}=v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$ unless $v$
    contains the last block of its epoch;
  \item a node $v$ of altitude $\log_2 E$ is one retarget epoch, has
    $v.\mathsf{w}=E\cdot v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$, and
    $v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}=
    D\bigl(\mathrm{Retarget}(2^\kappa/v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}},
    v.\mathsf{t})\bigr)$, the level that Definition~\ref{def:retarget}
    assigns after an epoch of $v.\mathsf{t}$ rounds at level
    $v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$;
  \item a node $v$ above altitude $\log_2 E$ is \emph{feasible}: its
    $(v.\mathsf{t},\allowbreak v.\mathsf{w},\allowbreak
    v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}},\allowbreak
    v.\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}},\allowbreak v.\mathsf{n})$ is attained by some
    sequence of clamped retargets (Propositions~\ref{prop:feasibility}
    and~\ref{prop:feasibility-bounds}).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
At the sampled leaf the checks (a) and (d) of
Construction~\ref{def:per-sample} apply; their effect on $\mathsf{w}$ and
$\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-binding}.
\emph{Weight-position check}: when a leaf is requested at a point $x$ of
cumulative weight, $0\le x<\mathsf{rt}.\mathsf{w}$, the returned leaf $j$
satisfies $W\le x<W+v_j.\mathsf{w}$, with $W$ the sum of the fields
$\mathsf{w}$ of the left siblings on its path; this interval is bound to
$(\mathsf{rt},n,j)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}(1). Any failure
rejects.
\end{construction}

The construction is ePrint 2019/226, Section~6.1 (``Difficulty MMR
Verification''); the paper states the weight-position check only informally
(Section~2).

% compute/sampling.py:
% [sec:protocol-diffmmr] raw retarget T' = T Delta f / E: a maximal drop in
%   difficulty (T' = tau T) needs Delta >= tau E / f rounds, a maximal raise
%   (T' = T/tau) Delta <= E/(f tau)
\begin{proposition}[Feasibility envelope]\label{prop:feasibility}
Let consecutive retarget epochs $0,\dots,z-1$ have levels
$D_0=\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}},D_1,\dots,D_{z-1}$, and let
$D_z=\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}=\tau^y\,\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$, where
every retarget obeys the clamp of Definition~\ref{def:retarget}, so that
$D_{e+1}/D_e\in[1/\tau,\tau]$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $|y|\le z$ and, for every $e$,
  \[
    \mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}\max(\tau^{-e},\tau^{y-z+e})\le D_e\le
    \mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}\min(\tau^{e},\tau^{y+z-e});
  \]
\item for integer $y$ with $z-y$ even both envelopes are attained by clamped
  sequences, so the total weight $E\sum_{e<z}D_e$ is maximal when the
  difficulty is raised by $\tau$ per epoch and then lowered by $\tau$ per
  epoch to $\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}$, and minimal when it is lowered first
  and then raised;
\item an epoch at target $T$ that lowers the difficulty maximally (new
  target $\tau T$) lasts $\Delta\ge\tau E/f$ rounds, and one that raises it
  maximally (new target $T/\tau$) lasts $\Delta\le E/(f\tau)$ rounds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Since $|\log_\tau D_{e+1}-\log_\tau D_e|\le 1$, induction on $e$ from
epoch $0$ gives $|\log_\tau(D_e/\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}})|\le e$, and
from epoch $z$ gives $|\log_\tau(D_e/\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}})-y|\le
z-e$; at $e=0$ the second bound is $|y|\le z$. (2) For $z-y$ even the
pointwise upper envelope has exponent steps $+1$ up to
$e=(y+z)/2$ and $-1$ after, and the lower envelope steps $-1$ up to
$e=(z-y)/2$ and $+1$ after; both are clamped, and the weight is increasing
in each $D_e$. (3) The raw retarget is $T\Delta f/E$
(\S\ref{sec:model-retarget}); the clamp is reached from above when
$T\Delta f/E\ge\tau T$ and from below when $T\Delta f/E\le T/\tau$.
A script checks the sums against brute force over
exponent sequences, and the two thresholds.
\end{proof}

% [sec:protocol-diffmmr] closed forms (upper; lower with exponent -(z - y)/2)
%   equal the brute-force envelope extremes in all 123 cases tau in
%   {2, 4, 3/2}, 1 <= z <= 11, 0 <= y <= z, z - y even; the printed lower form
%   (exponent (y + z)/2) is negative in every case
% [sec:protocol-diffmmr] printed upper summation equals the closed form iff
%   y < z; at y = z it overcounts by tau^z
% [sec:protocol-diffmmr] tau = 4, y = 0, two epochs: printed lower bound -5,
%   corrected 5/4 (levels 1 + 1/4); upper bound 5 (levels 1 + 4); per-block
%   weights scale both by E
\begin{proposition}[Feasibility bounds]\label{prop:feasibility-bounds}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:feasibility}, let $y$ be an integer
with $y\ge 0$ and $z-y$ even. If a node spanning the $z$ epochs is
attained by a clamped sequence, then
\begin{enumerate}
\item $y\le z$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:feasibility}(1));
\item $\displaystyle \mathsf{w}\le E\,\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}\,
  \frac{(\tau+1)\,\tau^{(y+z)/2}-\tau^{1+y}-1}{\tau-1}$;
\item $\displaystyle \mathsf{w}\ge E\,\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}\,
  \frac{\tau^{y}+\tau-(\tau+1)\,\tau^{-(z-y)/2}}{\tau-1}$;
\item if $y=z$, then $\mathsf{t}\le zE/(f\tau)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Bounds (2) and (3) are the geometric sums of the envelopes of
Proposition~\ref{prop:feasibility}(2); at $y=z$ both reduce to
$(\tau^z-1)/(\tau-1)$. For (4), $y=z$ forces a maximal raise in each of the
$z$ epochs, each of at most $E/(f\tau)$ rounds by
Proposition~\ref{prop:feasibility}(3). A script
confirms that the closed forms equal the brute-force extremes in all $123$
cases with $\tau\in\{2,4,3/2\}$, $1\le z\le 11$, $0\le y\le z$ and $z-y$
even; at
$\tau=4$, $y=0$, $z=2$ they give $5$ and $5/4$ per unit of $E$.
\end{proof}

The paper states these checks with $k$ and $n$ in place of $y$ and $z$,
which here are the catch parameter and the leaf count (ePrint 2019/226,
Section~6.1, pp.~18--19). Three departures are made. The printed lower bound
has exponent $(y+z)/2$ in place of $-(z-y)/2$ and is then negative, $-5$
against the corrected $5/4$ at $\tau=4$, $y=0$, $z=2$. The printed bounds
count one level per epoch, whereas the leaf rule
$\mathsf{w}=\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$ sums per block, so both scale by
$E$. The printed upper summation overcounts by $\tau^z$ at $y=z$, where its
closed form is right. The paper further requires that $\mathsf{t}$ cover the
epochs in which the difficulty is lowered, without a formula, and calls the
cases of non-integer and negative $y$ ``similar'' without stating them. The
conditions above are necessary, not sufficient: the general feasibility
predicate that item~3(c) of Construction~\ref{def:node-checks} requires is
not given by the paper.

\begin{assumption}[Transition rewriting]\label{ass:rewriting}
In the model of Definition~\ref{def:backbone}, for every adversary $\Adv$
that with non-negligible probability $p$ outputs a chain $C$ accepted by the
node checks on every sample, some of whose difficulty transitions violate
Definition~\ref{def:retarget}, there is an adversary $\Adv'$ that makes the
same number of oracle queries, respects the retarget rule, and with
probability at least $p-\negl(\kappa)$ outputs a chain $C'$ whose validly
mined blocks occupy the same positions as those of $C$.
\end{assumption}

The assumption is the statement of ePrint 2019/226, Lemma~4, taken as a
hypothesis because its proof is a sketch. It handles subtrees whose
aggregates are inconsistent with their children, which
Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}(2) does not cover. The paper's proof
strategy is as follows. Let $x'$ be the highest node on a claimed path at
which the node checks fail. Every path through $x'$, or carrying it as a
sibling, rejects, so barring a collision every leaf of the subtree $M'$
under the parent of $x'$ is unsampleable without rejection. The adversary
$\Adv'$ replaces the transitions inside $M'$ by clamped ones consistent with
the aggregates $(\mathsf{w},\mathsf{t},\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}},
\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}},\mathsf{n})$ of the root of $M'$, which is
possible because that node passes the feasibility check and the leaves of
$M'$, being invalid, need no proof of work; this is repeated until no
invalid transition remains. The paper writes ``consistency with $x_0$''
where the parent of $x'$ is meant.

\begin{remark}[Gaps in the rewriting argument]\label{rem:rewriting-gaps}
(1) The feasibility step presumes a complete feasibility predicate, which
the paper gives only in the simplified setting of
Proposition~\ref{prop:feasibility-bounds}. (2) Rewriting the leaves of $M'$
changes every later root and hence every later header, so $\Adv'$ must fix
the rewrite before it mines the later valid blocks, and must mine them anew;
the paper asserts without a simulation argument that this succeeds with
probability at least $p-\negl(\kappa)$. (3) ``The same valid blocks'' holds
for positions, not for header contents. Assumption~\ref{ass:rewriting} is
therefore the paper's claim, not a result of this volume.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The sampling distribution}\label{sec:protocol-sampling}

Uniform sampling fails on forks near the tip: almost every uniform sample
lands before a late fork point, where the two chains agree. If the fork
point were known, each sample after it would be invalid with probability at
least $1-c$ under Assumption~\ref{ass:cl}.

\begin{construction}[Dyadic-suffix sampling]\label{def:dyadic}
At constant difficulty, over a claimed chain of $n$ blocks and with $u$
samples per interval, the verifier draws, for every integer $i$ with
$0\le i<\log_2 n$, $u$ samples uniformly and independently from the last
$n/2^i$ blocks, and checks every one of the last $\min(L,u)$ blocks.
\end{construction}

The samples do not depend on the prover's answers, so the protocol is
one-shot and public-coin (\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Interactive protocols,
proofs, and arguments''). The construction is ePrint 2019/226, Section~5.3,
with $u$ in place of the paper's $k$.

% compute/sampling.py:
% [sec:protocol-sampling] dyadic suffix: miss probability <= ((1 + c)/2)^u
%   for c in {0.25, 0.5, 0.9}, u in {1, 5, 20}, intervals of 8, 64, 1024
%   blocks
\begin{lemma}[Dyadic miss probability]\label{lem:dyadic}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:cl}, at constant difficulty, for every fork point
$a$ and every fork from $a$ of more than $L$ blocks whose total work after
$a$ is at least that of the honest chain, the probability that the
$u\log_2 n$ samples of Construction~\ref{def:dyadic} include no invalid
block of the fork is at most $((1+c)/2)^u$, except with probability
negligible in $\lambda$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let the fork point be $a$ and choose $i$ with
$(2^i-1)n/2^i\le a<(2^{i+1}-1)n/2^{i+1}$. The interval of the last
$n'=n/2^i$ blocks then contains $a$ in its first half, so the fork length
$l=n-a$ satisfies $l>n'/2$, and by Assumption~\ref{ass:cl}, at constant
difficulty, the interval holds at least $(1-c)l>(1-c)n'/2$ invalid blocks.
Each of its $u$ independent uniform samples misses them with probability at
most $1-(1-c)/2=(1+c)/2$, and the samples of the other intervals are
discarded. A script checks the bound for
$c\in\{0.25,0.5,0.9\}$, $u\in\{1,5,20\}$ and intervals of $8$, $64$ and
$1{,}024$ blocks.
\end{proof}

The lemma is ePrint 2019/226, Lemma~2. A fork of at most $\min(L,u)$ blocks
lies in the checked suffix. When $u<L$, a fork of $l$ blocks with
$u<l\le L$ is covered by neither clause, since Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} does
not constrain it; the construction is sound for $u\ge L$, or with the last
$L$ blocks checked in full.

\begin{definition}[Continuum model and catch probability]\label{def:catch}
The claimed chain is the interval $[0,1]$ in relative cumulative work: a
block whose cumulative-work interval is $[W,W+w)$ within total committed
work $\omega$ occupies $[W/\omega,(W+w)/\omega)$; genesis is at $0$ and the
head at $1$. A \emph{fork point} is a point $a\in[0,1)$. By
Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} in its operative form, the adversary designates an
\emph{invalid set} $S\subseteq[a,1]$ of Lebesgue measure at least
$(1-c)(1-a)$; every such $S$ is \emph{admissible}. A \emph{sampling density}
is a probability density $\varphi$ on $[0,1]$ (\emph{Math Guide},
\S``Beyond finite spaces: countable additivity, limits, and densities'').
When a suffix $[1-\delta,1]$ is checked in full, a set $S$ that meets it in
positive measure is caught with probability $1$, and any other $S$ is caught
by one draw with probability $\int_S\varphi$. The \emph{single-draw catch
probability} at $a$ is the infimum $p_\varphi(a)$ of these probabilities
over admissible $S$. For $Q$ independent draws (\emph{Math Guide},
\S``Conditional probability and independence'') against a set $S$ fixed
before the draws, all miss with probability
$(1-\int_S\varphi)^Q\le(1-p_\varphi(a))^Q$. The \emph{design problem} is to
choose $\varphi$ to maximise $\min_a p_\varphi(a)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Best response]\label{lem:best-response}
Let $\varphi$ be non-decreasing on an interval $[a,b]$ with $b\le 1$, and let
$0\le m\le b-a$. Among measurable $S\subseteq[a,b]$ of measure $m$, the
integral $\int_S\varphi$ is minimal for $S=[a,a+m]$. Hence against a density
non-decreasing on $[a,1-\delta]$, with $[1-\delta,1]$ checked in full, an
adversary forking at $a\le 1-\delta/c$ places its valid weight last, and its
invalid set is $[a,1-(1-a)c]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $I=[a,a+m]$,
$\int_S\varphi-\int_I\varphi=\int_{S\setminus I}\varphi-
\int_{I\setminus S}\varphi$; the two sets have equal measure,
$S\setminus I$ lies right of $a+m$ and $I\setminus S$ left of it, and
$\varphi$ is non-decreasing, so the difference is non-negative. A set
meeting the suffix in positive measure is caught with certainty, so the
adversary's best set lies in $[a,1-\delta]$ and has the least admissible
measure $(1-c)(1-a)$; it ends at $a+(1-c)(1-a)=1-(1-a)c\le 1-\delta$.
\end{proof}

% [sec:protocol-sampling] indifference density K/(1 - x), K = (1 - c)/c:
%   every invalid segment [a, 1 - c(1 - a)] has mass (c - 1) log(c)/c
%   (c = 1/2: log 2 = 0.693147); mass on [0, 1 - eps] = K log(1/eps),
%   unbounded
\begin{construction}[Indifference density]\label{def:indifference}
Equal catch probability at every fork point, that is
$\Phi(1-c(1-a))-\Phi(a)$ constant in $a$ for an antiderivative $\Phi$ of
$\varphi$, gives on differentiation
\[
  \varphi(a)=c\,\varphi\bigl(1-c(1-a)\bigr).
\]
The function $\varphi(\xi)=K/(1-\xi)$ solves it. With $K=(1-c)/c$ every
invalid segment $[a,1-c(1-a)]$ has mass $(c-1)\log(c)/c$, which is
$\log 2\approx 0.693147$ at $c=1/2$. The equation fixes $\varphi$ only up to
a factor periodic in $\log(1-\xi)$ with period $\log(1/c)$. The function is
not integrable on $[0,1]$, its mass on $[0,1-\epsilon]$ being
$K\log(1/\epsilon)$, so it is not a density there.
\end{construction}

The segment mass is
$K\bigl(\log(1-a)-\log(c(1-a))\bigr)=-K\log c$; a script checks it for
$c\in\{0.3,0.5,0.9\}$ and $a\in\{0,0.2,0.7\}$, and the divergence. The
construction is ePrint 2019/226, Section~5.4, p.~16.

% [sec:protocol-sampling] g(x) = 1/((x - 1) log delta) integrates to 1 on
%   [0, 1 - delta]; G(x) = log(1 - x)/log delta; G(1 - delta^U) = U; catch
%   probability G(1 - (1 - a)c) - G(a) = log c / log delta = 1/k at
%   delta = c^k for a <= (c - delta)/c
\begin{construction}[FlyClient sampling density]\label{def:density}
Fix $0<c<1$ and $\delta\in(0,c]$; the suffix $[1-\delta,1]$ is checked in
full. The \emph{FlyClient sampling density} is
\[
  g(\xi)=\begin{cases}
    \dfrac{1}{(\xi-1)\log\delta} & 0\le\xi\le 1-\delta,\\[6pt]
    0 & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  G(\xi)=\frac{\log(1-\xi)}{\log\delta}\quad(0\le\xi\le 1-\delta).
\]
Both factors of the denominator are negative, so $g>0$ on its support, and
$g$ is increasing there; $G$ is its distribution function, with
$G(1-\delta)=1$. For $a\le(c-\delta)/c$ the invalid segment
$[a,1-(1-a)c]$ lies in $[0,1-\delta]$ and has catch probability
\[
  G\bigl(1-(1-a)c\bigr)-G(a)=\frac{\log c}{\log\delta},
\]
which is $1/k$ at $\delta=c^k$. For $a>(c-\delta)/c$ every admissible
invalid set meets the checked suffix in positive measure, and the fork is
caught with probability $1$.
\end{construction}

Normalisation, $G$ and the catch probability are direct integrations. For
the last clause, $[a,1-\delta]$ has measure $1-\delta-a<(1-c)(1-a)$ exactly
when $a>1-\delta/c=(c-\delta)/c$. A script checks
normalisation, $G$ and the catch probability $1/k$ for
$c\in\{0.25,0.3,0.5,0.75,0.9\}$ and $2\le k\le 9$. The density is ePrint
2019/226, Section~5.4, p.~16; Figure~\ref{fig:density} shows it.

\begin{definition}[Sampling map]\label{def:sampling-map}
Let the head's root tuple $\mathsf{rt}$ carry committed work
$\mathsf{rt}.\mathsf{w}$, with leaf count $n$. The \emph{sampling map} sends
a uniform $U\in[0,1]$ to the point $\xi=1-\delta^U$, the inverse of $G$ at
$U$, so that $\xi$ has density $g$; it sends $\xi$ to the point
$x=\xi\cdot\mathsf{rt}.\mathsf{w}$ of cumulative work, and $x$ to the leaf
$j$ whose interval $[W,W+v_j.\mathsf{w})$ contains $x$. That interval is
bound to $(\mathsf{rt},n,j)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}(1) and is
checked by the weight-position check of Construction~\ref{def:node-checks}.
The \emph{checked suffix} is the set of blocks whose interval meets
$[(1-\delta)\,\mathsf{rt}.\mathsf{w},\mathsf{rt}.\mathsf{w})$, together
with the last $L$ blocks and the head.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Discretisation]\label{ass:discretisation}
For every chain $C$ output by an adversary respecting
Definition~\ref{def:retarget}, and for the fork point $a$ of $C$ against the
honest chain, if $C$ has more than $L$
blocks after $a$ and total work after $a$ at least that of the honest chain,
then one draw of Definition~\ref{def:sampling-map} returns an invalid block,
or a block on which the per-sample check fails, with probability at least
$p_g(a)$ of Definition~\ref{def:catch} minus a function negligible in
$\kappa$; the $Q$ draws are independent.
\end{assumption}

The paper asserts that the continuum analysis ``still produces a good
distribution for the discrete case'' (ePrint 2019/226, Section~5.4) and gives
no proof; the hypothesis is named here.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal sampling distribution]\label{thm:optimal}
Let $0<c<1$, let $k\ge 1$ be real and $\delta=c^k$. Suppose that the
suffix $[1-\delta,1]$ is checked in full and that the adversary's valid
weight after any fork point $a$ is at most a $c$ fraction. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $k$ is an integer, for every sampling density $\varphi$,
  $\min_a p_\varphi(a)\le 1/k$;
\item the density $g$ of Construction~\ref{def:density} attains
  $\min_a p_g(a)=1/k$, against every placement of the invalid weight.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(1) Tiling. At the fork points $a_i=1-c^i$, $i=0,\dots,k-1$, the placement
with valid weight last is available to the adversary against every
$\varphi$, and leaves the invalid segment $[1-c^i,1-c^{i+1}]$, of measure
$c^i(1-c)=(1-c)(1-a_i)$, inside $[0,1-\delta]$. These $k$ segments tile
$[0,1-c^k]=[0,1-\delta]$, so
$1\ge\sum_i\int_{[1-c^i,1-c^{i+1}]}\varphi\ge k\min_i p_\varphi(a_i)$. No
monotonicity of $\varphi$ is used. (2) The density $g$ is increasing on
$[0,1-\delta]$, so for $a\le(c-\delta)/c$ Lemma~\ref{lem:best-response}
makes $[a,1-(1-a)c]$ the adversary's best set, whose catch probability is
$1/k$ by Construction~\ref{def:density}; later fork points meet the suffix
and are caught with probability $1$. A script
checks the $k$ tiling masses.
\end{proof}

The theorem is ePrint 2019/226, Theorem~2, pp.~16--17. The paper prints
$c\in(0,1]$, where $\log\delta=0$ at $c=1$ and $g$ is undefined; the domain
here is $0<c<1$. The paper's proof sums over $k+1$ segments, $i=0,\dots,k$,
the last of which lies in the checked suffix; the range is
$i=0,\dots,k-1$. The paper's Lemma~3, a monotonicity lemma, is not needed.

\begin{proposition}[Sample count]\label{prop:sample-count}
Let $k>1$. Then $Q$ independent draws from $g$ all miss an optimally placed
invalid set with probability at most $(1-1/k)^Q$, and
\[
  (1-1/k)^Q\le 2^{-\sigma}
  \iff
  Q\ge\frac{\sigma}{\log_2\bigl(1/(1-1/k)\bigr)} .
\]
At constant difficulty with the last $L=c^kn$ blocks checked,
$k=\log_{1/c}(n/L)$ and the least such $Q$ is $\Theta(\sigma k)$, which is
$O(\sigma\log_{1/c}n)$; for constant $L$ it satisfies
\[
  \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{Q}{\sigma\log_c(1/2)\,\log n}=1 .
\]
Here $\sigma$ is the statistical exponent, not the security parameter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The miss bound is Definition~\ref{def:catch} with
Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal}(2); the equivalence follows on taking logarithms.
Since $\log_2(1/(1-1/k))=1/(k\log 2)+O(1/k^2)$, the least $Q$ satisfies
$Q\sim\sigma k\log 2=\sigma\log_c(1/2)\log(n/L)$, and
$\log(n/L)/\log n\to 1$ for constant $L$. A script cross-checks the least $Q$
against exact rational arithmetic for $2\le k\le 9$ at $\sigma=50$.
\end{proof}

The proposition is ePrint 2019/226, Section~5.4 (``Optimizing the Proof
Size''), p.~17.

\begin{remark}[Domain of the catch parameter]\label{rem:k-domain}
At $k=1$, $\delta=c$: one draw suffices in the continuum model, and
$\log_2(1/(1-1/k))$ is undefined, so Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-count} is
stated for $k>1$. For $k<1$, $\delta>c$ and the checked suffix catches every
adversary; the paper assumes $k\ge 1$ without loss of generality. For
non-integer $k>1$, Construction~\ref{def:density} still gives catch
probability $\log c/\log\delta=1/k$ and hence the sample bound. The tiling
of part~(1) of Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal} needs integral $k$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Length of the checked suffix]\label{rem:suffix}
Under variable difficulty the suffix checked in full is the weight fraction
$\delta$, and a sound instantiation makes it contain at least the last $L$
blocks, as Definition~\ref{def:sampling-map} does
(Remark~\ref{rem:short-forks}). The length $L$ is bounded below by the $L$
of the $(c,L)$ assumption in force and trades against $Q$: a larger $L$
means a larger $\delta$, a smaller $k$ and fewer samples. The asymptotic
size bound holds for constant $L$; within it, $L$ may be chosen numerically.
Which $(c,L)$ holds for Zcash is classified in ``The deployment boundary''
(\S\ref{sec:boundary}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The interactive protocol}\label{sec:protocol-interactive}

\begin{construction}[Interactive FlyClient]\label{def:interactive}
The parties are a verifier $\Ver$ in the setting of
Definition~\ref{def:verifier-setting}, which knows the genesis block $B_0$,
and provers $\Prov_1,\dots,\Prov_m$, at least one of them honest. The
parameters are $c$, $L$, a real $k>1$, $\delta=c^k$ and $Q$. The
ranges are difficulty MMRs (Definition~\ref{def:diff-mmr}) committed by
Construction~\ref{def:chain-commitment}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Each prover sends its head $B_\eta$, whose header records
  $\mathsf{rt}_n.\mathsf{h}$ over $B_0,\dots,B_{\eta-1}$, together with the two
  children of the root tuple (for $n=1$, the single leaf tuple). The
  verifier recomputes the root tuple $\mathsf{rt}$ with $\mathrm{par}$,
  rejects unless $\mathsf{rt}.\mathsf{h}$ equals the recorded value, and
  reads $n=\mathsf{rt}.\mathsf{n}$ and the claimed total work
  $\omega=\mathsf{rt}.\mathsf{w}+D(T_{\mathrm{head}})$, where
  $T_{\mathrm{head}}$ is the target the head declares.
\item If all heads are identical, $\Ver$ accepts that head.
\item Otherwise $\Ver$ orders the claims by claimed total work, heaviest
  first, and for each prover draws $Q$ independent uniforms, maps each to a
  point of cumulative work by Definition~\ref{def:sampling-map}, and
  requests the leaf at each point.
\item For each requested point the prover returns the header, the leaf
  tuple and its inclusion path with full node tuples. The sample must pass
  Construction~\ref{def:per-sample}, whose checks (a) and (d) require a
  proof of work valid at a target that the difficulty-MMR fields declare,
  and Construction~\ref{def:node-checks} on every node of its path,
  including the weight-position check that the drawn point lies in the
  returned leaf's interval.
\item The prover sends the checked suffix of
  Definition~\ref{def:sampling-map}, and $\Ver$ checks it in full: every
  block passes the per-sample and node checks, and consecutive blocks,
  the head included, satisfy the linkage and target conditions of
  Definition~\ref{def:spv-predicate}, the target of each block being the
  one that the field $\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}$ of its predecessor's leaf
  declares.
\item Any failure rejects that prover. Among the surviving claims $\Ver$
  accepts the heaviest, outputting its head and claimed total work; claims
  equivalent under Definition~\ref{def:last-ell} count as one claim.
\end{enumerate}
\end{construction}

The construction is ePrint 2019/226, Algorithms~1 and~2 with Definition~5
and Sections~5.3, 5.4 and~6.1. Step~5 belongs to the protocol by
Sections~5.3 and~5.4 of the paper, although its Algorithm~1 omits it. For
claims of different weight the paper delegates the comparison to the generic
verifier of Kiayias, Miller and Zindros (``Non-interactive proofs of
proof-of-work''), which checks the longest claim first; under variable
difficulty the order is by claimed total work, as in step~3.

\subsection{The non-interactive protocol}\label{sec:protocol-fs}

\begin{construction}[Non-interactive FlyClient]\label{def:noninteractive}
Construction~\ref{def:interactive} is public-coin and one-round: the only
message of $\Ver$ is its sampling coins, which do not depend on the
provers' answers. The non-interactive protocol applies the Fiat--Shamir
transform (\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The Fiat--Shamir transform: from
interactive to non-interactive''), the challenge hashing the instance. Let
$\mathcal{O}$ be a random oracle (\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The random oracle
model''); the uniforms are $U_i=\mathcal{O}(H(\mathrm{hdr}(B_\eta)),i)$,
$i=1,\dots,Q$, read as points of $[0,1)$, seeded by the proof-of-work output
of the head. Through $\mathsf{rt}_n$ the head commits to the whole claimed
chain.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Prover.} It computes $U_1,\dots,U_Q$ from its head and the
  sampled leaves by Definition~\ref{def:sampling-map}, and sends one message:
  the head with the root's children, for each $i$ the sampled header, leaf
  tuple and inclusion path with full node tuples, and the checked suffix.
\item \emph{Verifier.} For each proof received it recomputes
  $U_1,\dots,U_Q$ from the head and runs steps~1 and 4 to~6 of
  Construction~\ref{def:interactive} on the points they determine.
\end{enumerate}
\end{construction}

The paper suggests a standard hash (ePrint 2019/226, Section~6.2) and fixes
no domain separation, no map from hash outputs to points and no proof
encoding. In the classes of Definition~\ref{def:classes} the construction is
designed but unspecified for Zcash.

\begin{assumption}[Head-recomputation budget]\label{ass:recompute-budget}
During one execution the adversary produces at most $R$ heads that carry a
valid proof of work.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} bounds the validly mined weight of a fork, not the
number of head recomputations, and does not imply
Assumption~\ref{ass:recompute-budget}. The paper's budget $R=cn$ (``our
security assumption gives a concrete bound on the number of PoW puzzles the
adversary can solve, which is $cn$'', ePrint 2019/226, Section~6.2) is
asserted, not derived.

% compute/sampling.py:
% [sec:protocol-fs] grinding threshold: R (1 - 1/k)^Q < 2^-sigma iff
%   (1 - 1/k)^Q < 2^-sigma / R iff Q > (sigma + log2 R)/log2(1/(1 - 1/k))
\begin{proposition}[Grinding bound]\label{prop:grinding}
Assume Assumptions~\ref{ass:recompute-budget}, \ref{ass:discretisation},
\ref{ass:rewriting} and~\ref{ass:cl}, let $\mathcal{O}$ be a random oracle,
let $H$ be the random oracle of Definition~\ref{def:backbone}, with $\kappa$-bit
outputs, and let $k>1$. Let at most $q_{\mathcal{O}}$ queries to $\mathcal{O}$
and $q_H$ queries to $H$ be made. The probability that an adversary makes
Construction~\ref{def:noninteractive} accept a claimed chain whose fork is
not caught is at most
\[
  R\,(1-1/k)^Q+q_{\mathcal{O}}\,q_H\,2^{-\kappa}
  +\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{col}}_H(\mathcal{B})+\negl(\kappa)
\]
for an explicit reduction $\mathcal{B}$. Hence
\[
  Q>\frac{\sigma+\log_2 R}{\log_2\bigl(1/(1-1/k)\bigr)}
\]
suffices for failure probability below $2^{-\sigma}$ plus negligible terms.
This $Q$ is $\Theta((\sigma+\log R)\,k)$ and, at constant difficulty with
constant $L$, $\Theta((\sigma+\log R)\log_{1/c}n)$; it exceeds the interactive
count of Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-count} by the $\log_2 R$ bits of grinding.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each head with a valid proof of work fixes, through $\mathsf{rt}_n$, the
claimed chain and its invalid set before its challenge
$\mathcal{O}(H(\mathrm{hdr}(B_\eta)),\cdot)$ is known, barring a collision of
$H$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}). The challenge of a header cannot be
computed before $H(\mathrm{hdr})$ is queried, and that same query decides
validity. Since $\mathcal{O}$ is independent of $H$, the challenges of the at
most $R$ valid heads are uniform and independent of which heads are valid,
except when $\mathcal{O}$ was queried at a value before it arose as an
$H$-output, which has probability at most $q_{\mathcal{O}}\,q_H\,2^{-\kappa}$.
So by Assumption~\ref{ass:discretisation} and Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal} the $Q$
samples of each valid head all miss with probability at most $(1-1/k)^Q$ plus a
negligible
term. A head without a valid proof of work fails step~5 of
Construction~\ref{def:interactive}. By
Assumption~\ref{ass:recompute-budget} at most $R$ heads qualify, and the
union bound (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``The union bound and a birthday
calculation'') gives the bound. The threshold follows on taking logarithms,
and its order as in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-count}.
\end{proof}

The bound is proved here because the \emph{Crypto Guide} proves Fiat--Shamir
security only for Sigma protocols (\S``The Fiat--Shamir transform: from
interactive to non-interactive''); the argument above uses only that the
challenge is a random-oracle image of the proof-of-work output of a header
committing to the whole instance. Seeded by the header itself, the challenge
could be evaluated before the proof of work is attempted, and an adversary
could mine only heads whose samples miss.

\begin{proposition}[Transferability]\label{prop:transferability}
A proof of Construction~\ref{def:noninteractive} is publicly verifiable: its
verification uses only the proof, the genesis block and the public oracles,
so any party can relay it unchanged, and it convinces every verifier that
the original convinces. For a given head the sampled points are
deterministic; under a canonical proof encoding there is therefore one
proof per head, which one party can compute for every verifier.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The verifier's decision is a deterministic function of the proof and public
data, and the points are a function of the head.
\end{proof}

The paper's statement that ``there only exists a single valid
non-interactive proof'' (ePrint 2019/226, Section~8.1) holds only under a
canonical encoding, which no ZIP specifies. ZIP 221, ``Security and Privacy
Considerations'' records that, because FlyClient proofs are non-interactive
and publicly verifiable, ``they could be shared among many light clients
after the initial server interaction''.

\subsection{The security theorem}\label{sec:protocol-theorem}

\begin{theorem}[FlyClient security and succinctness]\label{thm:flyclient}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $0<c<1$, a real $k>1$ and $\delta=c^k$;
\item the variable-difficulty backbone model
  (Definition~\ref{def:backbone}), in which every adversary is a
  $(c,L)$-adversary (Assumption~\ref{ass:cl});
\item $H$ collision resistant and, as in Definition~\ref{def:backbone}, a
  random oracle, and the sampling oracle $\mathcal{O}$ a random oracle;
\item Assumptions~\ref{ass:recompute-budget}, \ref{ass:discretisation},
  \ref{ass:rewriting} and~\ref{ass:persistence};
\item a verifier that knows only the genesis block, and at least one honest
  prover (Definition~\ref{def:verifier-setting});
\item no block header equals $\mathrm{ser}(\ell)\,\|\,\mathrm{ser}(r)$ for
  node tuples $\ell,r$ (leaf--internal separation, hypothesis~2 of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:no-honest}).
\end{enumerate}
Then an adversary wins the game of Definition~\ref{def:security} against
Construction~\ref{def:noninteractive} with probability at most
$R\,(1-1/k)^Q$ plus negligible terms, which is at most $2^{-\sigma}$ plus
negligible terms when $Q$ satisfies Proposition~\ref{prop:grinding}.
Proofs have
\[
  O\bigl(L_\delta+(\sigma+\log R)\,k\log n\bigr)
\]
headers and hashes, where $L_\delta$ is the number of blocks of the checked
suffix. The construction is succinct (Definition~\ref{def:succinct})
whenever $L_\delta$, $k$ and $\log R$ are $O(\mathrm{polylog}(n))$, in
particular at constant difficulty with constant $L$ and $\delta=L/n$, where
$L_\delta=L$ and $k=\log_{1/c}(n/L)$; there, with the paper's $R=cn$, the
size is $O(L+(\sigma+\log n)\log_{1/c}(n)\log n)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof structure]
The proof composes the obligations O1 to O6 of
Remark~\ref{rem:proof-obligations}, following the five steps of ePrint
2019/226, Section~6.3. (0) The honest prover's proof passes every check: its
labelling is consistent, its transitions are clamped, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}(2) makes each weight interval its true
prefix weight. (1) Assumption~\ref{ass:rewriting} replaces an adversary with
invalid transitions by one that respects the retarget rule, to which
Assumption~\ref{ass:cl} applies (O3). (2) O1 pins every sampled answer to
one committed chain; O2 makes every block after the fork point adversarial,
or places a leaf of the fork in its invalid set, and makes its validly mined
weight post-fork; O3 binds each sampled leaf's weight
interval to the root, while the agreement of that interval with the chain's
true prefix weight rests on the node checks and
Assumption~\ref{ass:rewriting}. (3) O4: a fork heavier than the honest
chain and longer than $L$ blocks is caught by one draw with probability at
least $1/k$, and a fork within the checked suffix with certainty. (4) O5:
the Fiat--Shamir step with the grinding bound. (5) O6: the size count.
Agreement of honest full nodes on the prefix up to the last $\ell$ blocks,
the second clause of Definition~\ref{def:security}, is
Assumption~\ref{ass:persistence}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is ePrint 2019/226, Theorem~1 (p.~10, restated p.~20); ZIP 221,
``Security and Privacy Considerations'' restates its honest-connection and
genesis hypotheses. The paper states the size as
$O(L+\lambda\log_{1/c}(n)\log n)$. That equals the constant-difficulty bound
above with $R=cn$ only when $\log n=O(\lambda)$, for instance for $n$ polynomial
in
$\lambda$; under the convention that $n$ grows with $\sigma$ fixed, the
theorem states the $\log R$ form and no stronger bound.

\begin{remark}[Proof obligations]\label{rem:proof-obligations}
Each obligation is listed with the results that discharge it.
\begin{enumerate}[label=O\arabic*]
\item Binding: Theorem~\ref{thm:position-binding},
  Corollary~\ref{cor:prefix-binding}, Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding} and
  Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}.
\item Fork structure: Lemmas~\ref{lem:no-honest} and~\ref{lem:no-reuse}.
\item Weight binding and transitions: Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-binding},
  Construction~\ref{def:node-checks} and, for subtrees whose aggregates are
  inconsistent, Assumption~\ref{ass:rewriting}.
\item Catch probability and sample count: Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal},
  Definition~\ref{def:sampling-map}, Assumption~\ref{ass:discretisation} and
  Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-count}.
\item Grinding: Proposition~\ref{prop:grinding}, which fixes the
  non-interactive $Q$.
\item Size: Corollary~\ref{cor:proof-size}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Proof size]\label{cor:proof-size}
For the interactive protocol with $Q$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-count}, constant $L$ and constant difficulty, a
proof has size $O(\sigma\log n\log_{1/c}n+L)$: $Q=\Theta(\sigma
\log_{1/c}n)$ samples, each a header and a path of at most
$\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ nodes under $\mathrm{Bag}_R$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}), and the $L$ suffix blocks. For the
non-interactive protocol, with $Q$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:grinding},
$\sigma$ is replaced by $\sigma+\log_2 R$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Multiply $Q$ by the cost of one sample. The suffix blocks form a contiguous
run of leaves ending at leaf $n$, so the union of their paths has
$O(L+\log n)$ nodes and is sent once.
\end{proof}

The corollary is ePrint 2019/226, Corollary~2 (Section~5.4), which states
$\Theta$; the matching lower bound is not proved here.

\begin{remark}[Status of the security theorem]\label{rem:theorem-status}
Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient} is the paper's conditional claim, not an
independently completed proof. Deriving the $(c,L)$ assumption from the
backbone parameters is open (Remark~\ref{rem:cl-status}); the paper's
Lemma~1 is invalid as printed (Remark~\ref{rem:lemma1}); its Lemma~4 is a
sketch with recorded gaps and enters as Assumption~\ref{ass:rewriting}; the
step from the continuum to blocks is Assumption~\ref{ass:discretisation};
and the budget $R=cn$ is asserted. Proved in this volume are binding (O1),
fork structure (O2) under leaf--internal separation, the binding of sampled
weight intervals to the root,
optimality, the sample count and the grinding bound.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Verification after acceptance}\label{sec:protocol-repeat}

\begin{proposition}[Inclusion after acceptance]\label{prop:inclusion-after}
Let a head $B_\eta$ with root tuple $\mathsf{rt}_n$ be accepted. Membership of
the block at height $j-1$ in the accepted chain is established by one
inclusion path of leaf $j$ against $\mathsf{rt}_n$, and membership of a
transaction in that block by one further Merkle path against the transaction
root its header carries; no intermediate header is needed. Both are binding
barring a collision.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the block, Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}(i); for the transaction
path, \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Soundness: forging a path implies a
collision''.
\end{proof}

The proposition is ePrint 2019/226, Sections~1 and~4.2.

\begin{proposition}[Verification against a fresh head]\label{prop:fresh-head}
A client may run the protocol against the freshest heads and store one
recent block $B^*$ of its previously accepted chain lying more than $2\ell$
blocks below that chain's head. Let the height of $B^*$ be less than that of
the newly accepted head minus $\ell$. Under the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient}, except with its soundness error, the accepted
head's committed range contains $B^*$ at its height, which the client checks
by one inclusion path (Proposition~\ref{prop:inclusion-after}); a head is
rejected if a sample exposes an invalid block.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient} both accepted chains are equivalent up to
their last $\ell$ blocks to chains of honest full nodes. A head $B_\eta$
commits to heights $0$ to $\eta-1$, so clause~(b) of
Definition~\ref{def:security} at the earlier round places $B^*$ in every
honest chain of that round with its last $\ell$ blocks removed, and
Assumption~\ref{ass:persistence} places it in every later honest chain.
The height of $B^*$ is below that of the new head minus $\ell$, so $B^*$
lies in the part of the new committed chain that clause~(b) makes a prefix
of an honest chain.
\end{proof}

The paper states without qualification that the range of a fresh head
``cannot contain invalid blocks and it must contain all stable blocks''
(ePrint 2019/226, Section~4.2, ``Unstable Blocks''); that sentence is not
carried.

\begin{proposition}[Subchain proofs, the paper's claim]\label{thm:subchain}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient}. Suppose a verifier
accepted a valid proof for a chain with head $B_\eta$ when the honest
chain had that head, and later, when the honest chain has head $B_{\eta'}$,
$\eta'>\eta$, receives a subproof for the blocks from $B_\eta$ to
$B_{\eta'}$, sampled as an instance of the protocol with $B_\eta$ in place of
genesis, together with one inclusion path showing that $B_\eta$ lies in the
range committed by $B_{\eta'}$. Then, except with the soundness error of
Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient} for the instance from $B_\eta$, it accepts no
chain that a verifier given a full proof for height $\eta'$ would reject. The
instance from $B_\eta$, which samples only leaves after $B_\eta$ of a range
committed from $B_0$, is the paper's design (ePrint 2019/226, Section~8.2)
and is not constructed here.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
This is ePrint 2019/226, Theorem~3 (Section~8.2), with the paper's proof
idea. A fork after $B_\eta$ faces a fresh instance of the protocol with
$B_\eta$ as genesis. A fork before $B_\eta$ fails the inclusion path, since
the verifier's $B_\eta$ is not in the adversary's range, barring a
collision (Proposition~\ref{prop:inclusion-after}).
\end{proof}

The proposition concerns one commitment range from the genesis block.

\section{The chain-history commitment}\label{sec:history}

This section states the commitment that Zcash block headers carry, each rule
cited to the ZIP or the protocol specification section that fixes it, and
each object before its first use: the upgrade epochs over which it ranges,
the node vector of its Merkle mountain range, the hashing of the tree and its
root commitment, the authorising-data root and the block commitment hash of
ZIP 244 that wraps the root from NU5, the header consensus rules, and the
range of blocks to which each header commits.

\subsection{The deployed chain commitment}\label{sec:history-deployed}

ZIP 221 (Final), ``FlyClient - Consensus-Layer Changes'', changes the
semantics of the block header and the consensus rules from Heartwood. The
$32$-byte header field that held the root of the Sapling note commitment tree
instead commits to the root of a Merkle mountain range
(Definition~\ref{def:mmr}; ZIP 221, ``Terminology'': a binary hash tree that
admits appends of new leaves ``without changing the value of existing
nodes'') whose nodes carry aggregated metadata. Its leaves are the blocks from
the last activation height below the committing block up to the parent of
that block: the \emph{epoch tree} of Definition~\ref{def:epoch-tree}. ZIP 221
thus deploys the invariant of Construction~\ref{def:chain-commitment}
restricted to one \emph{upgrade epoch}, the heights from one activation
height up to the next (Definition~\ref{def:upgrade-epoch}), and not from the
genesis block. The tree and its node fields $1$ to $14$ are ZIP 221's; fields
$15$ to $17$ are ZIP 258's (Draft, in force from NU6.3 by
Definition~\ref{def:zip-status}); and from NU5 the root is committed as the
first input of the \emph{block commitment hash}, a personalised hash of the
root together with the block's authorising-data root (ZIP 244; ``The block
commitment hash'', \S\ref{sec:history-blockcommit}). Seven node fields serve
reasoning about difficulty without matching the node of
Definition~\ref{def:diff-mmr} (ePrint 2019/226, Definition~7) field for field
(Remark~\ref{rem:flyclient-fields}). Each term of this paragraph carries here
only its defining clause; its definition is in the subsection named.

The purpose of the commitment, after ZIP 221, ``Motivation'', is to let a
light client verify, with a number of headers logarithmic rather than linear
in the chain length and correctly with high probability, the validity of a
chain received from a full node, the inclusion of a block in it, and metadata
of any block or range of blocks; the commitment also admits subtree proofs,
which check only blocks later than the most recently verified one.
Transaction inclusion within a block is left to ZIP 307 (Draft). No claim of
this paragraph is proved in this section; the protocol that would realise it
is that of ``The FlyClient protocol'' (\S\ref{sec:protocol}), and its
relation to the deployed commitment is classified in ``The deployment
boundary'' (\S\ref{sec:boundary}).

\subsection{Upgrade epochs and epoch trees}\label{sec:history-epochs}

\begin{definition}[Upgrade epoch]\label{def:upgrade-epoch}
Let $0 = A_0 < A_1 < A_2 < \cdots$ be the distinct activation heights of the
network upgrades of a chain (ZIP 200, ``Activation mechanism''), height $0$
counted as an activation height (ZIP 221, ``Specification''). The named
upgrades and their order are those of Definition~\ref{def:upgrades}; the list
of activation heights includes those of upgrades that definition does not
name. The \emph{upgrade epoch} beginning at $A_i$ is the interval of heights
$[A_i, A_{i+1})$, unbounded for the last activation height. For $i \ge 1$ its
blocks are validated under one consensus rule set and one \emph{consensus
branch ID}, the $32$-bit identifier of the last upgrade that activates at
$A_i$ (ZIP 200, ``Specification''; \emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``The
transaction format''). The upgrade epoch is distinct, by name and by object,
from the retarget epoch of Definition~\ref{def:retarget}; the word ``epoch''
is not used alone. The Heartwood activation height is written $A_H$, and
$B_{A_H}$ is the Heartwood activation block.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Epoch tree]\label{def:epoch-tree}
For a block height $\eta > 0$, the \emph{preceding activation height}
$\alpha(\eta)$ is the largest activation height $A_i < \eta$, the
``preceding network upgrade height'' of ZIP 221, ``Specification'';
$\alpha(0)$ is undefined. For $\eta > A_H$ the \emph{epoch tree}
$\mathcal{T}_\eta$ is the Merkle mountain range $M^L_n$ under the left fold
$\mathrm{Bag}_L$ (Definition~\ref{def:bagging}), bagging nodes included as
internal nodes, with the
\[
  n = \eta - \alpha(\eta) \ge 1
\]
leaves $B_{\alpha(\eta)}, \dots, B_{\eta-1}$ in height order: leaf $j$ is
$B_{\alpha(\eta)+j-1}$. Since $\alpha(\eta)$ is the last activation height
below $\eta$, all its leaves lie in the one upgrade epoch beginning at
$\alpha(\eta)$. Its node contents are fixed in ``The node vector''
(\S\ref{sec:history-nodes}) and its hashing in ``The epoch tree and its root
commitment'' (\S\ref{sec:history-tree}). No epoch tree is defined for
$\eta \le A_H$.
\end{definition}

Which header commits to $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ is not part of
Definition~\ref{def:epoch-tree}: it is Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}
in ``The header consensus rules'' (\S\ref{sec:history-rules}), and its
consequences for the committed ranges are stated in ``The committed range''
(\S\ref{sec:history-range}).

\subsection{The node vector}\label{sec:history-nodes}

\begin{definition}[Node vector]\label{def:node-vector}
The epoch tree $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ is an instance of
Definition~\ref{def:aggregating} under $\mathrm{Bag}_L$. Every node $v$ of
it, leaf, internal node, bagging node or root, carries one \emph{node
vector} of fields, serialised canonically by the map $\mathrm{ser}$ of
Definition~\ref{def:field-vector}; that serialisation is used whenever a
parent commitment is formed. The fields are numbered as in ZIP 221, ``Tree
Node specification''. For a block $B_{\eta'}$ with header $\mathrm{hdr}'$, the
\emph{leaf map} sets both members of each earliest/latest pair from
$B_{\eta'}$ itself:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),
  leftmargin=\widthof{(15)--(17)}+\labelsep]
\item field $\mathsf{hashSubtreeCommitment}$ is
  $\mathsf{BlockHash}(\mathrm{hdr}')$ (Definition~\ref{def:header}), in internal
  byte order and unpersonalised;
\item[(2), (3)] fields $\mathsf{nEarliestTimestamp}$ and
  $\mathsf{nLatestTimestamp}$ are the field $\mathsf{nTime}$ of $\mathrm{hdr}'$;
\item[(4), (5)] fields $\mathsf{nEarliestTargetBits}$ and
  $\mathsf{nLatestTargetBits}$ are the field $\mathsf{nBits}$ of $\mathrm{hdr}'$
  (the semantics of $\mathsf{nTime}$ and $\mathsf{nBits}$: \emph{Consensus
  Guide}, \S``Difficulty in motion'');
\item[(6), (7)] fields $\mathsf{hashEarliestSaplingRoot}$ and
  $\mathsf{hashLatestSaplingRoot}$ are $\mathsf{LEBS2OSP}_{256}(\mathsf{rt})$
  for $\mathsf{rt}$ the root of the Sapling note commitment tree of the
  final Sapling treestate of $B_{\eta'}$ (protocol specification,
  \S``Block Header Encoding and Consensus''), an opaque $32$-byte value in
  this volume;
\item[(8)] field $\mathsf{nSubTreeTotalWork}$ is the work
  \[
  \lfloor 2^{256} / (\mathsf{ToTarget}(\mathsf{nBits}) + 1) \rfloor
  \]
  of $B_{\eta'}$ (\emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``Work, and the best chain'',
  Definition ``Work of a block'');
\item[(9), (10)] fields $\mathsf{nEarliestHeight}$ and
  $\mathsf{nLatestHeight}$ are the height $\eta'$;
\item[(11)] field $\mathsf{nSaplingTxCount}$ is the number of transactions of
  $B_{\eta'}$ in which $\mathsf{vSpendsSapling}$ or $\mathsf{vOutputsSapling}$
  is non-empty;
\item[(12), (13)] fields $\mathsf{hashEarliestOrchardRoot}$ and
  $\mathsf{hashLatestOrchardRoot}$ are the Orchard-pool anchor of
  $B_{\eta'}$, the root of the Orchard pool's note commitment tree in the
  final treestate of $B_{\eta'}$ (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Anchors'',
  Definition ``Treestate and anchor'');
\item[(14)] field $\mathsf{nOrchardTxCount}$ is the number of transactions of
  $B_{\eta'}$ in which $\mathsf{vActionsOrchard}$ is non-empty
  (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``The transaction format'');
\item[(15)--(17)] fields $\mathsf{hashEarliestIronwoodRoot}$,
  $\mathsf{hashLatestIronwoodRoot}$ and $\mathsf{nIronwoodTxCount}$ are the
  Ironwood-pool anchor of $B_{\eta'}$ and the number of its transactions in
  which $\mathsf{vActionsIronwood}$ is non-empty, computed as fields $12$ to
  $14$.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{parent rule}: an internal node with left child $\ell$ and right
child $r$ takes each earliest field ($2$, $4$, $6$, $9$, $12$, $15$) from
$\ell$ and each latest field ($3$, $5$, $7$, $10$, $13$, $16$) from $r$;
it sums the additive fields, field $8$ modulo $2^{256}$ and the per-pool
counts $11$, $14$ and $17$ in $\Z$; and it sets field $1$ by
Construction~\ref{def:history-tree}, the personalised hash of
$\mathrm{ser}(\ell) \,\|\, \mathrm{ser}(r)$. Fields $12$ to $14$ are
ZIP 221's own, added at NU5 (ZIP 252). Fields $15$ to $17$ are ZIP 258's,
``Changes to ZIP 221'' (Draft), whose rules apply from NU6.3 activation
(Definition~\ref{def:zip-status}).
\end{definition}

For field $8$, ZIP 221, ``Tree Node specification'', states that
computations modulo $2^{256}$ are fine because cumulative chain work is
assumed to be at most $2^{256}$, an aggregate effort of $2^{256}$ or more

field of a node is the integer sum of the work of its leaves

\begin{remark}[The FlyClient fields]\label{rem:flyclient-fields}
ZIP 221, ``FlyClient Requirements and Recommendations'', designates seven
fields, $2$ to $5$ and $8$ to $10$, which ``enable FlyClient in the
variable-difficulty model'' by letting a light client reason about the
application of the difficulty adjustment over a range of blocks. They serve
the purpose of Definition~\ref{def:diff-mmr} without matching it field for
field. The leaf count of a node, the field $\mathsf{n}$ of the difficulty
MMR, is $\mathsf{nLatestHeight} - \mathsf{nEarliestHeight} + 1$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:leaf-count}). The field $\mathsf{w}$ of the difficulty MMR
sums difficulty levels (Definition~\ref{def:difficulty-level}), whereas
$\mathsf{nSubTreeTotalWork}$ sums work, a different normalisation. The field
$\mathsf{nEarliestTargetBits}$ is a compact target, ordered inversely to
$\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{start}}$. No field carries the elapsed time
$\mathsf{t}$; the timestamps of the first and last block stand in its place.
No field carries $\mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{next}}$: the next target of a Zcash
block is a function of an averaging window of preceding blocks
(\emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``Difficulty in motion''), which the aggregates of
a node do not determine.

Fields $6$, $7$ and $11$ to $14$ are ZIP 221's ``Non-FlyClient
Commitments'', chosen ``primarily to improve light client security
guarantees'', with no counterpart in ePrint 2019/226. Field $1$ ``forms the
structure of the commitment tree'' (ZIP 221, ``Tree nodes''), and fields
$15$ to $17$ are the ZIP 258 counterparts for the Ironwood pool. The
earliest roots let a client check the treestate transitions over a range of
blocks without recomputing the roots
from genesis. ZIP 221, ``Non-FlyClient Commitments'', states that
$\mathsf{hashLatestSaplingRoot}$ serves the purpose of the former header
field $\mathsf{hashFinalSaplingRoot}$, one block delayed
(Proposition~\ref{prop:delay}). The per-pool counts follow the rule of the
same section that a new shielded pool adds a count of its transactions,
applied at NU5 (fields $12$ to $14$) and at NU6.3 (fields $15$ to $17$,
ZIP 258).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Field vector by upgrade epoch]\label{def:field-vector}
The fields are encoded as follows: a char[32] field as its $32$ bytes; a
uint32 or uint256 field little-endian, as $\mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{32}$ or
$\mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{256}$ (protocol specification, \S``Integers, Bit
Sequences, and Endianness''); a height or count as its CompactSize encoding
in minimal form (Definition~\ref{def:compactsize}); and each pool-root field
as $\mathsf{LEBS2OSP}_{256}$ of the root, as the specification encodes the
Orchard anchor (protocol specification, \S``Transaction Encoding and
Consensus''). For a node $v$, $\mathrm{ser}(v)$ is the concatenation of the
encodings of its fields in field order. The \emph{field vector} of an
upgrade epoch is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item fields $1$ to $11$ in the Heartwood and Canopy upgrade epochs;
\item fields $1$ to $14$ in the NU5, NU6, NU6.1 and NU6.2 upgrade epochs;
\item fields $1$ to $17$ in the NU6.3 and NU7 upgrade epochs.
\end{enumerate}
The vectors are named by their fields. ZIP 221, ``Tree Node
specification'', omits fields $12$ to $14$ ``before NU5 activation'', and
ZIP 258, ``Changes to ZIP 221'', omits fields $15$ to $17$ ``before NU6.3
activation''; neither says whether the height of a leaf's block or that of
the committing block governs, and the two differ only for the tree committed
by an activation block. \emph{Adopted reading}, resolving that ambiguity:
every node of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ uses the field vector of the upgrade epoch
beginning at $\alpha(\eta)$, the upgrade epoch of its leaves.
\end{definition}

The reading is adopted for the following reason. Under the other reading the
tree $\mathcal{T}_A$ committed by $B_A$, at an activation height $A$ that adds
fields, would serialise every node of the previous upgrade epoch with the
added fields; every internal node would change value, and $\mathcal{T}_A$
would not be an append of $\mathcal{T}_{A-1}$, contrary to the property by
which ZIP 221, ``Terminology'', defines a Merkle mountain range. The ZIP's
pseudocode, which takes a leaf's Orchard fields from the leaf's own block and
serialises them only when present, is consistent with the reading. Two
consequences rest on it: $\mathcal{T}_A$ uses the previous upgrade epoch's
vector, and $\mathcal{T}_{A+1}$ is the first tree with the new one; under the
other reading $\mathcal{T}_A$ would carry the new vector. For NU7, no ZIP among
the sources of NU7 consensus changes listed in ZIP 259 (Draft), ``NU7 consensus
changes'', amends ZIP 221 or ZIP 244, so the nodes of the trees of the NU7
upgrade epoch carry fields $1$ to $17$ under either reading.

The fixed-width fields occupy $144$, $208$ and $272$ bytes in the three
vectors, and the three, four and five CompactSize fields $1$ to $9$ bytes
each, so a serialised node has $147$ to $171$, $212$ to $244$ and $277$ to
$317$ bytes, the ranges that ZIP 221 and ZIP 258 state. A toy chain computed
by script illustrates the reading, with an
activation block that commits a tree of the previous vector and the next
block the first to commit a tree of the new one; it establishes nothing.

% compute/mmr.py:
% [sec:history-nodes] every node of the Bag_L tree over consecutive heights,
%   1 <= n <= 1024: leaf count = nLatestHeight - nEarliestHeight + 1; a power
%   of two exactly at perfect-subtree nodes, never at bagging nodes
\begin{lemma}[Leaf count and shape]\label{lem:leaf-count}
Every node $v$ of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$, bagging nodes included, spans a run of
consecutive leaves $B_{\eta_1}, \dots, B_{\eta_2}$ with
$v.\mathsf{nEarliestHeight} = \eta_1$ and
$v.\mathsf{nLatestHeight} = \eta_2$, so its leaf count is
$\mathsf{nLatestHeight} - \mathsf{nEarliestHeight} + 1$. The node $v$ roots
a perfect subtree of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ if and only if that count is a power
of two. In particular a leaf is the only kind of node with count $1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the parent rule of Definition~\ref{def:node-vector}. A leaf
has both heights equal to its block's. An internal node inherits
$\mathsf{nEarliestHeight}$ from its left child and $\mathsf{nLatestHeight}$
from its right child, and in a range under $\mathrm{Bag}_L$ the run of the
left child immediately precedes that of the right child, the heights of the
leaves of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ being consecutive by
Definition~\ref{def:epoch-tree}. A perfect subtree of altitude $a$ spans
$2^a$ leaves (Definition~\ref{def:mmr}), and every node inside a peak roots a
perfect subtree. A bagging node of the left fold spans the peaks
$P_1, \dots, P_i$ for some $i \ge 2$ (Definition~\ref{def:bagging}), whose
sizes are distinct powers of two, set bits of $n$
(Definition~\ref{def:peaks}); a sum of two or more distinct powers of two has
at least two set bits and is not a power of two. An internal node spans at
least two leaves. A script checks the statement for
$1 \le n \le 1{,}024$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Leaves and header linkage]\label{lem:leaf-prev}
For $\alpha(\eta) \le \eta' \le \eta - 1$, field $1$ of the leaf of
$\mathcal{T}_\eta$ for $B_{\eta'}$ equals the field $\mathsf{hashPrevBlock}$
of the header of $B_{\eta'+1}$, which for $\eta' = \eta - 1$ is the
committing block $B_\eta$. Consequently a received header is matched to its
leaf by computing its block hash and comparing it with field $1$, and the
leaves add no hashing beyond the block hashes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:node-vector}, field $1$ of the leaf is
$\mathsf{BlockHash}$ of the header of $B_{\eta'}$ in internal byte order; by
Definition~\ref{def:header}, the field $\mathsf{hashPrevBlock}$ of
$B_{\eta'+1}$ is that same hash. ZIP 221, ``Tree Node specification'',
states the identity.
\end{proof}

% compute/history.py:
% [sec:history-nodes] median-time-past never decreases and rises by >= 1 s
%   every 11 blocks; blocks >= 11 * 5399 = 59389 heights apart have
%   non-strictly ordered timestamps, >= 11 * 5400 = 59400 strictly ordered
\begin{remark}[Non-monotone timestamps]\label{rem:timestamps}
Consensus bounds the field $\mathsf{nTime}$ of a block below, strictly above
the median of the timestamps of the preceding eleven blocks, and above, by
that median plus $90$ minutes (protocol specification, \S``Block Header
Encoding and Consensus''; \emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``Difficulty in
motion''). It orders two timestamps only at a distance: the
median-time-past never decreases and rises by at least one second every
eleven blocks, so a block at least $11 \cdot 5{,}400 = 59{,}400$ heights
after another has the strictly later timestamp. By the same count,
$\mathsf{nLatestTimestamp} \ge \mathsf{nEarliestTimestamp}$ holds for every node
with $\mathsf{nLatestHeight} - \mathsf{nEarliestHeight} \ge 11 \cdot 5{,}399 =
59{,}389$, and for a leaf, whose two timestamps are equal; for smaller spans
this argument gives no order. ZIP 221, ``Tree Node specification'', says that
the reverse order ``may occur within subtrees smaller than
$\mathsf{PoWMedianBlockSpan}$ blocks''; this is an
example, not a bound. A block whose timestamp lies up to $90$ minutes ahead
of the median exceeds the timestamps of the later blocks whose timestamps
track real time, until real time passes it, and $90$ minutes spans more than
eleven target spacings (protocol specification, \S``Constants''; from NU7,
ZIP 218).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The epoch tree and its root commitment}\label{sec:history-tree}

The tree is defined from the prose of ZIP 221, ``Background'',
``Specification'', ``Tree Node specification'' and ``Block header semantics
and consensus rules'', with ZIP 258, ``Changes to ZIP 221''. That prose is
ambiguous at two points, both at an activation block: the field vector,
settled by the adopted reading of Definition~\ref{def:field-vector}, and the
branch ID of the personalisation, settled by
Definition~\ref{def:tree-branch}. The pseudocode of ZIP 221 is cited only as
corroboration of the two readings; it carries editorial defects, among them
in the serialisation of the Orchard fields, none of which bears on either
reading.

\begin{definition}[Branch ID of a tree]\label{def:tree-branch}
ZIP 221, ``Tree Node specification'', states that the personalisation
encodes ``the consensus branch ID for the epoch of the block containing the
commitment'', and continues: ``Which is to say, each node in the tree
commits to the consensus branch that produced it.'' The two sentences agree
at every $\eta > A_H$ that is not an activation height, since $B_\eta$ and
the leaves of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ then lie in one upgrade epoch. At an
activation height $A > A_H$ the first names the upgrade epoch beginning at
$A$ and the second the upgrade epoch of the leaves
$B_{\alpha(A)}, \dots, B_{A-1}$. At $A_H$ no epoch tree is defined
(Definition~\ref{def:epoch-tree}). \emph{Adopted reading}, resolving an
ambiguity of specified text: the branch ID $b(\mathcal{T})$ of an epoch tree
$\mathcal{T}$ is the consensus branch ID of the upgrade epoch whose blocks
are the leaves of $\mathcal{T}$, and every internal node and the root
commitment of $\mathcal{T}$ are hashed under it.
\end{definition}

The reading is adopted for three reasons. (1) ``Which is to say'' introduces
the second sentence as a restatement of the first, and it is the sentence
that assigns a branch ID to each node. (2) Under the first reading the tree
committed by $B_A$ would not be an append of the tree committed by
$B_{A-1}$, every internal node changing value, contrary to the property by
which ZIP 221, ``Terminology'', defines a Merkle mountain range (the reason
for which Definition~\ref{def:field-vector} adopts the leaves' vector); and
the whole previous upgrade epoch would be rehashed in one block, the cost
that ZIP 221, ``Header Format Change'', calls impractical and avoids by a
special case at the Heartwood activation block and at no later one. (3) The
pseudocode of ZIP 221 hashes each parent under the branch ID of its left
child and the root commitment under that of the root node, each leaf
carrying the branch ID of its own block. Every later statement about the
tree committed by an activation block rests on this reading and cites it;
under the first reading each holds with the branch ID of the new upgrade
epoch in place of $b(\mathcal{T}_A)$.

\begin{construction}[Hashing of the epoch tree]\label{def:history-tree}
Let $\mathsf{h}(P, x)$ be BLAKE2b-256 with the $16$-byte personalisation $P$
on input $x$ (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Transaction digests and
signatures''; \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Domain separation and
personalisation''), and let $\beta$ be the $4$-byte little-endian encoding
of $b(\mathcal{T}_\eta)$. The personalisation is the $12$ ASCII bytes
$\texttt{ZcashHistory}$ followed by $\beta$, $16$ bytes in all. The tree
$\mathcal{T}_\eta$ is the range $M^L_n$ whose nodes are node vectors
(Definition~\ref{def:node-vector}) under the field vector of
Definition~\ref{def:field-vector}. Its bagging nodes, which join the two
leftmost remaining peaks until one remains,
\[
  \bigl(\cdots\bigl((P_1 \,\|\, P_2) \,\|\, P_3\bigr) \cdots\bigr)
  \,\|\, P_d ,
\]
are ordinary internal nodes, and a single peak is the root node. For an
internal node with children $\ell$ and $r$, field $1$ is
\[
  \mathsf{h}\bigl(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta,\
    \mathrm{ser}(\ell) \,\|\, \mathrm{ser}(r)\bigr),
\]
over the children's full serialisations; its other fields follow the parent
rule of Definition~\ref{def:node-vector}. The branch ID is not a node field:
it enters only through the personalisation, and whoever recomputes a node
hash supplies $b(\mathcal{T}_\eta)$ by Definition~\ref{def:tree-branch}, the
upgrade epoch being fixed by the node's heights. For the Heartwood branch ID
$\mathtt{0xF5B9230B}$ the last four bytes of the personalisation are
$\mathtt{0B}\ \mathtt{23}\ \mathtt{B9}\ \mathtt{F5}$.
\end{construction}

\begin{definition}[Chain-history root]\label{def:history-root}
For $\eta > A_H$ the \emph{chain-history root} of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ is
\[
  \mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}(\mathcal{T}_\eta) :=
  \mathsf{h}\bigl(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta,\
    \mathrm{ser}(\text{root node of } \mathcal{T}_\eta)\bigr),
\]
with $\beta$ encoding $b(\mathcal{T}_\eta)$ (ZIP 221, ``Tree Node
specification''). It is not field $1$ of the root node: the committed value
covers the root's aggregates, namely the total work modulo $2^{256}$, the
first and last timestamps, target bits and heights, the boundary pool roots
and the per-pool counts. When $\alpha(\eta) = \eta - 1$ the tree has one
leaf, which is its root node, and the value is defined there too. ZIP 258,
``Changes to ZIP 221'', leaves the definition unchanged apart from the added
fields.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[History input of a block]\label{def:history-input}
For $\eta \ge A_H$ the \emph{history input} of $B_\eta$ is
\[
  \mathsf{hashLightClientRoot}(\eta) :=
  \begin{cases}
    \text{the string of $32$ zero bytes} & \text{if } \eta = A_H, \\
    \mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}(\mathcal{T}_\eta) & \text{if } \eta > A_H.
  \end{cases}
\]
The value at $A_H$ is the sentinel that ZIP 221, ``Block header semantics
and consensus rules'', requires in the Heartwood activation block and states
``MUST NOT be interpreted as a root hash''. The history input is a total
function of the height from $A_H$ on, and it is the value that ZIP 244,
``Block Header Changes'', names ``hashLightClientRoot (as described in
ZIP 221)''.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Binding of the chain-history root]
\label{prop:history-binding}
Let $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}'$ be epoch trees hashed under one branch
ID $b$. If $\mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}(\mathcal{T}) =
\mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}(\mathcal{T}')$, then either $\mathcal{T}$ and
$\mathcal{T}'$ agree on every field of every node, or a collision of
$\mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta, \cdot)$ is computed from them
efficiently. If in addition SHA-256d is collision resistant, the leaves'
block hashes bind the headers of the leaf blocks.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proposition is the ZIP 221 instance of
Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}(1), whose hypotheses are checked as
follows. \emph{Serialisation.} The branch ID $b$ fixes one upgrade epoch and
hence one field vector for both trees (Table~\ref{tab:branch-ids};
Definitions~\ref{def:tree-branch} and~\ref{def:field-vector}). Its field
order is fixed, and each field is of fixed width or a minimal CompactSize,
whose first byte fixes its length (Definition~\ref{def:compactsize}). A
serialised node therefore parses field by field: $\mathrm{ser}$ is
injective, and no serialised node is a proper prefix of another, since the
two parse to the same fields up to the end of the shorter and the number of
fields is fixed; minimality makes $\mathrm{ser}$ a function of the node.
\emph{Root and shape.} If the root commitments are equal, then the two root
serialisations are equal, or they are distinct inputs with equal images, a
collision. Equal root nodes have equal $\mathsf{nEarliestHeight}$ and
$\mathsf{nLatestHeight}$, hence equal leaf count $n$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:leaf-count}, hence the same shape $M^L_n$. The shape fixes
which positions are leaves, so no leaf of one tree is compared with an
internal node of the other, although the tree carries no layer tags
(ZIP 221, ``Tree nodes'', identifies leaves by their block range of $1$).
\emph{Extraction.} Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}(1) then yields a
collision at the first level of disagreement. \emph{Headers.} Field $1$ of
each leaf is the SHA-256d block hash of its header
(Definition~\ref{def:header}; \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The transparent
layer's hashes''); equal leaves with distinct headers are a collision of
SHA-256d.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Binding of range transaction counts]\label{prop:counts}
Let a node $v$ of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ be authenticated by an inclusion path
(Definition~\ref{def:mmr-path}, in the tuple form of
Definition~\ref{def:aggregating}, siblings as full node vectors) accepted
against the chain-history root of the epoch tree of a chain the client
trusts. Barring a collision as in Proposition~\ref{prop:history-binding},
$v.\mathsf{nSaplingTxCount}$, and where present $v.\mathsf{nOrchardTxCount}$
and $v.\mathsf{nIronwoodTxCount}$, equal the numbers of transactions in the
blocks of the height range of $v$ in which $\mathsf{vSpendsSapling}$ or
$\mathsf{vOutputsSapling}$, $\mathsf{vActionsOrchard}$ and
$\mathsf{vActionsIronwood}$, respectively, are non-empty. Hence a client that
obtains the transactions of those blocks, each authenticated against its
block's transaction root, detects the withholding of any such transaction
by a count mismatch, and a range whose count is zero contains none and may
be skipped; the count does not identify the missing transaction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}(2), an accepted path binds the full
vector of every node it recomputes and of every sibling it carries, so $v$
is the node of the trusted tree at its position, barring a collision; the
serialisation is prefix-free by the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:history-binding}. In that tree the count fields are
consistent, and by the parent rule of Definition~\ref{def:node-vector} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:leaf-count} the value of each count field of $v$ is its sum
over the leaves of $v$'s height range. The reading as detection of
withholding is ZIP 221, ``Non-FlyClient Commitments''.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Cross-branch separation]\label{prop:branch-separation}
For branch IDs $b \ne b'$ with encodings $\beta \ne \beta'$ the
personalisations $\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta$ and
$\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta'$ differ. Model
$\mathsf{h}(P, \cdot)$, for each personalisation $P$, as an independent random
oracle with $256$-bit output. An adversary making $q$ oracle queries, the
queries of the verification of its output included, outputs $x, x'$ with
\[
  \mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta, x) =
  \mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta', x')
\]
with probability at most $q^2 / 2^{256}$. Hence no internal-node hash or
root commitment computed under $b$ equals one computed under $b'$, except
with that probability. Leaves are unpersonalised block hashes, identical in
every branch, and are not separated.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The modelling hypothesis is that of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Domain
separation and personalisation'', Proposition ``Domain separation yields
independent oracles'': distinct tags give independent random oracles. Both
values must be answers to queries, one to each oracle; each of the at most
$q^2$ pairs of such queries agrees with probability $2^{-256}$, and the
union bound (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``The union bound and a birthday
calculation'') gives the bound. ZIP 221, ``Tree nodes'', states the design
intent: ``valid nodes from one consensus branch cannot be used to make false
statements about parallel consensus branches''.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The authorising-data root}\label{sec:history-authdata}

The header field $\mathsf{hashMerkleRoot}$ (Definition~\ref{def:header}) is
the root of Bitcoin's SHA-256d transaction Merkle tree over the transaction
identifiers of the block (protocol specification, \S``Block Header Encoding
and Consensus''). The identifier of a version-$5$ or version-$6$ transaction
excludes its authorising data (ZIP 244; \emph{Ironwood Guide},
\S``Transaction digests and signatures'', Definition ``Authorising-data
digest; effecting and authorising data''), so for such transactions
$\mathsf{hashMerkleRoot}$ does not commit to the authorising data; the
identifier of a version-$4$ transaction covers its whole encoding. The
version-$4$ format is valid until NU7, from which ZIP 2003 (Draft), deployed
by ZIP 259 (Draft), makes it invalid.

\begin{construction}[Authorising-data root]\label{def:authdata-root}
Let a block have the transactions $t_1, \dots, t_m$, where $m \ge 1$ because
of the coinbase transaction. Leaf $i$ is the \emph{authorising-data
commitment} of $t_i$: for version $5$, the digest $\mathsf{auth\_digest}$ of
ZIP 244, ``Authorizing Data Commitment''; for version $6$, the digest of the
\emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Definition ``Authorising-data digest; effecting and
authorising data'' (ZIP 229 (Draft), ``Block Commitments'':
$\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}$ takes the version-$6$ digest ``with no further
structural change''); and for a transaction of a version before $5$, the
$32$ bytes $\mathtt{0xFF}$, a placeholder used only in this tree and
occurring only in blocks before NU7. The leaves are padded with leaves of
$32$ zero bytes to $2^{\lceil \log_2 m \rceil}$, and
$\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}$ is the root of the binary Merkle tree of height
$\lceil \log_2 m \rceil$ over them, each non-leaf node being
$\mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashAuthDatHash}, \mathit{left} \,\|\, \mathit{right})$,
under a $16$-byte personalisation without branch ID. The insertion order
MUST equal that of the transaction identifiers in the tree under
$\mathsf{hashMerkleRoot}$, so that one path identifies the same transaction
in both trees (ZIP 244, ``Block Header Changes'').
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}[Joint binding of the two transaction roots]
\label{prop:tx-roots}
Assume, with ZIP 244, ``Authorizing Data Commitment'', that the pair of a
transaction's identifier and its authorising-data commitment is binding:
distinct transactions with equal pairs form a collision of that pair map.
Let two lists of $m$ transactions have equal $\mathsf{hashMerkleRoot}$ and
equal $\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}$. Then the lists are equal, or a collision
of SHA-256d, of $\mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashAuthDatHash}, \cdot)$ or of the
pair map is computed from them. The binding is claimed for lists of the
given length only: the identifier tree is Bitcoin's transaction Merkle tree
and inherits its duplicate-last-node caveat, and the header commits to no
transaction count.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $m$, Bitcoin's duplication rule and the zero padding of
Construction~\ref{def:authdata-root} are fixed maps of the list, so the path
argument of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Binary Merkle trees'', Proposition
``Binding of the root'', applies to each tree: the two lists of identifiers
agree, else a collision of SHA-256d is extracted, and the two lists of
authorising-data commitments agree, else a collision of
$\mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashAuthDatHash}, \cdot)$. The common insertion order
aligns the two leaf sequences, so each position carries equal pairs, and
distinct transactions there form a collision of the pair map. ZIP 244,
``Authorizing Data Commitment'', states that the pair ``constitutes a
commitment to all the data of a serialized transaction''; for a transaction
of a version before $5$ the identifier covers all data and the second
member is the constant placeholder. The caveat is the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Layer and domain separation'', Remark ``Unbalanced trees, padding, and a
deployed failure''.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The block commitment hash}\label{sec:history-blockcommit}

In this subsection $0^{32}$ denotes the string of $32$ zero bytes.

% compute/history.py:
% [sec:history-blockcommit] block commitment input = 32 (history input) + 32
%   (hashAuthDataRoot) + 32 (zero terminator) = 96 bytes; 'ZcashBlockCommit'
%   is 16 bytes, no branch ID
\begin{definition}[Block commitment hash]\label{def:block-commitments}
For a block $B_\eta$ at a height in or after the NU5 upgrade epoch, the
\emph{block commitment hash} is
\begin{multline*}
  \mathsf{hashBlockCommitments}(B_\eta) := \\
  \mathsf{h}\bigl(\texttt{ZcashBlockCommit},\
    \mathsf{hashLightClientRoot}(\eta) \,\|\,
    \mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}(B_\eta) \,\|\, 0^{32}\bigr),
\end{multline*}
with $\mathsf{hashLightClientRoot}(\eta)$ the history input of
Definition~\ref{def:history-input}, $\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}(B_\eta)$ the
root of Construction~\ref{def:authdata-root} for $B_\eta$, and $0^{32}$ the
\emph{terminator} (ZIP 244, ``Block Header Changes''). The input has
$32 + 32 + 32 = 96$ bytes, and the $16$-byte personalisation carries no
branch ID. The history input is a function of the blocks before $B_\eta$;
the authorising-data root is a function of $B_\eta$ itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Reopening the block commitment]\label{prop:reopen}
From NU5 the chain-history root appears in no header. Let $c$ be the value
$\mathsf{hashBlockCommitments}(B_\eta)$ of a block in or after the NU5
upgrade epoch. A verifier given a claimed history input $r$, or a claimed
root node vector from which it computes
$r = \mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta, \mathrm{ser}(\text{root}))$
with $\beta$ encoding the branch ID of Definition~\ref{def:tree-branch}, and
a $32$-byte value $a$, accepts $r$ if and only if
\[
  c = \mathsf{h}\bigl(\texttt{ZcashBlockCommit},\ r \,\|\, a \,\|\,
    0^{32}\bigr);
\]
only the $32$-byte value of $\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}$ is needed, not its
tree. Two distinct accepted pairs $(r, a) \ne (r', a')$ exhibit a collision
of $\mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashBlockCommit}, \cdot)$. Hence an accepted $r$ is
the block's history input except with that collision probability, and an
inclusion path in the epoch tree is checked against a header through it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pair $(\mathsf{hashLightClientRoot}(\eta),
\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}(B_\eta))$ is accepted by
Definition~\ref{def:block-commitments}. The input
$r \,\|\, a \,\|\, 0^{32}$ has fixed length, so distinct pairs give distinct
inputs, and two accepted pairs with equal image $c$ are a collision.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Extension of the block commitment hash]\label{rem:terminator}
ZIP 244, ``Block Header Changes'', treats the value as ``a linked list of
hashes terminated by arbitrary data'': a later upgrade may replace the
terminator by the hash of a new structure that ends in its own terminator.
The same section states two interoperability rules: fully validating nodes
MUST always use the entire structure defined by the latest activated
protocol version they support, and a third-party server that supports a
later protocol version SHOULD provide a value that can be used in place of
the all-zero terminator, so that a light client can validate the parts it
needs. No upgrade through NU7 replaces the terminator: ZIP 258, ``Changes to
ZIP 221'', leaves $\mathsf{hashBlockCommitments}$ ``unaffected'' beyond the
added node fields; ZIP 229, ``Block Commitments'', changes no structure; and
no source of NU7 consensus changes listed in ZIP 259, ``NU7 consensus
changes'', amends ZIP 244.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The header consensus rules}\label{sec:history-rules}

\begin{definition}[The commitment field by upgrade epoch]
\label{def:commitment-field}
The $32$-byte header field that follows
$\mathsf{hashMerkleRoot}$ (Definition~\ref{def:header}) is named
$\mathsf{hashReserved}$ before Sapling, $\mathsf{hashFinalSaplingRoot}$ in the
Sapling and Blossom upgrade epochs, $\mathsf{hashLightClientRoot}$ in the
Heartwood and Canopy upgrade epochs, and $\mathsf{hashBlockCommitments}$ from
NU5 (protocol specification, \S``Block Header Encoding and Consensus''; ZIP 221,
``Block header semantics and consensus rules''; ZIP 244, ``Block Header
Changes''). The header byte format and version are unchanged by each renaming.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Header commitment rule]\label{def:commitment-rule}
For a block $B_\eta$ the commitment field satisfies the following rules.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For $\eta < A_H$ it follows the earlier rules of the protocol
  specification, \S``Block Header Encoding and Consensus'', not restated
  here: in the Sapling and Blossom upgrade epochs it is
  $\mathsf{LEBS2OSP}_{256}$ of the root of the Sapling note commitment tree
  of the block's final Sapling treestate.
\item For $A_H \le \eta$ in the Heartwood or Canopy upgrade epoch it MUST
  equal the history input $\mathsf{hashLightClientRoot}(\eta)$ of
  Definition~\ref{def:history-input}, which supplies the sentinel at $A_H$.
\item For $\eta$ in or after the NU5 upgrade epoch, the NU5 activation block
  included and without sentinel, it MUST equal
  $\mathsf{hashBlockCommitments}(B_\eta)$ of
  Definition~\ref{def:block-commitments}.
\end{enumerate}
(ZIP 221, ``Block header semantics and consensus rules''; ZIP 244, ``Block
Header Changes''; protocol specification, \S``Block Header Encoding and
Consensus''.) The value is undefined for the genesis block, which no case
covers.
\end{definition}

In the NU5 activation block $B_{A_{\mathrm{NU5}}}$, $A_{\mathrm{NU5}}$ the NU5
activation height, the history input is
$\mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}(\mathcal{T}_{A_{\mathrm{NU5}}})$, the root of
the completed tree of the Canopy upgrade epoch. It is hashed under the Canopy
branch ID $\mathtt{0xE9FF75A6}$ (ZIP 251) by the adopted reading of
Definition~\ref{def:tree-branch}, the first sentence of ZIP 221 giving the
NU5 branch ID instead, and it is built from nodes of fields $1$ to $11$ by
the adopted reading of Definition~\ref{def:field-vector}. By
Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule} every valid block after the Heartwood
activation block commits to the root of its epoch tree: directly in the
Heartwood and Canopy upgrade epochs, and from NU5 as the first input of the
block commitment hash.

\subsection{The committed range}\label{sec:history-range}

\begin{proposition}[One-block delay]\label{prop:delay}
For $\eta > A_H$ the tree to which $B_\eta$ commits
(Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}) has the leaves
$B_{\alpha(\eta)}, \dots, B_{\eta-1}$. No header commits to a tree that
contains its own block, and $B_{\eta-1}$ first enters a committed tree at
$B_\eta$. Consequently the final Sapling root of a block at a height of at
least $A_H$ is committed one block later, as the field
$\mathsf{hashLatestSaplingRoot}$ of the root node committed by the next
block. ZIP 221, ``Non-FlyClient Commitments'', accepts the delay because
``using the most recent treestate as a transaction anchor is already unsafe
in reorgs''.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:epoch-tree}, $\eta$ lies outside
$[\alpha(\eta), \eta - 1]$. Since $\eta - 1 \ge A_H$ and $\alpha(\eta)$ is
the largest activation height below $\eta$, $\alpha(\eta) \le \eta - 1$, so
$B_{\eta-1}$ is the last leaf of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$; it is no leaf of
$\mathcal{T}_{\eta'}$ for $\eta' \le \eta - 1$, whose leaves lie below
$\eta'$. Then Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}. The latest-root field of
the root node is that of its last leaf, by the parent rule of
Definition~\ref{def:node-vector}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reset at each activation height]\label{thm:epoch-reset}
Let $A > A_H$ be an activation height and $A' = \alpha(A)$ the preceding
one. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B_A$ commits to the complete tree $\mathcal{T}_A$ over
  $B_{A'}, \dots, B_{A-1}$, the whole upgrade epoch beginning at $A'$,
  hashed under that upgrade epoch's branch ID $b(\mathcal{T}_A)$ by the adopted
  reading of Definition~\ref{def:tree-branch} and built with that upgrade
  epoch's field vector by the adopted reading of
  Definition~\ref{def:field-vector}; the commitment field equals
  $\mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}(\mathcal{T}_A)$ before NU5, and
  $\mathsf{hashBlockCommitments}(B_A)$ with it as first input from NU5;
\item $B_{A+1}$ commits to the single-leaf tree over $B_A$;
\item for $A' < \eta < A$, the leaves of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ are a proper
  prefix of those of $\mathcal{T}_A$, so every ended upgrade epoch from
  Heartwood on is committed completely by exactly one header, that of the
  next activation block;
\item the reset occurs at every upgrade, including those that leave the
  field vector unchanged (Canopy, NU6, NU6.1, NU6.2 and NU7).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the definition of $\alpha$, $\alpha(A) = A'$ and $\alpha(A + 1) = A$. For
$A' < \eta < A$, $\alpha(\eta) = A'$, so $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ has the leaves
$B_{A'}, \dots, B_{\eta-1}$, a proper prefix of those of $\mathcal{T}_A$;
only $\mathcal{T}_A$ has the full leaf set, and for $\eta > A$ no tree has
the leaf $B_{A'}$, since $\alpha(\eta) \ge A$. The commitment fields follow
from Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}. Part (4) holds because $\alpha$
depends on the activation heights alone. A script illustrates (1) to (3) on a
toy chain, a one-block upgrade epoch included; it establishes nothing.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No commitment below the Heartwood activation height]
\label{cor:pre-heartwood}
The Heartwood activation block $B_{A_H}$ commits to no tree, its history
input being the sentinel of Definition~\ref{def:history-input}. For
$\eta > A_H$, $\alpha(\eta) \ge A_H$, so no committed tree has a leaf below
$A_H$. Below $A_H$ the commitment field holds a Sapling root or is reserved
(Definition~\ref{def:commitment-field}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The height $A_H$ is an activation height below every $\eta > A_H$, and
$\alpha(\eta)$ is the largest such; then
Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}, item~(ii).
\end{proof}

\section{The deployment boundary}\label{sec:boundary}

This section classifies every component of a Zcash FlyClient in the classes
of Definition~\ref{def:classes}. It opens with one table that places each
hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient} and each obligation of
Remark~\ref{rem:proof-obligations} in its class, with the cited fact that
places it there. The subsections that follow elaborate the rows: the
specified commitment and the absence of a specified consumer, the
differences between the paper's commitment and the deployed one, the
conditions that the ZIPs impose on any sample check of a Zcash verifier, and
the open problems.

\subsection{Classification of the security hypotheses}
\label{sec:boundary-classes}

\begin{remark}[Classification of the hypotheses]\label{rem:hypotheses}
The classes of Definition~\ref{def:classes} apply to components, not to
properties. A property proved in this volume of a specified object is
recorded as ``the object is specified, and the property is proved here'',
never as specified. Table~\ref{tab:boundary} classifies every hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient} and every obligation O1 to O6 of
Remark~\ref{rem:proof-obligations}, together with two components of the
deployed commitment, the chain commitment (row (1)) and its composition
across upgrade epochs (row (4)); O1 and O2 share row (3). Each row gives its
class and the cited fact that places it there or, for an assumption on the
client's environment, the source that records it. Hypothesis~1 of the theorem
fixes the parameters $c$, $k$ and $\delta$, whose values an instantiation
chooses; rows (7) and (9) classify what those values rest on. The rows read as
follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Chain commitment}: specified. It is the epoch-scoped,
  metadata-bearing, branch-separated epoch tree $\mathcal{T}_\eta$
  (Definition~\ref{def:epoch-tree}, Construction~\ref{def:history-tree}), to
  whose chain-history root every header after the Heartwood activation block
  commits under Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}, enforced by consensus
  (ZIPs 221 and 244; ZIP 258, Draft, whose rules are in force from NU6.3 by
  Definition~\ref{def:zip-status}).
\item \emph{Collision resistance of the hash functions}: the functions are
  specified, namely personalised BLAKE2b-256 $\mathsf{h}$ for tree nodes, the
  chain-history root, the authorising-data root and the block commitment hash
  (ZIPs 221 and 244; protocol specification, \S``BLAKE2 Hash Functions''),
  and SHA-256d for block hashes, hence for leaves (protocol specification,
  \S``Block Header Encoding and Consensus''). Their collision resistance is a
  standard assumption (\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Security notions: preimage,
  second-preimage, and collision resistance'').
\item \emph{O1, binding within one upgrade epoch}: the commitment is
  specified (Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}), and the property is
  proved here of the specified object
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:history-binding}); no normative text fixes the
  property. \emph{O2, fork structure for ZIP 221 trees}: open, since it is
  conditional on a sufficient sample check for those trees, which no
  specification or result supplies
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:zcash-sample}); Lemmas~\ref{lem:no-honest}
  and~\ref{lem:no-reuse} are stated for the paper's construction only.
  Hypothesis~6 of Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient}, leaf--internal separation,
  enters only through Lemma~\ref{lem:no-honest} and shares the class of O2:
  it is stated for the paper's construction, and for ZIP 221 trees no
  specification or result supplies its counterpart.
\item \emph{Composition across upgrade epochs}: open. Each upgrade epoch has
  its own tree (Theorem~\ref{thm:epoch-reset}), and ZIP 221 states no rule
  that chains them.
\item \emph{The verifier's anchor}, the genesis block in the paper: open
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:pre-heartwood}).
\item \emph{O3, weight binding}: the node vector is specified
  (Definition~\ref{def:node-vector}), and the binding of the field-$8$
  interval of a leaf to the root and its position within one upgrade epoch
  is proved here of the specified object
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}(1) with
  Proposition~\ref{prop:history-binding}). The link from a sampled header to
  its leaf's weight is conditional on the same sufficient sample check, and
  so open. Sampling by the chain's total work is open, field $8$ of a root
  summing one upgrade epoch (Definition~\ref{def:node-vector};
  Proposition~\ref{prop:total-work}).
\item \emph{Assumption~\ref{ass:cl}, the transition checks of O3
  (Construction~\ref{def:node-checks}) and Assumption~\ref{ass:rewriting}
  under the Zcash retarget rule}, in both of its regimes: open (protocol
  specification, \S``Difficulty adjustment''; ZIP 218, Draft).
\item \emph{Assumption~\ref{ass:discretisation}}: open for Zcash; no
  analysis relates the work of individual blocks to the invalid segments of
  a Zcash fork.
\item \emph{O4, catch probability and sample count}: designed but
  unspecified, and proved in the paper's model under its hypotheses
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal}; Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-count}).
\item \emph{The random-oracle hypothesis on the sampling hash}: open. No ZIP
  or specification section fixes a sampling hash for Zcash, so the
  hypothesis has no object; the model is that of the \emph{Crypto Guide},
  \S``The random oracle model''.
\item \emph{O5, grinding}: the transform of
  Construction~\ref{def:noninteractive} is designed but unspecified. Its
  Zcash transcript (header bytes, hash, domain separation, and map from
  output to weight points across upgrade epochs) and a derivation of the
  budget $R$ of Assumption~\ref{ass:recompute-budget} are open.
\item \emph{O6, size}: the bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:proof-size} is
  designed but unspecified; a Zcash proof format is open.
\item \emph{At least one honest prover}: an assumption on the client's
  connectivity, not a component, so no class applies. ZIP 221, ``Security
  and Privacy Considerations'', summarises the analysis of the paper, not
  the setting of ZIP 221 itself, as follows: ``The security analysis assumes
  adversary mining power is bounded by a known fraction of combined mining
  power of honest nodes, and cannot drop or tamper with messages between
  client and full nodes. It also assumes the client is connected to at least
  one full node and knows the genesis block.'' The bound on mining power is
  row (7); the absence of dropping or tampering and the connection to at
  least one full node are this row; knowledge of the genesis block is
  row (5).
\end{enumerate}
Open problems (\S\ref{sec:boundary-open}) derives rows (4), (5), (7), (11)
and (12), and the total work of row (6), from cited rules; O2 of row (3),
with the header-to-weight link of row (6), and rows (8) and (10) are open
for the reasons stated above.
\end{remark}

The verifier as a whole is designed but unspecified. The protocol of ``The

\subsection{The specified commitment and its consumers}
\label{sec:boundary-specified}

Row (1) of Remark~\ref{rem:hypotheses} is the following. ZIP 221 (Final)
specifies the tree, with FlyClient as its stated motivation (ZIP 221,
``Motivation''). ZIP 221, ``FlyClient Requirements and Recommendations'',
designates seven fields for reasoning about the difficulty adjustment over a
range of blocks: $\mathsf{nEarliestTimestamp}$, $\mathsf{nLatestTimestamp}$,
$\mathsf{nEarliestTargetBits}$, $\mathsf{nLatestTargetBits}$,
$\mathsf{nEarliestHeight}$, $\mathsf{nLatestHeight}$ and
$\mathsf{nSubTreeTotalWork}$ (Remark~\ref{rem:flyclient-fields}). The pool
roots and transaction counts are its ``Non-FlyClient Commitments''. No ZIP or
specification section specifies a consumer of the tree, a proof object or a
verifier. The specified class depends on no implementation:
Definition~\ref{def:zip-status} places the fields of ZIP 258 (Draft) in force
from NU6.3.

\begin{remark}[Recomputation against proof verification]\label{rem:recompute}
The consensus rule of Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule} is an equality
between the commitment field of a header and a value that the validator
recomputes from the chain it holds: $\mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}(
\mathcal{T}_\eta)$ in the Heartwood and Canopy upgrade epochs, and the block
commitment hash from NU5 (protocol specification, \S``Block Header Encoding
and Consensus''; ZIP 221, ``Block header semantics and consensus rules'';
ZIP 244, ``Block Header Changes''). No ZIP or specification section defines
an inclusion proof for the tree, a proof object or a verification
algorithm. The operation that the paper's verifier performs,
Construction~\ref{def:per-sample}, is therefore unspecified for Zcash.
\end{remark}

No specified light-client protocol consumes the commitment. ZIP 307 (Draft)
does not reference ZIP 221, while ZIP 221, ``Motivation'', defers the
verification of transaction inclusion to ZIP 307: it ``follows the usual
reference protocol''.

\subsection{The paper's commitment and the deployed commitment}
\label{sec:boundary-deltas}

\begin{remark}[Deltas from the paper's commitment]\label{rem:deltas}
Against Construction~\ref{def:chain-commitment} and
Definition~\ref{def:diff-mmr}, the deployed commitment differs in five
respects.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Scope.} It is epoch-scoped, not rooted at the genesis block
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:epoch-reset}). The one-block delay of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:delay} is shared with the paper, whose header also
  commits to exactly the blocks strictly before it.
\item \emph{Metadata.} The seven FlyClient fields serve the purpose of
  Definition~\ref{def:diff-mmr} (ePrint 2019/226, Definition~7) without
  matching it field for field (Remark~\ref{rem:flyclient-fields}); the pool
  roots and counts have no counterpart.
\item \emph{Hashing.} Every parent is hashed by the personalised,
  branch-separated BLAKE2b-256
  $\mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta,\ \mathrm{ser}(\ell) \,\|\,
  \mathrm{ser}(r))$ over full serialisations, the root commitment
  $\mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}$ covering the root's aggregates, and every
  leaf carries the SHA-256d block hash; the paper hashes with one generic
  collision-resistant $H$ (Propositions~\ref{prop:history-binding}
  and~\ref{prop:branch-separation}).
\item \emph{Placement.} The root enters through the repurposed commitment
  field (Definition~\ref{def:commitment-field}), and from NU5 only as the
  first input of the block commitment hash (Proposition~\ref{prop:reopen}),
  whereas the paper adds a header field that can replace the previous block
  hash (ePrint 2019/226, Section~4.2, page~12).
\item \emph{Bagging.} The deployed tree bags its peaks by the left fold
  $\mathrm{Bag}_L$, the paper by the right fold $\mathrm{Bag}_R$
  (ePrint 2019/226, Definition~11). Path tops, prefix folds and the maximal
  path length therefore differ whenever the leaf count has three or more set
  bits (Theorem~\ref{thm:fold-order}; Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}).
\end{enumerate}
ZIP 221, ``Tree Node specification'', states: ``Other than the metadata
commitments, the MMR tree's construction is standard.'' Items (1), (3),
(4) and (5) record the respects in which the deployed construction also
differs from the paper's.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A Zcash verifier}\label{sec:boundary-verifier}

The verifier is designed but unspecified (Remark~\ref{rem:hypotheses}). The
conditions below are obligations that ZIPs 221 and 244 impose on any sample
check for ZIP 221 trees; they are not a construction of this volume, and no
Zcash verifier is specified here. A \emph{sample check for ZIP 221 trees} is
a deterministic algorithm that takes the header of a head, an index $j$ and a
prover's message, and accepts or rejects; its purpose is to establish, for
the head's epoch tree, the conclusions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check} and Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-binding}. A
condition is \emph{necessary} when some message that meets every other
condition and violates it also violates such a conclusion, so that a check
that does not reject that message fails the conclusion.

\begin{proposition}[Necessary conditions of a Zcash sample check]
\label{prop:zcash-sample}
Let the head $B_\eta$, $\eta > A_H$, commit to $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ with
$n = \eta - \alpha(\eta)$ leaves, let $\beta$ encode $b(\mathcal{T}_\eta)$,
and let a sample be leaf $j$ of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$, $1 \le j \le n$, the
block at height $\alpha(\eta) + j - 1$, with received header
$\mathrm{hdr}_j$. Each of the following conditions, the height clause of (ii)
and the parsing clause of (iv) excepted, is necessary for a sample check for
ZIP 221 trees. Sufficiency is not claimed. The height clause of (ii) is
required for the leaf's stated heights to match its position
(Lemma~\ref{lem:leaf-count}); parsing under the field vector is the
hypothesis under which Proposition~\ref{prop:history-binding} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:aggregate-binding}(2) apply; the necessity of neither is
claimed.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Field $1$ of the leaf equals $\mathsf{BlockHash}(\mathrm{hdr}_j)$, the
  SHA-256d hash of the full received header, Equihash solution included
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:leaf-prev}).
\item Fields $2$ and $3$ of the leaf equal the field $\mathsf{nTime}$ of
  $\mathrm{hdr}_j$, fields $4$ and $5$ its field $\mathsf{nBits}$; field $8$
  equals the work $\lfloor 2^{256} / (\mathsf{ToTarget}(\mathsf{nBits}) + 1)
  \rfloor$ of that $\mathsf{nBits}$; and fields $9$ and $10$ equal
  $\alpha(\eta) + j - 1$, the height that the position fixes, the header
  carrying no height (Definition~\ref{def:header}).
\item The header $\mathrm{hdr}_j$ satisfies proof of work in the sense of
  Definition~\ref{def:header}: a valid Equihash solution, and the difficulty
  filter at the target $\mathsf{ToTarget}(\mathsf{nBits})$ it declares.
\item The sampled leaf of (i) and (ii) and the path siblings are received as
  full node serialisations, parsed under the field vector of
  Definition~\ref{def:field-vector} for the upgrade epoch beginning at
  $\alpha(\eta)$. Starting from that leaf, every parent is recomputed: field
  $1$ as $\mathsf{h}(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta,\
  \mathrm{ser}(\ell) \,\|\, \mathrm{ser}(r))$
  (Construction~\ref{def:history-tree}), the other fields by the parent rule
  of Definition~\ref{def:node-vector}. The recomputed root node yields
  $\mathsf{hashChainHistoryRoot}(\mathcal{T}_\eta)$, which is compared with
  the head's commitment field under Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}:
  directly in the Heartwood and Canopy upgrade epochs, and from NU5 through
  Proposition~\ref{prop:reopen} with the head's $\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}$.
\item The sides and the length of the path are those that $j$ and $n$ fix
  under $\mathrm{Bag}_L$, the length being $\mathrm{depth}_L(n, j)$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}).
\item For $j \ge 2$: the root node $\rho$ output by
  $\mathrm{PrefixRoot}_L(n, j, \pi)$ (Construction~\ref{def:prefix-root}),
  run on the path $\pi$ with the parent rule in place of
  $H(\cdot \,\|\, \cdot)$, is the claimed root node of
  $\mathcal{T}_{\alpha(\eta)+j-1}$, and the value
  \[
    r = \mathsf{h}\bigl(\texttt{ZcashHistory} \,\|\, \beta,\
      \mathrm{ser}(\rho)\bigr)
  \]
  is accepted against the commitment field of $\mathrm{hdr}_j$ under
  Definition~\ref{def:commitment-rule}: $r$ equals that field in the
  Heartwood and Canopy upgrade epochs, and from NU5 $r$ is accepted by
  Proposition~\ref{prop:reopen} with the $\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}$ of the
  sampled block.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each condition, a message that meets the other five and violates it
also violates the conclusion named.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item An honest header with valid proof of work, paired with a leaf whose
  field $1$ is the hash of a different, unmined block of the claimed tree.
  The proof of work checked is not that of the committed block, contrary to
  Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}(i) and
  Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-binding}(1).
\item An honest header paired with a leaf of inflated field $8$, in a tree that
  the adversary builds over such leaves under a head that it mines, so that
  every path verifies. The header-determined fields are not those of the
  header, contrary to Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}(ii), and the
  interval that Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}(1) binds belongs to a
  forged weight.
\item A tree built over fabricated headers without valid solutions. Every
  other condition is met by construction, and unmined headers pass,
  contrary to Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-binding}(1).
\item If a node is taken in any other form, the fields that do not enter
  the recomputation are unbound. With the siblings supplied as field-$1$
  digests and the path checked on field $1$ alone, the siblings' field $8$,
  which fixes the interval of Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}, and the
  leaf's pool fields ($6$, $7$ and, where the vector carries them, $11$ to
  $17$), which its header does not determine, are chosen freely; two
  passing samples at $j$ then carry distinct leaf tuples, contrary to
  Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}(i). If the
  recomputation starts from a node other than the leaf matched in (i) and
  (ii), the matched header is bound to nothing.
  Proposition~\ref{prop:history-binding} binds full serialisations only.
\item The single-block tuple of a leaf placed at a position where the shape
  $M^L_n$ has an internal node: a tree with fewer than $n$ leaves then
  passes, and a tuple committed at another depth is accepted as leaf $j$.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:position-binding}, which assumes the shape fixed by $n$
  and the bagging order, does not apply.
\item Honest blocks, mined on the honest chain after the fork point,
  appended as leaves of the fork's tree. They pass (i) to (v), while their
  own commitments bind the honest prefix, not the fork's first $j - 1$
  leaves, contrary to Proposition~\ref{prop:sample-check}(iii). This is the
  case that Lemma~\ref{lem:no-honest} excludes by the prefix check.
\end{enumerate}
No condition is shown sufficient.
\end{proof}

The conditions are the ZIP 221 counterparts of the checks of
Construction~\ref{def:per-sample}: (i) and (ii) of check (a), (iii) of check
(d), (iv) and (v) of check (b), and (vi) of check (c). Three further
statements bound their reach. For $j = 1$ the sample is the block
$B_{\alpha(\eta)}$. For $\alpha(\eta) > A_H$ it is an activation block, which
commits to the completed tree of the previous upgrade epoch
(Theorem~\ref{thm:epoch-reset}); for $\alpha(\eta) = A_H$ it carries the
sentinel of Definition~\ref{def:history-input}
(Corollary~\ref{cor:pre-heartwood}). In both cases its prefix check belongs
to epoch composition, which is open. The consensus condition
$\mathsf{nBits} = \mathsf{ThresholdBits}(\alpha(\eta) + j - 1)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:header} depends on preceding headers and is not decided
by one sample; it is the object of the transition checks of row (7), which
are open. And condition (iv) makes Proposition~\ref{prop:history-binding}
apply, but the fork structure of row (3) additionally needs a sufficient
check, which is open.

\begin{example}[Per-sample size]\label{ex:sample-size}
The size is evaluated at the scale of one upgrade epoch, since $n$ is the
leaf count of one epoch tree and never the length of the chain. The message
of Proposition~\ref{prop:zcash-sample} for one sample consists of the
$1{,}487$-byte header (\S\ref{sec:problem-spv}), the sampled leaf's own
serialisation of at most $317$ bytes (Definition~\ref{def:field-vector}), at
most $\lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + \mathrm{popcount}(n) - 1$ siblings of at
most $317$ bytes each (Lemma~\ref{lem:bag-depth}), and the $32$ bytes of the
sampled block's $\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}$. For $n = 2^{20}$ a path has at
most $20$ siblings, and a sample at most
\[
  1{,}487 + 317 + 20 \cdot 317 + 32 = 8{,}176 \text{ bytes},
\]
of which the header is $18.2$ per cent. For $n = 2^{20} - 1$, with $20$
peaks, a path has at most $38$ siblings, and a sample at most
\[
  1{,}487 + 317 + 38 \cdot 317 + 32 = 13{,}882 \text{ bytes},
\]
of which the header is $10.7$ per cent. The leaf's fields fixed by its
header and its position, $1$ to $5$ and $8$ to $10$, could be omitted by a
proof format, which is open. The figures are computed by script.
\end{example}

\subsection{Open problems}\label{sec:boundary-open}

The following open items, each a row of Remark~\ref{rem:hypotheses} except
the serving interface, are derived below from cited rules: epoch composition,
the anchor below the Heartwood activation height, total work, the proof format,
the serving interface, the
Fiat--Shamir transcript, and the security analysis under Zcash's retarget
rule with its NU7 regime. Composition with compact-block scanning follows.
Availability of prover data is not a protocol question.

\begin{remark}[Epoch composition]
Epoch trees are hashed under distinct branch IDs, and each complete root is
committed only by the next activation header
(Theorem~\ref{thm:epoch-reset}). No specified rule chains them, and ZIP 221,
``Specification'', is silent. Composition is needed for prefix proofs and for
difficulty checks: the averaging window and the median-time span of the
first blocks of an upgrade epoch reach into the previous upgrade epoch
(protocol specification, \S``Difficulty adjustment''), so a transition
check at an upgrade boundary needs two epoch trees.
Proposition~\ref{thm:subchain} needs an inclusion path of the accepted head
against the later head's root, and so applies within one epoch tree. Across
epoch trees it does not apply: no tree of a later upgrade epoch contains a
leaf of an earlier one (Definition~\ref{def:epoch-tree}), and only the
activation header commits to the completed earlier tree. No design exists:
the item is open.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Anchor]
The anchor below the Heartwood activation height is open, by
Corollary~\ref{cor:pre-heartwood}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[No committed node aggregates total work]
\label{prop:total-work}
Let $\eta > A_H$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item If SHA-256d is collision resistant, the header of $B_\eta$ commits
  through $\mathsf{hashPrevBlock}$ to the headers of $B_0, \dots,
  B_{\eta-1}$, hence to their fields $\mathsf{nBits}$ and to the total work
  $\omega$ of that chain (\S\ref{sec:problem-spv}; the model's counterpart is
  Definition~\ref{def:difficulty-level}).
\item For every node $v$ of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$, field $8$ is the sum modulo
  $2^{256}$ of the work of the blocks at heights $v.\mathsf{nEarliestHeight}$
  to $v.\mathsf{nLatestHeight}$, all in $[\alpha(\eta), \eta - 1]$; field
  $8$ of no node of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ depends on $B_0, \dots,
  B_{\alpha(\eta)-1}$.
\item Hence no inclusion path against the root to which $B_\eta$ commits
  opens $\omega$. Sampling by the chain's cumulative work
  (Definition~\ref{def:sampling-map}) and the acceptance rule need field $8$
  of the root of every completed epoch tree $\mathcal{T}_A$, $A_H < A \le
  \alpha(\eta)$ an activation height, each committed only by $B_A$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:epoch-reset}), together with the work of the blocks
  below $A_H$, which no tree commits
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:pre-heartwood}).
\end{enumerate}
No ZIP addresses the item; it is open.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The block hash covers the whole header, $\mathsf{nBits}$ and
$\mathsf{hashPrevBlock}$ included (Definition~\ref{def:header}); induction
down the chain, a divergence yielding a collision of SHA-256d. (b) Field $8$
of a leaf is its block's work and that of an internal node the sum of its
children's (Definition~\ref{def:node-vector}); the leaves of
$\mathcal{T}_\eta$ are $B_{\alpha(\eta)}, \dots, B_{\eta-1}$
(Definition~\ref{def:epoch-tree}); the height range of $v$ is that of
Lemma~\ref{lem:leaf-count}. (c) A path opens only fields of nodes of
$\mathcal{T}_\eta$, and by (b) the only work-valued field, field $8$, sums
work over leaves of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$; the pool-root fields depend on
earlier blocks, but through treestates, from which work is not
computed. The work of each block is at least $1$, since
$\mathsf{ToTarget}(\mathsf{nBits}) + 1 \le 2^{256}$, so $\omega$ exceeds the
work of the leaves of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ whenever $\alpha(\eta) > 0$. The
remaining work is committed only as stated, by Theorem~\ref{thm:epoch-reset}
and Corollary~\ref{cor:pre-heartwood}. ZIP 221, ``Tree Node specification'',
assumes that cumulative chain work stays below $2^{256}$, which makes each
modular sum exact (Lemma~\ref{lem:weight-interval}(2)).
\end{proof}

Part (b) does not contradict the statement of (a) that every header commits
to the total work: that commitment is through the linkage of every header,
which a sampling verifier does not receive.

\begin{remark}[Proof format]
The proof format is open; no ZIP or specification section defines one. From
NU5 any format carries, for every header whose commitment it opens, the
$32$-byte $\mathsf{hashAuthDataRoot}$ needed to reopen the block commitment
hash (Proposition~\ref{prop:reopen}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Serving interface]
The serving interface is open: no ZIP or specification section defines a
message for requesting or serving tree nodes, inclusion paths or assembled
proofs. Availability of prover data is not a protocol question. Every node,
and hence every inclusion path, of $\mathcal{T}_\eta$ is a function of the
chain up to its last leaf $B_{\eta-1}$, not of the blocks of the upgrade
epoch alone, since the pool-root fields ($6$, $7$, $12$, $13$, $15$ and
$16$) are roots of final treestates, which depend on every note commitment
since the activation of the pool (Definition~\ref{def:node-vector};
Construction~\ref{def:history-tree}; ZIP 221, ``Tree Node specification'';
ZIP 258, ``Changes to ZIP 221''). Any party that holds that chain computes
them; what is unspecified is the message.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Transcript]
The transcript is open. Construction~\ref{def:noninteractive} derives the
sampling randomness from a hash of the head (ePrint 2019/226, Section~6.2).
For Zcash no ZIP or specification section fixes the header bytes, the hash,
the domain separation or the map from output to weight points across upgrade
epochs, so Proposition~\ref{prop:grinding} has no Zcash instance.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Security analysis]
The security analysis is open. Zcash retargets after every block from an
averaging window with median-time spans, damping and asymmetric clamps,
which is not Definition~\ref{def:retarget}. The blocks of the upgrade epochs
from Heartwood through NU6.3 are governed by a $17$-block window at a
$75$-second target spacing (protocol specification, \S``Difficulty
adjustment''); NU7 as specified uses $102$ blocks at $25$ seconds (ZIP 218,
Draft, ``Averaging window''; \emph{Consensus Guide}, \S``Difficulty in
motion''). Neither regime is covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:flyclient}, and the
paper contains no derivation of $(c, L)$ from either
(Remark~\ref{rem:cl-status}). The published analysis of a Zcash instantiation
(Perazzo and Capecchi, arXiv 2604.26736, Section~IV-A, Definition~IV.1) assumes
its adversarial work budget rather than deriving it from the retarget rule, so
it does not close the gap; it is recorded here because the open
classification must account for it. ZIP 221, ``Security and Privacy
Considerations'', records that the paper leaves such chains with only
heuristic guarantees, that ``The use of these fields has not been analysed
in the academic security literature'', and that ``A more in-depth security
analysis of FlyClient should be performed before designing a
FlyClient-based light client ecosystem for Zcash''. No ZIP addresses the
gap.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[NU7]
ZIP 218 (Draft) changes the target spacing from $75$ to $25$ seconds, the
averaging window from $17$ to $102$ blocks and the expected stale rate
(ZIP 218, ``Block target spacing'', ``Averaging window'', ``Stale block
rate''). These bear on the $(c, L)$ that an instantiation could assume, not
on Theorem~\ref{thm:optimal}, which is stated in $c$ and $\delta$. Transition
checks must use the height-dependent spacing and window, span two regimes
across one more upgrade boundary (Corollary~\ref{cor:nu7-epoch}), and meet
first adjustments that ZIP 218, ``Effect on difficulty adjustment'', expects
to be limited by $\mathsf{PoWMaxAdjustDown}$ (\emph{Consensus Guide},
\S``NU7: 25-second blocks and bounded shielded work'').
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Compact-block scanning]
Composition with compact-block scanning is open. A FlyClient replaces trust
in the header chain, not in the channel through which a wallet detects its
transactions (ZIP 307, Draft).
\end{remark}
