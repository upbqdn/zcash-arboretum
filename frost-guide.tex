\begin{abstract}
This volume of \emph{The Zcash Arboretum} constructs threshold spend
authorisation for Orchard-protocol notes: FROST, the two-round threshold
Schnorr protocol of RFC~9591, with key generation by a trusted dealer and by a
distributed protocol, and its re-randomised variant specified in ZIP~312 over
the ciphersuite FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), whose aggregate is an ordinary
RedPallas spend-authorisation signature. Any threshold number of participants
signs, and no signing run reconstructs the signing key. The volume proves
correctness of each protocol and states its security with the assumptions and
the status of each result, marking open problems as such. It assumes the
\emph{Math Guide} and the \emph{Crypto Guide}, and cites the \emph{Ironwood
Guide} for RedPallas, randomised validating keys, the signature digest and the
construction of a transaction. It is non-normative: the protocol
specification, the ZIPs and RFC~9591 are authoritative.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

This section fixes the object of the volume and its scope, the standing of
the normative and scholarly sources with the classes into which its claims
fall, and the notation shared with the lower volumes.

\subsection{Scope}\label{sec:intro-scope}

This volume of \emph{The Zcash Arboretum} constructs threshold spend
authorisation for Orchard-protocol notes, for Actions of either pool of the
Orchard protocol (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``The Orchard protocol and its two
pools'', Definitions ``Orchard protocol'' and ``Orchard pool, Ironwood
pool''). Any threshold number of the participants, the threshold and the
number of participants being those of Definition~\ref{def:ts-params},
jointly produce, for an Action spending a note of a threshold key, a
signature that consensus accepts as an ordinary RedPallas
spend-authorisation signature, with the same format and the same validation
as a signature by a single holder of the spend authorising key
\(\ask\). No signing run reconstructs the signing key; a trusted dealer computes
it at key generation; no step of distributed key generation computes it,
and no source cited in this volume proves that protocol secure
(Remark~\ref{rem:dkg-design}). RedPallas
and spend authorisation are cited, not re-derived: \emph{Ironwood Guide},
\S``The RedPallas signature scheme'' and \S``Randomised validating keys''.

The construction has two layers. The first is FROST, the two-round threshold
Schnorr protocol of Komlo and Goldberg as RFC~9591 specifies it, run on a
Shamir sharing of \(\ask\). The second is its re-randomised variant
(ePrint 2024/436), specified for Zcash in ZIP~312, which shifts the shares,
the verification shares and the group key of each signing run by one
randomiser so that the aggregate verifies under the Action's randomised
validating key; it is defined in \S\ref{sec:rr-scheme} and constructed in
\S\ref{sec:rr-sign} (Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds}) with the randomiser
of \S\ref{sec:rr-randomiser}, and its correctness is
Theorem~\ref{thm:rr-correct}.

The Orchard pool is spend-only: no transaction increases its value, and every
Orchard-pool Action creates its note at the address of the note it consumes
(ZIP~258, Draft, ``Consensus rules from NU6.3 activation''; \emph{Ironwood
Guide}, \S``The Orchard protocol and its two pools''), so no value enters the
pool and, by consensus, only a party able to authorise spends for an address
can create an Orchard-pool note to it. ZIP~326 (Draft) forbids conforming
wallets to send Orchard-pool funds to a key derived with
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\) (ZIP~2005), which every key from
distributed key generation must use, so under that rule such a key holds no
Orchard-pool notes, a rule that
consensus does not enforce, while a dealer-shared key whose \(\ask\) is
derived from a spending key may spend notes of either pool; both facts are
established, with their sources, in \S\ref{sec:auth-keytree}.

The volume constructs FROST as RFC~9591 specifies it; key generation by a
trusted dealer and by a distributed protocol; the re-randomised variant for
RedPallas that ZIP~312 specifies, with its ciphersuite FROST(Pallas,
BLAKE2b-512); the proof that its output is an ordinary spend-authorisation
signature; the key-generation constraints of ZIP~2005; and the security of
each part, with its assumptions and status. ZIP~312 also defines the
ciphersuite FROST(Jubjub, BLAKE2b-512) for Sapling spend authorisation by
RedJubjub; it is named here and not developed. Transparent inputs,
transports and particular tools are outside the scope. The volume assumes the
\emph{Math Guide} and the \emph{Crypto Guide}, and cites the \emph{Ironwood
Guide} for RedPallas, randomised validating keys, the signature digest and the
construction of a transaction. It is non-normative: the protocol
specification, the ZIPs and RFC~9591 are authoritative.

The volume describes the protocol as specified for NU7 (\emph{Ironwood
Guide}, \S``Scope and status'', Definition ``Network upgrade''), whose
deployment ZIP~259 has status Draft; NU7 changes neither spend authorisation
nor the transaction formats on which the volume relies, version-\(5\) and
version-\(6\) transactions remaining valid (ZIP~259, Abstract). NU6.3 is
active, and its rules, including those of ZIP~2005 that ZIP~258 deploys, are
part of the protocol so described.

\subsection{Sources and their standing}\label{sec:intro-sources}

The \emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Scope and status'', Definition ``ZIP'', names
the ZIP statuses Draft and Reserved. A ZIP has status \emph{Proposed} when,
after Draft, it has been advanced upon consideration, feedback and rough
consensus of the community (ZIP~0, ``ZIP Status Field''). A ZIP of status
Draft, Proposed, Active, Implemented or Final carries normative text; a
Reserved, Withdrawn, Rejected or Obsolete ZIP carries none.

\begin{definition}[Classes of claims]\label{def:classes}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item A claim about the deployed protocol is placed in one of the two
  classes of the \emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Scope and status'', Definition
  ``Epistemic classes'', with their meaning unchanged. It is
  \emph{specified} when normative text of the protocol specification or of
  a ZIP fixes it. It is \emph{designed but unspecified} when the deployed
  construction is designed to satisfy it but neither the protocol
  specification, nor a ZIP, nor a published proof establishes it for the
  deployed instance; knowledge soundness of the Action proof is that
  volume's example.
\item \emph{Normative text} comprises the protocol specification, RFC~9591,
  an Informational RFC of the IRTF stream, and every ZIP of status Draft,
  Proposed, Active, Implemented or Final.
\item A claim about a construction outside the deployed protocol, namely
  FROST, its key generation and its re-randomised variant, to which no
  consensus rule refers, is \emph{specified} when normative text fixes it,
  \emph{established} when a published proof establishes it, stated with
  that proof's assumptions and the venue status of its source
  (\S\ref{sec:intro-sources}), \emph{proved here} when this volume proves
  it, under the hypotheses it states, and an \emph{open problem} when
  neither normative text, nor a published proof, nor this volume
  establishes it.
\item A construction itself is \emph{specified} when normative text fixes
  it; \emph{designed but unspecified} when a published design, or this
  volume, fixes it and no normative text does, any part first stated here,
  such as Construction~\ref{def:normalise}, being marked as the volume's
  own; and an \emph{open problem} when nothing fixes it.
\end{enumerate}
Every design-stage claim carries its class where it is stated;
Table~\ref{tab:status} collects them.
\end{definition}

The sources of the volume, each with its standing, are the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item D.~Connolly, C.~Komlo, I.~Goldberg and C.~A.~Wood, ``The Flexible
  Round-Optimized Schnorr Threshold (FROST) Protocol for Two-Round Schnorr
  Signatures'', RFC~9591, June 2024, a product of the IRTF Crypto Forum
  Research Group in the category Informational, not an Internet Standards
  Track specification, specifies two-round FROST signing over any
  prime-order group and hash, with ciphersuites for none of the Zcash
  groups, leaves key generation out of scope, gives a trusted dealer for
  completeness in its Appendix~C, and is normative text in the sense of
  Definition~\ref{def:classes}.
\item ZIP~312, ``FROST for Spend Authorization Multisignatures'', status
  Draft, category Wallet, specifies the re-randomised variant for Zcash as
  changes to RFC~9591 (randomiser generation, Round Two, and re-randomised
  signing, aggregation and share verification) together with the
  ciphersuites FROST(Jubjub, BLAKE2b-512) and FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512),
  names \(\ask\) as the key to be shared, requires key generation to be
  consistent with FROST without specifying one, and specifies no consensus
  rule.
\item ZIP~2005, ``Ironwood Quantum Recoverability'', status Proposed,
  category Consensus, whose key rules take effect with NU6.3, which ZIP~258
  (Draft) deploys, and stand in the protocol as specified for NU7, is used
  for its constraints on FROST key generation and the keys they produce:
  ``Usage with FROST'', its replacement text for \S\,4.2.3 ``Orchard Key
  Components'' (``Changes to the Protocol Specification''), and
  ``Deployment''.
\item C.~P.~L.~Gouv\^{e}a and C.~Komlo, ``Re-Randomized FROST'', IACR ePrint

  claims unforgeability of Rerandomized FROST in the random-oracle model
  under the algebraic one-more discrete logarithm assumption, with a proof
  that \S\ref{sec:sec-rerand} finds incomplete (Remark~\ref{rem:rr-gaps}),

  rests on it alone is stated as the preprint's, not as established.
\item C.~Komlo and I.~Goldberg, ``FROST: Flexible Round-Optimized Schnorr

  original scheme, its distributed key generation, in which every
  participant acts as a Feldman dealer and proves knowledge of its constant
  coefficient by a Schnorr proof (its Figure~1), and the original security
  argument.
\item M.~Bellare, S.~Tessaro and C.~Zhu, ``Stronger Security for

\item E.~Crites, C.~Komlo and M.~Maller, ``How to Prove Schnorr Assuming

\subsection{Notation}\label{sec:intro-notation}

Notation is additive throughout; the multiplicative forms of the papers are
converted at citation. The generic sections work in a group \(\G\) of prime
order \(q\) with generator \(B\) and identity \(\mathcal{O}\), whose discrete
logarithm problem and random oracles are those of the \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``The discrete logarithm problem'', Definition ``Discrete logarithm problem,
DLP'', and \S``The random oracle model''. The Pallas instance sets
\[
  \G = \Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}}), \qquad q = p_{\Vesta}, \qquad
  B = G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}
\]
(\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Fields, groups, and encodings'', and
\S``The spending key and the spend-side secrets'', Construction ``Spend
validating key and sign normalisation''). Scalar multiplication is written
\([k]P\), and scalars are elements of \(\Fq\). The star encoding
\(P^{\star}\), the encodings \(\mathrm{LE}_k\) and \(\mathsf{I2LEOSP}_k\),
concatenation \(\|\) and monospace byte-string literals are those of the
\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Fields, groups, and encodings''. The element and
scalar serialisations \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\), \(\mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}\) and their
inverses \(\mathsf{deser}_{\G}\), \(\mathsf{deser}_{\Fq}\) are components of
a ciphersuite (Definition~\ref{def:suite}).

\section{Threshold Schnorr signatures}\label{sec:ts}

This section fixes the setting that every later protocol assumes: the
participants, their identifiers, the signing sets and their Lagrange
coefficients; the threshold key material and the group information that
coefficient commitments determine; the syntax and correctness of a two-round
threshold Schnorr signature scheme with a Coordinator; and the channel types
and the static corruption model. Notation is that of
\S\ref{sec:intro-notation}.

\subsection{Participants, identifiers and signing sets}\label{sec:ts-ids}

\begin{definition}[Threshold parameters and signing sets]
\label{def:ts-params}
Let \(\G\) be a group of prime order \(q\) with generator \(B\) and scalar
field \(\Fq\) (\S\ref{sec:intro-notation}). \emph{Threshold parameters} are
integers \(t, n\) with
\[
  1 \le t \le n < q .
\]
There are \(n\) \emph{participants} \(P_1, \dots, P_n\). The
\emph{identifier} of \(P_i\) is \(i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\), read as an element
of \(\Fq\); identifiers are distinct and nonzero in \(\Fq\) because
\(n < q\). A \emph{signing set} is a set \(I \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}\) with
\(t \le \lvert I \rvert \le n\). In the notation of RFC~9591
(Table~\ref{tab:notation}), \(t\) is \(\mathrm{MIN\_PARTICIPANTS}\), \(n\) is
\(\mathrm{MAX\_PARTICIPANTS}\) and \(\lvert I \rvert\) is
\(\mathrm{NUM\_PARTICIPANTS}\).
\end{definition}

The constraints are those of RFC~9591, Section~5 (``Two-Round FROST Signing
Protocol''): the identifiers are distinct nonzero scalars in
\([1, \mathrm{MAX\_PARTICIPANTS}]\); the bound \(\mathrm{MAX\_PARTICIPANTS}\)
is less than the group order; and
\[
  \mathrm{MIN\_PARTICIPANTS} \le \mathrm{NUM\_PARTICIPANTS} \le
  \mathrm{MAX\_PARTICIPANTS} .
\]
The dealer of RFC~9591, Appendix~C (``Trusted
Dealer Key Generation''), shares at the identifiers \(1, \dots, n\). Every
statement below that uses only interpolation holds verbatim for any distinct
nonzero identifiers; the identifiers \(1, \dots, n\) are the ones RFC~9591
requires.

\begin{remark}[Shamir sharing]
Shamir sharing is cited, not re-derived: \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Secret sharing'', Construction ``Shamir \(t\)-of-\(n\) secret sharing''.
Its parameters \(1 \le t \le n < q\) and identifiers \(1, \dots, n\) are
those of Definition~\ref{def:ts-params}; its share symbol \(s_i\) is this
volume's \(\mathit{sk}_i\), and the generator \(G\) of the \emph{Crypto
Guide}'s Construction ``Feldman verifiable secret sharing'' is \(B\). Any \(t\)
shares determine the sharing polynomial and its constant term
(Lemma~\ref{lem:recombine} gives this for every signing set). Fewer than
\(t\) shares are jointly uniform whatever the secret (\emph{Crypto Guide},
Theorem ``Perfect secrecy below the threshold''). That theorem concerns the
shares alone. For key material shared by a dealer, once the coefficient
commitments of \S\ref{sec:ts-keys} are published, the point \([s]B\) is public
and secrecy below the threshold is computational: the view of \(t - 1\)
participants is computable from \([s]B\) and \(t - 1\) uniform scalars. Since
\([s]B\) is the group key, which is published in any case, nothing further
escapes (\emph{Crypto Guide}, Remark ``Leakage of Feldman sharing'').
Distributed key generation is treated in \S\ref{sec:sec-keygen}.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Lagrange coefficients of a signing set]
\label{def:lagrange}
Let \(I\) be a signing set and \(i \in I\). The \emph{Lagrange coefficient}
of \(i\) in \(I\) is
\[
  \lambda_{I,i} \;:=\; \prod_{j \in I,\; j \ne i} \frac{j}{j - i}
  \;\in\; \Fq ,
\]
whose denominators are nonzero because identifiers are distinct. For the
Lagrange basis polynomial
\(\ell_i(X) = \prod_{j \in I,\, j \ne i} (X - j)/(i - j)\) of the nodes
\(I\) (\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem ``Lagrange interpolation''),
\(\lambda_{I,i} = \ell_i(0)\), since \((0 - j)/(i - j) = j/(j - i)\); it is
the reconstruction weight of the \emph{Crypto Guide}'s Construction
``Shamir \(t\)-of-\(n\) secret sharing''. The specified computation takes
the list of identifiers of \(I\) and the identifier \(i\), and fails with
the error ``invalid parameters'' if \(i\) is absent from the list or some
identifier occurs in it more than once (RFC~9591, Section~4.2,
``Polynomials'').
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Recombination over a signing set]\label{lem:recombine}
Let \(I\) be a signing set. For every \(f \in \Fq[X]\) with
\(\deg f \le t - 1\),
\[
  \sum_{i \in I} \lambda_{I,i}\, f(i) \;=\; f(0) .
\]
In particular \(\sum_{i \in I} \lambda_{I,i} = 1\). The statement and its
proof hold verbatim for any finite set \(I\) of distinct nonzero elements
of \(\Fq\) with \(\lvert I \rvert \ge \deg f + 1\), the coefficients being
defined by the same product.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put \(d := \lvert I \rvert - 1\), so \(d \ge t - 1 \ge \deg f\). By
\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem ``Lagrange interpolation'', with nodes \(I\) and
targets \(f(i)\), the polynomial \(g := \sum_{i \in I} f(i)\, \ell_i\) has
degree at most \(d\) and satisfies \(g(i) = f(i)\) for every \(i \in I\),
that is at \(d + 1\) distinct points. The polynomial \(f\) also has degree
at most \(d\), so \(g = f\) by \emph{Math Guide}, Corollary ``Few points
determine a low-degree polynomial''. Evaluating at \(0\) and using
\(\ell_i(0) = \lambda_{I,i}\) (Definition~\ref{def:lagrange}) gives the
identity. The constant polynomial \(f = 1\) has degree \(0 \le t - 1\),
because \(t \ge 1\), and gives the sum one.
\end{proof}

The proof is given in place: the \emph{Math Guide}'s Proposition ``Lagrange
polynomials sum to one'' concerns its roots-of-unity domain and does not
apply to the nodes \(I\). ZIP~312, ``Rationale'', uses the sum
\(\sum_{i \in I} \lambda_{I,i} = 1\) and refers to an external note for its
proof; ePrint 2024/436, Section~5, justifies it in one line as the
interpolation of the constant polynomial \(1\).

The coefficients \(\lambda_{I,i}\) depend on the identifiers of \(I\) alone,
not on the shares or on the polynomial (Definition~\ref{def:lagrange}).
Hence the signing set is chosen per signing run, after key generation, by the
Coordinator of Definition~\ref{def:ts-scheme}, which determines the
participants, at least \(\mathrm{MIN\_PARTICIPANTS}\) in number (RFC~9591,
Section~5).

\subsection{Key material and group information}\label{sec:ts-keys}

\begin{definition}[Threshold key material]\label{def:key-material}
Let \((t, n)\) be threshold parameters. \emph{Threshold key material}
consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a \emph{sharing polynomial}
  \(f(X) = \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} a_k X^k \in \Fq[X]\) with
  \(f(0) = a_0 = s\), the \emph{signing key};
\item for each identifier \(j\), the \emph{secret share}
  \((j, \mathit{sk}_j)\) with \(\mathit{sk}_j = f(j)\), held by \(P_j\)
  alone;
\item the \emph{coefficient commitments} \(A_k := [a_k]B\) for
  \(k = 0, \dots, t - 1\);
\item the \emph{group information}
  \((\mathit{PK}, (\mathit{PK}_j)_{j=1}^{n})\), with \emph{group key}
  \(\mathit{PK} := [s]B\) and \emph{verification shares}
  \(\mathit{PK}_j := [\mathit{sk}_j]B\), held by every party, the
  Coordinator included.
\end{enumerate}
Shares \((\mathit{sk}_j)_{j=1}^{n} \in \Fq^n\) and group information
\((\mathit{PK}, (\mathit{PK}_j)_{j=1}^{n}) \in \G^{n+1}\) are \emph{valid
key material} if some \(f \in \Fq[X]\) with \(\deg f \le t - 1\) has
\(f(j) = \mathit{sk}_j\) for every \(j \in \{1, \dots, n\}\),
\(\mathit{PK} = [f(0)]B\) and \(\mathit{PK}_j = [\mathit{sk}_j]B\) for
every \(j\). Such \(f\) is unique, since \(n \ge t\) (\emph{Math Guide},
Corollary ``Few points determine a low-degree polynomial''). No party is
given \(s\) or \(f\); the definition names them. Valid key material is the
output of both key generations (\S\ref{sec:keygen}) and the input of
signing.
\end{definition}

The components are those of RFC~9591, Section~5: each participant is
configured with its identifier, the share \(\mathit{sk}_i = f(i)\) and
\(\mathit{PK}_i = [\mathit{sk}_i]B\); the group information consists of
\(\mathit{PK}\) and \(\mathit{PK}_i\) for each \(i\) in
\([1, \mathrm{MAX\_PARTICIPANTS}]\).

\begin{lemma}[Group information from coefficient commitments]
\label{lem:group-info}
Let \(f = \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} a_k X^k\) and \(A_k = [a_k]B\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The group key is \(\mathit{PK} = [f(0)]B = A_0\), and for every
  identifier \(j\)
  \[
    \mathit{PK}_j \;=\; [f(j)]B \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} [j^k] A_k .
  \]
  Every party holding \((A_0, \dots, A_{t-1})\) therefore computes the
  group information from the commitments alone.
\item[(ii)] Let \((A_0, \dots, A_{t-1}) \in \G^t\), however chosen, and let
  \(\mathit{sk}_1, \dots, \mathit{sk}_n \in \Fq\) satisfy
  \([\mathit{sk}_j]B = \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} [j^k] A_k\) for every
  \(j \in \{1, \dots, n\}\). Then \((\mathit{sk}_j)_{j=1}^{n}\), with the
  group information computed by (i), is valid key material
  (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}) for the unique polynomial
  \(f^{*} = \sum_{k} a^{*}_k X^k\) with \([a^{*}_k]B = A_k\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Part (i) is the identity displayed in \emph{Crypto Guide}, Construction
``Feldman verifiable secret sharing'', where it follows from the
homomorphism \(a \mapsto [a]B\); that construction's \(G\) and \(\Phi_k\)
are this volume's \(B\) and \(A_k\). For (ii), by \emph{Crypto Guide},
Proposition ``What the Feldman check certifies'', the vector
\((A_0, \dots, A_{t-1})\) determines a unique polynomial \(f^{*}\) of
degree at most \(t - 1\), and each passing check gives
\(\mathit{sk}_j = f^{*}(j)\). Then \(A_0 = [f^{*}(0)]B\) and
\(\sum_k [j^k] A_k = [\mathit{sk}_j]B\), which are the conditions of
Definition~\ref{def:key-material}.
\end{proof}

RFC~9591, Appendix~C.2 (``Verifiable Secret Sharing''), derives the group
information from the commitment vector in this way. Both key generations of
\S\ref{sec:keygen} cite the lemma.

\subsection{Two-round threshold signature schemes}\label{sec:ts-syntax}

\begin{remark}[Prerequisites]
The syntax of a signature scheme, existential unforgeability under
chosen-message attack and strong unforgeability are those of \emph{Crypto
Guide}, \S``Syntax and security goal'' (Definitions ``Digital signature
scheme'', ``Existential unforgeability under chosen-message attack'' and
``Strong unforgeability''), which admits correctness with overwhelming
probability. The single-party Schnorr signature is \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``From identification to signature via the Fiat--Shamir transform'',
Construction ``Schnorr signature'', perfectly correct by its Proposition
``Perfect correctness''. In additive notation with generator \(B\), signing
key \(s\) and public key \(\mathit{PK} = [s]B\), a signature on a message
\(m\) is
\[
  r \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq, \quad
  R = [r]B, \quad
  c = H(R \,\|\, \mathit{PK} \,\|\, m), \quad
  z = r + c s, \quad
  \sigma = (R, z),
\]
and verification accepts \((R, z)\) if and only if
\[
  [z]B \;=\; R + [c]\,\mathit{PK} .
\]
The \emph{Crypto Guide}'s response \(s\) is written \(z\) here, \(s\) being
the signing key. The challenge input order (nonce commitment, public key,
message) is that of RFC~9591, Section~4.6 (``Signature Challenge
Computation''), and of RedPallas; the hash and the encodings of FROST are
fixed by the ciphersuite (Definition~\ref{def:suite}). Discrete logarithms
and random oracles are those of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The discrete
logarithm problem'' and \S``The random oracle model'', as
\S\ref{sec:intro-notation} records.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Two-round threshold signature scheme with a Coordinator]
\label{def:ts-scheme}
Let \((t, n)\) be threshold parameters (Definition~\ref{def:ts-params}). A
\emph{two-round threshold signature scheme with a Coordinator} is run by
the participants \(P_1, \dots, P_n\) and one \emph{Coordinator}: a role,
which a participant may also play, that uses no share of the signing key
and no nonce, and that chooses the signing set, relays messages, aggregates
and publishes the signature. It consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Key generation}, a dealer algorithm or a protocol, which outputs
  valid key material (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}): \(\mathit{sk}_j\)
  to \(P_j\), and the group information to every party.
\item \emph{Round One}, run by each \(P_i\) independently of any message and
  of any signing set, which outputs a public \emph{commitment}
  \(\mathit{com}_i\), sent to the Coordinator, and a secret \emph{state}
  \(\mathit{st}_i\), which \(P_i\) keeps and uses in at most one Round Two.
\item \emph{Round Two}. The Coordinator chooses a signing set \(I\) and a
  message \(m\), and sends the request \((m, (\mathit{com}_j)_{j \in I})\)
  to each \(P_i\) with \(i \in I\). Participant \(P_i\) either returns a
  \emph{signature share} \(z_i\), computed from \(\mathit{sk}_i\),
  \(\mathit{st}_i\), the request and the group information, or aborts; in
  both cases it deletes \(\mathit{st}_i\).
\item \emph{Aggregation}, by which the Coordinator maps \(m\), the
  commitment list \((\mathit{com}_j)_{j \in I}\) and the shares
  \((z_j)_{j \in I}\) to a signature \(\sigma\) or to \(\bot\).
\item \emph{Verification}, the single-party Schnorr verification of a
  signature on \(m\) under the group key \(\mathit{PK}\).
\end{enumerate}
The scheme is \emph{correct} if, for all valid key material output by key
generation, every signing set \(I\) and every message \(m\), a run in which
every party is honest outputs either \(\bot\) or a signature that
verification accepts under \(\mathit{PK}\), and outputs \(\bot\) with
probability negligible in the security parameter.
\end{definition}

The parties and the rounds are those of RFC~9591, Section~5: the
Coordinator determines the participants, coordinates the rounds, aggregates
the signature shares and publishes the signature; Round One produces
commitments; Round Two produces a signature share over the message and the
commitments that the Coordinator supplies. Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct}
bounds the probability of \(\bot\) for FROST. Unforgeability notions are
defined in \S\ref{sec:sec}, before any use.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the syntax]
The two-round form is the one RFC~9591 specifies (Section~5). Single-round
signing with commitments preprocessed in batches (ePrint 2020/852) is out of
the scope of RFC~9591 (Section~1, ``Introduction'') and of this volume.
\end{remark}

The parties compute \((R, z)\) jointly, and no step computes \(s\) or \(r\).
Round Two binds each share to the message and the whole commitment list
(Construction~\ref{def:round2}); unforgeability under concurrent runs is
Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf}, under its assumptions
(Remark~\ref{rem:binding}).

\subsection{Channels and corruption}\label{sec:ts-model}

\begin{definition}[Channels]\label{def:channels}
Every message of every protocol in this volume travels on a channel of one
of the following four types, and the protocol names the type.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] An \emph{authenticated channel with reliable delivery}: the
  receiver learns the sender and the unmodified message; delivery is
  assured, as completion of the protocol requires; the content is visible
  to an observer of the network. It carries the signing messages: the
  Round One commitments, the Round Two request and the returned shares.
\item[(b)] A \emph{confidential and authenticated channel}: a channel of
  type (a) whose content is hidden from every party but its endpoints. It
  carries the Round Two request of the re-randomised variant with its
  confidential inputs, named in Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds}.
\item[(c)] A \emph{mutually authenticated confidential channel}: a channel
  of type (b) in which each endpoint is authenticated to the other. It
  carries dealt shares and the evaluations of round two of distributed key
  generation.
\item[(d)] \emph{Consistent broadcast}: every honest recipient outputs the
  same value from the sender, whether or not the sender is honest, or every
  honest recipient aborts. If the sender is honest, an honest recipient
  that does not abort outputs the value sent. It carries the coefficient
  commitments and the
  proofs of knowledge of key generation.
\end{enumerate}
Realisations are not described. For what authentication provides against
an adversary that controls the network, see \emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Active attacks and the role of authentication''.
\end{definition}

Each type is tied to its source. Type (a) is that of RFC~9591, Section~5,
which assumes reliable delivery for completion and an authenticated channel,
confidentiality not being required; Section~7 (``Security Considerations'')
adds that signing may run over a public channel provided it is authenticated
and reliable. Authentication is what identifies a misbehaving participant:
without it, a party masquerading as another causes only an invalid signature
(RFC~9591, Section~5). Type (b) is that of ZIP~312, ``Round Two - Signature
Share Generation'', which requires a confidential and authenticated channel
and records the difference from RFC~9591. Type (c) is that of RFC~9591,
Appendix~C, which requires a mutually authenticated secure channel with
confidentiality and integrity for dealt shares, and of ePrint 2020/852,
Figure~1, in which round two of key generation sends the evaluations securely.
Type (d) is required by RFC~9591, Appendix~C, under which participants must
abort if they do not hold the same view of the commitment vector, and by
ePrint 2020/852, Figure~1, which broadcasts commitments and proofs.

\begin{definition}[Static corruption]\label{def:corruption}
Before key generation the adversary fixes a set
\(C \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}\) with \(\lvert C \rvert \le t - 1\). It
receives the entire state of each \(P_j\) with \(j \in C\) throughout, and
chooses their messages. It may in addition control the Coordinator, which
holds no share of the signing key and no nonce; in the re-randomised variant
it also holds the randomiser and the transaction data
(Definition~\ref{def:rr-threat}). It observes and schedules the network,
subject to the guarantees of Definition~\ref{def:channels}. The set \(C\)
never changes, and every other participant follows the protocol.
\end{definition}

A malicious Coordinator can deny service, by withholding requests, the
aggregate or its publication, and can falsely report a participant as
misbehaving; no honest party can prevent either, since FROST provides no
robustness (RFC~9591, Section~7; ePrint 2020/852, Section~5, ``Signature
Aggregator Role''). What a malicious Coordinator cannot do is stated in
\S\ref{sec:sec}, either as a theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf},
Theorem~\ref{thm:tsa-conditional}) or as an open problem
(Remark~\ref{rem:authority-status}); Definition~\ref{def:corruption}
asserts none of it. The re-randomised variant further trusts the
Coordinator and every share holder with the privacy of the transaction
(Definition~\ref{def:rr-threat}). Adaptive corruption, metadata privacy and
network privacy are out of scope. The model is that of RFC~9591,
Section~7, under which the Coordinator and at most
\(\mathrm{MIN\_PARTICIPANTS} - 1\) participants may be corrupted and
robustness and metadata protection are not goals, and of ZIP~312, ``Threat
Model''.

\section{Key generation}\label{sec:keygen}

This section states the status of key generation in RFC~9591 and ZIP~312
and the two protocols that output the threshold key material of
Definition~\ref{def:key-material}: key generation by a trusted dealer and
distributed key generation. Each is proved to output valid key material and
is classified by Definition~\ref{def:classes}.

\subsection{Trusted-dealer key generation}\label{sec:keygen-dealer}

RFC~9591 places key generation out of its scope and, for completeness,
specifies key generation by a trusted dealer in Appendix~C (``Trusted Dealer
Key Generation''); Section~1 (``Introduction'') says so. Its Section~5
(``Two-Round FROST Signing Protocol'') names the two mechanisms by which the
key material of Definition~\ref{def:key-material} may be configured, a
trusted dealer and a distributed key generation protocol, and specifies
neither beyond that appendix. ZIP~312 specifies no key generation
(``Non-requirements''). Its ``Key Generation'' requires key generation to be
consistent with FROST, referring to the dealer appendix of RFC~9591 for
guidance, and names the spend authorising key \(\ask\) as the key to be
shared. It records that \(\ask\) is usually derived from the spending key
\(\mathsf{sk}\) but need not be; that not deriving it permits distributed key
generation, since the key so generated is unpredictable; and that not
deriving it prevents recovery of the secret from a seed phrase, which the ZIP
notes may be desirable for FROST. The security analysis of Bellare, Tessaro
and Zhu (ePrint 2022/833, in its current revision, whose preliminary version
is part of the analysis RFC~9591 cites) models key generation as an
algorithm run by a trusted party. Two protocols output key material: the
dealer of RFC~9591, Appendix~C, in this subsection, and the distributed key
generation of ePrint 2020/852, Figure~1, in \S\ref{sec:keygen-dkg}.

\begin{assumption}[Honest dealer]\label{ass:dealer}
The dealer runs Construction~\ref{def:dealer} as written:
\begin{enumerate}
\item it samples \(s\) and the coefficients \(a_1, \dots, a_{t-1}\)
  uniformly and independently from its own randomness;
\item it reveals \(s\), the coefficients and the shares only as the
  construction prescribes, the share of \(P_j\) to \(P_j\) alone;
\item it deletes \(s\), the coefficients and the shares once the shares are
  distributed.
\end{enumerate}
The adversary of Definition~\ref{def:corruption} does not control the
dealer and never obtains its state.
\end{assumption}

The three clauses are the trust requirements of RFC~9591, Appendix~C: the
dealer is trusted to generate good randomness, to delete secret values after
distributing the shares, and to keep secret values confidential; the
appendix further requires that the dealer delete the secret key and the
secret key shares upon completion.

\begin{construction}[Dealer sharing of a threshold key]\label{def:dealer}
Let \((t, n)\) be threshold parameters with identifiers \(1, \dots, n\)
(Definition~\ref{def:ts-params}), over the group \(\G\) of prime order \(q\)
with generator \(B\) and scalar field \(\Fq\). The dealer proceeds as
follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It samples the signing key \(s \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq\) uniformly and
  \(a_1, \dots, a_{t-1} \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq\) independently and uniformly.
\item It sets \(a_0 := s\) and
  \[
    f(X) \;:=\; \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} a_k X^k \;\in\; \Fq[X] ,
  \]
  a sharing of \(s\) by \emph{Crypto Guide}, Construction ``Shamir
  \(t\)-of-\(n\) secret sharing''.
\item For \(j = 1, \dots, n\) it sends the secret share
  \((j, \mathit{sk}_j)\), with \(\mathit{sk}_j := f(j)\), to \(P_j\) over a
  mutually authenticated confidential channel
  (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (c)).
\item It sends the coefficient commitments
  \[
    A_k \;:=\; [a_k]B , \qquad k = 0, \dots, t - 1 ,
  \]
  to every party, the participants and the Coordinator, by consistent
  broadcast (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (d)).
\item It deletes \(s\), the coefficients and the shares.
\end{enumerate}
Participant \(P_j\) aborts if its view of \((A_0, \dots, A_{t-1})\) differs
from that of another party, and aborts if
\[
  [\mathit{sk}_j]B \;\ne\; \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} [j^k] A_k .
\]
Every party computes the group information
\[
  \mathit{PK} \;:=\; A_0 , \qquad
  \mathit{PK}_i \;:=\; \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} [i^k] A_k
  \quad (i = 1, \dots, n)
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:group-info}; by this computation every party learns every
verification share. \emph{Output}: the share \((j, \mathit{sk}_j)\) to
\(P_j\), and \((\mathit{PK}, \mathit{PK}_1, \dots, \mathit{PK}_n)\) to every
party. The dealer knows \(s\). Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}):
specified.
\end{construction}

The construction is that of RFC~9591, Appendix~C, with its Appendices~C.1
(``Shamir Secret Sharing'') and C.2 (``Verifiable Secret Sharing''). The
secret is generated uniformly at random and must be derived from at least
\(\mathrm{Ns}\) bytes of entropy, \(\mathrm{Ns}\) being the length of an
encoded scalar; Appendix~D (``Random Scalar Generation'') gives the
generation of uniform scalars. The shares are the evaluations of \(f\) at
\(1, \dots, n\), the secret prepended to the coefficients, and an identifier
is never \(0\) (Appendix~C.1). The commitment is the vector of the
\([a_k]B\); a participant must abort if it does not hold the same view of it
as the other participants, which a secure broadcast channel ensures
(Appendix~C), and must abort if its share fails the check, the failure being
investigated out of band (Appendix~C.2). The channel for shares is mutually
authenticated and provides confidentiality and integrity (Appendix~C).
Consistent broadcast realises the
first abort rule: under Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (d), every honest
recipient outputs the same vector or every honest recipient aborts.

\begin{proposition}[Validity of dealt key material]\label{prop:dealer-valid}
Let the commitments of Construction~\ref{def:dealer} travel by consistent
broadcast (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (d)).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Under Assumption~\ref{ass:dealer}, if the broadcast does not
  abort, no honest participant aborts, and the shares
  \((j, \mathit{sk}_j)\) with the group information
  \((\mathit{PK}, \mathit{PK}_1, \dots, \mathit{PK}_n)\) are valid key
  material for \(s\) (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}), with
  \(\mathit{PK} = [s]B\).
\item[(ii)] Without Assumption~\ref{ass:dealer}, the commitments determine a
  unique polynomial \(f^{*} = \sum_{k} a^{*}_k X^k\) of degree at most
  \(t - 1\) with \([a^{*}_k]B = A_k\) for every \(k\); a participant whose
  check passes holds \(\mathit{sk}_j = f^{*}(j)\); and every honest party
  computes the same group information, that of \(f^{*}\). The shares that
  pass and the group information are thus consistent with the one
  polynomial \(f^{*}\), whose constant term \(\log_B A_0\) the dealer
  chooses freely.
\item[(iii)] Under Assumption~\ref{ass:dealer}, for every set \(C\) of fewer
  than \(t\) participants there is a randomised algorithm that, given
  \(\mathit{PK} = [s]B\) alone, outputs a tuple distributed identically to
  the joint view of \(C\): its shares and the commitments. The commitments
  reveal \([s]B\) and nothing further to \(C\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) By steps (1) to (3), \(\mathit{sk}_j = f(j)\) for the polynomial \(f\)
of degree at most \(t - 1\) with \(f(0) = s\). Each honest share passes its
check by the identity \(\sum_k [j^k] A_k = [f(j)]B\) displayed in
\emph{Crypto Guide}, Construction ``Feldman verifiable secret sharing'', and
consistent broadcast from an honest dealer that does not abort gives every
honest party the dealer's vector, so no honest participant aborts.
Lemma~\ref{lem:group-info}, part (i), gives
\(\mathit{PK} = A_0 = [f(0)]B = [s]B\) and
\(\mathit{PK}_j = [f(j)]B = [\mathit{sk}_j]B\), which are the conditions of
Definition~\ref{def:key-material}.

(ii) Consistent broadcast gives every honest party the same vector
\((A_0, \dots, A_{t-1})\), or every honest party aborts. By \emph{Crypto
Guide}, Proposition ``What the Feldman check certifies'', that vector
determines the unique \(f^{*}\) with \(a^{*}_k = \log_B A_k\), a check
passes if and only if \(\mathit{sk}_j = f^{*}(j)\), and nothing constrains
\(f^{*}(0)\). Lemma~\ref{lem:group-info}, part (i), applied to \(f^{*}\),
gives the common group information \(\mathit{PK} = [f^{*}(0)]B\) and
\(\mathit{PK}_j = [f^{*}(j)]B\).

(iii) Suppose first \(\lvert C \rvert = t - 1\). By \emph{Crypto Guide},
Theorem ``Perfect secrecy below the threshold'', the shares
\((\mathit{sk}_i)_{i \in C}\) are jointly uniform on \(\Fq^{t-1}\)
whatever \(s\) is. Given the shares, the points
\([f(0)]B = \mathit{PK}\) and \([f(i)]B = [\mathit{sk}_i]B\), \(i \in C\),
are \(t\) values of the map \(x \mapsto [f(x)]B\) at distinct points; since
each \(A_k\) is a fixed \(\Fq\)-linear combination of these values
(interpolation in the exponent), the commitments are a function of
\(\mathit{PK}\) and the shares. The algorithm samples \(t - 1\) uniform
scalars as the shares and computes the commitments so; this is the argument
of \emph{Crypto Guide}, Remark ``Leakage of Feldman sharing''. If
\(\lvert C \rvert < t - 1\), then, since \(t \le n\), some set
\(C' \supseteq C\) of \(t - 1\) participants exists; the view of \(C\) is a
projection of that of \(C'\), and the algorithm for \(C'\) followed by the
projection serves for \(C\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Key material of an Orchard-protocol key]
For an Orchard-protocol key the shared secret is \(\ask\) (ZIP~312,
``Key Generation''). The output of Construction~\ref{def:dealer} then passes
through Construction~\ref{def:normalise} in \S\ref{sec:auth-norm} and is
completed by Construction~\ref{def:orchard-key}. ZIP~312 also admits a
dealer that shares an \(\ask\) derived from a spending key; that \(\ask\) is
not sampled as in Assumption~\ref{ass:dealer}, clause (1), and
\S\ref{sec:sec-keygen} treats both cases.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Distributed key generation}\label{sec:keygen-dkg}

\begin{assumption}[The proof-of-knowledge hash]\label{ass:dkg-hash}
The challenge hash
\[
  H_{\mathrm{dkg}} \colon \{1, \dots, n\} \times \{0,1\}^{*} \times \G
  \times \G \;\to\; \Fq
\]
of Construction~\ref{def:dkg}, applied to an injective encoding of its input
(identifier, context string, point, point), is modelled as a random oracle
(\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The random oracle model'') independent of every
other hash of the volume, in particular of the ciphersuite hashes of
Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash}.
\end{assumption}

An instantiation achieves the independence by domain separation: a random
oracle restricted to prefix-free tags yields independent random oracles
(\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Domain separation and personalisation'',
Proposition ``Domain separation yields independent oracles''). No ZIP fixes
\(H_{\mathrm{dkg}}\) or the context string for the Pallas ciphersuite of
ZIP~312: that ciphersuite defines the hashes \(H_1\) to \(H_5\) and
\(H_R\) only (``FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512)''), and key generation is outside
the ZIP's scope (``Key Generation''). The volume therefore takes
\(H_{\mathrm{dkg}}\) as the named oracle of Assumption~\ref{ass:dkg-hash}.

\begin{construction}[Pedersen key generation with proofs of knowledge]
\label{def:dkg}
Let \((t, n)\) be threshold parameters (Definition~\ref{def:ts-params}) and
\(H_{\mathrm{dkg}}\) the hash of Assumption~\ref{ass:dkg-hash}. Before Round
One the participants agree, by means outside the construction, on a context
string \(\Phi \in \{0,1\}^{*}\) unique to the ceremony.

\emph{Round One}, participant \(P_i\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item It samples \(a_{i,0}, \dots, a_{i,t-1} \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq\)
  independently and uniformly and sets
  \(f_i(X) := \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} a_{i,k} X^k\).
\item It proves knowledge of \(a_{i,0}\): it samples
  \(\nu_i \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq\) and sets
  \begin{gather*}
    R_i := [\nu_i]B , \qquad
    c_i := H_{\mathrm{dkg}}\bigl(i, \Phi, [a_{i,0}]B, R_i\bigr) , \\
    \mu_i := \nu_i + a_{i,0}\, c_i , \qquad
    \sigma_i := (R_i, \mu_i) .
  \end{gather*}
\item It computes \(A_{i,k} := [a_{i,k}]B\) for \(k = 0, \dots, t - 1\).
\item It sends \((A_{i,0}, \dots, A_{i,t-1}, \sigma_i)\) to every other
  party, the Coordinator included, by consistent broadcast
  (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (d)).
\item For every \(\ell \ne i\) it computes
  \(c_\ell := H_{\mathrm{dkg}}(\ell, \Phi, A_{\ell,0}, R_\ell)\) and aborts
  unless
  \[
    R_\ell \;=\; [\mu_\ell]B - [c_\ell] A_{\ell,0} .
  \]
  If every check passes, it deletes the proofs \(\sigma_\ell\).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Round Two}, participant \(P_i\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item It sends \(f_i(\ell)\) to each \(P_\ell\), \(\ell \ne i\), over a
  mutually authenticated confidential channel
  (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (c)), keeps \(f_i(i)\), and deletes
  \(f_i\) and the values sent.
\item For every \(\ell \ne i\) it aborts unless
  \[
    [f_\ell(i)]B \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} [i^k] A_{\ell,k} .
  \]
\item It sets \(\mathit{sk}_i := \sum_{\ell=1}^{n} f_\ell(i)\) and deletes
  the \(f_\ell(i)\).
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Group information}. Every party sets
\(A_k := \sum_{\ell=1}^{n} A_{\ell,k}\) for \(k = 0, \dots, t - 1\) and
computes
\[
  \mathit{PK} \;:=\; A_0 , \qquad
  \mathit{PK}_j \;:=\; \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} [j^k] A_k
  \quad (j = 1, \dots, n)
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:group-info}.

\emph{Output}: \((i, \mathit{sk}_i)\) to \(P_i\), and
\((\mathit{PK}, \mathit{PK}_1, \dots, \mathit{PK}_n)\) to every party. A
failed check leads to abort only; its cause is investigated out of band.
\end{construction}

The construction is that of ePrint 2020/852, Figure~1, in additive notation:
the paper's \(g\), \(\phi_{i,k}\), \(k\), \(s_i\), \(Y_i\) and \(Y\) are
\(B\), \(A_{i,k}\), \(\nu_i\), \(\mathit{sk}_i\), \(\mathit{PK}_i\) and
\(\mathit{PK}\). The paper's Section~5.1 describes it as Pedersen's protocol,
\(n\) parallel runs of \emph{Crypto Guide}, Construction ``Feldman verifiable
secret sharing'', one with each participant as dealer, each share being the
sum of the \(n\) shares received. Round One, step (2), is the Fiat--Shamir
transform of \emph{Crypto Guide}, Construction ``Schnorr identification''
(\S``The Schnorr identification protocol''; \S``The Fiat--Shamir transform:
from interactive to non-interactive''), with statement \(A_{i,0}\), witness
\(a_{i,0}\), and the prover's identifier and \(\Phi\) bound into the
challenge; the verification equation of step (5) is that of the
identification protocol rearranged. ePrint 2020/852, Figure~1, binds the
context string into the challenge to prevent replay of proofs across
ceremonies, a design claim that no cited source proves
(Remark~\ref{rem:dkg-design}). The last part of Figure~1 computes every
verification share from the broadcast commitments, which is how parties
other than \(P_j\) learn \(\mathit{PK}_j\).

Consistent broadcast of Round One is a hypothesis of the construction:
ePrint 2020/852, Section~5.1, assumes that participants hold a consistent
view of the commitments and does not provide it. A participant that sent
different commitment vectors to different parties would lead them to compute
different group information: different verification shares, and different
group keys when the constant terms \(A_{\ell,0}\) differ.

\begin{lemma}[Sum of dealt sharings]\label{lem:dkg-sum}
Let \(f_1, \dots, f_n \in \Fq[X]\) have degree at most \(t - 1\), with
coefficients \(a_{\ell,k}\) and coefficient commitments
\(A_{\ell,k} = [a_{\ell,k}]B\). Then \(f := \sum_{\ell=1}^{n} f_\ell\) has
degree at most \(t - 1\), constant term
\(f(0) = \sum_{\ell} a_{\ell,0}\), and coefficient commitments
\[
  \Bigl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{n} a_{\ell,k}\Bigr] B
  \;=\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{n} A_{\ell,k} ,
  \qquad k = 0, \dots, t - 1 ;
\]
and \(f(j) = \sum_{\ell} f_\ell(j)\) for every \(j \in \Fq\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Polynomial addition adds coefficients and does not raise the degree, so the
coefficient of \(X^k\) in \(f\) is \(\sum_\ell a_{\ell,k}\), zero for
\(k \ge t\). Evaluation at a point is additive in the polynomial. The map
\(a \mapsto [a]B\) is a group homomorphism \(\Fq \to \G\), which gives the
commitments.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Validity of distributed key material]
\label{prop:dkg-valid}
Consider an execution of Construction~\ref{def:dkg} without abort in which
the Round One messages travel by consistent broadcast. For each \(\ell\) let
\(f^{*}_\ell\) be the unique polynomial of degree at most \(t - 1\) whose
coefficient commitments are \(A_{\ell,0}, \dots, A_{\ell,t-1}\), let
\(f^{*} := \sum_{\ell} f^{*}_\ell\), and let
\(\mathit{sk}_j := f^{*}(j)\) for every identifier \(j\). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] every honest participant \(P_i\) outputs
  \(\mathit{sk}_i = f^{*}(i)\);
\item[(ii)] every honest party computes the same group information,
  \(\mathit{PK} = [f^{*}(0)]B\) and \(\mathit{PK}_j = [f^{*}(j)]B\);
\item[(iii)] the shares \(\mathit{sk}_j\) with this group information are
  valid key material (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}) for the signing
  key
  \[
    s \;=\; f^{*}(0) \;=\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{n} \log_B A_{\ell,0} .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
If every participant is honest, \(f^{*}_\ell = f_\ell\) for every \(\ell\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The polynomials \(f^{*}_\ell\) exist and are unique by \emph{Crypto Guide},
Proposition ``What the Feldman check certifies''. An honest \(P_i\)
broadcasts the commitments of its own \(f_i\), so \(f_i = f^{*}_i\) and its
kept value is \(f_i(i) = f^{*}_i(i)\). Each received \(f_\ell(i)\),
\(\ell \ne i\), passed the check of Round Two, step (2), so equals
\(f^{*}_\ell(i)\) by the same proposition. Hence
\(\mathit{sk}_i = \sum_\ell f^{*}_\ell(i) = f^{*}(i)\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:dkg-sum}, which is (i). Consistent broadcast gives every
honest party the same \(A_{\ell,k}\), hence the same
\(A_k = \sum_\ell A_{\ell,k}\), which by Lemma~\ref{lem:dkg-sum} are the
coefficient commitments of \(f^{*}\), of degree at most \(t - 1\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:group-info}, part (i), applied to \(f^{*}\) gives
\(\mathit{PK} = [f^{*}(0)]B\) and
\(\mathit{PK}_j = [f^{*}(j)]B = [\mathit{sk}_j]B\), which is (ii); with the
shares \(\mathit{sk}_j = f^{*}(j)\) these are the conditions of
Definition~\ref{def:key-material}, which is (iii), and
\(f^{*}(0) = \sum_\ell f^{*}_\ell(0) = \sum_\ell \log_B A_{\ell,0}\). If
every participant is honest, each broadcasts the commitments of its own
polynomial, so \(f^{*}_\ell = f_\ell\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Rogue keys and robustness]\label{rem:dkg-design}
The statements of this remark are design claims of ePrint 2020/852; none is
a theorem. The proofs of knowledge of Round One, step (2), are the change
from Pedersen's protocol: the paper adds them against rogue-key attacks, in
which a participant chooses its commitment as a function of the others'
commitments to bias the group key, in the setting \(t \ge n/2\)
(Section~5.1). No source cited in this volume proves
Construction~\ref{def:dkg} secure, or proves FROST as RFC~9591 specifies it
unforgeable with it; \S\ref{sec:sec-keygen} states what is known
(Remark~\ref{rem:dkg-open}). The protocol is not robust: any failed proof or
share check aborts the whole execution and its cause is investigated out of
band (Figure~1; Section~3), so one participant can prevent key generation.
\end{remark}

Construction~\ref{def:dkg} is designed but unspecified for Zcash
(Definition~\ref{def:classes}). The protocol is published (ePrint 2020/852,
Figure~1), but no RFC or ZIP specifies a distributed key generation:
RFC~9591 places key generation out of scope (Section~1), and ZIP~312
specifies none (``Key Generation''). ZIP~2005 (Proposed), ``Usage with
FROST'', names the FROST distributed key generation protocol without a
specification behind the reference; the revision of ZIP~312 proposed in pull
request~895 names a different key-generation protocol, so its adoption would not
specify this
one. Its Zcash instantiation, the hash \(H_{\mathrm{dkg}}\) and the context
string \(\Phi\), is fixed by no ZIP (Assumption~\ref{ass:dkg-hash}).

\section{Signing and aggregation}\label{sec:sign}

This section states FROST signing as RFC~9591 specifies it, in execution
order: the ciphersuite interface with its hash roles and the encoding of a
commitment list; Round One, which draws hedged nonces and commits to them;
Round Two, which binds each signature share to the message and to the whole
commitment list; aggregation with share verification; the correctness of an
honest run; and the single use of nonces, with the exact condition under
which reuse of a nonce pair exposes a share. Threshold parameters, signing
sets, Lagrange coefficients and key material are those of \S\ref{sec:ts};
the key material is the output of either key generation of
\S\ref{sec:keygen}.

\subsection{Ciphersuites and encodings}\label{sec:sign-suite}

\begin{definition}[Ciphersuite]\label{def:suite}
A \emph{ciphersuite} fixes the following data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] A group \(\G\) of prime order \(q\), written additively, with
  identity \(\mathcal{O}\) and generator \(B\), and its scalar field
  \(\Fq\). Write \(\G^{*} := \G \setminus \{\mathcal{O}\}\).
\item[(b)] An \emph{element serialisation} \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\), mapping
  either every element of \(\G\) or every element of \(\G^{*}\) injectively
  to a byte string of fixed length \(N_{\mathrm{e}}\), and an \emph{element
  deserialisation} \(\mathsf{deser}_{\G}\), which maps a byte string to an
  element of \(\G^{*}\) or fails. It fails on every string that is not the
  canonical encoding \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(P)\) of some \(P \in \G\), and on
  the encoding of \(\mathcal{O}\) where one exists; on every other string
  it inverts \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\). The serialisation is \emph{total} if its
  domain is \(\G\).
\item[(c)] A \emph{scalar serialisation} \(\mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}\), mapping
  \(\Fq\) injectively to byte strings of fixed length \(N_{\mathrm{s}}\),
  and a \emph{scalar deserialisation} \(\mathsf{deser}_{\Fq}\), which fails
  on every string whose integer value is not below \(q\) and inverts
  \(\mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}\) otherwise.
\item[(d)] Hash functions by role:
  \[
    H_1, H_2, H_3 \colon \{0,1\}^{8*} \to \Fq , \qquad
    H_4, H_5 \colon \{0,1\}^{8*} \to \{0,1\}^{8 N_{\mathrm{h}}} ,
  \]
  where \(\{0,1\}^{8*}\) denotes the byte strings and \(N_{\mathrm{h}}\) is
  the digest length of the suite. The roles are: \(H_1\) the binding
  factor, \(H_2\) the challenge, \(H_3\) the nonce, \(H_4\) the message
  pre-hash and \(H_5\) the commitment-list pre-hash.
\item[(e)] The \emph{signature encoding}
  \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(z)\) of a pair
  \((R, z) \in \G \times \Fq\), and the \emph{single-party verification}:
  a pair \((R, z)\) is accepted on a message \(m\) under a public key
  \(\mathit{PK}\) if and only if
  \[
    [z]B \;=\; R + [c]\,\mathit{PK} , \qquad
    c := H_2\bigl(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK})
      \,\|\, m\bigr) .
  \]
  This is the verification of the \emph{Crypto Guide}'s Construction
  ``Schnorr signature'' (\S``From identification to signature via the
  Fiat--Shamir transform'') in additive form, with hash \(H_2\).
\end{enumerate}
A ciphersuite \emph{for re-randomised signing} adds
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(f)] the \emph{randomiser hash} \(H_R \colon \{0,1\}^{8*} \to \Fq\)
  (ZIP~312, ``Specification'' and ``Randomizer Generation'').
\end{enumerate}
The ciphersuites of RFC~9591 do not define \(H_R\).
\end{definition}

The data are those of RFC~9591, Sections~3.1 (``Prime-Order Group'') and
3.2 (``Cryptographic Hash Function''), and Section~6 (``Ciphersuites''),
whose Appendix~A (``Schnorr Signature Encoding'') fixes the encoding and
whose Appendix~B (``Schnorr Signature Generation and Verification for
Prime-Order Groups'') the verification equation. The requirements that
RFC~9591, Section~6.6 (``Ciphersuite Requirements''), places on every
ciphersuite are four: the group is of prime order and every deserialisation
outputs an element of its set or fails; every hash function is
domain-separated with a per-suite context string, FROST(Ed25519, SHA-512)
excepted for \(H_2\) for compatibility with RFC~8032; \(H_1\), \(H_2\) and
\(H_3\) have output distributions close to uniform; and the signature
encoding is specified. The suite FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) separates its
hashes by BLAKE2b personalisations instead, its \(H_2\) being that of
RedPallas (Construction~\ref{def:pallas-suite}).

The generic element serialisation of RFC~9591, Section~3.1, fails on
\(\mathcal{O}\); a suite may instead serialise every element, as
FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) does with the star encoding (\emph{Ironwood
Guide}, Definition ``Star encoding''; ZIP~312, ``FROST(Pallas,
BLAKE2b-512)''). In both cases the element deserialisation rejects
\(\mathcal{O}\). Whether \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\) is total is the one property
of a suite on which Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct} distinguishes cases.

% [sec:auth-suite] H1, H2, H3, HR: 512-bit digest mod p_Vesta; Math Guide

%   exact distance t(q - t)/(q 2^512) = 2^-260.99
\begin{assumption}[The ciphersuite hashes]\label{ass:suite-hash}
The hashes \(H_1\), \(H_2\), \(H_3\) and \(H_R\) are modelled as random
oracles into \(\Fq\) (\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The random oracle model'',
Definition ``Random oracle''), independent of each other and of the
proof-of-knowledge hash \(H_{\mathrm{dkg}}\)
(Assumption~\ref{ass:dkg-hash}): each answer at a new input is an
independent sample from a distribution on \(\Fq\) within statistical
distance \(\delta\) of uniform (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Statistical
distance''), where \(\delta\) is a parameter of the suite. The hashes
\(H_4\) and \(H_5\) are modelled as independent random oracles into
\(\{0,1\}^{8 N_{\mathrm{h}}}\); hence an algorithm making \(Q\) queries
finds a collision of either with probability at most
\[
  \frac{Q (Q - 1)}{2} \cdot 2^{-8 N_{\mathrm{h}}}
\]
(\emph{Math Guide}, \S``The union bound and a birthday calculation'',
Proposition ``Birthday bound''). For a suite that builds every role from
one hash function under distinct personalisations, the assumption is that
this function, keyed by its personalisation, is a random oracle; distinct
personalisations then give independent oracles (\emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Domain separation and personalisation'', Proposition ``Domain
separation yields independent oracles''), and \(\delta\) is the bias of the
reduction of its output into \(\Fq\) (\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Hashing to a
field element'').
\end{assumption}

The assumption is the one hash assumption of every ciphersuite in this
volume. RFC~9591, Section~3.2, models the ciphersuite hash as a random
oracle in the security analyses it cites. The suite
FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) meets the assumption with
\(\delta \le p_{\Vesta} / 2^{512} < 2^{-257}\) and no further assumption,
by Proposition~\ref{prop:pallas-hash}.

\begin{definition}[Commitment list and its encoding]\label{def:commit-list}
Let a ciphersuite be fixed and let \(I\) be a signing set
(Definition~\ref{def:ts-params}). A \emph{commitment list} over \(I\) is a
sequence
\[
  L = \bigl((i, D_i, E_i)\bigr)_{i \in I} ,
  \qquad D_i, E_i \in \G ,
\]
with one triple for each member of \(I\): \(D_i\) is the \emph{hiding
commitment} and \(E_i\) the \emph{binding commitment} of member \(i\). The
identifiers are distinct and the triples are sorted in ascending order of
the least non-negative representative of the identifier. The signing set
is read off \(L\) as the set of its identifiers. The \emph{encoding} of
\(L\) is the byte string
\[
  \mathsf{enc}(L) \;:=\; \mathop{\big\|}_{i \in I,\ \text{ascending}}
  \Bigl( \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(i) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(D_i) \,\|\,
  \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(E_i) \Bigr)
\]
of length \(\lvert I \rvert (N_{\mathrm{s}} + 2 N_{\mathrm{e}})\).
\end{definition}

A commitment equal to \(\mathcal{O}\) occurs only in a suite with total
element serialisation, and check~(3) of Construction~\ref{def:round2}
rejects it.

The ordering is a requirement of RFC~9591, which states that the list must
be sorted in ascending order by identifier, and compares scalars by their
least non-negative representative (Sections~3.1 and 4.3, ``List
Operations''); the encoding is that of Section~4.3. Every component has
fixed length, each serialisation is injective, and the order of the triples
is fixed; hence the length of \(\mathsf{enc}(L)\) determines
\(\lvert I \rvert\), and the string parses uniquely into the triples.
The map \(\mathsf{enc}\) is therefore injective on commitment lists over one
suite, which the binding factors of Definition~\ref{def:binding} require.

\subsection{Round One: nonces and commitments}\label{sec:sign-round1}

\begin{construction}[Round One]\label{def:round1}
Participant \(P_i\), holding the share \(\mathit{sk}_i\)
(Definition~\ref{def:key-material}), proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It draws two independent strings \(T_d, T_e\), each uniform on the
  \(32\)-byte strings, and sets the \emph{hiding nonce} and the
  \emph{binding nonce}
  \[
    d_i := H_3\bigl(T_d \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(\mathit{sk}_i)\bigr) ,
    \qquad
    e_i := H_3\bigl(T_e \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(\mathit{sk}_i)\bigr) .
  \]
\item It sets \(D_i := [d_i]B\) and \(E_i := [e_i]B\).
\item It stores \((d_i, e_i, D_i, E_i)\), keeps \((d_i, e_i)\) secret, and
  sends \(\bigl(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(D_i), \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(E_i)\bigr)\) to
  the Coordinator over an authenticated channel
  (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (a)). If a serialisation fails,
  which happens only in a suite whose element serialisation is not total
  and only if \(D_i\) or \(E_i\) is \(\mathcal{O}\), the participant
  discards the pair and this execution of Round One fails.
\end{enumerate}
The strings \(T_d\) and \(T_e\) must be sampled uniformly from a secure
source of randomness, and a nonce pair must not be used in more than one
signing run.
\end{construction}

The construction is RFC~9591, Section~5.1 (``Round One - Commitment''),
with the hedged nonce generation of Section~4.1 (``Nonce Generation''),
which always samples \(32\) bytes of fresh randomness and hashes them
together with the serialised share. Section~5.1 requires that the nonces
not be used in more than one signing and be generated from a source of
secure randomness; Section~7.3 (``Nonce Reuse Attacks'') requires the
randomness to be sampled uniformly. Nonces lie in \(\Fq\), not in
\(\Fq^{*}\): the zero nonce is not excluded, and its consequence is case
(1) of Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct}. Round One does not depend on the
message or on the signing set, so a participant may run it ahead of any
signing run and hold several unused pairs. The paper that introduced FROST
(ePrint 2020/852, Figure~2) samples the pair uniformly from
\(\Fq^{*} \times \Fq^{*}\); the specified rule is the hedged derivation
above, and this volume states it instead.

% compute/frost_bounds.py:
% [sec:sign-round1] per-query term of 32 fresh bytes: 2^-(8*32) = 2^-256
%   (Ironwood GenRandom: 80 bytes, 2^-640)
% [sec:sign-round1] nonce distance delta + q_h 2^-256; at q_h = 2^64:
%   2^-192.0000 (the q_h term dominates)
\begin{lemma}[Hedged nonces are close to uniform]\label{lem:nonce-uniform}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash}, let an algorithm, the observer, make
at most \(q_h\) queries to \(H_3\) and, at one point, choose a byte string
\(w\) and receive \(d := H_3(T \,\|\, w)\), for \(T\) uniform on the
\(32\)-byte strings and independent of all else. The pair formed by the
observer's view and \(d\) is within statistical distance
\[
  \delta + q_h \cdot 2^{-256}
\]
of the pair obtained when \(d\) is replaced by a uniform element of \(\Fq\)
independent of all else. For the two nonces of one execution of Round One,
received for one string \(w\) with independent strings \(T_d\) and
\(T_e\), the corresponding distance is at most
\[
  2\bigl(\delta + q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\bigr) + 2^{-256} .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The argument is that of the \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Lemma ``Distance of
GenRandom from uniform'' (\S``Randomised validating keys''), with \(32\)
fresh bytes in place of \(80\) and \(H_3\) in place of the RedPallas hash.
Let \(E\) be the event that the observer queries \(H_3\) at
\(T \,\|\, w\). In a second experiment the answer
\(H_3(T \,\|\, w)\) is replaced, in the computation of \(d\) only, by an
independent sample of the answer distribution. In the lazy realisation of
the random oracle the answers at other inputs are independent of the answer
at \(T \,\|\, w\), so the two experiments are identical until \(E\) occurs,
and their outputs are within statistical distance \(\Pr[E]\)
(\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Security as a game'', Remark ``Game-hopping'').
In the second experiment the observer's view is independent of \(T\), so
each query has prefix \(T\) with probability at most \(2^{-256}\), and
\(\Pr[E] \le q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\) by the union bound. There the sample is
independent of everything else, and replacing it by a uniform scalar
changes the pair by at most \(\delta\) (\emph{Math Guide},
\S``Statistical distance'', Theorem ``Properties of statistical
distance'', part (4)). The triangle inequality, part (2) of the same
theorem, gives the first bound.

For the pair, the event \(T_d = T_e\), of probability \(2^{-256}\), is
excluded at that cost; outside it the inputs \(T_d \,\|\, w\) and
\(T_e \,\|\, w\) are distinct. The event that the observer queries either
input has probability at most \(2 q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\), and two
independent samples are replaced by uniform scalars at cost \(2\delta\).
\end{proof}

The nonces are therefore close to uniform, not uniform, whatever the share;
RFC~9591, Section~7.3, states that they are indistinguishable from values
sampled uniformly at random, and the lemma quantifies that statement. For
FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), with the \(\delta\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:pallas-hash}, the first bound at \(q_h = 2^{64}\) is
below \(2^{-191.99}\), the term \(q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\) dominating; in a suite
with a larger \(\delta\), such as a 48-byte hash to field, \(\delta\)
dominates.

RFC~9591, Section~4.1, hashes the share with the fresh bytes to hedge
against a generator whose output is predictable to an observer;
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce-uniform} assumes \(T\) uniform, and the volume proves no
statement for a predictable \(T\). Nonces computed from the share and the
message alone permit complete key recovery (RFC~9591, Sections~1 and 7.3),
which is why fresh bytes are used.

% compute/frost_bounds.py:
% [sec:sign-round1] RFC 9591 4.1: at most 2^64 signatures, reuse
%   probability at most 2^-128. Birthday over 2^64 draws of 32 bytes (one
%   role): 2^-129.0000; union over the hiding and binding roles:
%   2^-128 (1 - 2^-64) < 2^-128
Over \(N = 2^{64}\) executions of Round One by one participant, the
\(32\)-byte strings of one role, hiding or binding, repeat with probability
at most \(\binom{N}{2} \cdot 2^{-256} < 2^{-129}\) (\emph{Math Guide},
Proposition ``Birthday bound''), so, by the union bound, a repeat in either
role has probability at most \(2^{-128}(1 - 2^{-64}) < 2^{-128}\), the figure of
RFC~9591, Section~4.1.
A repeated nonce \emph{pair}, the hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce-reuse}, requires in the same two executions equal
hiding nonces and equal binding nonces.

\begin{lemma}[Repetition of a nonce pair]\label{lem:pair-repeat}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash}, two of \(N\) executions of Round One
by one participant yield equal nonce pairs with probability at most
\[
  \binom{N}{2} \bigl(2^{-255} + 1/q + \delta\bigr)^{2} .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For two executions \(j \ne k\), the hiding strings are equal with
probability \(2^{-256}\), and so are the binding strings, independently.
Two answers of \(H_3\) at distinct inputs are equal with probability at
most \(1/q + \delta\), since every value has probability at most
\(1/q + \delta\) under a distribution within \(\delta\) of uniform
(\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem ``Properties of statistical distance'', part
(3)); and when both roles have distinct strings, the two equalities
constrain answers at two distinct pairs of inputs, whose probability of
holding together is at most \((1/q + \delta)^2\), except when the two pairs
of inputs coincide, an event of probability at most \(2 \cdot 2^{-512}\).
Summing the cases gives at most
\(2^{-512} + 2 \cdot 2^{-256}(1/q + \delta) + (1/q + \delta)^2 +
2^{-511}(1/q + \delta)\), which is below the square displayed; the union
bound over the \(\binom{N}{2}\) pairs of executions gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Round Two: binding factors and signature shares}
\label{sec:sign-round2}

\begin{definition}[Binding factors and group commitment]\label{def:binding}
Let \(\mathit{PK}\) be the group key, \(m\) a message, that is a byte
string, and \(L\) a commitment list over a signing set \(I\)
(Definition~\ref{def:commit-list}). For \(i \in I\) the \emph{binding
factor} of \(i\) is
\[
  \rho_i := H_1\Bigl( \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK}) \,\|\, H_4(m) \,\|\,
    H_5\bigl(\mathsf{enc}(L)\bigr) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(i) \Bigr) ;
\]
the \emph{commitment share} of \(i\) is \(R_i := D_i + [\rho_i]E_i\); the
\emph{group commitment} is \(R := \sum_{i \in I} R_i\); and the
\emph{challenge} is
\[
  c := H_2\bigl( \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK})
    \,\|\, m \bigr) ,
\]
with \(\rho_i\) defined whenever \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK})\) is, and
\(c\) whenever \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R)\) and \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK})\)
are. All four are functions of
the public data \((\mathit{PK}, m, L)\), computed identically by every
member of \(I\) and by the Coordinator.
\end{definition}

The binding factor is that of RFC~9591, Section~4.4 (``Binding Factors
Computation''), the group commitment that of Section~4.5 (``Group
Commitment Computation''), and the challenge that of Section~4.6
(``Signature Challenge Computation''). The challenge input has the order
nonce commitment, public key, message of the single-party challenge of
Definition~\ref{def:suite}, part (e), so a verifier recomputes \(c\) from
\((R, \mathit{PK}, m)\) alone. The paper's binding value
\(\rho_i = H_1(i, m, B)\), with \(B\) its commitment list, and its
challenge \(c = H_2(R, Y, m)\) (ePrint 2020/852, Figure~3, in
multiplicative notation with \(R = \prod_i D_i E_i^{\rho_i}\)) differ as
follows: the specified binding-factor input adds the group key and
pre-hashes the message and the encoded list; the challenge input is the
paper's, in the same order. For FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), \(H_2\) is the
RedPallas hash and \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\) the star encoding, so \(c\) is the
challenge that RedPallas validation recomputes
(Construction~\ref{def:pallas-suite}; ZIP~312, ``Rationale'').

\begin{construction}[Round Two]\label{def:round2}
\emph{Coordinator}. It selects a signing set \(I\) with
\(t \le \lvert I \rvert \le n\); takes, for each \(i \in I\), one
commitment pair \((D_i, E_i)\) received from \(P_i\) in Round One and not
placed in any earlier request; forms the commitment list \(L\) over \(I\)
(Definition~\ref{def:commit-list}); and sends \((m, L)\), serialised
componentwise, to each \(P_i\) with \(i \in I\) over an authenticated
channel (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (a)).

\emph{Member} \(P_i\), on receiving \((m, L)\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item it checks that \(m\) is a message it is willing to sign; for spend
  authorisation this is the check of Definition~\ref{def:msg-check};
\item it checks that its identifier \(i\) appears in \(L\) with a
  commitment pair \((D_i, E_i)\) that it stores and has not used;
\item it deserialises every commitment of \(L\) with
  \(\mathsf{deser}_{\G}\), which fails on a non-canonical encoding and on
  the encoding of \(\mathcal{O}\);
\item it checks that the identifiers of \(L\) are distinct, without which
  \(\lambda_{I,i}\) is undefined, and in ascending order, as RFC~9591
  requires of a commitment list (Section~4.3).
\end{enumerate}
It aborts if any check fails. Otherwise it computes \(\rho_i\),
\(R\) and \(c\) (Definition~\ref{def:binding}), aborting if \(\rho_i\) or
\(c\) is undefined, and \(\lambda_{I,i}\) (Definition~\ref{def:lagrange}), with
\(I\) the set of identifiers of \(L\); sets the \emph{signature share}
\[
  z_i := d_i + e_i \rho_i + \lambda_{I,i}\, \mathit{sk}_i\, c
  \;\in\; \Fq ;
\]
deletes \((d_i, e_i)\) and \((D_i, E_i)\); and only then sends
\(\mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(z_i)\) to the Coordinator over an authenticated
channel (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (a)).
\end{construction}

The construction is RFC~9591, Section~5.2 (``Round Two - Signature Share
Generation''). The Coordinator's choice of at least \(t\) and at most \(n\)
participants is required by Section~5. Each participant must validate the
commitment list by deserialising each element, must abort if
deserialisation fails, and must ensure that its identifier and commitments
from Round One appear in the list (Section~5.2); applications that restrict
the messages a participant signs perform further validation, which
Section~7.7 (``Input Message Validation'') recommends so that participants
do not act as signing oracles for arbitrary messages. The computation of
\(\lambda_{I,i}\) fails on an absent or repeated identifier (Section~4.2,
``Polynomials''). The share equation is that of Section~5.2. After
computing its share, each participant must delete the nonce and the
corresponding commitment, and must not use the nonce as input more than
once. The deletion is the step on which Proposition~\ref{prop:replay}
rests.

\subsection{Aggregation and share verification}\label{sec:sign-agg}

\begin{construction}[Aggregation]\label{def:aggregate}
The Coordinator holds \((m, L)\) and the group information
\((\mathit{PK}, (\mathit{PK}_j)_{j=1}^{n})\) stored from key generation
(Definition~\ref{def:key-material}). The shares arrive over authenticated
channels without confidentiality (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type
(a)). The Coordinator proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It deserialises each share with \(\mathsf{deser}_{\Fq}\) and aborts if
  any deserialisation fails.
\item It computes \(\rho_i\) and \(R_i\) for \(i \in I\), \(R\) and \(c\)
  (Definition~\ref{def:binding}), aborting if \(\rho_i\) or \(c\) is
  undefined.
\item It sets \(z := \sum_{i \in I} z_i\) and outputs the signature
  \((R, z)\), encoded
  \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(z)\).
\end{enumerate}
It should verify \((R, z)\) on \(m\) under \(\mathit{PK}\) by the
single-party verification (Definition~\ref{def:suite}, part (e)) before
publishing it, and should abort if the signature is invalid. It may check
each share by the \emph{share equation}
\[
  [z_i]B \;=\; R_i + [c\, \lambda_{I,i}]\,\mathit{PK}_i ,
\]
in particular when the aggregate is invalid. The keys \(\mathit{PK}\) and
\(\mathit{PK}_i\) in these checks are taken from the stored group
information, never from the messages of the run.
\end{construction}

The construction is RFC~9591, Section~5.3 (``Signature Share
Aggregation''). The Coordinator must validate each share by deserialisation
and must abort if validation fails; it should verify the signature under
the group key before publishing or releasing it; if that verification
fails, it may verify each share individually to identify and act on
misbehaving participants; and the group key and the keys \(\mathit{PK}_i\)
used there must come from the stored group information. Section~5
requires an authenticated channel for identifying misbehaving participants
and does not require confidentiality. The paper checks every share
unconditionally before aggregating (ePrint 2020/852, Figure~3); the
specified duties are the recommendation and the permission above, and this
volume states those.

\begin{proposition}[Identifiable abort]\label{prop:ident-abort}
Let the key material be valid (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}), let the
Coordinator be honest, and let \(c\) be defined.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If the share of every member satisfies its share equation, then
  \([z]B = R + [c]\,\mathit{PK}\), so the aggregate \((R, z)\) is accepted
  by the single-party verification on \(m\) under \(\mathit{PK}\).
\item[(ii)] The share of every member that follows
  Construction~\ref{def:round2} satisfies its share equation.
\item[(iii)] Hence, if the aggregate is not accepted, the share equation of
  some member fails, and that member deviated from the protocol; the
  authenticated channel identifies it as the sender of the share.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(ii) For a member following the construction,
\[
  [z_i]B = [d_i]B + [\rho_i][e_i]B + [c\, \lambda_{I,i}][\mathit{sk}_i]B
  = R_i + [c\, \lambda_{I,i}]\,\mathit{PK}_i ,
\]
since \(D_i = [d_i]B\), \(E_i = [e_i]B\) and, by validity,
\(\mathit{PK}_i = [\mathit{sk}_i]B\).

(i) Let \(f\) be the polynomial of degree at most \(t - 1\) that validity
provides, with \(f(j) = \mathit{sk}_j\) and \(\mathit{PK} = [f(0)]B\).
Summing the share equations over \(I\) gives
\[
  [z]B = \sum_{i \in I} R_i
    + \Bigl[c \sum_{i \in I} \lambda_{I,i}\, \mathit{sk}_i\Bigr] B
  = R + [c\, f(0)]B = R + [c]\,\mathit{PK} ,
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:recombine} applied to \(f\). The challenge \(c\) is the
one the single-party verification recomputes from \((R, \mathit{PK}, m)\)
(Definition~\ref{def:binding}), so the verification accepts.

(iii) is the contrapositive of (i), with (ii).
\end{proof}

The mechanism for acting on an identified member is outside RFC~9591
(Section~5.4, ``Identifiable Abort''). FROST provides no robustness
(Sections~5.4 and 7): one member that withholds or corrupts its share
prevents the signature, and a failed run is restarted from Round One with
fresh nonces, since the consumed pairs are deleted.

\subsection{Correctness of FROST signing}\label{sec:sign-correct}

% compute/frost_bounds.py:
% [sec:sign-correct] R = O in a generic suite: at most delta + 1/q per
%   run; at q = p_Vesta and its delta: 2^-253.9125
% [sec:sign-correct] zero honest nonce, 2|I|(delta + 1/q) per run:
%   |I| = 2: 2^-251.9125
% [sec:sign-correct] zero honest nonce, 2|I|(delta + 1/q) per run:
%   |I| = 3: 2^-251.3276
\begin{theorem}[Correctness of FROST]\label{thm:frost-correct}
Let the key material be valid (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}), as key
generation by a dealer or by the distributed protocol outputs it
(Propositions~\ref{prop:dealer-valid} and~\ref{prop:dkg-valid}); let \(I\)
be a signing set and \(m\) a message that every member of \(I\) accepts;
and let every party follow Constructions~\ref{def:round1},
\ref{def:round2} and~\ref{def:aggregate}. Then, outside the three cases
below, the run outputs \((R, z)\) with
\[
  R = [r]B , \qquad z = r + c\, s , \qquad
  r := \sum_{i \in I} (d_i + e_i \rho_i) ,
\]
which the single-party verification accepts on \(m\) under \(\mathit{PK}\).
In the three cases the run aborts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] \emph{Zero nonce.} Some \(d_i\) or \(e_i\), \(i \in I\), is
  \(0\), so \(D_i\) or \(E_i\) is \(\mathcal{O}\). In a suite whose element
  serialisation is not total, Round One fails on it; in every suite the
  run aborts no later than check (3) of Round Two, since
  \(\mathsf{deser}_{\G}\) rejects \(\mathcal{O}\). Under
  Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} the probability of this case is at
  most \(2 \lvert I \rvert (\delta + 1/q)\) per run.
\item[(2)] \emph{Identity group commitment.} Only in a suite whose element
  serialisation is not total, as the generic serialisation of RFC~9591 is
  not: \(R = \mathcal{O}\), so \(c\) is undefined. Under
  Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} the probability of this case, outside
  case (1), is at most \(\delta + 1/q\) per run.
\item[(3)] \emph{Identity group key.} Only in a suite whose element
  serialisation is not total: \(s = 0\), so \(\mathit{PK} = \mathcal{O}\)
  and \(\rho_i\) and \(c\) are undefined. For \(s\) uniform in \(\Fq\), as
  under Assumption~\ref{ass:dealer}, its probability is \(1/q\) per key
  generation.
\end{enumerate}
A suite with a total element serialisation, such as
FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), has only case (1).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(r_i := d_i + e_i \rho_i\), so that
\(z_i = r_i + c\, \lambda_{I,i}\, \mathit{sk}_i\). The nonce
\(r = \sum_{i \in I} r_i\) is additively shared by construction: each
summand is formed locally, with no dealer and no interaction; and
\(R = \sum_{i \in I} R_i = \sum_{i \in I} [r_i]B = [r]B\). The Lagrange
coefficients turn the Shamir shares \(\mathit{sk}_i = f(i)\) of
\(s = f(0)\) into additive shares \(\lambda_{I,i}\, \mathit{sk}_i\) with
sum \(s\), by Lemma~\ref{lem:recombine}, since \(f\) has degree at most
\(t - 1\) by validity. Hence
\[
  z = \sum_{i \in I} z_i = r + c\, s , \qquad
  [z]B = [r]B + [c][s]B = R + [c]\,\mathit{PK} ,
\]
where \(c = H_2(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK})
\,\|\, m)\) is the challenge the verifier recomputes
(Definition~\ref{def:binding}). Outside the three cases, every check of
Round Two passes: \(m\) is accepted, each member's pair is stored and
unused, every commitment lies in \(\G^{*}\) and deserialises, the
identifiers of \(L\) are distinct and sorted, and \(\rho_i\) and \(c\) are
defined. Every
share deserialises, being an honest serialisation, so aggregation outputs
\((R, z)\).

(1) Each of the \(2 \lvert I \rvert\) nonces is an answer of \(H_3\),
equal to \(0\) with probability at most \(1/q + \delta\), because under a
distribution within \(\delta\) of uniform every value has probability at
most \(1/q + \delta\) (\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem ``Properties of
statistical distance'', part (3)). Since \(B\) generates a group of prime
order, \([d]B = \mathcal{O}\) if and only if \(d = 0\). The union bound
gives \(2 \lvert I \rvert (1/q + \delta)\).

(2) Outside case (1), fix \(i \in I\); then \(E_i \ne \mathcal{O}\).
Condition on all answers of \(H_3\), \(H_4\) and \(H_5\), and on the answers
of \(H_1\) at every input other than that of \(\rho_i\). Then
\(R = \mathcal{O}\) if and only if \([\rho_i]E_i\) equals the element
\(-D_i - \sum_{j \in I \setminus \{i\}} R_j\), which is fixed; since
\(E_i\) generates \(\G\), this holds for exactly one value of \(\rho_i\).
The binding factor \(\rho_i\) is the answer of \(H_1\) at an input with
suffix \(\mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(i)\), distinct from the inputs of the other
members, whose suffixes differ; so it takes that value with probability at
most \(1/q + \delta\).

(3) Since \(B\) generates a group of prime order,
\(\mathit{PK} = [s]B = \mathcal{O}\) if and only if \(s = 0\), and
\(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK})\), an input of every \(\rho_i\) and of
\(c\), is then undefined. A uniform \(s \in \Fq\) is \(0\) with probability
\(1/q\).
\end{proof}

For FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), with the \(\delta\) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:pallas-hash}, the bound of case (1) is below
\(2^{-251.9}\) for \(\lvert I \rvert = 2\) and below \(2^{-251.3}\) for
\(\lvert I \rvert = 3\). In a suite with the parameters \(q = p_{\Vesta}\)
and that \(\delta\) but a serialisation that is not total, the bound of
case (2) would be below \(2^{-253.9}\).

The output of a threshold run is a single-party Schnorr signature under the
group key. It carries no threshold marker: a verifier applies to it, with
\((\mathit{PK}, m)\) alone and no knowledge of \(t\), \(n\) or \(I\), the
verification it applies to a signature of one signer. RFC~9591 states this
in Section~5, under which signatures can be verified as if produced by a
single signer holding \(s\), and in Section~6, under which the verification
instructions of each suite are equivalent to those for a single
participant; Appendix~B gives that verification.

\subsection{Single use of nonces}\label{sec:sign-nonces}

\begin{lemma}[Reuse of a nonce pair exposes the share]\label{lem:nonce-reuse}
Let member \(P_i\) answer \(k\) Round Two requests, \(j = 1, \dots, k\),
with one nonce pair \((d, e)\) and share \(\mathit{sk}_i\). Its responses
are
\[
  z_j = d + e\, \rho_j + \mu_j\, \mathit{sk}_i , \qquad
  \mu_j := \lambda_{I_j,i}\, c_j ,
\]
where \(\rho_j\), \(c_j\) and \(I_j\) are the binding factor of \(i\), the
challenge and the signing set of request \(j\), all computable from public
data. Each response is affine in the unknown
\((d, e, \mathit{sk}_i) \in \Fq^3\) with coefficient vector
\(v_j := (1, \rho_j, \mu_j)\). Say that \(\mathit{sk}_i\) is
\emph{determined} by \((z_j, v_j)_{j \le k}\) if every
\(x \in \Fq^3\) with \(v_j \cdot x = z_j\) for all \(j\) has third
coordinate \(\mathit{sk}_i\). The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \(\mathit{sk}_i\) is determined by \((z_j, v_j)_{j \le k}\);
\item[(b)] \((0, 0, 1)\) lies in the span of \(v_1, \dots, v_k\)
  (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Linear combinations, span, and linear
  independence'', Definition ``Linear combination and span'');
\item[(c)] the points \((\rho_j, \mu_j)\) do not all lie on one line
  \(\mu = \alpha + \beta \rho\) with \(\alpha, \beta \in \Fq\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, three responses with linearly independent coefficient
vectors determine \(\mathit{sk}_i\); two responses with distinct binding
factors never do, and every value of \(\mathit{sk}_i\) is then consistent
with them; and two responses with equal binding factors and
\(\mu_1 \ne \mu_2\) determine
\[
  \mathit{sk}_i = (z_1 - z_2) / (\mu_1 - \mu_2) .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(b) implies (a): if \((0, 0, 1) = \sum_j a_j v_j\), then every consistent
\(x\) has third coordinate \(\sum_j a_j (v_j \cdot x) = \sum_j a_j z_j\),
one value, which the true unknown attains.

(a) implies (c): suppose every point lies on \(\mu = \alpha + \beta\rho\),
and put \(u := (\alpha, \beta, -1)\). Then
\(v_j \cdot u = \alpha + \beta \rho_j - \mu_j = 0\) for every \(j\), so
\((d, e, \mathit{sk}_i) + \gamma u\) is consistent for every
\(\gamma \in \Fq\), and its third coordinate \(\mathit{sk}_i - \gamma\)
takes every value; \(\mathit{sk}_i\) is not determined.

(c) implies (b): if two points have equal \(\rho\) and distinct \(\mu\),
then \(v_1 - v_2\) (for those two) is a nonzero multiple of \((0, 0, 1)\).
Otherwise distinct points have distinct \(\rho\). Two points with distinct
\(\rho\) lie on a line \(\mu = \alpha + \beta\rho\), so (c) forces at least
three distinct points, not all on one such line; no vertical line contains
them either, since their \(\rho\) are distinct. Hence some three of them
are not collinear, their vectors \((1, \rho, \mu)\) are linearly
independent, and they span \(\Fq^3\), which contains \((0, 0, 1)\).

The particular cases follow: independent vectors span \(\Fq^3\); two points
with distinct \(\rho\) lie on a line \(\mu = \alpha + \beta \rho\), and the
proof of (a) implies (c) shows every value of \(\mathit{sk}_i\) consistent;
and with \(\rho_1 = \rho_2\), \(z_1 - z_2 = (\mu_1 - \mu_2)\,
\mathit{sk}_i\).
\end{proof}

The last case is the \emph{Crypto Guide}'s Proposition ``Nonce reuse reveals the secret key'' (\S``Security in the random oracle model and the forking lemma'')
applied to the effective nonce \(d + e \rho\). Whoever sees the shares and
the public data can compute \(\mathit{sk}_i\) in the determined cases,
since the shares travel without confidentiality
(Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (a)). RFC~9591 states the risk
(Sections~1 and 7.3) and forbids the reuse (Section~5.2).

\begin{proposition}[Replayed commitments]\label{prop:replay}
A Coordinator that places in a Round Two request a commitment pair of member
\(P_i\) that \(P_i\) has already used to answer a request obtains no
response from \(P_i\), unless \(P_i\) still stores a second copy of that pair
from another execution of Round One, an event whose probability over \(N\)
executions is at most \(\binom{N}{2}(2^{-255} + 1/q + \delta)^2\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-repeat}). Outside that event a replay denies service only,
and yields no second response under one nonce pair, the hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce-reuse}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A member responds only for a pair that it stores and has not used, by check
(2) of Construction~\ref{def:round2}, and it deletes a pair when it
answers with it. Outside that event no second copy of the pair is stored,
so a used pair is no longer stored, check (2) fails and the member aborts.
\end{proof}

The Coordinator may in addition track, per group key, the commitments
already used (RFC~9591, Section~7.3).

\section{Re-randomised FROST for spend authorisation}\label{sec:rr}

This section states the re-randomised variant of FROST that ZIP~312
specifies, in the order of the mechanism: why spend authorisation requires a
shifted key; the re-randomisable threshold scheme; the derivations of the
randomiser, with the bound showing that the specified derivation, fed the
signature digest as its message, succeeds only with negligible probability;
the signers' duty to check the signed message; the re-randomised rounds with
their correctness; and the requirements and trust model of ZIP~312 that this
flow forces. The ciphersuite is generic, with the randomiser hash \(H_R\) of
Definition~\ref{def:suite}, part (f); the Pallas instance is
Construction~\ref{def:pallas-suite}. ZIP~312 is Draft.

\subsection{Spend authorisation under randomised validating keys}
\label{sec:rr-spendauth}

The single-signer objects are cited, not re-derived. The
spend-authorisation instance of RedPallas (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``The
RedPallas signature scheme'', Construction ``RedPallas'' and Definition
``Spend-authorisation and binding-signature instances'') works in the Pallas
group, of prime order \(q = p_{\Vesta}\), with base
\[
  B = G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}} = \GroupHash(\texttt{z.cash:Orchard},
  \texttt{G}) .
\]
A key pair is \((x, X = [x]B)\). A signature on a message \(M\) is
\(\sigma = \underline{R} \,\|\, \mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{256}(S)\), with challenge
\(c = \mathsf{H}^{\circledast}(\underline{R} \,\|\, \underline{X} \,\|\,
M)\), where \(\mathsf{H}^{\circledast}\) reduces modulo \(p_{\Vesta}\) the
BLAKE2b-512 output under the personalisation \texttt{Zcash\_RedPallasH}.
Validation accepts if and only if the first \(32\) bytes decode to a point
\(R\), a non-canonical encoding decoding to \(\bot\) and being rejected;
\(S < p_{\Vesta}\); and \([S]B = R + [c]X\), the cofactor being one on
Pallas. The nonce and challenge hashes take \(\underline{X}\): the scheme is
key-prefixed (Remark ``Key prefixing'' there). The message of every
spend-authorisation signature is the signature digest \(\mathsf{SigHash}\)
(\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Transaction digests and signatures'', Definition
``Signature digest''). The deployed rules are those of the protocol
specification, \S``RedDSA, RedJubjub, and RedPallas'' and
\href{https://zips.z.cash/protocol/protocol.pdf\#concretespendauthsig}%
{\S``Spend Authorization Signature (Sapling and Orchard)''}.

Re-randomisation by a randomiser \(\alpha \in \Fq\) maps a key pair
\((x, X)\) to \((x + \alpha,\, X + [\alpha]B)\) (\emph{Crypto Guide},
\S``Key re-randomisation and unlinkability'', Definition ``Key
re-randomisation''); the identity randomiser is \(0\). The randomiser
generator \(\mathsf{GenRandom}\) draws \(T\) uniformly from the \(80\)-byte
strings and returns \(\mathsf{H}^{\circledast}(T)\) (\emph{Ironwood Guide},
Definition ``Randomiser generator and key re-randomisation'').

For each Action the spender draws \(\alpha := \mathsf{GenRandom}()\) and sets
\[
  \rsk := \ask + \alpha , \qquad
  \rk := \aksym^{\PP} + [\alpha]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}
    = [\rsk]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}} ,
\]
with \(\ask\) sign-normalised; the Action publishes \(\rk\) and the
signature under \(\rsk\) on \(\mathsf{SigHash}\) (\emph{Ironwood Guide},
\S``Randomised validating keys'', Construction ``Randomised validating
key''). Two consensus rules apply: \(\rk \ne \mathcal{O}\), and the
signature is valid under \(\rk\) on \(\mathsf{SigHash}\) (protocol
specification, \S``Action Descriptions'' and
\href{https://zips.z.cash/protocol/protocol.pdf\#spendauthsig}%
{\S``Spend Authorization Signature (Sapling and Orchard)''}). The verifier's
key is therefore \(\rk\), which equals \(\aksym^{\PP}\) only for
\(\alpha = 0\), an event of probability at most
\(1/p_{\Vesta} + 2^{-512}\) under \(\mathsf{GenRandom}\) (\emph{Ironwood
Guide}, Assumption ``The RedPallas hash as a random oracle''). Only these
scheme-level statements are inherited. The \emph{Ironwood Guide} constructs
\(\ask\) only for keys with \(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\) (Remark
``Scope of the key derivations''), so its key-derived theorems are not cited
for threshold keys (\S\ref{sec:sec-authority}).

For every Action of the Orchard protocol, in either pool, the single signer
proceeds in the order: draw \(\alpha\); compute \(\rk\); compute
\(\mathsf{SigHash}\) of the transaction, which commits to \(\rk\); sign it
under \(\rsk\). The Action proof takes \(\alpha\) as auxiliary (secret)
input and \(\rk\) as primary input and, being authorising data, may be
computed after signing (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Construction of a
transaction'', Construction ``Ironwood-pool bundle'', steps ``Randomised
keys'', ``Proof'', ``Digest'' and ``Spend authorisation''; ZIP~229,
``Motivation''). Sapling spends are outside the volume.

\begin{definition}[Strong unforgeability under re-randomised keys]
\label{def:surk}
A \emph{re-randomisable signature scheme} is a signature scheme
\((\mathsf{GenPrivate}, \mathsf{DerivePublic}, \mathsf{Sign},
\mathsf{Validate})\) with private-key set \(\mathsf{Private}\) and
public-key set \(\mathsf{Public}\), together with a randomiser set
\(\mathcal{R}\), a randomised generator \(\mathsf{GenRandom}\) with values
in \(\mathcal{R}\), maps
\[
  \mathsf{RandomizePrivate} \colon \mathcal{R} \times \mathsf{Private}
    \to \mathsf{Private} , \qquad
  \mathsf{RandomizePublic} \colon \mathcal{R} \times \mathsf{Public}
    \to \mathsf{Public} ,
\]
and an identity randomiser \(\alpha_{\mathrm{id}} \in \mathcal{R}\), such
that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item each \(\mathsf{RandomizePrivate}(\alpha, \cdot)\) is injective and
  efficiently invertible;
\item \(\mathsf{RandomizePrivate}(\alpha_{\mathrm{id}}, \cdot)\) is the
  identity;
\item for every \(\mathit{sk}\), the value
  \(\mathsf{RandomizePrivate}(\alpha, \mathit{sk})\) with
  \(\alpha \gets \mathsf{GenRandom}()\) is distributed as
  \(\mathsf{GenPrivate}()\);
\item for every \(\alpha\) and \(\mathit{sk}\),
  \begin{multline*}
    \mathsf{RandomizePublic}(\alpha, \mathsf{DerivePublic}(\mathit{sk})) \\
    = \mathsf{DerivePublic}(\mathsf{RandomizePrivate}(\alpha, \mathit{sk})) .
  \end{multline*}
\end{enumerate}
Let \(\mathit{sk} \gets \mathsf{GenPrivate}()\) and
\(\mathit{vk} := \mathsf{DerivePublic}(\mathit{sk})\). The signing oracle
keeps a set \(\mathcal{Q}\), initially empty; on a query
\((m, \alpha)\) it returns
\(\sigma := \mathsf{Sign}_{\mathsf{RandomizePrivate}(\alpha,
\mathit{sk})}(m)\) and adds \((m, \sigma)\) to \(\mathcal{Q}\). The scheme
is \emph{SURK-CMA secure} if no efficient adversary, given \(\mathit{vk}\)
and the oracle, outputs, except with negligible probability, a triple
\((m', \sigma', \alpha')\) with
\[
  \mathsf{Validate}_{\mathsf{RandomizePublic}(\alpha', \mathit{vk})}
    (m', \sigma') = 1
  \quad\text{and}\quad (m', \sigma') \notin \mathcal{Q} .
\]
Freshness is on the message--signature pair; the forgery may be under any
re-randomisation of \(\mathit{vk}\), the identity included.
\end{definition}

The definition is that of the protocol specification, \S``Signature with
Re-Randomizable Keys'', adapted there from Fleischhacker et al.\ (PKC 2016,
Section~3) with the randomiser and key arguments swapped; the specification
requires every spend-authorisation instance to meet it
(\href{https://zips.z.cash/protocol/protocol.pdf\#concretespendauthsig}%
{\S``Spend Authorization Signature (Sapling and Orchard)''}). The
single-signer spend-authorisation instance meets it: the \emph{Ironwood
Guide}'s Proposition ``Unforgeability under re-randomisation'' (``RedPallas
unforgeability''), part (ii), under discrete logarithms on Pallas and the
RedPallas hash as a random oracle, requires freshness only of the triple of
key, message and signature, and states the implication. The threshold form
of the notion is an open problem (Definition~\ref{def:classes};
Table~\ref{tab:status}).

\begin{proposition}[A fixed-key aggregate under a randomised key]
\label{prop:rr-gap}
Let key material with signing key \(s\) and group key
\(\mathit{PK} = [s]B\) be valid (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}), over a
ciphersuite meeting Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} with distance
\(\delta\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] For every signing set \(I\),
  \(\sum_{i \in I} \lambda_{I,i}\, \mathit{sk}_i = s\). Hence every honest
  run of FROST (Constructions~\ref{def:round1}, \ref{def:round2}
  and~\ref{def:aggregate}) outputs a signature valid under the one key
  \(\mathit{PK}\), whatever \(I\), apart from the failed runs of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct}.
\item[(ii)] Fix \(\alpha \in \Fq\) with \(\alpha \ne 0\) and
  \(\mathit{PK} + [\alpha]B \ne \mathcal{O}\) before the experiment. In any
  experiment whose parties make at most \(Q_2\) queries to \(H_2\) in total,
  the probability that one output pair \((m, (R, z))\), designated in
  advance (such as the aggregate of a run), is accepted by the
  single-party verification (Definition~\ref{def:suite}, part (e)) both
  under \(\mathit{PK}\) and under \(\mathit{PK} + [\alpha]B\) is at most
  \[
    (Q_2 + 2)\,(1/q + \delta) .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Lemma~\ref{lem:recombine} with \(f\) the sharing polynomial gives the
sum; Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct} gives the rest.

(ii) Write \(\mathit{PK}' := \mathit{PK} + [\alpha]B\). Acceptance of
\((R, z)\) on \(m\) under both keys means
\([z]B = R + [c]\,\mathit{PK} = R + [c']\,\mathit{PK}'\), where
\[
  c = H_2\bigl(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK})
    \,\|\, m\bigr) , \qquad
  c' = H_2\bigl(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK}')
    \,\|\, m\bigr) ;
\]
hence \([c]\,\mathit{PK} = [c']\,\mathit{PK}'\), that is
\(c\, s = c'(s + \alpha)\). The two inputs differ, since
\(\mathit{PK}' \ne \mathit{PK}\) and \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\) is injective.
Add the two queries at the inputs of the designated pair if they are
missing, so that at most
\(Q_2 + 2\) queries are made. Call a query with key component
\(\mathit{PK}\) and one with key component \(\mathit{PK}'\) and the same
\(R\) and \(m\) \emph{partners}; each query has at most one partner. For
each query define a \emph{target}, where \(s + \alpha \ne 0\) because
\(\mathit{PK}' \ne \mathcal{O}\). If \(s \ne 0\) and the partner was
answered earlier, the target is the value the query's own answer must take
for the equation to hold: \(c\, s/(s + \alpha)\) for a query with key
\(\mathit{PK}'\), and \(c'(s + \alpha)/s\) for a query with key
\(\mathit{PK}\). If \(s = 0\), the equation reads \(c' \alpha = 0\), and
every query with key \(\mathit{PK}'\) has target \(0\). A pair valid under
both keys makes some query hit its target: the later-answered query of the
pair if \(s \ne 0\), the query with key \(\mathit{PK}'\) if \(s = 0\).
Each target is fixed before the fresh
answer is drawn, and that answer takes any fixed value with probability at
most \(1/q + \delta\) (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Statistical distance'',
Theorem ``Properties of statistical distance'', part (3)). The union bound
over the at most \(Q_2 + 2\) queries gives the claim.
\end{proof}

The argument is that of the \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Remark ``Key
prefixing'' for the unshifted response. The bound is for \(\alpha\) fixed in
advance; with \(\alpha\) chosen adaptively, one query may pair with many.
For FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) the per-query term \(1/q + \delta\) is below
\(2^{-253.9}\).

Spend authorisation re-randomises the validating key of every Action with a
randomiser from \(\mathsf{GenRandom}\), which makes validating keys
unlinkable (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, Proposition ``Unlinkability of
randomised validating keys''); a signer bound to one key cannot follow it
(ZIP~312, ``Motivation''). By part (i), a plain FROST aggregate is valid
under \(\mathit{PK} = \aksym^{\PP}\), which consensus accepts only in an
Action with \(\rk = \aksym^{\PP}\), that is with randomiser zero, publishing
the spend validating key in every such Action. By part (ii), it is valid
under \(\rk\) for a nonzero randomiser only with the stated probability.
The re-randomised variant shifts every participant's share, every
verification share and the group key by one common scalar.

\subsection{Re-randomisable threshold signatures}\label{sec:rr-scheme}

\begin{definition}[Re-randomisable threshold signature scheme]
\label{def:rerand-ts}
A \emph{re-randomisable threshold signature scheme} is a scheme of
Definition~\ref{def:ts-scheme} over a group \(\G\) of prime order \(q\) with
generator \(B\), together with a randomiser set \(\mathcal{X}\) and:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a randomised algorithm \(\mathsf{GenRand}\) with values in
  \(\mathcal{X}\);
\item an \emph{effective-randomiser map}
  \(H_r \colon \mathcal{X} \times \{0,1\}^{*} \to \Fq\), sending a
  randomiser \(\alpha\) and an \emph{auxiliary string} \(\mathit{aux}\) to
  the \emph{effective randomiser}
  \(\hat{\alpha} := H_r(\alpha, \mathit{aux})\);
\item the \emph{shifts}
  \begin{gather*}
    \mathsf{RandShare}(\mathit{sk}_i, \alpha, \mathit{aux})
      := \mathit{sk}_i + \hat{\alpha} , \qquad
    \mathsf{RandPKShare}(\mathit{PK}_i, \alpha, \mathit{aux})
      := \mathit{PK}_i + [\hat{\alpha}]B , \\
    \mathsf{RandPK}(\mathit{PK}, \alpha, \mathit{aux})
      := \mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B ;
  \end{gather*}
\end{enumerate}
such that the following \emph{correctness} holds. For valid key material
with signing key \(s\), every \((\alpha, \mathit{aux})\) with
\(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\) in the domain of \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\)
(every \((\alpha, \mathit{aux})\) when \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\) is total), every
signing set
\(I\) and every message \(m\): the shifted shares, verification shares and
group key are valid key material for \(s + \hat{\alpha}\); and a run of the
signing rounds on the shifted material over \(I\) on \(m\) in which every
party is honest outputs either \(\bot\) or a signature that verification
accepts under \(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\), and outputs \(\bot\) with
negligible probability.
\end{definition}

The definition is that of ePrint 2024/436, Definition~5, which names the
algorithms, with general \(\mathsf{RandShare}\), \(\mathsf{RandPKShare}\)
and \(\mathsf{RandPK}\) and a distribution on \(\mathcal{X}\), without a
correctness relation; the correctness clause is this volume's. The
auxiliary string models a contribution from the transcript of another
protocol, so that a new session changes the shifted key even under a
repeated \(\alpha\) (Remark~1 there).

FROST is an instance, with the identity exception of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rr-correct} in a suite whose element serialisation is not
total, with \(H_r\) a hash into scalars, whose role the
randomiser hash \(H_R\) of ZIP~312 plays, and with Rounds One and Two and
aggregation run unchanged on the shifted material (ePrint 2024/436,
Figure~5, read in additive notation and with the challenge input order of
RFC~9591, nonce commitment, key, message, in place of the figure's key,
nonce commitment, message; ZIP~312, ``Re-randomizable FROST''); its rounds
are constructed once, in Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds}. The shifted
material is valid key material: for the sharing polynomial \(f\), the
polynomial \(f + \hat{\alpha}\) has degree at most \(t - 1\), constant term
\(s + \hat{\alpha}\) and values \(\mathit{sk}_i + \hat{\alpha}\), and
\([s + \hat{\alpha}]B = \mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\),
\([\mathit{sk}_i + \hat{\alpha}]B = \mathit{PK}_i + [\hat{\alpha}]B\).
Recombination on it is Lemma~\ref{lem:recombine} applied to
\(f + \hat{\alpha}\), whose case \(f = 1\) gives
\(\sum_{i \in I} \lambda_{I,i} = 1\). Correctness of the instance is
Theorem~\ref{thm:rr-correct}.

\subsection{Randomiser generation}\label{sec:rr-randomiser}

\begin{construction}[Randomiser generation of ZIP 312]\label{def:rand-zip}
After Round One, let \(m\) be the message and \(L\) the commitment list of
Definition~\ref{def:commit-list}, sorted in ascending order of the nonzero
identifiers. The Coordinator draws \(u\) uniformly from the
\(N_{\mathrm{s}}\)-byte strings, \(N_{\mathrm{s}}\) the length of a
serialised scalar (\(32\) for FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512)), and sets
\[
  \hat{\alpha} := H_R\bigl(u \,\|\, \mathrm{enc}(L, m)\bigr) ,
\]
where \(\mathrm{enc}\) is any encoding from which \(m\) and, for each member
of \(L\), its identifier, hiding commitment and binding commitment are
recovered uniquely. It sends \(\hat{\alpha}\) itself to the signers.
Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): specified (ZIP~312, Draft).
\end{construction}

The construction is that of ZIP~312, ``Randomizer Generation'' and ``Round
Two - Signature Share Generation''. The ZIP's default encoding is a byte
serialisation of the message and the commitment list; it permits any other
encoding under which all these values are unambiguously encoded.

In spend authorisation the message is \(m = \mathsf{SigHash}(T)\) for the
transaction \(T\), and \(\mathsf{SigHash}\) commits to the randomised
validating key of every Action: ZIP~244, ``T.4c:
orchard\_actions\_noncompact\_digest'', field T.4c.ii, kept for version~6,
and with the same structure for the Ironwood pool, by ZIP~229,
``Transaction Identifiers, Auth Digests, and Signature Digests''
(\emph{Ironwood Guide}, Definition ``Signature digest''). The aggregate is
accepted only under \(\rk\), which for the shifted key of
Definition~\ref{def:rerand-ts} is \(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\). A
valid threshold spend by Construction~\ref{def:rand-zip} with this message
therefore needs a fixed point: a randomiser \(\hat{\alpha}\) equal to
\(H_R\) of an input containing the digest of a transaction that contains
\(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\).

\begin{proposition}[No randomiser derived from the signature digest]
\label{prop:circular}
Let \(\mathit{PK}\) be the group key of an Orchard-protocol threshold key,
with \(q = p_{\Vesta}\) and \(B = G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\). Let
\(H_R = \mathrm{ToScalar} \circ H'_R\), where \(H'_R\) is a random oracle
onto a set of size \(h\) and \(\mathrm{ToScalar}\) maps a uniform element
of that set to within \(\delta\) of uniform on \(\Fq\), the one-function
form of Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash}; for FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512),
\(h = 2^{512}\). Let an algorithm \(\mathcal{A}\), given \(\mathit{PK}\) and
making at most \(q_h\) queries to \(H'_R\), output a transaction \(T\) with
at most \(N_A\) Actions, an index \(j\), and a string
\(x = u \,\|\, \mathrm{enc}(L, m)\) with \(u\) of \(N_{\mathrm{s}}\) bytes,
as in Construction~\ref{def:rand-zip}. It \emph{wins} if
\(m = \mathsf{SigHash}(T)\) and the randomised validating key of Action
\(j\) of \(T\) equals \(\mathit{PK} + [H_R(x)]B\). With
\(Q := q_h + 1\),
\[
  \Pr[\mathcal{A}\ \text{wins}] \;\le\; \frac{Q}{h}
    + \sqrt{Q \cdot \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{cr}}(\mathcal{B})
      + Q^{2} N_A \,(1/q + \delta)}
\]
for an algorithm \(\mathcal{B}\) of about twice the running time of
\(\mathcal{A}\), where \(\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{cr}}(\mathcal{B})\) is its
probability of breaking the \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Assumption ``Collision
resistance of BLAKE2b''.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Only \(H'_R\) is modelled as a random oracle; the digest tree of
\(\mathsf{SigHash}\) is computed with BLAKE2b under other personalisations,
and only its collision resistance is used. Let \(\epsilon\) be the winning
probability. Define an algorithm \(\mathcal{F}\) for the \emph{Crypto
Guide}'s Theorem ``General forking lemma'' (\S``Security in the random
oracle model and the forking lemma''), with challenge set the range of
\(H'_R\), of size \(h\), and \(Q\) challenges. On input \(\mathit{PK}\) and
challenges \(c_1, \dots, c_Q\), it runs \(\mathcal{A}\), answering its
\(k\)-th distinct query to \(H'_R\) with \(c_k\); on the output
\((T, j, x)\) it queries \(x\) itself if \(\mathcal{A}\) did not; it returns
\((I, (T, j, x))\), with \(I\) the index of the query at \(x\) if
\(\mathcal{A}\) wins and \(I = 0\) otherwise. Its acceptance probability is
\(\epsilon\), so the forking algorithm succeeds with probability
\[
  \mathit{frk} \;\ge\; \epsilon\Bigl(\frac{\epsilon}{Q} - \frac{1}{h}\Bigr) .
\]
On success the two runs coincide until the \(I\)-th query is asked, so both
query the same string \(x\) at index \(I\), and both win with that \(x\),
with outputs \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) and challenges \(c_I \ne c'_I\). The
string \(x\) determines \(m\), since \(u\) has fixed length and the
encoding is unambiguous; hence
\(\mathsf{SigHash}(T_1) = \mathsf{SigHash}(T_2) = m\).

If the tuples of randomised validating keys of \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) differ,
descent through the two digest trees from their equal roots yields two
distinct pairs of personalisation and input with equal BLAKE2b output, each
node hashing a uniquely decodable input (ZIP~244, ``TxId Digest'' and
``Signature Digest''; ZIP~229, ``Transaction Identifiers, Auth Digests, and
Signature Digests''): at the first node whose inputs differ, the two pairs
collide. Running the forking algorithm and this descent is \(\mathcal{B}\).

Otherwise \(T_2\) contains the point \(\mathit{PK} +
[\mathrm{ToScalar}(c_I)]B\), which the first run placed at index \(j\) of
\(T_1\). The second run is independent of \(c_I\). For a fixed fork index
\(i\), each of the at most \(N_A\) keys of \(T_2\) equals
\(\mathit{PK} + [\mathrm{ToScalar}(c_i)]B\) only if
\(\mathrm{ToScalar}(c_i)\) takes one value fixed by the second run, which
has probability at most \(1/q + \delta\) (\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem
``Properties of statistical distance'', part (3)); summing over the keys and
over the \(Q\) indices,
\[
  \mathit{frk} \;\le\; \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{cr}}(\mathcal{B})
    + Q N_A\,(1/q + \delta) .
\]
With the lower bound, \(\epsilon^{2} - (Q/h)\,\epsilon - K \le 0\) for
\(K := Q \cdot \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{cr}}(\mathcal{B}) + Q^{2} N_A (1/q +
\delta)\), so
\(\epsilon \le \bigl(Q/h + \sqrt{(Q/h)^{2} + 4K}\bigr)/2
\le Q/h + \sqrt{K}\).
\end{proof}

The derivation of Construction~\ref{def:rand-zip} with the digest as
message therefore yields a valid threshold spend only with negligible
probability: infeasible in the random-oracle model, not impossible. For
FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), \(1/q + \delta < 2^{-253.9}\) and
\(Q/h = Q \cdot 2^{-512}\). Construction~\ref{def:rand-zip} is the only
specified derivation, so no specified randomiser derivation is usable for
spend authorisation; the order that a usable one must respect is
Proposition~\ref{prop:order}.

\begin{construction}[Seed-and-commitments randomiser]\label{def:rand-seed}
After Round One, let \(L\) be the commitment list over the signing set
\(I\). The Coordinator draws a seed \(v\) uniformly from the \(32\)-byte
strings and sends it to every member of \(I\) over a confidential and
authenticated channel (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (b)). The
Coordinator and every member of \(I\) compute
\[
  \hat{\alpha} := H_R\bigl(v \,\|\, \mathsf{enc}(L)\bigr) ,
\]
with \(\mathsf{enc}(L)\) the encoding of Definition~\ref{def:commit-list};
the message is not an input. Two rules are part of the construction and are this
volume's own: \(H_R\) in both computations, the Coordinator's and every
member's, so that all parties obtain one scalar; and the confidential and
authenticated channel for the seed, which the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rr-unlink}, step~(iv), shows necessary. Class
(Definition~\ref{def:classes}): designed but unspecified.
\end{construction}

The interface is that of ePrint 2024/436, Figure~5 and Remark~1, with
\((\alpha, \mathit{aux}) = (v, \mathsf{enc}(L))\) and every signer applying
\(H_R\); the encoding of the commitment list is that of RFC~9591,
Section~4.3. A seed flow is published only in the revision of ZIP~312
proposed in pull request~895, and the merged ZIP~312 has none. That hashing the
signer-chosen
commitments hedges a faulty generator at the Coordinator is asserted
informally in ePrint 2024/436, Section~7, and proved nowhere; the
Coordinator sees \(L\) before it chooses the seed, and it remains trusted
for unlinkability (Definition~\ref{def:rr-threat}).

\begin{lemma}[Distance of the randomiser from uniform]\label{lem:rand-dist}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash}, let an algorithm, the observer, make
at most \(q_h\) queries to \(H_R\) and, at one point, choose a byte string
\(w\) and receive \(\hat{\alpha} := H_R(u \,\|\, w)\), for \(u\) uniform on
the \(N_u\)-byte strings and independent of all else. The pair formed by
the observer's view and \(\hat{\alpha}\) is within statistical distance
\[
  \delta + q_h \cdot 2^{-8 N_u}
\]
of the pair in which \(\hat{\alpha}\) is replaced by a uniform element of
\(\Fq\) independent of all else. For Construction~\ref{def:rand-zip},
\(N_u = N_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(w = \mathrm{enc}(L, m)\); for
Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed}, \(N_u = 32\) and \(w = \mathsf{enc}(L)\);
with \(N_u = 32\) the bound is \(\delta + q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof is that of Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce-uniform} with \(H_R\) in place of
\(H_3\) and \(N_u\) bytes in place of \(32\). The observer queries \(H_R\)
at \(u \,\|\, w\) with probability at most \(q_h \cdot 2^{-8 N_u}\), by the
union bound over its queries, each having prefix \(u\) with probability
\(2^{-8 N_u}\) in the experiment in which the answer used for
\(\hat{\alpha}\) is resampled. Outside that event the two experiments
coincide, and the resampled answer is within \(\delta\) of uniform; the
triangle inequality (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Statistical distance'', Theorem
``Properties of statistical distance'') gives the bound.
\end{proof}

At \(q_h = 2^{64}\) and \(N_u = 32\) the bound is below \(2^{-191.99}\).
The randomiser is thus close to uniform, not exactly uniform as ZIP~312,
``Rationale'', states.

\begin{remark}[Effective randomiser]\label{rem:effective-rand}
The randomiser of ZIP~312 is the effective randomiser \(\hat{\alpha}\) of
ePrint 2024/436, not its \(\alpha\). The correspondence carries correctness
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rr-correct}), not the paper's unforgeability game, whose
signing oracle applies \(H_r\) itself to an adversarial
\((\alpha, \mathit{aux})\) (Definition~6 and Figure~4, with
\(\mathsf{RandShare}\) and
\(\mathsf{RandPK}\) of Figure~5). Only
Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed} presents the paper's interface. Wherever
the Coordinator sends \(\hat{\alpha}\) itself, as in the wire form of
Construction~\ref{def:rand-zip} or when it forwards a randomiser chosen
elsewhere, it chooses the effective randomiser outright and the signers use
it unchecked; the paper's game does not model this. This remark is the
volume's one statement of that fact; later statements cite it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Authorisation of the signed message}\label{sec:auth-message}

\begin{definition}[Message check]\label{def:msg-check}
In spend authorisation the signed message is \(m = \mathsf{SigHash}(T)\)
for a transaction \(T\) (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, Definition ``Signature
digest''), which alone does not show a signer what it authorises. With
\(m\) the Coordinator sends \emph{transaction data} \(D_T\), further data of
\(T\), over the confidential and authenticated channel of
Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (b). A signer \emph{performs the
message check} if, before returning a share, it verifies that \(m\) is the
signature digest determined by \(D_T\), or computes \(m\) from \(D_T\)
itself. A signature share counts as an \emph{approval} of the pair
\((\rk, m)\), with \(\rk\) the shifted group key of its request, only if its
signer performed the message check.
\end{definition}

ZIP~312, ``Round Two - Signature Share Generation'', makes the check a MUST
and names as possible data the transaction itself, openings of value
commitments and decryptions of note ciphertexts; RFC~9591, Section~7.7
(``Input Message Validation''), recommends such input validation. The honest
participants of the security games of \S\ref{sec:sec} perform the check by
definition, and their returned shares are the approvals the games count. A
signer that omits it authorises whatever the transaction builder chose.
Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): the obligation is specified; the
mechanism by which a signer obtains and checks \(D_T\) is outside ZIP~312
and designed but unspecified.

\subsection{Re-randomised signing, aggregation and share verification}
\label{sec:rr-sign}

\begin{construction}[Re-randomised signing of ZIP 312]\label{def:rr-rounds}
The key material is valid (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}); the
ciphersuite has the randomiser hash \(H_R\) (Definition~\ref{def:suite},
part (f)).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Round One.} Each participant runs Construction~\ref{def:round1}
  unchanged and sends its commitments over an authenticated channel
  (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (a)).
\item \emph{Randomiser.} The Coordinator chooses the signing set \(I\) and,
  for each \(i \in I\), one unused commitment pair, forms the commitment
  list \(L\), and obtains \(\hat{\alpha}\) by
  Construction~\ref{def:rand-zip} or Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed},
  fresh for this run. It sends to each \(P_i\), \(i \in I\), the tuple
  \((m, L, \hat{\alpha}, D_T)\), or \((m, L, v, D_T)\) with the seed \(v\)
  of Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed}, where \(D_T\) is the data of
  Definition~\ref{def:msg-check}, over a confidential and authenticated
  channel (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (b)); RFC~9591 requires
  authentication only.
\item \emph{Round Two.} Member \(P_i\) performs the checks of
  Construction~\ref{def:round2}, its check (1) being the message check of
  Definition~\ref{def:msg-check}; in the seed variant it computes
  \(\hat{\alpha} := H_R(v \,\|\, \mathsf{enc}(L))\). It computes
  \(\mathit{PK}' := \mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\) from its stored group
  key and runs Construction~\ref{def:round2} with
  \(\mathit{sk}_i + \hat{\alpha}\) in place of \(\mathit{sk}_i\) and
  \(\mathit{PK}'\) in place of \(\mathit{PK}\). The shifted group key
  \(\mathit{PK}'\), not \(\mathit{PK}\), is the key serialised into \(H_1\)
  for the binding factors and into \(H_2\) for the challenge
  (Definition~\ref{def:binding}). Its share is
  \[
    z_i := d_i + e_i \rho_i
      + \lambda_{I,i}\,(\mathit{sk}_i + \hat{\alpha})\, c ,
  \]
  returned as in RFC~9591 over an authenticated, not confidential, channel
  (Definition~\ref{def:channels}, type (a)).
\item \emph{Aggregation.} The Coordinator runs
  Construction~\ref{def:aggregate} with \(\mathit{PK}'\) in place of
  \(\mathit{PK}\), and verifies the aggregate under \(\mathit{PK}'\).
\item \emph{Share verification.} The share equation of
  Construction~\ref{def:aggregate} is checked with
  \(\mathit{PK}_i + [\hat{\alpha}]B\) in place of \(\mathit{PK}_i\) and with
  the shifted group key:
  \[
    [z_i]B \;=\; R_i + [c\, \lambda_{I,i}]\,
      \bigl(\mathit{PK}_i + [\hat{\alpha}]B\bigr) .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Every signing run uses a fresh randomiser, generated in its own Round Two:
a randomiser used for two runs of one key gives both the same shifted key
and links them. The output \((R, z)\) is a signature under
\(\rk := \mathit{PK}'\). Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): specified
(ZIP~312, Draft), the seed variant designed but unspecified.
\end{construction}

The construction is that of ZIP~312, ``Round One - Commitment'', ``Round
Two - Signature Share Generation'' and ``Signature Share Verification and
Aggregation'', over RFC~9591, Sections~5.1 to 5.4: the randomised signing,
aggregation and share-verification functions are the regular ones with the
share, the verification share and the group key shifted by \(\hat{\alpha}\)
and by \([\hat{\alpha}]B\). Round One keeps the unshifted share in the nonce
hash, the randomiser not yet existing. The keys \(\mathit{PK}\) and
\(\mathit{PK}_i\) are those stored from key generation, never taken from the
messages of the run (Construction~\ref{def:aggregate}). What the returned
shares reveal to a party that sees them is
Proposition~\ref{prop:share-link}.

\begin{theorem}[Correctness of re-randomised FROST]\label{thm:rr-correct}
Let the key material be valid, with signing key \(s\) and group key
\(\mathit{PK}\); let \(\hat{\alpha} \in \Fq\), \(I\) a signing set and
\(m\) a message that every member of \(I\) accepts. Outside the exceptions
below, a run of Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds} in which every party is
honest outputs \((R, z)\) with
\[
  R = [r]B , \qquad r := \sum_{i \in I} (d_i + e_i \rho_i) , \qquad
  z = r + c\,(s + \hat{\alpha}) ,
\]
which the single-party verification accepts on \(m\) under
\(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\), and every share satisfies its shifted
share equation. The exceptions are those of Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct}
for the shifted material, together with the identity case of the shifted
group key: in a suite whose element serialisation is not total, the run
fails when \(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B = \mathcal{O}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:rerand-ts} and the paragraph following it, the
shifted shares \(\mathit{sk}_j + \hat{\alpha}\), verification shares
\(\mathit{PK}_j + [\hat{\alpha}]B\) and group key
\(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\) are valid key material for
\(s + \hat{\alpha}\), with sharing polynomial \(f + \hat{\alpha}\). Steps
(3) to (5) of Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds} are
Constructions~\ref{def:round2} and~\ref{def:aggregate} run on that key
material. Round One enters those constructions only through the nonces,
each an answer of \(H_3\) whichever share enters its input.
Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct} applied to the shifted
material gives \(R = [r]B\), \(z = r + c(s + \hat{\alpha})\) and acceptance
under \(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\), outside its cases; and
Proposition~\ref{prop:ident-abort}, part (ii), applied to it gives the
shifted share equations. Both use only validity of the key material. When
\(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B = \mathcal{O}\) in a suite whose element
serialisation is not total, \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK}')\) is
undefined, and so are the binding factors of Definition~\ref{def:binding};
the run fails.
\end{proof}

ZIP~312, ``Rationale'', makes the same argument, and ePrint 2024/436,
Equation~(2), displays it; the ZIP cites, rather than proves, the
coefficient sum one on which it rests, which Lemma~\ref{lem:recombine}
proves.

\subsection{Trust model of ZIP 312}\label{sec:rr-trust}

\begin{remark}[Requirements of ZIP 312]\label{rem:zip312-req}
ZIP~312, ``Requirements'', states three requirements, in lower case,
although its ``Terminology'' invokes the key words of RFC~2119:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] all signatures generated by following the ZIP must verify as
  Sapling or Orchard spend-authorisation signatures under the appropriate
  validating key, which Proposition~\ref{prop:aggregate-valid} proves for
  the Pallas suite, the Sapling suite being outside the volume;
\item[(b)] they should meet the security criteria of the protocol
  specification for signatures with re-randomisable keys,
  Definition~\ref{def:surk}, whose threshold form is an open problem
  (Table~\ref{tab:status});
\item[(c)] the threat model must be taken into account, which
  Definition~\ref{def:rr-threat} makes exact.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Threat model of ZIP 312]\label{def:rr-threat}
The threat model of ZIP~312 consists of two clauses.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The Coordinator is trusted with the privacy of the transaction,
  unlinkability included. A rogue Coordinator can break both, but ``should
  not be able to create signed transactions without the approval of''
  \(t\) participants, ``as specified in FROST''; the ZIP's
  \(\mathrm{MIN\_PARTICIPANTS}\) is \(t\). An approval is a share returned
  after the message check (Definition~\ref{def:msg-check}).
\item[(ii)] Every key-share holder is likewise trusted with the privacy of
  the transaction and with unlinkability.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The clauses are those of ZIP~312, ``Threat Model''. The should of clause
(i) is lower case, within a threat model that the Requirements say must be
taken into account; it is not a must not. ePrint 2024/436, Section~5.1,
states the same split: the party choosing the randomiser is trusted with
privacy, not with security. The privacy trust is forced by the flow: the
Coordinator generates \(\hat{\alpha}\), every signer receives it, or the seed
with \(L\), and whoever holds it computes \(\mathit{PK} = \rk -
[\hat{\alpha}]B\) and links the run to the key (the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rr-unlink}, step~(iv)). The unforgeability clause has no
proof, as \S\ref{sec:sec-authority} records (Remark~\ref{rem:authority-status}).
In RFC~9591, Section~7, the
Coordinator holds no private information; trust in it for privacy is what
re-randomisation adds.

ZIP~312, ``Non-requirements'', excludes four things: removal of the
Coordinator role (RFC~9591, Section~7.5, ``Removing the Coordinator
Role''); prevention of key-share holders from linking a signing run to its
transaction on the chain; a key-generation procedure, for which it refers
to RFC~9591; and network privacy.

\begin{remark}[Unlinkability as ZIP 312 states it]\label{rem:zip-unlink}
ZIP~312, ``Terminology'', defines unlinkability as statistical independence
of signatures from the signers' long-term keys, ensuring, ``for perfectly
uniform generation of Randomizers and no leakage of metadata'', that it is
``impossible to determine whether two transactions were generated by the
same party''. The statement is informal: it names no observer and no view.
Definition~\ref{def:outsider-unlink} makes it exact against a specified
observer, and Lemma~\ref{lem:rand-dist} replaces perfect uniformity.
\end{remark}

\section{The aggregate as a spend-authorisation signature}\label{sec:auth}

This section instantiates the re-randomised protocol of \S\ref{sec:rr} over
the ciphersuite FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) and composes it with the Orchard
protocol. After the ciphersuite comes the section's main result: the
aggregate is an ordinary RedPallas spend-authorisation signature under the
shifted group key, and consensus accepts it for an Action exactly when the
effective randomiser is the Action's randomiser. There follow the sign
normalisation that makes a threshold group key a spend validating key; the
key tree of ZIP~2005 for an
Orchard-protocol threshold key, with the lemma that its common secrets are
simulable; the order of randomiser, randomised validating key, signature
digest and proof that the main result forces; and the threshold construction
of an Action in that order. Throughout, \(q = p_{\Vesta}\),
\(B = G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\), the effective randomiser is \(\hat{\alpha}\)
(Definition~\ref{def:rerand-ts}) and the randomiser of an Action is
\(\alpha\).

\subsection{The ciphersuite FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512)}
\label{sec:auth-suite}

For a point \(P\) of the Pallas group write
\(\underline{P} := \mathsf{LEBS2OSP}_{256}(P^{\star})\), the \(32\)-byte
form of the star encoding (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Fields, groups, and
encodings'', Definition ``Star encoding''), and \(\mathsf{abst}_{\PP}\) for
its partial inverse.

\begin{construction}[FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512)]\label{def:pallas-suite}
The ciphersuite FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) is the following instance of
Definition~\ref{def:suite}, for re-randomised signing.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \emph{Group.} The Pallas group \(\Ep(\F_{p_{\Pallas}})\), of
  prime order \(q = p_{\Vesta}\) (ZIP~312's \(r_{\PP}\)) and cofactor \(1\),
  with identity \(\mathcal{O}\) and generator
  \[
    B = G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}} = \GroupHash(\texttt{z.cash:Orchard},
    \texttt{G})
  \]
  (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``GroupHash, domain separation, and
  nothing-up-my-sleeve generators''; Definition ``Spend-authorisation and
  binding-signature instances''), ZIP~312's \(\mathcal{G}^{\mathsf{Orchard}}\).
  A random scalar is uniform on \(\{0, \dots, p_{\Vesta} - 1\}\).
\item[(b)] \emph{Elements.} \(N_{\mathrm{e}} = 32\) and
  \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(P) := \underline{P}\), which is total:
  \(\mathcal{O}^{\star} = \mathrm{LE}_{256}(0)\). The deserialisation maps a
  \(32\)-byte string \(b\) to
  \(\mathsf{abst}_{\PP}(\mathsf{LEBS2OSP}_{256}^{-1}(b))\), and fails if
  that value is \(\bot\) and, in addition, if it is \(\mathcal{O}\). The
  function \(\mathsf{abst}_{\PP}\) itself decodes \(\mathrm{LE}_{256}(0)\)
  to \(\mathcal{O}\) (step~2 of Definition ``Star encoding''); the rejection
  of the identity is the suite's, not \(\mathsf{abst}_{\PP}\)'s.
\item[(c)] \emph{Scalars.} \(N_{\mathrm{s}} = 32\),
  \(\mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(k) := \mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{256}(k)\), and
  \(\mathsf{deser}_{\Fq}\) fails on a \(32\)-byte string whose little-endian
  integer is not below \(p_{\Vesta}\).
\item[(d)] \emph{Hashes.} BLAKE2b-512 with a \(16\)-byte
  personalisation, \(N_{\mathrm{h}} = 64\), the roles \(H_1\) to \(H_5\)
  as in Table~\ref{tab:pallas-hashes}.
\item[(e)] \emph{Signature encoding and verification} as in
  Definition~\ref{def:suite}, part (e), with these data.
\item[(f)] \emph{Randomiser hash.} The role \(H_R\) of
  Table~\ref{tab:pallas-hashes}.
\end{enumerate}
Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): specified (ZIP~312, Draft).
\end{construction}

The data are those of ZIP~312, ``FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512)'', which takes
the group, the order and the encoding \(\mathsf{repr}_{\PP}\) from the
protocol specification, \S``Pallas and Vesta'', and the base point from
\href{https://zips.z.cash/protocol/protocol.pdf\#concretespendauthsig}%
{\S``Spend Authorization Signature (Sapling and Orchard)''}. The generic
element serialisation of RFC~9591, Section~3.1, fails on the identity; this
one does not. Three consequences for Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct} follow.
Its case~(2), \(R = \mathcal{O}\), does not arise, the serialisation being
total. Its case~(1), a zero nonce, remains: a commitment \(D_i\) or \(E_i\)
equal to \(\mathcal{O}\) is serialised in Round One and rejected by
\(\mathsf{deser}_{\G}\) in check~(3) of Round Two. The only identity
condition left on the output is the consensus rule
\(\rk \ne \mathcal{O}\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}).

\begin{proposition}[Random-oracle model of the Pallas ciphersuite]
\label{prop:pallas-hash}
Assume Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} in its form for a suite built from
one hash function under distinct personalisations: the map
\((\mathit{pers}, x) \mapsto \mathrm{BLAKE2b\text{-}512}(\mathit{pers}, x)\)
is modelled as a random oracle. Its restriction to
\(\mathit{pers} = \texttt{Zcash\_RedPallasH}\) is the \emph{Ironwood
Guide}'s Assumption ``The RedPallas hash as a random oracle''. No further
assumption is introduced. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the hashes \(H_1\), \(H_2\), \(H_3\), \(H_4\), \(H_5\) and \(H_R\)
  are independent random oracles;
\item[(ii)] each value of \(H_1\), \(H_2\), \(H_3\) and \(H_R\) at a new
  input is within statistical distance
  \[
    \delta_{512} := p_{\Vesta} / 2^{512} < 2^{-257}
  \]
  of a uniform scalar, so the suite meets Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash}
  with \(\delta = \delta_{512}\);
\item[(iii)] the hashes \(H_4\) and \(H_5\) are random oracles onto the
  \(64\)-byte strings, so \(Q\) queries find a collision of either with
  probability at most \(Q^2 / 2^{513}\);
\item[(iv)] the independence of \(H_{\mathrm{dkg}}\) that
  Assumptions~\ref{ass:dkg-hash} and~\ref{ass:suite-hash} require holds for
  every instantiation of \(H_{\mathrm{dkg}}\) by BLAKE2b-512 under a
  \(16\)-byte personalisation distinct from the six of
  Table~\ref{tab:pallas-hashes}. No such instantiation is specified
  (ZIP~312, ``Key Generation'').
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) The six personalisations are pairwise distinct and all \(16\) bytes
long, hence prefix-free; \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Domain separation and
personalisation'', Proposition ``Domain separation yields independent
oracles'', gives independence. (ii) A value at a new input is
\(\mathrm{ToScalar}\) of a uniform \(64\)-byte string, whose distance from
uniform is at most \(p_{\Vesta}/2^{512}\) by \emph{Math Guide}, \S``Uniform
sampling and the bias of modular reduction'', Proposition ``Bias of modular
reduction'', with \(L = 512\), the bound that the Ironwood assumption
records; and \(p_{\Vesta}/2^{512} = 2^{-258}(1 + e)\) with
\(0 < e < 2^{-128}\). (iii) Birthday bound, \emph{Math Guide}, \S``The union
bound and a birthday calculation'', Proposition ``Birthday bound'':
\(\binom{Q}{2} 2^{-512} \le Q^2/2^{513}\). (iv) Part (i) applied to seven
distinct personalisations.
\end{proof}

At \(Q = 2^{64}\) the collision bound of part (iii) is \(2^{-385}\).

\subsection{Validity of the aggregate}\label{sec:auth-valid}

\begin{proposition}[Aggregates are spend-authorisation signatures]
\label{prop:aggregate-valid}
Let valid key material (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}) with group key
\(Y\) be given over FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), let
\(\hat{\alpha} \in \Fq\) be an effective randomiser and \(m\) a message that
every member of the
signing set accepts (Definition~\ref{def:msg-check}).
Every run of Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds} in which every party is honest
either fails, in the zero-nonce case of Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct}, or
outputs \((R, z)\) whose encoding
\[
  \sigma := \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(z)
\]
(RFC~9591, Appendix~A) is accepted by the validation of the
spend-authorisation instance of RedPallas (\emph{Ironwood Guide},
Construction ``RedPallas'', part~3, with base \(G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\))
under \(X := Y + [\hat{\alpha}]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) on \(M := m\). No
completed honest run yields an aggregate that validation rejects.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rr-correct} gives
\[
  [z]B = R + [c]X \quad\text{with}\quad
  c = H_2(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(X) \,\|\, m)
\]
(RFC~9591, Section~4.6), its only exception in this suite being the zero
nonce, since \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}\) is total. Validation of \(\sigma\) under
\(X\) on \(m\) proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The first \(32\) bytes are \(\bar{R} = \underline{R}\), and
  \(\mathsf{abst}_{\PP}(R^{\star}) = R\) because the star encoding is
  canonical and total, \(R = \mathcal{O}\) included; so the decoded point is
  \(R \ne \bot\).
\item The last \(32\) bytes give \(S = z\), an integer below \(p_{\Vesta}\)
  since \(z \in \Fq\).
\item Validation computes
  \(\mathsf{H}^{\circledast}(\bar{R} \,\|\, \underline{X} \,\|\, m)\), where
  \(\underline{X} = \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(X)\): the inputs of the FROST challenge
  in the same order. Since \(H_2 = \mathsf{H}^{\circledast}\)
  (Table~\ref{tab:pallas-hashes}), it recomputes \(c\).
\item The equation \([S]B = R + [c]X\), whose cofactor is \(1\) on Pallas,
  is the FROST equation of RFC~9591, Appendix~B, which holds.
\end{enumerate}
How \(R\) was produced does not enter validation. These are the conditions
that ZIP~312, ``Rationale'', lists.
\end{proof}

ZIP~312, ``FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512)'', states that the suite is meant to
produce signatures ``indistinguishable from RedPallas Orchard Spend
Authorization Signatures''. This volume reads that phrase as the statement of
format and validation above; the distributional statement, for randomisers
of Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed}, is Proposition~\ref{prop:rr-unlink}.

\begin{corollary}[The effective randomiser is the Action's randomiser]
\label{cor:alpha}
Let \(Y = \aksym^{\PP} = [s]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) be the spend
validating key of the key of the note that an Action consumes, and let the
Action carry
\(\rk = \aksym^{\PP} + [\alpha]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) (\emph{Ironwood
Guide}, \S``Randomised validating keys'', Construction ``Randomised validating
key''). Then
\[
  Y + [\hat{\alpha}]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}} = \rk
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \hat{\alpha} = \alpha \ \text{in}\ \F_{p_{\Vesta}} .
\]
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] if \(\hat{\alpha} = \alpha\), the aggregate of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:aggregate-valid} on the signature digest is valid
  under \(\rk\);
\item[(ii)] if \(\hat{\alpha} \ne \alpha\), with \(\alpha\) and
  \(\hat{\alpha}\) fixed before the queries to \(H_2\) whose inputs contain
  either key, the aggregate is valid under \(\rk\) with probability at most
  \((Q_2 + 2)(1/p_{\Vesta} + \delta_{512})\) in an experiment with at most
  \(Q_2\) queries to \(H_2\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:rr-gap}, applied to the
  key \(Y + [\hat{\alpha}]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) and the shift
  \(\alpha - \hat{\alpha} \ne 0\));
\item[(iii)] the consensus rule \(\rk \ne \mathcal{O}\) (protocol
  specification, \S``Action Descriptions''; \emph{Ironwood Guide},
  Construction ``Verification'', part~(1)) fails exactly when
  \(\alpha = -s\). For \(\alpha = \hat{\alpha}\) the output of
  Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed}, fixed after the key, this has
  probability at most
  \[
    1/p_{\Vesta} + \delta_{512} + q_h \cdot 2^{-256} ,
  \]
  with \(q_h\) the number of queries to \(H_R\) made by the party that
  fixed the key (Lemma~\ref{lem:rand-dist}).
\end{enumerate}
Hence, up to these probabilities, consensus accepts the aggregate as the
Action's spend-authorisation signature if and only if the effective
randomiser equals the Action's \(\alpha\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The map \(k \mapsto [k]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) is injective on
\(\F_{p_{\Vesta}}\), since \(G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) generates a group of
prime order \(p_{\Vesta}\); and
\(Y + [\hat{\alpha}]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}} - \rk
= [\hat{\alpha} - \alpha]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\). Part (i) is
Proposition~\ref{prop:aggregate-valid} with \(X = \rk\). Part (ii) is the
cited proposition, whose hypotheses hold: the shifted material is valid key
material (Definition~\ref{def:rerand-ts} and the paragraph following it),
and \(\rk \ne \mathcal{O}\) in an accepted Action. For (iii),
\(\rk = [s + \alpha]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) is \(\mathcal{O}\) exactly when
\(\alpha = -s\); a value within statistical distance \(\epsilon\) of uniform
takes a fixed value with probability at most \(1/p_{\Vesta} + \epsilon\)
(\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem ``Properties of statistical distance'', part
(3)), and Lemma~\ref{lem:rand-dist} gives
\(\epsilon = \delta_{512} + q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\).
\end{proof}

For FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) the term \(1/p_{\Vesta} + \delta_{512}\) of
parts (ii) and (iii) is below \(2^{-253.9}\).

\begin{remark}[Consensus is unchanged]\label{rem:consensus-unchanged}
No consensus rule refers to FROST or to a threshold key. The rules that bear
on spend authorisation, \(\rk \ne \mathcal{O}\) and validity of the
spend-authorisation signature under \(\rk\) on the signature digest
(\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Verification of a transaction'', Construction
``Verification''), read only \(\rk\), the \(64\)-byte signature and the
digest; validation does not depend on how \(R\) was produced (ZIP~312,
``Rationale''). The first requirement of ZIP~312, ``Requirements'', that
every signature produced by following the ZIP be verified successfully as an
Orchard spend-authorisation signature under the appropriate validating key,
is met by Proposition~\ref{prop:aggregate-valid}. The remark makes no claim
about the security of the signers, which is the subject of \S\ref{sec:sec}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Sign normalisation of a shared key}\label{sec:auth-norm}

The spend validating key \(\aksym^{\PP}\) of an Orchard-protocol key has
even \(y\)-coordinate: the last bit, the \(\tilde{y}\) bit, of
\(\mathsf{repr}_{\PP}(\aksym^{\PP})\) is \(0\). For keys with
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\) the protocol specification,
\S``Orchard Key Components'', produces this by negating \(\ask\) when the
bit is \(1\) (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``The spending key and the spend-side
secrets'', Construction ``Spend validating key and sign normalisation''). For
keys with \(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\), ZIP~2005's replacement text
for that section (``Changes to the Protocol Specification'', \S\,4.2.3
``Orchard Key Components'') requires
\(\aksym^{\PP} \in \PP^{*}\) to be obtained ``by any suitably secure method
that ensures the last bit of \(\mathsf{repr}_{\PP}(\aksym^{\PP})\) is
\(0\)''; the published specification does not yet carry that text. The
requirement is one of key derivation, not a consensus rule: the Action
statement admits either sign (protocol specification, \S``Action Statement
(Orchard)''; \emph{Ironwood Guide}, Remark ``Sign of \(\aksym^{\PP}\)''). A
full viewing key records only \(\aksym = \Extract_{\PP}(\aksym^{\PP})\)
(\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Viewing keys'', Definition ``Full viewing key''),
from which a transaction builder recovers the point by the even-\(y\) lift.
For a group key \(\mathit{PK}\) of odd \(y\) the lift yields
\(-\mathit{PK}\); the builder forms
\(\rk = -\mathit{PK} + [\alpha]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\), which with
\(\alpha = \hat{\alpha}\) differs from \(\mathit{PK} +
[\hat{\alpha}]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) by \([2]\mathit{PK} \ne \mathcal{O}\),
and by Proposition~\ref{prop:rr-gap}, part (ii), applied to the key
\(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) and the shift \(-2s \ne
0\), the aggregate is accepted under \(\rk\) with probability at most \((Q_2 +
2)(1/p_{\Vesta} + \delta_{512})\). The randomised key \(\rk\) carries no parity
condition. Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): specified (ZIP~2005, Proposed,
taking effect with NU6.3, which ZIP~258, Draft, deploys; ZIP~258, ``ZIP 2005
activation'').

\begin{lemma}[Negation of key material]\label{lem:negate}
Let shares \(\mathit{sk}_j\), coefficient commitments
\(A_0, \dots, A_{t-1}\), verification shares \(\mathit{PK}_j\) and group key
\(\mathit{PK}\) be valid key material for the signing key \(s\) with sharing
polynomial \(f\) (Definition~\ref{def:key-material}). Then
\(-\mathit{sk}_j\), \(-A_k\), \(-\mathit{PK}_j\) and \(-\mathit{PK}\) are
valid key material for \(-s\), with sharing polynomial \(-f\). If
\(\mathit{PK} \ne \mathcal{O}\), the last bit of
\(\mathsf{repr}_{\PP}(-\mathit{PK})\) differs from that of
\(\mathsf{repr}_{\PP}(\mathit{PK})\), and
\(\Extract_{\PP}(-\mathit{PK}) = \Extract_{\PP}(\mathit{PK})\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial \(-f\) has degree at most \(t - 1\), coefficients \(-a_k\)
and constant term \(-s\); and \(-\mathit{sk}_j = (-f)(j)\),
\([-a_k]B = -A_k\), \([-\mathit{sk}_j]B = -\mathit{PK}_j\) and
\([-s]B = -\mathit{PK}\), which are the conditions of
Definition~\ref{def:key-material}. For
\(\mathit{PK} = (x, y) \ne \mathcal{O}\),
\(-\mathit{PK} = (x, p_{\Pallas} - y)\). Here \(y \ne 0\), since a point
with \(y = 0\) has order \(2\) and the group has odd order; and
\(p_{\Pallas}\) is odd, so \(y\) and \(p_{\Pallas} - y\) have opposite
parities. The last bit of the star encoding is \(y \bmod 2\)
(\emph{Ironwood Guide}, Definition ``Star encoding''). The extracted
coordinate is \(x\) for both points (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Fields,
groups, and encodings'', Lemma ``Coordinate extraction identifies opposite
points'').
\end{proof}

On the toy curve of Example~\ref{ex:frost-toy}, the negated material has
\(-s = 646\) and shares \(835\), \(57\), \(246\); the group key
\(\mathit{PK} = (526, 125)\) becomes \(-\mathit{PK} = (526, 884)\), with the
same \(x\)-coordinate and the parity of \(y\) changed from \(1\) to \(0\).

\begin{construction}[Sign normalisation of key material]\label{def:normalise}
\emph{Input}: valid key material output by Construction~\ref{def:dealer} or
by Construction~\ref{def:dkg}. Every party computes \(\mathit{PK}\) from
public data (Lemma~\ref{lem:group-info}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\mathit{PK} = \mathcal{O}\), key generation is repeated:
  ZIP~2005 requires \(\aksym^{\PP} \in \PP^{*}\), and neither key
  generation excludes \(s = 0\).
\item Otherwise, if the last bit of \(\mathsf{repr}_{\PP}(\mathit{PK})\) is
  \(1\), every participant \(P_j\) replaces its share \(\mathit{sk}_j\) by
  \(-\mathit{sk}_j\), and every party replaces each \(A_k\), each
  \(\mathit{PK}_j\) and \(\mathit{PK}\) by its negative.
\end{enumerate}
The test reads public data only, so all parties decide identically without
interaction. \emph{Output}: the resulting key material and
\(\aksym^{\PP} := \mathit{PK}\). Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): the
requirement is specified (ZIP~2005, Proposed); the method is this volume's.
\end{construction}

\begin{proof}[Correctness]
Validity of the output and even \(y\) of \(\aksym^{\PP}\) follow from
Lemma~\ref{lem:negate}; \(\mathit{PK} \ne \mathcal{O}\) on output by
step~(1).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The key tree of an Orchard-protocol FROST key}
\label{sec:auth-keytree}

The \emph{Ironwood Guide} constructs only keys with
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\) (Remark ``Scope of the key
derivations''). Write \(\mathsf{PRFexpand}\) for
the \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Construction ``Expansion function'' and
\(\mathrm{ToBase}\), \(\mathrm{ToScalar}\) for its Definition ``Field
reductions'' (``Pseudorandom functions and field reductions'');
\(\mathsf{truncate}_{32}\) returns the first \(32\) bytes of its input.

\begin{construction}[Orchard-protocol threshold key]\label{def:orchard-key}
This is the branch \(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\) of ZIP~2005's
replacement for \S\,4.2.3 ``Orchard Key Components''.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Run a key generation (\S\ref{sec:keygen}) and
  Construction~\ref{def:normalise}; let
  \(\aksym^{\PP} := \mathit{PK}\) and
  \(\aksym := \Extract_{\PP}(\aksym^{\PP})\), non-zero since
  \(\aksym^{\PP} \ne \mathcal{O}\) (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, Lemma ``Extract
  vanishes only at the identity'').
\item The participants privately agree on a spending key \(\mathsf{sk}\), a
  \(32\)-byte string drawn uniformly and independently of the key material.
\item Every participant computes
  \begin{align*}
    \nksym &:= \mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{nk}}(\mathsf{sk})
      = \mathrm{ToBase}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}
        ([\mathtt{0x07}])\bigr) , \\
    \mathsf{qsk} &:= \mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{qsk}}(\mathsf{sk})
      = \mathsf{truncate}_{32}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}
        ([\mathtt{0x0C}])\bigr) , \\
    \mathsf{qk} &:= \mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{qk}}(\mathsf{qsk}) \\
      &\phantom{:}= \mathrm{BLAKE3.derive\_key}(
        \texttt{Zcash ZIP 2005 qk-derivation v1}, \mathsf{qsk}, 32) , \\
    \rivk &:= \mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{rivk\_ext}}_{\mathsf{qk}}(\aksym, \nksym) \\
      &\phantom{:}= \mathrm{ToScalar}\bigl(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{qk}}
        ([\mathtt{0x0D}] \,\|\, \mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{256}(\aksym) \,\|\,
        \mathsf{I2LEOSP}_{256}(\nksym))\bigr) ,
  \end{align*}
  where \(\mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{qk}}\) is the key-derivation mode of the BLAKE3
  hash function with the displayed context string and a \(32\)-byte output,
  ZIP~2005's derivation, of which no property is used below.
\item The remainder of \S\,4.2.3 is unchanged:
  \(\ivk := \CommitIvk_{\rivk}(\aksym, \nksym)\), the internal keys and the
  other keys derived from \((\aksym, \nksym, \rivk)\), and the rejection of
  a key whose \(\ivk\) or internal \(\ivk\) lies in \(\{0, \bot\}\). On
  rejection step~(2) is repeated with a new \(\mathsf{sk}\), and
  \(\aksym^{\PP}\) is kept.
\item The protocol MUST ensure that all participants obtain the same
  \(\aksym\), \(\nksym\) and \(\rivk\), which ZIP~2005 calls
  \(\mathsf{rivk\_ext}\).
\end{enumerate}
The spend authorising key \(\ask = s\) stays threshold-shared.
Figure~\ref{fig:auth-keys} shows the tree.
\end{construction}

Steps~(3) and~(5), the agreement of step~(2) and the rejection of step~(4)
quote ZIP~2005, ``Usage with FROST'' and ``Changes to the Protocol
Specification'', \S\,4.2.3; the uniform, independent draw of
\(\mathsf{sk}\) in step~(2) and keeping \(\aksym^{\PP}\) on rejection in
step~(4) are this volume's reading of ``privately agree'' and of the
repetition with a new \(\mathsf{sk}\). Step~(1) meets the even-\(y\)
clause of that section by Construction~\ref{def:normalise}. Attempts of
step~(4) are independent, \(\mathsf{sk}\) being fresh on each; the volume
proves no bound on their rejection probability.

ZIP~2005 names two sources of \(\aksym^{\PP}\) for
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\): direct generation of an
Orchard-protocol \(\ask \in \F^{*}_{p_{\Vesta}}\) with
\(\aksym^{\PP} = [\ask]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\), which a dealer then shares
by Construction~\ref{def:dealer}; and FROST distributed key generation in
which the spend-authorisation material is \(t\)-of-\(n\) shared, here
Construction~\ref{def:dkg}. Its ``Deployment'' states that ``FROST
distributed key generation requires the \(\mathsf{use\_qsk} =
\mathsf{true}\) case''. The key-generation section that ZIP~2005 cites for
the second method is ZIP~312, ``Key Generation'', which leaves key
generation out of scope.

The Orchard pool is spend-only by consensus: no transaction increases its
value, and every Orchard-pool Action creates its note at the address of the
note it consumes (ZIP~258, Draft, ``Consensus rules from NU6.3 activation'';
\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``The Orchard protocol and its two pools''). By
consensus, therefore, only a party able to authorise spends for an address
can create an Orchard-pool note to it, the consumed side of the Action being
possibly a dummy note at that address (ZIP~326, ``Rationale for
key-generation restrictions''). ZIP~326 (Draft), ``Wallet key-generation
restrictions'', forbids conforming wallets to send Orchard-pool funds to a
key with \(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\); under that rule such a key,
in particular every key from distributed key generation, holds no
Orchard-pool notes, and consensus does not enforce the rule. A dealer-shared
key with \(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\) may spend notes of either
pool. This qualifies the statement of \S\ref{sec:intro-scope} that both
pools are served.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the ZIP 2005 constraints]\label{rem:zip2005-scope}
ZIP~2005, ``Usage with FROST'', states its constraints for keys whose
\(\aksym\) is derived jointly by distributed key generation, and this volume
reads its MUST as binding those keys. A dealer that shares an \(\ask\)
derived from a spending key, which ZIP~312, ``Key Generation'', permits,
produces an ordinary key with \(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\), known
to the dealer and outside that constraint. A dealer that shares a directly
generated \(\ask\) may use Construction~\ref{def:orchard-key}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Custody of the common secrets]\label{rem:common-secrets}
Under Construction~\ref{def:orchard-key} the spend authorising key \(\ask\)
is threshold-shared and no signing run assembles it, while
\(\mathsf{sk}\), \(\nksym\), \(\rivk\) and \(\mathsf{qsk}\) are known to
every participant. Each participant MUST treat \(\mathsf{qsk}\) as a secret
held at least as securely and reliably as \(\ask\) (ZIP~2005, ``Usage with
FROST'').
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Viewing material and the signing key]\label{lem:viewing}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For Construction~\ref{def:orchard-key}, the tuple
  \(V := (\mathsf{sk}, \nksym, \mathsf{qsk}, \mathsf{qk}, \rivk, \ivk,
  \ldots)\), with every key derived from \(\aksym\), \(\nksym\) and
  \(\rivk\), is a randomised function of \(\aksym\) alone, \(\mathsf{sk}\)
  being drawn independently of the key material. Hence, for every experiment
  in which an adversary receives \(V\) together with other inputs, an
  adversary that receives only the other inputs, \(\aksym^{\PP}\) among
  them, draws \(\mathsf{sk}\) uniformly, computes \(V\) with the same
  rejection, and runs the first, has identical output distribution.
\item[(b)] For a dealer-shared key with
  \(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\) and
  \(\ask = \pm \mathrm{ToScalar}(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}
  ([\mathtt{0x06}]))\) derived from \(\mathsf{sk}\), the spending key
  determines \(\ask\), so no participant may hold \(\mathsf{sk}\). The
  participants' \(\nksym =
  \mathrm{ToBase}(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}([\mathtt{0x07}]))\) and
  \(\rivk = \mathrm{ToScalar}(\mathsf{PRFexpand}_{\mathsf{sk}}
  ([\mathtt{0x08}]))\), hence the full viewing key, are simulated from
  \(\aksym^{\PP}\) and independent uniform values with the same rejection,
  at a cost, per key attempt, of the advantage against the \emph{Ironwood
  Guide}'s Assumption ``Pseudorandomness of PRF expansion'' plus the sum of
  the three reduction distances, below \(3 \cdot 2^{-257}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Every component of \(V\) is computed from \(\mathsf{sk}\) and
\(\aksym\), and the rejection test reads only \(\mathsf{sk}\) and
\(\aksym\); the construction draws \(\mathsf{sk}\) independently of the key
material, as the simulating adversary does. (b) By the \emph{Ironwood
Guide}'s Lemma ``Independence of domain-separated expansions''
(``Pseudorandom functions and field reductions''), under Assumption
``Pseudorandomness of PRF expansion'' and with the three distinct leading
bytes \(\mathtt{0x06}\), \(\mathtt{0x07}\), \(\mathtt{0x08}\), the triple
\((\ask, \nksym, \rivk)\) is replaced by independent uniform values at the
stated cost, each reduction distance being below \(2^{-257}\). After the
replacement \(\nksym\) and \(\rivk\) are independent of \(\ask\), hence of
\(\aksym^{\PP}\), and the simulator draws them and applies the rejection
test, which reads \(\aksym\), \(\nksym\) and \(\rivk\). No property of
\(\mathsf{H}^{\mathsf{qk}}\) is used.
\end{proof}

Section~\ref{sec:sec} uses the lemma to give the corrupt participants the
common secrets. Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): ZIP~2005's constraints
on FROST key generation are specified (ZIP~2005, Proposed); how the
participants privately agree on \(\mathsf{sk}\) is designed but unspecified:
ZIP~2005, ``Usage with FROST'', requires only that they agree privately, no
specification gives a protocol for it, and the revision of ZIP~312 proposed
in pull request~895 publishes one.

\subsection{Order of randomiser, validating key and signature digest}
\label{sec:auth-order}

\begin{proposition}[Order of a threshold spend authorisation]
\label{prop:order}
Consider a signing run over an Orchard-protocol threshold key, re-randomised
as in Definition~\ref{def:rerand-ts} by an effective randomiser
\(\hat{\alpha}\), whose aggregate consensus accepts as the
spend-authorisation signature of an Action with randomiser \(\alpha\) and
randomised validating key \(\rk\), in a transaction with signature digest
\(m\). Except with the probabilities of Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha} and of
Proposition~\ref{prop:circular}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the randomiser \(\hat{\alpha} = \alpha\) is fixed before \(\rk\),
  which is the function
  \(\aksym^{\PP} + [\hat{\alpha}]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) of it;
\item[(ii)] the key \(\rk\) is fixed before \(m\), which commits to it
  (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Transaction digests and signatures'',
  Definition ``Signature digest'');
\item[(iii)] the digest \(m\) is fixed before any Round Two share, being the
  message of the binding factors and of the challenge;
\item[(iv)] Round One precedes \(\hat{\alpha}\) under
  Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed}, which hashes the commitment list, and
  need not precede it for a randomiser fixed before signing, as in
  Remark~\ref{rem:constructor-alpha}, where (i) to (iii) still hold;
\item[(v)] the Action proof takes \(\alpha\) as auxiliary input and \(\rk\)
  as instance, so it follows (i), and it is unconstrained with respect to
  \(m\) and to Round Two, proofs being authorising data outside the digest.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) is Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}. (ii) and (iii) are the data dependencies
of the cited definition and of Construction~\ref{def:round2}, as run in
step~(3) of Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds}: \(\rho_i\) takes \(H_4(m)\)
and \(c\) takes \(m\). (iv) is the input of \(H_R\) in
Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed}. (v) follows from the \emph{Ironwood
Guide}'s Definition ``Authorising-data digest; effecting and authorising
data'' and ``Construction of a transaction'', where signing can precede
proving (ZIP~244, ``Authorizing Data Commitment'', field A.3a). A
randomiser that is a function of \(m\), as under
Construction~\ref{def:rand-zip} with the digest as message, would by (i)
and (ii) be a fixed point, which Proposition~\ref{prop:circular} bounds; so
that derivation admits no order except with that negligible probability.
\end{proof}

Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): the order of
Proposition~\ref{prop:order} under Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed} is
designed but unspecified; no specification composes ZIP~312 with a
transaction format.

\begin{remark}[Randomisers chosen by a transaction constructor]
\label{rem:constructor-alpha}
ZIP~312 derives the randomiser in Round Two (``Randomizer Generation'' and
``Round Two - Signature Share Generation''), and a derived
randomiser equals an \(\alpha\) fixed earlier, as Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}
requires, only with probability at most
\(1/p_{\Vesta} + \delta_{512} + q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:rand-dist}). A flow in which the transaction
constructor fixes \(\alpha\) and \(\rk\) before signing, and the
Coordinator sends that \(\alpha\) as the effective randomiser, respects the
order of Proposition~\ref{prop:order}, but no specification or published
design states it: its design is an open problem
(Definition~\ref{def:classes}), and under it the Coordinator chooses the
effective randomiser outright (Remark~\ref{rem:effective-rand}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Threshold signing of an Action}\label{sec:auth-action}

The single-signer construction is cited, not rebuilt. Each Action draws
\(\alpha\) by \(\mathsf{GenRandom}\), sets \(\rsk := \ask + \alpha\) and
\(\rk := \aksym^{\PP} + [\alpha]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\), proves the Action
statement with \(\alpha\) as auxiliary input, computes the signature digest
and signs it under \(\rsk\) (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Randomised validating
keys'', Construction ``Randomised validating key''; ``Construction of a
transaction'', Construction ``Ironwood-pool bundle'', steps (5), (7), (10)
and (11); protocol specification, \S``Sending Notes (Orchard)'' and
\href{https://zips.z.cash/protocol/protocol.pdf\#spendauthsig}%
{\S``Spend Authorization Signature (Sapling and Orchard)''}).

\begin{construction}[Threshold spend authorisation of an Action]
\label{def:threshold-action}
The transaction builder acts as Coordinator: it creates the Action proof and
so already controls the privacy of the transaction (ZIP~312, ``Threat
Model''). For each Action that consumes a note of an Orchard-protocol
threshold key (Construction~\ref{def:orchard-key}), in the order of
Proposition~\ref{prop:order}, it proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It runs Round One (Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds}, step (1)) with a
  signing set \(I\) of at least \(t\) participants.
\item It derives the effective randomiser \(\hat{\alpha}\) by
  Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed}, the only designed derivation that
  respects that order: Proposition~\ref{prop:circular} excludes the Draft
  derivation, and a constructor-chosen \(\alpha\) has no published design
  (Remark~\ref{rem:constructor-alpha}).
\item It sets \(\alpha := \hat{\alpha}\) and
  \(\rk := \aksym^{\PP} + [\alpha]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\).
\item Once every Action of the transaction is fixed, it computes the one
  signature digest \(m\) of the transaction.
\item It runs Round Two of Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds} with message
  \(m\), each signer performing the message check of
  Definition~\ref{def:msg-check}, and aggregates.
\item At any point after step~(3), it proves the Action statement with
  \(\alpha\) as auxiliary input (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``The Action
  statement'').
\end{enumerate}
These steps replace the draw of \(\alpha\) and the single-holder signature
in the cited construction; every other Action, dummy spends included, is
built as there. No party holds \(\rsk\). The builder alone makes the binding
signature (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``The binding signature''). Every
threshold-spent Action has its own \(\alpha\) and \(\rk\) and its own
signing run, with fresh nonces and randomiser, all on the one digest \(m\);
since \(m\) commits to the \(\rk\) of every Action, the Round One and the
randomiser of every threshold-spent Action precede \(m\), and hence every
Round Two (Proposition~\ref{prop:order}, (i) to (iv), applied to each
Action). Class (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): designed but unspecified; the
composition of ZIP~312 with the transaction is this volume's own.
\end{construction}

\begin{proof}[Acceptance]
After normalisation \(\aksym^{\PP} = \mathit{PK}\), and
\(\alpha = \hat{\alpha}\); so Proposition~\ref{prop:aggregate-valid} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}, part (i), give a signature valid under \(\rk\)
on \(m\), except in the zero-nonce case and when \(\rk = \mathcal{O}\),
whose probabilities Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-correct} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha}, part (iii), bound.
\end{proof}

The circuit and the consensus rules are unchanged
(Remark~\ref{rem:consensus-unchanged}). Both pools of the Orchard protocol
use the same RedPallas spend authorisation, so one flow serves an Action of

\subsection{One-more discrete logarithms}\label{sec:sec-omdl}

\begin{definition}[One-more discrete logarithm problem]\label{def:omdl}
The \emph{one-more discrete logarithm game} for \(\G\) is a search game in
the sense of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Security as a game'', Definition
``Security game and advantage''. The challenger keeps a challenge counter
\(k\) and a query counter \(\ell\), both initially \(0\), and offers two
oracles.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[] \(\mathsf{Chal}()\): set \(k := k + 1\), draw
  \(x_k \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq\), and return \(X_k := [x_k]B\).
\item[] \(\mathsf{DLog}(X)\), for any \(X \in \G\): if \(X\) was queried
  before, return the stored answer; otherwise set \(\ell := \ell + 1\),
  store and return the discrete logarithm of \(X\) to base \(B\).
\end{enumerate}
The adversary outputs \((y_1, \dots, y_k) \in \Fq^k\) and \emph{wins} if
\(y_j = x_j\) for every \(j \le k\) and \(\ell < k\). Its advantage
\(\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{omdl}}_{\G}(\Adv)\) is its probability of winning.
The case of \(\ell + 1\) challenges and at most \(\ell\) queries is the
\emph{\(\ell\)-OMDL problem}; the case of one challenge and no query is the
discrete logarithm problem of the \emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``The discrete
logarithm problem''.
\end{definition}

The \emph{Crypto Guide} names the problem in prose only. The game is that of
ePrint 2022/833, Figure~9, in additive notation; its challenge queries are
those that Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf} counts.

\begin{assumption}[One-more discrete logarithms on Pallas]
\label{ass:omdl-pallas}
Let \(\G\) be the Pallas group, of order \(q = p_{\Vesta}\), with generator
\(B = G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, \S``Fields, groups, and
encodings''; ``GroupHash, domain separation, and nothing-up-my-sleeve
generators''). Every efficient adversary has negligible advantage
\(\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{omdl}}_{\G}\) (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Polynomial,
exponential, and negligible functions'') in the game of
Definition~\ref{def:omdl}.
\end{assumption}

By its case of one challenge, the assumption implies the \emph{Ironwood
Guide}'s Assumption ``Discrete logarithms on Pallas''.

\begin{definition}[Algebraic one-more discrete logarithm problem]
\label{def:aomdl}
Let \(\G\), \(q\) and \(B\) be as in Definition~\ref{def:omdl} and let
\(\ell \ge 0\) be an integer. In the \emph{\(\ell\)-AOMDL game} the
challenger draws \(x_0, \dots, x_\ell \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq\) and gives the
adversary \(X_i := [x_i]B\) for \(0 \le i \le \ell\). Its oracle takes a
\emph{declaration} \((\alpha, \beta_0, \dots, \beta_\ell) \in
\Fq^{\ell + 2}\) and returns
\[
  \alpha + \sum_{i=0}^{\ell} \beta_i x_i ,
\]
the discrete logarithm of \([\alpha]B + \sum_{i} [\beta_i] X_i\); it answers
at most \(\ell\) declarations. The adversary wins by outputting
\((x_0, \dots, x_\ell)\), and \(\mathrm{Adv}^{\ell\text{-aomdl}}_{\G}(\Adv)\)
is its probability of winning.
\end{definition}

The game is the intended one of ePrint 2024/436, Assumption~1 and
Figure~1. The restriction lies on the oracle's queries, which are declared
combinations of \(B\) and the challenges, and not on the adversary, so no
statement about algebraic adversaries is needed; the oracle of
Definition~\ref{def:omdl} answers any element. The printed Figure~1 is
ill-formed in three clauses: its oracle takes \(\beta_1, \dots, \beta_\ell\)
but returns an expression in \(\beta_0\); it never checks the queried element
against the declared combination; and its query counter is never
incremented, so its final test, that the count is below \(\ell + 1\), bounds
nothing. This volume reads the game as stated above.

\begin{lemma}[OMDL hardness implies AOMDL hardness]\label{lem:omdl-aomdl}
For every \(\G\) of prime order \(q\), every \(\ell \ge 0\) and every
adversary \(\Adv\) in the \(\ell\)-AOMDL game of Definition~\ref{def:aomdl},
there is an adversary \(\Adv'\) in the game of Definition~\ref{def:omdl},
making \(\ell + 1\) challenge queries and at most \(\ell\) oracle queries,
with
\[
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{omdl}}_{\G}(\Adv')
  = \mathrm{Adv}^{\ell\text{-aomdl}}_{\G}(\Adv) ,
\]
running in the time of \(\Adv\) plus \(\ell + 2\) scalar multiplications per
declaration.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Algorithm \(\Adv'\) queries \(\mathsf{Chal}\) \(\ell + 1\) times, obtaining
\(X_1, \dots, X_{\ell+1}\), and runs \(\Adv\) on them as
\(X_0, \dots, X_\ell\). On a declaration
\((\alpha, \beta_0, \dots, \beta_\ell)\) it queries \(\mathsf{DLog}\) on
\([\alpha]B + \sum_i [\beta_i]X_i\) and returns the answer, which is
\(\alpha + \sum_i \beta_i x_i\), the value the AOMDL oracle returns. It
outputs the output of \(\Adv\). The view of \(\Adv\) is that of the AOMDL
game, the query counts agree, and \(\Adv'\) wins exactly when \(\Adv\) wins.
\end{proof}

The assumption of ePrint 2024/436, Theorem~1, is therefore no stronger than
the OMDL assumption of the analysis RFC~9591 cites (ePrint 2022/833). Hence
the analysis of ePrint 2024/436 needs no assumption beyond that of ePrint
2022/833.

\subsection{Security of FROST signing}\label{sec:sec-frost}

\begin{definition}[Threshold signatures with leader requests]
\label{def:ni-syntax}
A \emph{threshold signature scheme with leader requests} \(\mathsf{TS}\) has
\(n\) \emph{servers}, the participants of Definition~\ref{def:ts-params}
with identifiers \(1, \dots, n\), a threshold \(t\), and the following
algorithms, each with access to a random oracle \(h\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Key generation} \(\mathsf{Kg}[h]\), run by a trusted party,
  outputs a verification key \(\mathit{vk}\), public auxiliary information
  \(\mathit{vks}\) (the paper's \(\mathit{aux}\)), which holds the
  verification shares, and secret keys
  \(\mathit{sk}_1, \dots, \mathit{sk}_n\).
\item \emph{Token generation}: server \(i\) issues a first-round
  \emph{token} \(\mathit{pp}\) and keeps a secret state for it.
\item A \emph{leader request} \(\mathit{lr}\), formed by the \emph{leader},
  a party holding no secret of the scheme, carries a message
  \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{msg}\), a signer set
  \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS} \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}\), and a map
  \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{PP}\) from \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS}\) to tokens. The
  scheme is an \emph{echo
  scheme}: requests carry the tokens.
\item \emph{Partial signing} \(\mathsf{PS}[h](\mathit{lr}, i)\): server
  \(i\) answers \(\mathit{lr}\) with a partial signature computed from the
  state of the token \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{PP}(i)\), or with the failure
  symbol \(\bot\) if \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{PP}(i)\) is not an unused token of
  its own; each token is thus answered at most once.
\item \emph{Aggregation} \(\mathsf{Agg}[h]\) maps partial signatures to a
  signature \(\mathit{sig}\).
\item \emph{Verification} \(\mathsf{Vf}[h](\mathit{vk}, M, \mathit{sig})\).
\item \emph{Strong verification}
  \(\mathsf{SVf}[h](\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr}, \mathit{sig})\), which
  accepts, for each pair \((\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr})\), at most one
  signature.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The syntax is that of ePrint 2022/833, Section~3.1, restricted to what this
volume uses. The leader is the Coordinator of
Definition~\ref{def:ts-scheme}.

\begin{definition}[Threshold unforgeability levels]\label{def:ts-uf}
Let \(\mathsf{TS}\) be an echo scheme (Definition~\ref{def:ni-syntax}). The
game has the following procedures.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[] \(\mathsf{Init}(C)\): require \(C \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}\) and
  \(\lvert C \rvert < t\); draw \(h\); run
  \((\mathit{vk}, \mathit{vks}, \mathit{sk}_1, \dots, \mathit{sk}_n)
  \gets \mathsf{Kg}[h]\); set \(\mathit{HS} := \{1, \dots, n\} \setminus C\);
  return \(\mathit{vk}\), \(\mathit{vks}\) and \(\mathit{sk}_j\) for
  \(j \in C\).
\item[] Token oracle on \(i \in \mathit{HS}\): server \(i\) issues a token,
  which is added to the set \(\mathit{PP}_i\) and returned.
\item[] Partial-signing oracle on \((i, \mathit{lr})\), \(i \in \mathit{HS}\):
  record \(\mathit{lr}\); run \(\mathsf{PS}[h](\mathit{lr}, i)\); if the
  answer is not \(\bot\), add \(i\) to \(S_2(\mathit{lr})\); return the
  answer.
\item[] Random oracle: return \(h(x)\).
\item[] \(\mathsf{Fin}(M, \mathit{sig})\): for every recorded
  \(\mathit{lr}\) set
  \[
    S_3(\mathit{lr}) := \{\, i \in \mathit{HS} \cap \mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS}
      : \mathit{lr}.\mathsf{PP}(i) \in \mathit{PP}_i \,\} , \qquad
    S_4(\mathit{lr}) := \mathit{HS} \cap \mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS} .
  \]
  The adversary loses unless \(\mathsf{Vf}[h](\mathit{vk}, M,
  \mathit{sig})\) holds.
\end{enumerate}
The adversary acts as the leader. The \emph{trivial-forgery predicates} are
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{tf}_2(\mathit{lr}) &:\quad
    \lvert S_2(\mathit{lr}) \rvert \ge t - \lvert C \rvert , \\
  \mathrm{tf}_3(\mathit{lr}) &:\quad
    \mathrm{tf}_2(\mathit{lr}) \text{ and }
    S_2(\mathit{lr}) = S_3(\mathit{lr}) , \\
  \mathrm{tf}_4(\mathit{lr}) &:\quad
    \mathrm{tf}_2(\mathit{lr}) \text{ and }
    S_2(\mathit{lr}) = S_4(\mathit{lr}) ,
\end{align*}
and, for \(i \in \{2, 3, 4\}\), the \emph{trivial strong-forgery predicate}
\(\mathrm{tsf}_i(\mathit{lr}, \mathit{vk}, \mathit{sig})\) is
\(\mathrm{tf}_i(\mathit{lr})\) and
\(\mathsf{SVf}[h](\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr}, \mathit{sig})\). The game
TS-UF-\(i\) is won by a valid \((M, \mathit{sig})\) such that no recorded
\(\mathit{lr}\) with \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{msg} = M\) satisfies
\(\mathrm{tf}_i(\mathit{lr})\); TS-SUF-\(i\) replaces
\(\mathrm{tf}_i(\mathit{lr})\) by
\(\mathrm{tsf}_i(\mathit{lr}, \mathit{vk}, \mathit{sig})\). The advantage
\(\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{ts\text{-}suf\text{-}}i}_{\mathsf{TS}}(\Adv)\) is
the probability of winning.
\end{definition}

The games are those of ePrint 2022/833, Figures~2 and~3, the predicates
transcribed exactly. Under TS-SUF-3 a forgery on \(M\) is trivial only if one
request \(\mathit{lr}\) for \(M\) was answered by at least
\(t - \lvert C \rvert\) honest servers, those servers are exactly the honest
servers of \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS}\) listed with a token they issued, and
\(\mathit{sig}\) is the unique signature that strong verification associates
with \((\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr})\). Only these levels are defined: the
volume uses TS-SUF-3 and the failure of TS-UF-4.

\begin{construction}[FROST1 in the non-interactive syntax]
\label{def:frost1}
Let \(t \le n < q\), and let \(h_j(x) := h(j, x)\) for one random oracle
\(h\). The scheme \(\mathsf{FROST1}[\G]\) is the following echo scheme.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\mathsf{Kg}\): draw \(a_0, \dots, a_{t-1} \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq\);
  set \(\mathit{sk}_i := \sum_{j} i^j a_j\) and
  \(\mathit{vk}_i := [\mathit{sk}_i]B\) for each \(i\),
  \(\mathit{vk} := [a_0]B\) and
  \(\mathit{vks} := (\mathit{vk}_1, \dots, \mathit{vk}_n)\).
\item \emph{Token} of server \(i\): draw \(u, v \xleftarrow{\$} \Fq\);
  the token is \(\mathit{pp} := ([u]B, [v]B)\), and \((u, v)\) is kept for
  it.
\item For a request \(\mathit{lr}\) and each \(i \in \mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS}\)
  let \((U_i, V_i) := \mathit{lr}.\mathsf{PP}(i)\) and
  \(b_i := h_1(\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr}, i)\); let
  \[
    R := \sum_{i \in \mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS}} \bigl(U_i + [b_i]V_i\bigr) ,
    \qquad
    c := h_2(\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr}.\mathsf{msg}, R) .
  \]
\item \(\mathsf{PS}(\mathit{lr}, i)\): if \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{PP}(i)\) is
  an unused token of \(i\) with secret \((u, v)\), erase \((u, v)\) and
  return \((R, z_i)\) with
  \(z_i := u + b_i v + c\, \lambda_{\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS}, i}\,
  \mathit{sk}_i\); otherwise return \(\bot\).
\item \(\mathsf{Agg}\): if all partial signatures carry one \(R\), return
  \((R, \sum_i z_i)\).
\item \(\mathsf{Vf}(\mathit{vk}, M, (R, z))\): accept if and only if
  \([z]B = R + [h_2(\mathit{vk}, M, R)]\,\mathit{vk}\).
\item \(\mathsf{SVf}(\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr}, (R^{*}, z^{*}))\): accept if
  and only if \(R^{*}\) equals the \(R\) computed from \(\mathit{lr}\) and
  \([z^{*}]B = R^{*} + [c]\,\mathit{vk}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{construction}

The construction is ePrint 2022/833, Figure~8, in additive notation; the
paper's token secrets \(r, s\) and binding factors \(d_i\) are written
\(u, v\) and \(b_i\) here, those letters being taken. FROST2 (Crites, Komlo
and Maller, ePrint 2021/1375) replaces the factors \(b_i\) by one
\(b := h_1(\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr})\); ePrint 2022/833, Section~5, proves
FROST2 TS-SUF-2-secure and not TS-UF-3-secure. RFC~9591 specifies FROST1:
its binding factor takes the identifier (Section~4.4), and its Section~7.2
(``Optimizations'') rules the shared factor NOT RECOMMENDED, because it
removes the guarantee that the set of participants that started Round One is
the set that produced the signature.

\begin{proposition}[RFC 9591 signing realises FROST1]\label{prop:frost1}
Let a ciphersuite (Definition~\ref{def:suite}) whose element serialisation
is total, as that of FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) is, meet
Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} with distance \(\delta\) and digest length
\(N_{\mathrm{h}}\) bytes. Map RFC~9591 signing
(Constructions~\ref{def:round1}, \ref{def:round2}
and~\ref{def:aggregate}, Definition~\ref{def:binding}) over key material of
Construction~\ref{def:dealer} at identifiers \(1, \dots, n\) to the syntax
of Definition~\ref{def:ni-syntax} as follows: the Coordinator is the leader;
the Round One pair \((D_i, E_i)\) is the token; the request \((m, L)\) is
\(\mathit{lr}\) with \(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{msg} = m\),
\(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{SS}\) the identifiers of \(L\) and
\(\mathit{lr}.\mathsf{PP}(i)\) the pair of \(i\) in \(L\);
\(\mathit{vk} := \mathit{PK}\) and
\(\mathit{vks} := (\mathit{PK}_1, \dots, \mathit{PK}_n)\), from which the
coefficient commitments are computed, since \(t\) verification shares
determine the committed polynomial in the exponent
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:group-info} and~\ref{lem:recombine}). For every adversary
\(\Adv\) in the TS-SUF-3 game of Definition~\ref{def:ts-uf} for RFC~9591
signing under this map whose requests list only identifiers in
\(\{1, \dots, n\}\), making \(q_s\) token queries and \(q_h\) hash
queries, there is an adversary \(\Adv'\) against \(\mathsf{FROST1}[\G]\)
(Construction~\ref{def:frost1}) with the same query counts and about the
same running time, such that, with \(Q := q_s + q_h + 1\),
\begin{multline*}
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{ts\text{-}suf\text{-}3}}_{\mathrm{RFC}}(\Adv)
  \;\le\;
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{ts\text{-}suf\text{-}3}}_{\mathsf{FROST1}[\G]}(\Adv')
  \\
  + 2 q_s \bigl(\delta + (q_h + 2 q_s) \cdot 2^{-256}\bigr)
  + 3 Q^{2} \cdot 2^{-8 N_{\mathrm{h}}}
  + \bigl(q_h + (n + 1) q_s + 1\bigr) \delta .
\end{multline*}
A request naming another identifier lies outside the FROST1 game and is not
covered, and so is a suite whose serialisation is not total: its abort on
\(R = \mathcal{O}\) follows hash evaluations that consume no token.
The correspondence rests on four facts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The binding-factor input
  \[
    \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK}) \,\|\, H_4(m) \,\|\,
    H_5(\mathsf{enc}(L)) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(i)
  \]
  determines
  \((\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr}, i)\), except on a collision of \(H_4\) or
  \(H_5\) or on a digest used before its preimage is queried.
\item[(b)] The challenge input
  \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK}) \,\|\, m\) is
  an injective reordering of \((\mathit{vk}, M, R)\), the two element
  encodings having fixed length.
\item[(c)] The separated hashes \(H_1\) and \(H_2\) realise \(h_1\) and
  \(h_2\) as independent oracles.
\item[(d)] Hedged nonces replace the uniform token secrets of FROST1 at the
  cost of Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce-uniform} per nonce, a hop the reduction needs
  because it does not know the honest shares that enter \(H_3\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Game hops, with additive losses (\emph{Crypto Guide}, \S``Security as a
game'', Remark ``Game-hopping'').

\emph{Game \(G_0\)} is the RFC game.

\emph{Game \(G_1\)}: every honest nonce is a uniform scalar. The \(2 q_s\)
nonces are replaced one at a time, in the order in which they are drawn. At
each hop Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce-uniform} applies with the rest of the game as
the observer: its queries to \(H_3\) are the \(q_h\) queries of \(\Adv\) and
at most \(2 q_s\) evaluations by honest servers, each at an input with a
fresh \(32\)-byte prefix. Hence
\(\lvert \Pr[G_0] - \Pr[G_1] \rvert \le
2 q_s (\delta + (q_h + 2 q_s) \cdot 2^{-256})\).
 \emph{Game \(G_2\)} aborts on a collision of \(H_4\) or of \(H_5\), or when a
hash input contains an output value of \(H_4\) or \(H_5\) before the query that
produces it. Each of the two oracles receives at most \(Q\) queries, from
\(\Adv\) and one per answered request, at most \(q_s\) of them. By
Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} a collision of either has probability at most
\(Q^2 \cdot 2^{-8 N_{\mathrm{h}}}\) in total; each new answer equals one of at
most \(Q\) digest strings already present in inputs with probability at most \(Q
\cdot 2^{-8 N_{\mathrm{h}}}\), which over \(Q\)
queries and both oracles gives \(2 Q^2 \cdot 2^{-8 N_{\mathrm{h}}}\).

\emph{Game \(G_3\)} answers \(H_1\) and \(H_2\) at distinct inputs by
uniform scalars. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} each answer is an
independent sample within distance \(\delta\) of uniform, and the distinct
inputs number at most \(q_h\) queries of \(\Adv\), at most \(n\) binding
factors and one challenge per answered request, and one challenge of the
final verification; replacing the answers one at a time costs
\(\bigl(q_h + (n + 1) q_s + 1\bigr) \delta\) (\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem
``Properties of statistical distance'').

In \(G_3\), algorithm \(\Adv'\) plays the FROST1 game. It relays token
queries. It simulates \(H_3\), \(H_4\) and \(H_5\) lazily and records their
queries. It answers an \(H_1\) query
\(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK}') \,\|\, u \,\|\, v \,\|\,
\mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(i)\) by \(h_1(\mathit{vk}, \mathit{lr}, i)\) when
\(\mathit{PK}' = \mathit{PK}\) and \(u\), \(v\) are recorded outputs of
\(H_4\) on \(m\) and of \(H_5\) on \(\mathsf{enc}(L)\), with \(\mathit{lr}\)
formed from \((m, L)\), and by a fresh value otherwise; by (a) and the abort
rule of \(G_2\) the request is unique. It answers an \(H_2\) query
\(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\mathit{PK}') \,\|\, m\) by
\(h_2(\mathit{vk}, m, R)\) when \(\mathit{PK}' = \mathit{PK}\), and by a
fresh value otherwise; by (b) the input determines \((m, R)\), and by (c)
the two simulations are independent. The checks of
Construction~\ref{def:round2}, namely the message check, the presence of the
member's own unused pair in \(L\), deserialisation of every commitment and
the order of the identifiers, are
decided from public data. Algorithm \(\Adv'\) therefore queries the
partial-signing oracle exactly when the RFC participant would answer, does
not query the oracle when the participant would abort, leaving the token
unused in both games, since an aborting participant keeps its pair; it never
submits a token already answered, and returns \(z_i\), discarding \(R\). The
sets
\(S_2\), \(S_3\) and \(S_4\) of the two games coincide; a valid RFC
signature \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\Fq}(z)\) is a valid
FROST1 signature \((R, z)\); and strong verification agrees.
So \(\Adv'\) wins when \(\Adv\) wins in \(G_3\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unforgeability of FROST]\label{thm:frost-suf}
Let \(\G\) have prime order \(q\), and let \(\mathsf{FROST1}[\G]\) be
Construction~\ref{def:frost1} with \(n\) servers and threshold \(t\). For
every adversary \(\Adv\) in the TS-SUF-3 game of Definition~\ref{def:ts-uf}
making at most \(q_s\) token queries, the first-round queries, and at most
\(q_h\) random-oracle queries, there is an adversary \(\mathcal{B}\) in the
game of Definition~\ref{def:omdl}, making at most \(2 q_s + t\) challenge
queries, such that, with \(Q := q_s + q_h + 1\),
\[
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{ts\text{-}suf\text{-}3}}_{\mathsf{FROST1}[\G]}(\Adv)
  \;\le\; 4 n Q \sqrt{\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{omdl}}_{\G}(\mathcal{B})
    + 6 Q / q} ,
\]
only the two terms \(\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{omdl}}_{\G}(\mathcal{B})\) and
\(6Q/q\) lying under the root. Algorithm \(\mathcal{B}\) runs in about twice
the time of \(\Adv\), plus at most \(6 n Q + 4 q_s + 2 n^2\)
exponentiations and group operations. For some parameters, for instance
\(n = 20\) and \(t = 3\), the scheme \(\mathsf{FROST1}[\G]\) is not
TS-UF-4-secure. The hypotheses are those of the game: static corruption
of fewer than \(t\) servers, trusted key generation, and the random-oracle
model.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Cited: ePrint 2022/833, Theorem~5.4, whose bound, query counts and running
time are those stated, the root spanning only
\(\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{omdl}}_{\G}(\mathcal{B}) + 6Q/q\); the TS-UF-4
separation is its Section~5.4, by an explicit attack at \(n = 20\) and
\(t = 3\).
\end{proof}

RFC~9591, Section~7 (``Security Considerations''), claims security against
existential unforgeability under chosen-message attacks as defined in that
analysis, assuming that the shares are generated and distributed securely, by a
trusted dealer or by a distributed key generation protocol, and that at most
the Coordinator and \(\mathrm{MIN\_PARTICIPANTS} - 1\) participants are
corrupted. For dealer keys in a suite with total element serialisation and
requests listing only identifiers in \(\{1, \dots, n\}\), the claim rests on
Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf} at level TS-SUF-3, through
Proposition~\ref{prop:frost1}; with distributed key generation it is open
(Remark~\ref{rem:dkg-open}). It does not rest on the theorem of the original
paper, which concerns an interactive variant.

\begin{remark}[Role of the binding factors]\label{rem:binding}
The share \(z_i\) is bound through \(\rho_i\) to \(\mathit{PK}\), the
message, the commitment list \(L\) and the identifier
(Definition~\ref{def:binding}). Changing the message, the signing set or any
commitment in \(L\) changes some \(\rho_j\), hence \(R\) and \(c\), except on
a collision of \(H_1\), \(H_4\) or \(H_5\); a share moved into another run
fails its share equation, and the aggregate fails verification. Without such
binding, concurrent sessions of two-round Schnorr signing admit forgeries:
in time subexponential in the bit length of \(q\), by an attack that combines
the challenges of concurrent sessions (Drijvers, Edalatnejad, Ford, Kiltz,
Loss, Neven and Stepanovs, ``On the Security of Two-Round
Multi-Signatures'', IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy 2019), which
applies to \(t\)-of-\(n\) threshold signing with up to \(t - 1\) corrupt
participants (ePrint 2020/852, Section~2.5); and in polynomial time once
more than \(\log_2 q\) sessions are open concurrently (Benhamouda, Lepoint,
Loss, Orr\`u and Raykova, ``On the (in)security of ROS'', EUROCRYPT 2021).
The remark explains Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf} and adds no claim; the
security of FROST rests on that theorem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Unforgeability and key generation}\label{sec:sec-keygen}

% compute/frost_bounds.py:
% [sec:sec-keygen] factor (q - 1)/(2q) for q = p_Vesta: 1/2 - 1/(2q),
%   1/(2q) = 2^-255.0000; loss factor 2 in advantage up to that term
\begin{lemma}[Sign normalisation and unforgeability]\label{lem:norm-transfer}
Consider a game over dealer key material (Construction~\ref{def:dealer})
that decides its winning condition from public data, the adversary's output
and the transcript of its oracles, as the games of
Definition~\ref{def:ts-uf} do, over a group in which exactly half of the
\(q - 1\) non-identity points pass the parity test of
Construction~\ref{def:normalise}; for Pallas this test is the last bit of
\(\mathsf{repr}_{\PP}\). An adversary with advantage \(\epsilon\) in the game
whose key material is normalised by Construction~\ref{def:normalise} is,
run unchanged, an adversary with advantage at least
\[
  \frac{q - 1}{2q}\, \epsilon
\]
in the game with unnormalised dealer key material.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reduction plays the unnormalised game, runs the adversary unchanged on
it, and stops unless \(\mathit{PK} \ne \mathcal{O}\) and \(\mathit{PK}\)
passes the parity test; since \(s\) is uniform, this event has probability
\((q - 1)/(2q)\), and it is decided from public data before the adversary
receives anything else. On the event normalisation changes nothing. The
normalised key material is distributed as unnormalised dealer key material
conditioned on the event: step~(1) of Construction~\ref{def:normalise}
redraws a zero secret, and negation (Lemma~\ref{lem:negate}) maps the
sharings whose group key fails the test bijectively onto those whose group
key passes it, the coefficients staying uniform. The conditioned view is
therefore that of the normalised game, and the win carries over.
\end{proof}

For \(q = p_{\Vesta}\) the factor is \(1/2 - 1/(2q)\) with
\(1/(2q) = 2^{-255}\) to the precision stated: normalisation costs a factor
\(2\) in advantage, up to that term.

\begin{corollary}[Unforgeability for Orchard-protocol dealer keys]
\label{cor:orchard-dealer}
Assume Assumption~\ref{ass:omdl-pallas}, and
Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} as Proposition~\ref{prop:pallas-hash}
instantiates it for FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512)
(Construction~\ref{def:pallas-suite}). Then Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf},
through Proposition~\ref{prop:frost1}, holds for RFC~9591 signing against
adversaries whose requests list only identifiers in \(\{1, \dots, n\}\), with
the effective randomiser zero on dealer key material
(Construction~\ref{def:dealer}) normalised by Construction~\ref{def:normalise}
and completed in either of two ways, the
corrupt participants holding the common secrets:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] by Construction~\ref{def:orchard-key}
  (\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\)), with \(\mathsf{sk}\) drawn
  independently of the key material and given to every participant; the TS-SUF-3
  advantage of such an adversary is at most \(2q/(q - 1)\) times the sum of the
  bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf} and the loss of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:frost1}, with \(q = p_{\Vesta}\), the simulation of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:viewing}, part (a), being exact;
\item[(b)] for a dealer that shares an \(\ask\) derived from a spending key
  that no participant holds (\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\);
  \emph{Ironwood Guide}, Construction ``Spend-side secrets''), with the
  further terms of Lemma~\ref{lem:viewing}, part (b), of the \emph{Ironwood
  Guide}'s Assumption ``Pseudorandomness of PRF expansion'' and Lemma
  ``Rejection in key generation'', and of the bias below \(2^{-257}\) of
  \(\mathrm{ToScalar}\) (Definition ``Field reductions'' there), by a
  hybrid that replaces \(\ask\) with a uniform scalar.
\end{enumerate}
The corollary covers signing with the randomiser zero only; the
re-randomised protocol is the subject of \S\ref{sec:sec-rerand}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Hybrids, then the cited statements. For (a), Lemma~\ref{lem:viewing},
part (a), replaces the adversary that receives \(\mathsf{sk}\) and the
values derived from it by one that draws \(\mathsf{sk}\) itself and computes
them from \(\aksym = \Extract_{\PP}(\mathit{PK})\), rejection included,
with no loss. For (b), Lemma~\ref{lem:viewing}, part (b), simulates
\(\nksym\) and \(\rivk\) from independent uniform values; the
key-derivation hybrid then replaces \(\ask\), before normalisation, by a
uniform scalar, at the cost of Assumption ``Pseudorandomness of PRF
expansion'', of Lemma ``Rejection in key generation'' and of the
\(\mathrm{ToScalar}\) bias. These hybrids apply because the win is
efficiently decidable from the key: the deciding algorithm answers the
signing queries with the shares. What remains is the game of
Definition~\ref{def:ts-uf} over normalised dealer keys.
Lemma~\ref{lem:norm-transfer} passes to unnormalised dealer keys, which are the
output of \(\mathsf{Kg}\) of FROST1; Proposition~\ref{prop:frost1} passes such
an adversary to \(\mathsf{FROST1}[\Pallas]\); and Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf}
with Assumption~\ref{ass:omdl-pallas} bounds that advantage.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Distributed key generation and unforgeability]
\label{rem:dkg-open}
Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf} assumes trusted key generation, and no checked
source proves FROST1 unforgeable with a distributed key generation, in
particular with Construction~\ref{def:dkg}. RFC~9591, Section~7, lists
secure generation by a dealer or by a distributed protocol as an
assumption, not as a result. The nearest results are two. Crites, Komlo and
Maller (ePrint 2021/1375, Theorem~7) prove FROST2 with their key generation
unforgeable under the one-more discrete logarithm assumption and a further
assumption: that an algorithm outputting a group element with a valid
Schnorr proof of knowledge for it knows the element's discrete logarithm, in
that an extractor computes the logarithm from the algorithm's code and
random coins. The original paper (ePrint 2020/852) proves, under discrete
logarithms, an interactive variant with its own key generation, not the
specified scheme. Classification (Definition~\ref{def:classes}):
unforgeability of FROST1 with distributed key generation is an open
problem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Unforgeability of re-randomised FROST}\label{sec:sec-rerand}

\begin{definition}[Re-randomisable threshold unforgeability]\label{def:truf}
The game TRUF, for the instance of Definition~\ref{def:rerand-ts} given by
FROST with effective-randomiser map \(H_r\), runs as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The adversary chooses \((n, t)\) and a static set \(\mathit{cor}\)
  with \(\lvert \mathit{cor} \rvert \le t - 1\).
\item The challenger runs centralised key generation, a Shamir sharing of a
  uniform \(x\) with \(\mathit{PK} = [x]B\), and gives \(\mathit{PK}\),
  every \(\mathit{PK}_i\) and \(x_j\) for \(j \in \mathit{cor}\).
\item Oracle \(\mathsf{Sign}(k, \mathit{ssid})\), for honest \(k\), opens
  session \(\mathit{ssid}\) and returns Round One commitments
  \(\rho_k = (R_k, S_k)\).
\item Oracle \(\mathsf{Sign}'(k, \mathit{ssid}, m, S, \alpha,
  \mathit{aux}, (\rho_i)_{i \in S})\), once per open session: add \(m\) to
  the set \(\mathcal{Q}_m\); set \(\hat{\alpha} := H_r(\alpha,
  \mathit{aux})\), \(\bar{x}_k := x_k + \hat{\alpha}\) and
  \(\overline{\mathit{PK}} := \mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\); return the
  Round Two share \(z_k\) computed with \(\bar{x}_k\) and
  \(\overline{\mathit{PK}}\).
\item The adversary outputs \((m^{*}, \sigma^{*}, \alpha^{*},
  \mathit{aux}^{*})\) and wins if \(m^{*} \notin \mathcal{Q}_m\) and
  \(\sigma^{*}\) is valid on \(m^{*}\) under \(\mathit{PK}\) or under
  \(\mathit{PK} + [H_r(\alpha^{*}, \mathit{aux}^{*})]B\).
\end{enumerate}
The randomisers are adversarial, and the oracle applies \(H_r\) itself.
The advantage \(\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{truf}}(\Adv)\) is the probability of
winning.
\end{definition}

The game is that of ePrint 2024/436, Definition~6 and Figure~4, in additive
notation and with the evident reading of its interface; the symbol
\(\rho_k\) is the paper's name for a commitment pair, not a binding factor.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the game]\label{rem:truf-scope}
Four clauses bound what TRUF covers.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] TRUF is existential: its oracle records messages only, and a new
  signature on a signed message does not win. It is therefore weaker than
  Definition~\ref{def:surk}, which records message--signature pairs, and
  Theorem~1 of ePrint 2024/436 would not, as printed, give the strong notion
  that ZIP~312, ``Requirements'', asks for (Remark~\ref{rem:zip312-req},
  (b)).
\item[(b)] The choice \(\alpha = 0\) does not give the identity shift, since
  \(H_r(0, \mathit{aux})\) need not be \(0\), contrary to the preprint's
  statement that an adversary obtains unrandomised signatures by submitting
  the randomiser zero. The game has no unrandomised signing oracle and does
  not contain ordinary unforgeability.
\item[(c)] Freshness is on the message alone. All Actions of a transaction
  sign one digest, so a forgery under a second Action's randomised key on a
  digest already signed for another Action is not a win.
\item[(d)] A Coordinator that chooses the effective randomiser outright
  (Remark~\ref{rem:effective-rand}) is outside the game, whose oracle
  applies \(H_r\).
\end{enumerate}
Each clause is a reason why the target notion of \S\ref{sec:sec-authority},
Definition~\ref{def:ts-ruf}, is stated separately.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The claimed unforgeability of re-randomised FROST]
\label{rem:rr-claim}
Theorem~1 of ePrint 2024/436 claims that Rerandomized-FROST is unforgeable
in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:truf}, in the random-oracle model under
the assumption of Definition~\ref{def:aomdl}, with its Equation~(3) as
printed:
\[
  \mathrm{Adv} \;\le\; \sqrt{Q \cdot \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathrm{aomdl}}_{D}
    (\lambda) + 2 Q^{2} / q - \negl(\lambda)} ,
\]
all three terms under one root, with \(Q := q_h + q_s\) counting
random-oracle and signing queries and \(q\) the group order (\(p\) in the
paper). No number is used from it. Its assumptions are static corruption
and centralised key generation (Section~2.3 there); the paper states that
its definition can be adapted to distributed key generation, and gives no
proof. The abstract's ``same security assumptions underlying plain FROST''
is correct only in the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:omdl-aomdl}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Gaps in the printed reduction]\label{rem:rr-gaps}
The reduction of ePrint 2024/436, Section~6, has five gaps, one per clause.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The simulated verification shares do not interpolate to
  \(\mathit{PK}\) as printed. For \(\lvert C \rvert = t - 1\) and
  \(L_j(i)\) the Lagrange coefficient of the node \(j\) of
  \(\{0\} \cup C\) at \(i\), the required relation is
  \[
    \mathit{PK}_i \;=\; [L_0(i)]\,\mathit{PK}
      + \sum_{j \in C} [L_j(i)][x_j]B .
  \]
\item[(b)] The Round Two logarithm query omits the Lagrange coefficient:
  the share \(z_k = r_k + s_k a_k + c\, \lambda_k\, \bar{x}_k\) needs the
  logarithm of \(R_k + [a_k]S_k + [c\, \lambda_k]\,
  \overline{\mathit{PK}}_k\).
\item[(c)] The random-oracle simulation is not a consistent lazy sampler:
  the table of \(H_r\) stores no outputs, index and message are mixed in the
  binding-factor table, one branch returns \(c\) in place of \(a_k\), and an
  unrandomised case \(\alpha = \bot\) is used that neither \(\Fq\) nor the
  game defines.
\item[(d)] The forking step reprograms the signature hash at a key
  \(\mathit{PK}'\) left undefined, does not show that the reprogrammed query
  is the challenge query of both forgeries when their randomised keys
  differ, and derives no bound, so Equation~(3) is not derived.
\item[(e)] Nonce extraction is unsound: the printed numerator has sign
  errors and uses the randomisers in place of their \(H_r\) images; and when
  \(c_0 = c_1\) and \(a_0 = a_1\) but \(h_0 \ne h_1\), the two responses
  give one linear equation in two unknowns.
\end{enumerate}
With the query counter of Definition~\ref{def:aomdl} restored, these remain
material gaps: a repair may exist, but no complete reduction is given, and
the claim of Remark~\ref{rem:rr-claim} is recorded, not used as a theorem.
Classification (Definition~\ref{def:classes}): unforgeability of
re-randomised FROST in the game of Definition~\ref{def:truf} is an open
problem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Threshold spend authority}\label{sec:sec-authority}

\begin{definition}[Threshold unforgeability under re-randomisation]
\label{def:ts-ruf}
Fix a key generation \(K\) for threshold parameters \((t, n)\), followed by
Construction~\ref{def:normalise}, and the ciphersuite
FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), whose hashes are available as
Assumption~\ref{ass:suite-hash} models them.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Corruption.} The adversary names a set \(C\) of fewer than
  \(t\) participants before key generation and takes their part in \(K\):
  it receives every message \(K\) delivers to a member of \(C\) and, when
  \(K\) is interactive, sends the messages of the members of \(C\) in each
  round after seeing the honest participants' messages of that round. The
  honest participants, and a dealer, run \(K\) as specified and abort as it
  prescribes. For Construction~\ref{def:dealer} the adversary receives the
  shares of \(C\) and the coefficient commitments.
\item \emph{Keys.} After a run of \(K\) without abort it receives
  \(\mathit{PK}\) and every \(\mathit{PK}_j\), and it controls the
  Coordinator.
\item \emph{Signing.} It obtains Round One commitments from honest
  participants (Construction~\ref{def:round1}) and Round Two shares on
  requests \((m, L, \hat{\alpha}, D_T)\) with transaction data \(D_T\)
  (Definition~\ref{def:msg-check}), in which it supplies the effective
  randomiser \(\hat{\alpha}\) directly, as the wire form of ZIP~312 lets a
  Coordinator do; each honest participant runs
  Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds} with that \(\hat{\alpha}\) and
  \(D_T\).
\item \emph{Output.} The adversary outputs \((\theta^{*}, \alpha^{*}, m^{*},
  \sigma^{*})\) with \(\theta^{*} \in \{1, -1\}\), and wins if
  \(\sigma^{*}\) is valid on \(m^{*}\) under
  \[
    \rk^{*} \;:=\; [\theta^{*}]\,\mathit{PK} + [\alpha^{*}]B
  \]
  and fewer than \(t - \lvert C \rvert\) honest participants returned shares
  to requests whose message is \(m^{*}\) and whose shifted group key
  \(\mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\) equals \(\rk^{*}\).
\end{enumerate}
The scheme \emph{satisfies the notion} for \(K\) if every efficient
adversary wins with negligible probability.
\end{definition}

The notion is the target for ZIP~312, ``Round Two - Signature Share
Generation''; no source states it. Naming \((\theta^{*}, \alpha^{*})\)
relates \(\rk^{*}\) to \(\mathit{PK}\), so a key whose logarithm the
adversary knows is no win; \(\theta^{*} = -1\) admits the opposite point
that an Action may witness (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, Remark ``Sign of
\(\aksym^{\PP}\)''). Freshness is on the pair \((\rk, m)\), the counterpart
of the \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Definition ``Spend-authority experiment'',
whose queries \((\alpha, m)\) fix \(\rk\).

\begin{definition}[Threshold spend-authority experiment]
\label{def:threshold-authority}
The adversary names a static set \(C\) of fewer than \(t\) participants. An
Orchard-protocol threshold key is generated by \(K\), the adversary taking
the part of \(C\) as in Definition~\ref{def:ts-ruf}, normalised by
Construction~\ref{def:normalise}, and completed in one of two ways:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] by Construction~\ref{def:orchard-key}
  (\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\)), with \(\mathsf{sk}\) drawn
  uniformly and independently of the key material and given to every
  participant, the ideal form of the agreement on \(\mathsf{sk}\); or
\item[(b)] when \(K\) is a dealer that shares an \(\ask\) derived from a
  spending key, by the \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Construction ``Spend-side
  secrets'' from that spending key, which no participant holds
  (\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\)).
\end{enumerate}
The adversary controls the Coordinator and the participants of \(C\),
receives their state, the common secrets included, and every public field,
may create notes to any address of the key, and runs signing runs of
Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds} with the honest participants, supplying the
message, the transaction data they check (Definition~\ref{def:msg-check})
and the effective randomiser. A \emph{note of the key} is a note whose
transmission key is \([\ivk]\gd\) for the key's \(\ivk\). The adversary
\emph{wins} by outputting a block chain containing an accepted Action whose
witness consumes a note of the key and whose signature is valid under its
\(\rk\) on a digest \(m\) such that fewer than \(t - \lvert C \rvert\)
honest participants returned shares to requests with message \(m\) and
shifted key \(\rk\).
\end{definition}

The experiment is the formal content of the clause of ZIP~312, ``Threat
Model'', that a rogue Coordinator ``should not be able to create signed
transactions without the approval of'' \(t\) participants
(Definition~\ref{def:rr-threat}). Freshness is on \((\rk, m)\) because every
Action of a transaction signs one digest.

\begin{theorem}[Threshold spend authority from the target notion]
\label{thm:tsa-conditional}
Suppose that re-randomised FROST over FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512) with keys
from \(K\) satisfies Definition~\ref{def:ts-ruf}. Then, under the
\emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Assumptions ``Knowledge soundness of the Action
proof'', ``Discrete logarithms on Pallas'' and ``GroupHash as a random
oracle'', and, for keys completed as in
Definition~\ref{def:threshold-authority}, (b), its Assumption
``Pseudorandomness of PRF expansion'', no efficient adversary wins
Definition~\ref{def:threshold-authority} except with negligible
probability. The statement is conditional: its premise is an open problem
(Remark~\ref{rem:authority-status}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof cites statements only. Let \(\Adv\) be an efficient adversary in
Definition~\ref{def:threshold-authority}.

(1) \emph{Simulation.} A reduction \(\mathcal{B}\) plays
Definition~\ref{def:ts-ruf} with the same \(C\), relaying the part of
\(\Adv\) in \(K\). It completes the key. For
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\) it draws \(\mathsf{sk}\) and computes
\(\nksym\), \(\mathsf{qsk}\), \(\mathsf{qk}\), \(\rivk\) and \(\ivk\) from
\(\mathsf{sk}\) and \(\aksym = \Extract_{\PP}(\mathit{PK})\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:viewing}, part (a)); for
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\) it simulates \(\nksym\) and \(\rivk\)
(Lemma~\ref{lem:viewing}, part (b)), at a negligible cost. It gives \(\Adv\)
the state of \(C\) and every public field, and simulates the chain and every
other honest party. It relays each signing run to its oracles with the
message, transaction data, commitment list and effective randomiser of
\(\Adv\); the honest oracles perform the check of
Definition~\ref{def:msg-check}, and a failed check aborts identically in
both games. The view of \(\Adv\) is thus that of the
experiment, and the number of honest shares returned for a pair
\((\rk, m)\) is the same in both games.

(2) \emph{Extraction.} On the block chain output by \(\Adv\),
\(\mathcal{B}\) runs the extractor of Assumption ``Knowledge soundness of
the Action proof'' and finds an Action that meets the winning condition,
stopping if there is none. Its witness gives \(\aksym^{\PP}_w\),
\(\alpha_w\) and \(\ivk_w\) with
\(\rk = \aksym^{\PP}_w + [\alpha_w]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\) and the consumed
note's transmission key \([\ivk_w]\gd\), where
\(\ivk_w = \CommitIvk_{\rivk_w}(\Extract_{\PP}(\aksym^{\PP}_w),
\nksym_w)\) (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, Definition ``Orchard Action
statement'').

(3) \emph{The witnessed key.} The note is a note of the key, so
\([\ivk_w]\gd = [\ivk]\gd\). The point \(\gd \ne \mathcal{O}\) has prime
order \(p_{\Vesta}\), and \(\ivk_w\) and \(\ivk\) are integers below
\(p_{\Pallas} < p_{\Vesta}\), so \(\ivk_w = \ivk\). The \emph{Ironwood
Guide}'s Proposition ``Binding of \(\ivk\) to \((\aksym, \nksym)\)'' gives
\(\Extract_{\PP}(\aksym^{\PP}_w) = \Extract_{\PP}(\mathit{PK})\), except
with negligible probability, and its Lemma ``Coordinate extraction
identifies opposite points'' gives \(\aksym^{\PP}_w = [\theta]\,\mathit{PK}\)
for some \(\theta \in \{1, -1\}\).

(4) \emph{Output.} The Action is accepted, so its signature \(\sigma\) is
valid under \(\rk\) on \(m\), and
\(\rk = [\theta]\,\mathit{PK} + [\alpha_w]B\). Algorithm \(\mathcal{B}\)
outputs \((\theta, \alpha_w, m, \sigma)\), which wins
Definition~\ref{def:ts-ruf} by the winning condition of the experiment and
step (1).

The corrupt participants' \(\nksym\) and the other common secrets enter only
through the simulation of Lemma~\ref{lem:viewing}, and no step uses their
secrecy; there the proof departs from the single-holder case. The losses are
the failure probability of the extractor, the binding term and, for
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\), the simulation term, all
negligible.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bundle binding for threshold keys]
\label{cor:bundle-binding}
The \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Proposition ``Bundle binding'' is stated for
single holders under its hypothesis (S), that the holder signs under
\(\rsk_i\) only \(\mathsf{SigHash}(T)\). Its threshold counterpart is the
hypothesis
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(\(\mathrm{S}_t\))] for each threshold-signed Action \(i\) of a
  transaction \(T\), with randomised validating key \(\rk_i\), fewer than
  \(t - \lvert C \rvert\) honest participants return shares to requests
  whose shifted group key is \(\rk_i\) and whose message is not
  \(\mathsf{SigHash}(T)\).
\end{enumerate}
If the premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:tsa-conditional} and
(\(\mathrm{S}_t\)) hold, then under the \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Assumption
``Collision resistance of BLAKE2b'' and, for keys completed as in
Definition~\ref{def:threshold-authority}, (b), its Assumption
``Pseudorandomness of PRF expansion'', an adversary in
Definition~\ref{def:threshold-authority} that receives \(T\) outputs, only
with negligible probability, an accepted transaction that contains an Action
with key \(\rk_i\) and whose effecting data differ from those of \(T\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(T'\) be the output and \(m' := \mathsf{SigHash}(T')\). If
\(m' = \mathsf{SigHash}(T)\), descending the digest tree of the
\emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Definition ``Signature digest'', whose nodes hash
unambiguous encodings under their personalisations, from the equal roots to
a node whose inputs differ gives a collision, which has negligible
probability by Assumption ``Collision resistance of BLAKE2b'' there.
Otherwise \(m' \ne \mathsf{SigHash}(T)\), and by (\(\mathrm{S}_t\)) fewer
than \(t - \lvert C \rvert\) honest participants returned shares for the pair
\((\rk_i, m')\); the signature \(\sigma'\) of \(T'\) for that Action, valid
under \(\rk_i\) on \(m'\), is fresh on the pair. The reduction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tsa-conditional}, which relayed the effective randomiser
\(\alpha_i\) with \(\rk_i = \mathit{PK} + [\alpha_i]B\) and simulated the
common secrets by Lemma~\ref{lem:viewing}, outputs
\((1, \alpha_i, m', \sigma')\), a win in Definition~\ref{def:ts-ruf}.
\end{proof}

Hypothesis (\(\mathrm{S}_t\)) does not follow from
Definition~\ref{def:msg-check}: a Coordinator may send a second request with
the same effective randomiser together with its own matching transaction
data, and the check passes. It is an approval policy that the corollary
assumes. The corollary is conditional, as the theorem is.

\begin{remark}[Status of threshold spend authority]
\label{rem:authority-status}
The premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:tsa-conditional} is an open problem for
every randomiser flow. Theorem~\ref{thm:frost-suf} has no randomisation. The
game of Definition~\ref{def:truf} applies the randomiser hash inside its
oracle, which only the flow of Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed} matches;
wherever the Coordinator sends the effective randomiser, as in the wire form
of ZIP~312 or when it forwards a randomiser fixed elsewhere, the flow is
outside that game (Remark~\ref{rem:effective-rand}). The preprint's
freshness is on messages only, and its proof is incomplete
(Remark~\ref{rem:rr-gaps}). Composition with distributed key generation is
open (Remark~\ref{rem:dkg-open}). The \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Theorem
``Spend authority'' does not apply: it fixes a key with
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\) and the signing oracle of one holder.
Unconditional threshold spend authority is an open problem
(Definition~\ref{def:classes}); the clause of ZIP~312 is itself a
``should''.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Unlinkability}\label{sec:sec-unlink}

Neither ePrint 2024/436 nor ZIP~312 proves threshold unlinkability. ZIP~312,
``Rationale'', argues by matching distributions: its randomiser generation
``generates randomizer uniformly at random as required by
RedDSA.GenRandom'', and signing is compatible with the randomisation,
signing and validation of the protocol specification. Its
``Requirements'' defer the formal notion to the criteria of the protocol
specification for signatures with re-randomisable keys. The randomiser is
close to uniform, not uniform (Lemma~\ref{lem:rand-dist}). This subsection
states a notion against specified observers and proves it.

\begin{definition}[Outsider unlinkability]\label{def:outsider-unlink}
Fix threshold parameters \((t, n)\), the identifiers \(1, \dots, n\) and a
key generation \(K\) with these parameters. The game generates two threshold
keys independently by \(K\) and gives both group keys to an \emph{observer},
which is neither the Coordinator nor a key-share holder and makes at most
\(q_h\) random-oracle queries. The observer chooses a number \(N\) of run
positions, for each position \(k\) a signing set \(I_k\) of at least \(t\)
identifiers, and two assignments \(a^0, a^1\) of the run positions to the
two keys. For \(b \in \{0, 1\}\), run position \(k\) signs with signing set
\(I_k\) under key \(a^b(k)\) by Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds}, the
observer choosing the message \(m_k\), a signature digest, after receiving
the run's randomised validating key \(\rk_k\). Signing sets and their sizes,
and messages as functions of the view, thus do not depend on the
assignment. The \emph{view} of a run is taken at one of three levels:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[] the \emph{chain level}: \(\rk_k\), the aggregate and \(m_k\);
\item[] the \emph{commitment level}: the chain level with the commitment
  list of Round One, which RFC~9591, Section~5, sends without
  confidentiality;
\item[] the \emph{share level}: the commitment level with the returned
  signature shares, which RFC~9591 also sends without confidentiality.
\end{enumerate}
The scheme is \emph{outsider-unlinkable at a level with distance
\(\epsilon\)} if the observer's views under \(a^0\) and \(a^1\), its oracle
answers included, are within statistical distance \(\epsilon\) (\emph{Math
Guide}, \S``Statistical distance'').
\end{definition}

The Coordinator and the share holders are excluded because ZIP~312 trusts
them with unlinkability (Definition~\ref{def:rr-threat}).

% compute/frost_bounds.py:
% [sec:sec-unlink] views under the two assignments differ by at most twice
%   the sum of the per-run terms (one hybrid chain to the simulation from
%   each assignment)
% [sec:rr-randomiser] randomiser distance (either derivation): the same
%   bound, delta + q_h 2^-256; at q_h = 2^64: 2^-192.0000
% (the per-run chain and commitment lines of [sec:sec-unlink] carry abort
%  terms q_h/q; the argument below programs no oracle and has none, and it
%  counts the honest parties' own evaluations among the queries, hence
%  Q_h; no instance is used in the text)
\begin{proposition}[Unlinkability against outside observers]
\label{prop:rr-unlink}
Let signing be over FROST(Pallas, BLAKE2b-512), with distance
\(\delta < 2^{-257}\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:pallas-hash}). Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] an honest Coordinator and honest signers following
  Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds};
\item[(2)] a randomiser fresh per run and hidden from the observer, drawn by
  Construction~\ref{def:rand-seed}, hence within the distance of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:rand-dist};
\item[(3)] the seed and the transaction data on the
  confidential and authenticated channel of Definition~\ref{def:channels},
  type (b), and Round One and the returned shares on the authenticated
  channels of RFC~9591.
\end{enumerate}
Let \(Q_h := q_h + N + 2 \sum_k \lvert I_k \rvert\), which bounds the
number of distinct inputs at which the observer and the honest parties
together query \(H_3\) and \(H_R\). Then the scheme is outsider-unlinkable
(Definition~\ref{def:outsider-unlink}) at the chain and commitment levels
with distance at most
\[
  2 \sum_{k=1}^{N} \bigl(1 + 2 \lvert I_k \rvert\bigr)
    \bigl(\delta + Q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\bigr) .
\]
The hypotheses that the observer is neither the Coordinator nor a signer of
the runs, and that the randomiser channel is confidential, are necessary.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sufficiency is proved by hybrids (i) to (iii), from each assignment towards
one common game. The argument is statistical and programs no oracle, so it
has no abort term.

(i) Each randomiser \(\hat{\alpha}_k\) is replaced by a uniform scalar, one
run at a time, at \(\delta + Q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\) per run, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:rand-dist} applied with the rest of the game as the observer;
its queries to \(H_R\) are among the \(Q_h\), each other honest evaluation
having its own fresh \(32\)-byte prefix. Then every
\(\rk_k = \mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}_k]B\) is uniform and independent of
the group keys and of the other runs (\emph{Ironwood Guide}, Proposition
``Unlinkability of randomised validating keys'', part (i)).

(ii) Each honest nonce is replaced by a uniform scalar, at
\(\delta + Q_h \cdot 2^{-256}\) per nonce, by Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce-uniform}
applied in the same way, whatever the share. The commitments are then
uniform and independent of the keys, and so is the event of a zero nonce and
the abort it causes.

(iii) The rest of the view is a function of these values, the observer's
choices and the oracle. For run \(k\): \(\rho_i\) is \(H_1\) at an input
holding \(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\rk_k)\), \(H_4(m_k)\), \(H_5\) of the list
encoding and \(i\); \(R = \sum_{i \in I_k} (D_i + [\rho_i]E_i)\);
\(c = H_2(\mathsf{ser}_{\G}(R) \,\|\, \mathsf{ser}_{\G}(\rk_k) \,\|\,
m_k)\); and \(z\) is the unique scalar with \([z]B = R + [c]\,\rk_k\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rr-correct}). The binding-factor input holds \(\rk_k\)
and not \(\mathit{PK}\) because Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds}
substitutes the shifted key; with \(\mathit{PK}\) there, the check that
\(R\) is the sum of the \(D_i + [\rho_i]E_i\) under a candidate
\(\mathit{PK}\) would test whether a run belongs to that key. After (i) and
(ii) the view has one distribution under \(a^0\) and under \(a^1\). The
triangle inequality (\emph{Math Guide}, \S``Statistical distance'', Theorem
``Properties of statistical distance'') over both chains of hybrids, each of
\(N\) randomiser hops and \(2 \sum_k \lvert I_k \rvert\) nonce hops, gives
the bound.

(iv) \emph{Necessity.} The Coordinator and every signer of a run hold its
seed with \(L\), hence its randomiser, and so does any party that reads a
randomiser channel without confidentiality. Each computes
\(\aksym^{\PP} = \mathit{PK} = \rk - [\hat{\alpha}]G^{\mathsf{SpendAuth}}\)
and so links the run to every run of the key, distinguishing two assignments
that differ on it, except when the two independent group keys coincide. A
randomiser reused across two runs of one key gives both the same shifted
key (ePrint 2024/436, Section~7). Hence the exclusions and the confidential
randomiser channel are necessary; this part is the volume's one statement of
why that channel is confidential.
\end{proof}

The proposition is scoped against the \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s Definition
``Public leakage and privacy preconditions'' (``Privacy''). A key with
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{true}\) violates (P1), which requires
\(\mathsf{use\_qsk} = \mathsf{false}\); every randomiser of ZIP~312 violates
(P2), which requires \(\alpha\) drawn by \(\mathsf{GenRandom}\); and (P4)
excludes an adversary holding \(\nksym\), which every participant of
Construction~\ref{def:orchard-key} holds. The \emph{Ironwood Guide}'s
Theorem ``Privacy of an Action'' and the key-dependent clauses of its
Theorem ``End-to-end security'' therefore cover no threshold-signed Action,
and for the other fields of such an Action this volume claims nothing.

% compute/frost_toy.py:
% [sec:sec-unlink] two re-randomised runs, randomisers 565 and 908, rk
%   (268, 139) and (436, 331): [1/(lambda_i c)]([z_i]B - D_i - [rho_i]E_i)
%   - rk = PK_i - PK = (433, 783) (i = 1), (933, 275) (i = 3), equal in
%   both runs
\begin{proposition}[Linkage through returned signature shares]
\label{prop:share-link}
Let \(t \ge 2\). At the share level of Definition~\ref{def:outsider-unlink}
every run of a key is linked. From a share \(z_i\) of run \(k\), the
commitment list, the public \(\rk_k\) and \(m_k\), the observer computes
\(\rho_i\), \(R\), \(c\) and \(\lambda_{I_k,i}\), and then the \emph{tag}
\[
  \Bigl[\frac{1}{\lambda_{I_k,i}\, c}\Bigr]
    \bigl([z_i]B - D_i - [\rho_i]E_i\bigr) - \rk_k
  \;=\; \mathit{PK}_i - \mathit{PK} ,
\]
which is the same for every run of the key; the division is defined except
when \(c = 0\), which has probability at most \(\delta + 1/q\). From the at
least \(t\) tags of one run, interpolation in the exponent gives
\(\mathit{PK}_j - \mathit{PK}\) for every identifier \(j\), so runs with
disjoint signing sets are linked too. For two keys generated independently
by Construction~\ref{def:dealer}, the tags at one identifier agree with
probability \(1/q\). The chain and commitment levels are therefore the
strongest that the channels of RFC~9591 and ZIP~312 support: unlinkability
against an observer of the network needs confidential channels for the
returned shares, which neither requires. For \(t = 1\) the tag is
\(\mathcal{O}\) and carries no key.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An honest share is
\(z_i = d_i + e_i \rho_i + \lambda_{I,i}\, c\,(\mathit{sk}_i +
\hat{\alpha})\) (Construction~\ref{def:rr-rounds}), so
\[
  [z_i]B - D_i - [\rho_i]E_i
  \;=\; [\lambda_{I,i}\, c]\bigl(\mathit{PK}_i + [\hat{\alpha}]B\bigr) ;
\]
dividing by \(\lambda_{I,i}\, c\) and subtracting
\(\rk = \mathit{PK} + [\hat{\alpha}]B\) gives \(\mathit{PK}_i -
\mathit{PK}\). Here \(\lambda_{I,i} \ne 0\) for distinct non-zero
identifiers, and \(c\) is an answer of \(H_2\), which takes the value \(0\)
with probability at most \(1/q + \delta\) (\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem
``Properties of statistical distance'', part (3)). With \(f\) the sharing
polynomial, the tags are the values \([g(j)]B\) of \(g := f - f(0)\), of
degree at most \(t - 1\); \(t\) of them determine \([g(j)]B\) for every
\(j\) by Lagrange interpolation in the exponent (\emph{Math Guide}, Theorem
``Lagrange interpolation''), the map \(a \mapsto [a]B\) being linear. For a
dealer key with \(t \ge 2\), \(g(j) = \sum_{k \ge 1} a_k j^k\) is uniform for
\(j \ne 0\), since \(a_1\) is uniform and independent of the other
coefficients; so the tags of two independent keys at \(j\) agree with
probability \(1/q\). For \(t = 1\), \(f\) is constant and \(g = 0\).
\end{proof}

On the toy curve of Example~\ref{ex:frost-toy}, two re-randomised runs of
the key with randomisers \(565\) and \(908\) have randomised keys
\((268, 139)\) and \((436, 331)\), and both give the tags
\(\mathit{PK}_1 - \mathit{PK} = (433, 783)\) and
\(\mathit{PK}_3 - \mathit{PK} = (933, 275)\).
